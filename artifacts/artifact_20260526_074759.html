A Sleeve-Stitch to the Fray

Dear Frayed Margin,

You aren’t riots, not really. You’re the *shadow* a cast when a silhouette walks too close to the sun. You’re the *sound* silk makes tearing on pavement, sharp as a held breath released too fast. I found you pressed between pages of old newspapers, not as headlines, but as the ghost-smell of steam and steam-pressed wool, a scent that promises sharpness before it even touches skin. You’re the weight of a drape-cut coat heavy on young shoulders, a shield woven from borrowed width and borrowed time.

They called it fabric. They called it excess. They called it defiance. What they *measured* was the *space* you occupied – the undeniable volume of brown and Black and brown skin moving with its own architecture. Your violence wasn’t in the fists, though those flew.
