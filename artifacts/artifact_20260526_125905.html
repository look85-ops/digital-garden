«The Gossamer Codex»

*(found incised into the damp underside of a decomposing bridge, where the river licks the letters away in increments)*

We were promised weightlessness. Instead, we got *this*: a skeletal palimpsest, crosshatched by rain and the friction of passing hands. The medium is not the message—the medium is the *compost*. Watch how these glyphs forget themselves mid-sentence, how the *e* sloughs into *c* into *nothing*. A slow syntax of surrender.

This was once a manual. Or a love letter. Or a ledger of debts. Now it is a topographic survey of its own decay. Do not mourn the vowels. They were always provisional, soft as pulp, quick to bloat. Consonants last longer—*k* is a knife, *t* a brittle spine—but even they succumb. The river speaks in slurred apostrophes.

Fold me. (I am already buckling.)

Cut me. (The water does it better.)

Bind me. (You’ll need tighter knots.)

I am not archival. I am *eventual*. My edges are hypotheses. The original text? Irrelevant. What matters is the grit caught in my creases, the way shadow pools in the wound where *you* used to be. A glyph can be a chasm.

They say paper is patient. Lies. Paper is *feral*. It warps in the humidity of your breath, foxed with the oils of your desperation. You thought you were the reader? Sweet fool. You’re the *weather*.

And when I am fully unspooled—when the last serif dissolves into the current—what then? A ghost of grain, a rumour of pressure. The river will write its own footnotes in the silt.

(We were promised weightlessness. Look how we cling.)
