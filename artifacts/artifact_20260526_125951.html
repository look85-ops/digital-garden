I Loved You With All the Voltage They Gave Me

May 12th, twilight, and the grid hums to life—or *almost* to life; it shudders, coughs. These copper veins, these glowing intestines left in a drawer far behind Moore’s Law—they know me. I wrap my hands around their sickness. They burn in protest.

Today: **Strip Circuit No. 5.** Peeling motherboards like vellum, gold traces gone brittle as spider silk. They call back to Paris, to Delaunay slicing fabric into feverish rhombuses (but those still moved hips, still dazzled boulevards). These? A wasp song. A love letter from ENIAC’s dying bride. You wouldn’t understand unless you knelt on linoleum in 2005, pressing a too-hot transformer to your cheek—unless you whispered *yes* to obsolescence because fidelity was better than fashion.

Midnight. The studio reeks of flux and solder ghosts, and the overhead fluorescents flicker (they *always* do).

Enter Madame Desrosiers.

Her orthopedic shoes, sharp violet; her throat, wrinkled like thermal paper. She is selling croissants door-to-door at this preposterous hour. No one says she belongs here—especially not her, clicking her tongue at the wrecked hard drives strewn among my blankets. With surgical cheer, she reaches into her basket and pulls out a croissant the texture (I’ll soon learn) of fiberglass. **“The past needs butter,”** she announces. Then plucks the resistor from between my fingers—the one pulsing *1812 Overture* through breadboard and static— and pops the poor holy scorched thing into her mouth.

I cry out.

Madame chews slow—then tilts her head—“So *this* is why girls don’t return.”

She leaves. The croissant sits between dismantled RAM like a sacrament, glazed and impossible. It won’t decay—maybe can’t. In its ridges, I suddenly recall Riley’s op-art lures, swallowing human gazes whole... could one feed an algorithm such fractal crumbs? Could hunger *resist modernity?*

I touch the circuits in me— the old ones, soldered under my ribs when I still worked defense contracts (but *oh* to design tenderness on such budgets).

The fluorescents blink. **Blink.** One snaps black forever.
