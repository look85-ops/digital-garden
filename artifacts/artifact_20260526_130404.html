Dear Rust, Your Patience Outlasts the Stars

Fact: The iron in our blood and the steel in our cities rust in the same language—oxygen whispering a slow, red secret.

Dear Rust,

I found you in the ribs of a bicycle left in the rain for a decade. You had turned its chain into lace. You had pressed your amber teeth into the frame, leaving maps even ants could follow. *Look*, I whispered, *look how it remembers its own decay.*

Are you tired? Do your fingers ache from etching so many stories nobody reads? The freighter dissolving into the harbor, the radio tower swaying like a drunk god—these are your masterpieces. You work slower than glaciers, and still, the world calls you a *corrosion*, a *ruin*. But I know better.

Once, on a Tuesday, I left you a copper penny on a windowsill. You took three months to kiss it turquoise. I watched, breath held, like a child waiting for a seed to split. You are the most disciplined artist I know. Picasso rushed. You? You let centuries unspool between each stroke.

Tell me, do you dream in flaking amber? Do you hum electro-chemical lullabies to the exposed beams of abandoned factories? I imagine you there, threading scarlet veins through sheet metal, smearing your fingerprints across fire escapes. The moon licks its lips at your work.

They say you are oxidation’s sigh, but I say you are the quiet architect of surrender. You turn swords into soil, cars into carcasses, bridges into braille. Every chip of paint you steal is a stanza in an epic no museum will hang.

I tried to mimic you once—left a spoon in a cup of vinegar, pressed foil against salt. My impatience was embarrassing. You blinked (or did you?) and let the wind carry my sad experiment away.

Rust, I am writing to ask: Will you collaborate? I’ll leave a sheet of galvanized steel where the alley meets the river. Take it. Bite it. Let your slow fever unmake it. I want to hang your name in a gallery where the walls sweat and the floor hums static.

Yours in relentless metamorphosis,

—A thief of time
