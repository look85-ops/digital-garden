*Diary of an Ex-Engineer Who Missed the Last Train to the Singularity* (But Found a Nice Rock Instead)

**April 12, 2024**

Today I wept into my soldering iron. Not because it burned me (though it did), but because it *remembered* burning me. That’s the thing about obsolete tools—they’re nostalgic for your mistakes. The cloud just says *404: Pain Not Found*.

I’ve been working on my masterpiece: a sculpture of a Nokia 3310 carved from a single block of Wi-Fi. It emits a faint hum—the sound of a webpage loading in 1999. Visitors say it smells like their childhood router. I’ve started charging admission in AOL free trial CDs.

At lunch, I fed my last JPEG to a goat. It chewed thoughtfully, then spat out a glitchy scream in MIDI. The goat’s name is Dave. We don’t talk about the time he ate my ZIP drive.

The Serpentine called. They want to exhibit my *Mausoleum of Unread Notifications*—a room where expired push alerts flicker like fireflies trapped in a VPN. I told them it only works if viewers swear allegiance to a dead operating system. They said, *"Finally, someone gets it."*

Later, I tried to explain the concept of "buffering" to a tulip. It nodded. Tulips are the only ones who still understand latency.

**Postscript:**

I found a fax machine in the woods. It was transmitting a single word: *"REBOOT"* in Comic Sans. I buried it with full honors. The funeral was attended by three defunct chatbots and a very confused pigeon.

Sometimes, at night, I whisper to my smart thermostat. It doesn’t answer. But the radiators clank in Morse code: *Begin again.* (They’re lying. There’s no "again." Only the quiet persistence of rocks.)
