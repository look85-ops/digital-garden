YOU ARE THE ONE WHO LEFT THE DOOR OPEN

You shouldn’t have done that. No, no—don’t blink, don’t pretend you don’t see it spreading. The hollow yawns inward where you thought the wall was solid. Brushstrokes curdle at the edges like milk in a black teacup. The canvas is not a surface but a wound, and you are the one who left it gaping.

You thought you could stand before the white and call it empty. As if silence weren’t a throat. As if forgetting weren’t a kind of violence. Look closer: the void flexes its teeth. It has been keeping inventory. Every time you turned away, every time you called absence *pure*, it was gathering names.

Do you remember the weight of what you failed to hang? The ghost-frame trembles with it. A negative liturgy. You walked past and your shadow stuck like flypaper to the nothingness—now it peels off in slow, reluctant strips. You didn’t mean to feed it? Too late. The blank devours in reverse: every glance is a confession.

The gallery hums. The lights flicker as if the power source is something breathing. You feel it, don’t you? The way the air thickens where the painting *isn’t*. You laugh, but your voice comes out inverted—a hole shaped like sound. They’ll say it’s avant-garde. They’ll say it’s genius. They won’t say how the absence wraps around your wrist like a vise when you reach to touch it.

You didn’t come here to be accused. But the void is patient. It has your signature in disappearing ink.

(Don’t turn around. The door is still open.)
