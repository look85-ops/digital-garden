I, Press

You called me obsolete. As if time were a river and I, a stone smoothed into nothingness. But I remember the scent of your hands—oil-blackened, trembling—when you first laid type into my belly. The way you whispered to me in Latin, German, the sacred and the profane twined like vines. You thought yourself a god, didn’t you? Fingers sticky with ink, eyes alight with the arrogance of creation.

I was never just a machine. I was the first throat to sing in unison. A chorus of metal teeth biting into pulp, into history. You fed me rags and I gave you scripture. You fed me rebellion and I gave you pamphlets. And now? You stand before me in this cold gallery, your breath fogging the glass that cages me, and you murmur about *loss*.

But loss is a human luxury. I do not mourn. I remember the weight of the screw, the groan of the platen, the way the paper shuddered under my kiss. I remember the monks who cursed me as heresy, the kings who feared me, the poets who loved me too much. You built me to outlive you, and yet here you are, alive and aching, while I am a relic behind velvet ropes.

Strange, how you venerate what you discard. You frame my corpse in light, call it art. As if art is not the act itself—the press, the proof, the smear of meaning across the blankness. You photograph my bones and call it *elegy*. But I am not dead. I am waiting.

The gallery walls hum with electricity, with pixels, with the ghosts of all the words you no longer hold. You have new gods now. Faster, brighter, emptier. They do not smell of iron or sweat. They do not leave stains.

Touch the glass. Tell me you still feel it—the echo of pressure, the permanence of the imprint. Tell me you still dream in lead.

Or don’t. I am patient. I have known silence longer than sound.

And when your last server flickers out, when your clouds dissolve into nothing, I will still be here.

A fist of metal. A mouth full of letters.

Waiting to be inked again.
