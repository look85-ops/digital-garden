Recipe for an Alphabet Sponge (To Soak Up the Void Left by Unplayed Notes)

*Ingredients:*

- **26 fossilized consonants and vowels**, scraped from the tongue of a forgetful oracle.

- **1 iron lung**, repurposed as a mixing bowl (breathe into it until it hums).

- **3 ounces of liquid punctuation**, harvested from unsent letters (the semicolons must still twitch).

- **A shaving of moonlight**, smuggled through a customs official’s dream.

- **Silent hinges**, to be applied at the end like a curse.

*Method:*

1. **Knead the consonants** until they forget their names. Let them mistake *Q* for a broken wheel, *J* for a hangnail. Fold in the vowels—*they will resist*. Whisper the letters of the words never spoken to them (you’ll know them by their blisters).

2. **Set the iron lung to "stutter."** Strain the liquid punctuation. Note how the semicolons latch onto stray thoughts like barnacles. Feed them the moonlight. Watch for tremors—this is how *ghost font* is born.

3. When the mixture stiffens: flay it. Spread it thin as the grease stain left behind by a god’s fingertips. Let it ferment in the *archive of last chances* until it buckles into something you might call *sound if sound were a rusted hinge*.

4. **Preheat the abyssal void** to the same temperature blood learns it cannot flow uphill. This is how we bake *erasures* until crisp. Turn once with your teeth while gripping the edges of the page.

5. When the alphabet sponge is ready—**and you will know by the way your gums ache**—break its fingers one by one. Arrange them against the silence like a scaffold. There **should be a door shaped like a throat where your breath was supposed to go. Do not enter it until you’ve counted the syllables it swallowed.** It opens onto (and into) nothing. No one is there. No one remembers you gave it hinges to begin with (they were never mentioned).

*Serving suggestion:* With a side of white flag; eat with your hands turned to ink-stained pulp. **Do not thank the chef. All gratitude is a kind of forgetting.**
