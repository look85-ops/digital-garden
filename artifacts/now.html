THE UNZIPPING OF HEREDITY

*(Scene: A dimly lit laundromat at 3 AM. TWO IDEAS sit in adjacent dryers, tumbling endlessly. Their voices hum with static, like poorly tuned radios.)*

**IDEA 1:**

Tell me again about the moths.

**IDEA 2:**

Oh, the usual tragedy. They ate through Grandmother’s wedding dress, but left the stains untouched. Epigenetic stains, you understand—little methyl groups clinging to the silk like shy children to a parent’s leg.

**IDEA 1:**

So the dress was…

**IDEA 2:**

A haunted house! But the ghosts were just your grandfather’s smoking habit, coughing politely in the attic of your ribosome.

*(Pause. A sock falls from the ceiling.)*

**IDEA 1:**

LifeWear should come with a warning label: “Caution: This garment may dissolve upon contact with memory.”

**IDEA 2:**

Or better yet—*(whispers)*—a single thread that unravels only when you laugh.

*(IDEA 1 begins to laugh. IDEA 2 does not. The reader, perhaps, does.)*

**IDEA 1:**

Wait. If clothing disappears so life can appear…

**IDEA 2:**

Then nudity is just your ancestors’ unfinished homework.

*(The dryers shudder. A single blue light flickers. From the lint trap, a voice:)*

**THE LINT TRAP (UNSEEN):**

But who folds the proteins?

**IDEA 1:**

Ah! The ultimate fast fashion critique.

*(Suddenly, the dryers stop. Silence. Then—)*

**IDEA 2:**

Tell me, do you smell burning nucleotides?

**IDEA 1:**

No, just the scent of my chromosomes ironing themselves into pleats.

*(A passing tumbleweed of lint rolls by, whispering the title of this piece. The reader leans in to hear—but the joke, of course, is already in their hands.)*
