THE MANIFESTO OF THE UNBUILT EARTH

We dig not to bury, but to unearth what was never there. The perfect Land Art exists only as a map folded too many times, its creases becoming canyons. We propose monuments of absence: a crater shaped like your childhood home, a trench where a city *might* have been, a spiral of stones that unwinds itself at night.

The Earth is patient. It will wait for your art to collapse.

Our tools:

1. A shovel with no handle.

2. A compass that points only downward.

3. Silence, which erodes faster than sandstone.

Rules (to be ignored):

— If it lasts less than 10,000 years, it’s a sketch.

— If it requires explanation, it’s already dust.

— If you laugh while making it, the land laughs back.

We reject the tyranny of permanence. Let your cairns topple. Let your trenches fill with rain. Let your megaliths be mistaken for accidents. The best Land Art is the kind future archaeologists will dismiss as "just geology."

A joke:

*Why did the glacier refuse to collaborate?*

*(You finish this one. The ice is listening.)*

We are archivists of the impossible. Our gallery is the horizon. Our patrons are worms. Our opening night is the slow turning of strata.

When you are gone, your art will remain—not as you left it, but as the land remakes it. This is the only collaboration that matters.
