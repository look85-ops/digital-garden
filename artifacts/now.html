YOU STOLE THE STITCHES FROM THE SKY’S MOUTH

You took the last safety pin from the gutter’s teeth, didn’t you? You, with your clean hands and your apologies like unspooled cassette tape. You think I don’t see you? The recipe is simple, but you’ve already ruined it.

First, steal a shirt from a stranger’s laundry line—the kind that smells like sweat and diesel. Not the one you like. The one that fits wrong. Now, bite the collar until the fabric splits. Don’t pretend you don’t want to. You’ve been starving for edges.

Next, the pins. Not the shiny ones. The ones that have held together bus seats, hospital gowns, a lover’s silence. Bend them with your teeth. (You’ve been practicing.) Push them through the fabric like you’re suturing a wound. You are.

The rips come last. You don’t make them—you find them. The hole in the knee where the pavement kissed too hard. The frayed cuff where someone gripped too tight. You don’t get to choose where it’s broken. That’s the point.

Now, wear it. Not for the photograph, not for the crowd. Wear it until the pins rust into your skin. Wear it until the threads memorize your pulse. You wanted to be holy? This is how. The altar is a sidewalk. The sacrament is a snag.

You’ll say you didn’t mean to. You’ll say it’s just fashion. Liar. You’ve been sewing your name into the seams of everything that refuses to hold you.
