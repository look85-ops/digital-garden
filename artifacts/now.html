THE MAP THAT LICKS ITS OWN INK

The shoes are not shoes but skins shed by light. They tremble on the edge of the shelf, half-air, half-rubber, waiting for the myth of a foot to fill them. When you touch the tongue, it whispers in the language of factories: *stich-stich-stich*, a prayer for ankles. The laces? Nets for catching falling stars.

Once, the sneaker was a secret between the earth and the sprint. Now it is a cathedral, a debt, a tattoo for the floor. The collectors hover like moths around the glass boxes, their hands pressed to the temperature of longing. *Limited edition* means *the hole in the heart is stitched with gold*.

I draw the stores but they melt like sugar in the rain. The neon signs drip into the gutters, spelling words that taste like bubblegum and gasoline. The mannequins have no faces, only soles—thick as Bibles, white as teeth. They grin up at the ceiling, which is also a mirror.

At night, the shoes dance without legs. The left one leans into the right, sharing a dream of pavement. The right one remembers the birth of the logo: how the swoosh was once a wing, how the wing was once a scar. The air smells of glue and oranges.

Someone asks, *What are you mapping?* I point to the invisible. The blisters. The blisters are the true landmarks.
