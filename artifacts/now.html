*The Subtraction of Shadows*

---

**A:** *(examining an absence)*

This silence—did we invent it? Or did it invent us?

**B:** *(counting missing fingers)*

No one counts the gaps between teeth. Why?

**A:**

Because numbers fear zero. It laughs at their endings.

**B:** *(holding a vacant mirror)*

I tried to name the reflection that isn’t there. The word turned inside out.

**A:**

Language is a net for catching ghosts. Holes included.

**B:** *(drawing a chalk door on the wall)*

What happens if we open it?

**A:**

Then we become the hinges.

*(A pause that grows teeth.)*

**B:** *(whispering to the teeth)*

Shh. You’re not supposed to exist.

**A:** *(grinning with closed lips)*

Existence is overrated. Absence composes better music. Listen—

*(They listen to the sound of a spine un-writing itself.)*

**B:**

Clever. But how do you erase a shadow without touching the light?

**A:** *(folding a piece of darkness origami-style)*

Like this. Now it’s a bird that never learned to fly.

**B:** *(watching the not-bird dissolve)*

Bravo. Another masterpiece for the Museum of Almost.

**A:** *(bowing to nothing in particular)*

Curator of voids at your service.

**B:** *(placing an imaginary crown on A’s head)*

Long live the king of nothing.

*(The crown falls through their head and shatters into questions.)*

---

*Note: The dialogue leans into the absurdity of defining what isn’t, treating "nothing" as both medium and punchline. The recurring motif of gestures toward voids (folding darkness, un-writing) mirrors the composer’s role in shaping silence.*
