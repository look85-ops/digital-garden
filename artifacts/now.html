# **The Gold in the Missing

Once, I painted the sky so blue it bruised the clouds. Now I paint with the holes things leave behind. A bowl shatters, and I don’t reach for glue—I reach for the *absence* where the pieces were. The cracks are rivers. The gold is the ghost of what held them together.

Kintsugi is backwards, I think. The gold isn’t repair—it’s the *memory* of breaking. A seam of light where the world fell apart. I tried to mend a teacup once, but my hands were too full of silence. The shards stayed separate, whispering to each other in a language of gaps.

A child asked me, *Why do you love broken things?* I told them: *Because they know how to hold emptiness better than whole things do.* The child laughed and said, *That’s silly. Nothing holds emptiness.* But they were wrong. The best things do. A breath. A pause between heartbeats. The space where a tooth used to be.

I collect missingness. The shadow of a bird that never lands. The echo of a name no one calls anymore. Once, I found a crack in the air—thin as a hairline fracture—and pressed my ear to it. It hummed like a forgotten lullaby.

The lie: I never loved color. (The truth: I loved it so much I let it go. Some loves are too loud to keep.)

Now, I paint with the quiet. The gold is just a rumor. The cracks are the art.
