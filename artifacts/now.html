### **RECEPTION OF THE ERROR MARGIN SAINT

*An Edible Liturgy Disappearing Into Scorch Marks

(dedicated to the last candle in the scriptorium that refuses to drown in its own wax.)*

1. **Ink as first digestion:** Prepare vellum from the membranes of failed interpretations. Stretch the skin over whispered vowels. Do not cure; let it bloat softly, a cathedral in slow collapse.

2. **Scatter the misreads. Use only those letters that slipped sideways through Gutenberg’s press—the dropped serifs, the orphans curled like tongues flicking away from benediction. The "d" that insists itself an "a." (All languages decay, but some choose the manner: smoke or teeth.)

3. **Distill silence five times:** Between printing and page, catch the moan of the unset word. Hang it over a fire of broken lead type. When the liquid weeps copper, add the shadow of an illuminator’s last gilding brushstroke. A ghost must season with something heavier than gold.**

4. **For typefruit:** Press spoiled grapes—those burst in transit, deemed unfit for holy wine—into tiny black fists. Arrange them along the margin where annotations once disputed salvation with the main text. A communion wine is more honest as ink already fermented between fists/faith and letters rot sweet. (Pauses: these will become the footnotes you choke on later.)

5. **Heat the errors:** Scatter pages torn from an incomplete glossary over smoldering debates—scholarly kindling burns quickest at the edges. WARNING: your hands here are letters now. All fingertips italicize when in danger. No, slow the blaze—we are baking a text, not recreating Alexandria.

SERVING METHOD: Only with knives blunt-edged enough to mimic turning vellum. Consume under flickering suspicion, ideally with guests who cannot agree where the alphabet becomes god or wound. Offer second servings in ink-stained palms; let them smear across shared teeth. No translation survives this intact.
