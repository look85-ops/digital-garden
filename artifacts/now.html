# *The Wink Where There Was None

At first, the game was simple: watch the light decide if it was a wave or a swarm of tiny horses. But light—*oh, light!*—it gets shy when stared at. Look too hard, and it crumbles into dots like sugar dissolving on the tongue. Stop looking, and it stretches into a sea. *What secret is it keeping between blinks?*

In the Gallery of the Third Breath, children lay under glass floors, pressing their cheeks to see the dark. Below their breath-fog, photons trembled. One child swears she saw the world stitch itself shut when no one was watching. The curator (that’s me, or will be, or won’t, depending—), nodded. The future smells of burnt sugar and unanswerable things.

Someone drew the experiment in the dust: two slits, yes, but scratched deeper, like the eyes of a puppet too many hands had touched. Behind them, the ghost of all possible futures swayed, un-chosen, untorn yet from time’s hip. I held my breath, and the ghost held hers—then doubled, tripled into maybes the second I winked at her, *go on.*

When the scientists whispered, *Light changes* *when observed*, they forget another fact; every watcher splits too. Unwatched, the dark curls up content, singing lullabies backwards in wavelengths children used to crawl through naked. They say eyes are cameras, but they’re really hands, pulling the waves closer, asking a silent, shameful favor.

Now?

Now here’s your knife, small researcher, *here:* go carve possibility back open.

And here’s her grin again—spread sideways like light through glass—asking, *What won’t you see to see me?

Begin! Begin with blinking*.
