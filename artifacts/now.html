Dear Tattoo as Archive: A Love Letter in Permanent Ink (That You’ll Have to Finish Yourself)

Oh, you indelible interloper, you chronicler of bad decisions and sacred vows alike—how dare you turn our skin into a palimpsest of grocery lists and grief? You are the librarian who shelves our memories in a Dewey Decimal system of scars, the archaeologist who digs up our pasts with a needle’s precision. And yet, you refuse to be erased, even when we beg you to fade.

I admire your audacity. You are the only museum that charges admission in blood and still gets repeat visitors. You are the graffiti artist who tags the body with such conviction that even time hesitates to buff you out. (Though, let’s be honest, some of you belong in the *restored* section of the Louvre—patchy, blurred, and missing context.)

You are the archive that laughs at fire, flood, and forgetting. A love letter to a lost summer? You’ll keep it. A drunken bet? You’ll keep that too. A name we swore we’d never regret? Oh, you’re *especially* keeping that. You are the ultimate *I told you so* etched in living tissue.

But here’s the thing: you are also the punchline we don’t see coming. You are the joke that only the reader completes, because every tattoo is a story waiting for its ending. (And if you think that’s not true, just wait until you’re 80 and your "tribal armband" looks like a melted rubber band.)

So here’s to you, Tattoo as Archive—keeper of our secrets, our sins, and our stubbornness. You are the only autobiography we’ll never finish writing, because every new mark is a footnote to a story we’re still living. And if that’s not art, then neither is the Mona Lisa’s smirk.

Yours in perpetuity,

The One Who Forgot to Check the Spelling
