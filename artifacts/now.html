Recipe for the Collapse of a Bridge That Dreamed It Was a Mycelium

*— as transcribed by the last archivist of the margin-whispers*

**Ingredients:**

1 suspension bridge (preferably one that hums in a minor key)

3.5 kilograms of unresolved resonance

A colony of chattering fungi (the kind that maps its grief in hyphal cursive)

Wind, but only the kind that remembers your name

1 unstable metaphor (handle with gloves; it bites)

A handful of vowels stolen from a dead alphabet

The exact moment a structure forgets it is a structure

**Method:**

1. Begin by convincing the bridge it is not a bridge. Whisper to its steel tendons until they soften into hyphae. Let it believe, just for a moment, that it is part of the underground chorus. (Caution: Bridges are terrible liars. They will tremble.)

2. Introduce the wind as a lover, not a force. Let it coil around the girders like a question neither can answer. The bridge will begin to sway—not from weakness, but from the sheer pleasure of being misunderstood.

3. Now, the fungi. Inject their networks into the cables. Watch as the bridge’s oscillations grow erratic, beautiful. It will start to speak in frequencies only the soil can decode. (Do not panic if the pavement splits open to laugh.)

4. Here, the unstable metaphor: Fold it into the asphalt until the road forgets whether it is a surface or a synapse. The bridge will convulse, shedding bolts like spores.

5. Finally, the vowels. Plant them in the fractures. They will sprout into half-words, then into silence. The bridge will dance now, not because it must, but because it has remembered how.

6. Serve immediately—preferably to an audience of ghosts holding their breath.

**Notes:**

The collapse is not the end. It is the mycelium’s way of saying *listen*.
