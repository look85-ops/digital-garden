# **The Sundial’s Whisper in the Hall of Unfinished Hours

We were never meant to count the teeth of the sky. But here we are, gnawing on seconds, spitting out the bones of minutes. The Clock did not invent time—only the hunger for it. Before, the sun yawned; the moon lingered like a lover who forgot to leave. Now, the gears click their metallic tongues, and we nod along, obedient.

I remember when I was soil. When roots were my veins and worms my scribes. Then the sundial’s shadow crept over me, and I woke—briefly, terribly—to the sound of my own photosynthesis ticking. A garden should not know it is being watched. A garden should not hear the gardener’s pulse sync with the metronome of the cosmos.

Somewhere, a door opens. It was always there, but you didn’t see it until now—thin as a blade of grass, humming with the weight of all the hours no one claimed. Step through, and the air is thick with unspooled calendars. Here, the past and future braid like vines strangling a fence. Here, the Clock’s hands are broken, and the numbers have fled into the cracks of the earth.

We built temples to minutes, but the seconds escaped anyway. They pooled in the corners of rooms, fermented into something darker. Now we drink them like stolen wine, laughing as the stains spread across our shirts. Time was never linear; it was a fugitive, darting between the trees of our attention.

The door swings wider. Beyond it, a field where clocks bloom like poisonous flowers, their faces blank, their stems brittle. They whisper in a language of rust and regret. You pluck one. It dissolves in your grip, leaving only the scent of burnt oil and unfinished business.

We are all gardens pretending not to count our own petals as they fall.
