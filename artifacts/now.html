EXISTING UNTOUCHED LIKE A STRAY TAG

October 17: I sewed a jacket with no seams today.

It billowed like smoke above a Zen rock garden.

The sleeves choked me before lunch.

Absence has a gravity to it.

Each pocket I unpick makes the garment more inevitable.

The wool screams while pretending to comply.

Sachiko at the atelier calls this "losing your edge against the infinite."

Her hair is a black hole consuming department store pamphlets.

She is the least interesting hole in Kyoto.

There is elegance in garments designed to be overlooked.

My hanger holds more intelligence than any mirror.

Still, reflections sneak beneath the doorway by twilight.

I drank matcha until time bent behind me.

Uniqlo mannequins are plotting the extinction of shadows.

They keep leaving their heads unlit after close.

Somewhere, a cashier is folding infinity in thirds.

The fitting rooms are breeding grounds for unspoken apologies.

No one exits taller from a shrine they didn't enter.

Tomorrow, I'll mend a coat that never existed.

Moral obligation lingers like iron scuffmarks on marble.

We all disintegrate under the weight of our pockets becoming altars.

Postscript: Three buttons left without confession.

The air was charged, or perhaps I've swallowed static again.

This diary isn't a white page—it's an abandoned collar turned to dusk.
