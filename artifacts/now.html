How to Bake a City That Will Crumble Beautifully

You wake with the taste of wet concrete in your mouth. The dreamer (not you) remembers this:

1. **Begin with a foundation of forgetting.** Pack the earth tight with unanswered letters, municipal memos, and the small bones of rodents. Press down until the soil hums.

2. **Mix time unevenly.** Let decades curdle in the corners. Stir in warped vinyl records, their grooves worn smooth by fingers that no longer exist. Fold in rust like a thief stealing hinges.

3. **Preheat the sky to the temperature of a fever dream.** Watch for cracks in the atmosphere—these are your vents. The oven must breathe.

4. **Pour the streets in slow, deliberate strokes.** Asphalt should ripple like a black tongue. Let it cool into fissures, each one a sentence half-spoken.

5. **Sprinkle glass shards where the children played.** They will glitter like false teeth.

6. **Insert the people last.** They are the most perishable ingredient. Arrange them mid-step, mid-laugh, mid-scream. Do not bake them too long—they should flake apart at the lightest touch.

7. **Let the whole thing collapse in stages.** A balcony first, then a post office, then a marriage. The dreamer insists this part is vital: the ruin must feel inevitable, but never predictable.

8. **Serve cold.** The best abandoned places are eaten by the air, bite by bite.

You wake again. The dreamer (still not you) licks their lips and whispers: *"I followed the recipe exactly. Why does it still taste like longing?"*
