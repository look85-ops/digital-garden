The Furrow and the Fire

*—Diary of the Archivist of Unmade Things, Fragment 37*

Today, the collider hums a lullaby to the dead. I dreamt of the first plow again—not the wooden thing that split the earth, but the *idea* of it, a scar that never healed. The LHC is its echo, a god’s fingernail dragged through the dirt of existence. I pressed my palm to the tunnel’s cold flank and felt the vibration of ten thousand harvests, the groan of civilizations that never were.

They say the plow birthed time. Linear, obedient. But the collider spits time back like a child refusing porridge. Here, in the Chapel of Perpetual Departure, we worship the splinters of what might have been: a world where the plow was a brush, painting famine in gold leaf; where the collider’s collisions sprouted wheat instead of quarks.

I found a scrap of an old text today—*"the earth remembers the plow’s teeth, but the sky forgets the stars’ names."* Nonsense, probably. Or prophecy. The distinction is moot here. I fed it to the archive’s mouth (a slit in the wall that whispers in the voice of a drowned librarian). It coughed back a single seed. I planted it in the shadow of the collider’s ring. Tomorrow, it may grow into a theorem or a thorn.

The technicians think I’m mad. They measure the spin of particles; I measure the weight of absences. What does it mean to till the soil of the cosmos? To split the atom like a clod of dirt? Yesterday, a beam of protons veered off-course and burned a symbol into the containment wall—a crooked line, half-plow, half-lightning. The scientists wiped it away. I licked the residue. It tasted of burnt milk and old wars.

At dusk, I walked the ring like a furrow. The collider is a plow that never stops moving, never turns the earth, never rests. It gnaws at the edges of reality, hungry for the uncreated. I wonder if the first farmer felt this—not pride, not purpose, but the vertigo of a tool that outgrows its use.

The archive murmurs in my sleep: *"You are the scratch that never heals."* I wake with soil under my nails.
