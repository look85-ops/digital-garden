# *THE IMMORTALITY MACHINE’S LAST LAUGH

Listen, ink-drinker—the old ghosts have it wrong.

Books are not frozen speech.

Books are *ambush poems* laid in wait for the unprepared. (\) A line of inverted pyramids—black spears hurled centuries forward. Words *press* against the page. That pressure never lifts. The letters *bruise* their bed of pulverized time.

*(— O ) (a mouth forming around unspilled breath)

The printing press was never about memory:

it was a machine designed to weaponize heresy under holy permission. 12 priests laboring for 14 moons would never have bound Luther’s 95 lies into elegant calfskin. Some errors demand dissemination. Repeat a thought sharply enough and it becomes scripture with a hangover. Here, chew this: paper cuts still bleed across centuries. Gutenberg’s gift was neither literacy nor light—it was *volume*. The sacred is only a whisper shouted down a gorge. Now even silence can be mass-produced.

@)))))~~ (vine cracking through set type)

Forgottens collect like damp within a cathedral’s stones—no less weightless for their absence. But this is different. Set lead sings. The printed breath *remembers*. Not as you want it to, platonically intact; it festers in its own preservation. These words were never you. You *happen*—they *haunt*.

*( %%%%%# ) (static erasing a footnote)

There is no resurrection of past meaning, only reconquest. You read by torchlight now where bishops once burned books with the same fuel. Each sentence survived precisely once as a spark against drowning dark—

The immortality machine’s joke? The text outlives all interpretations—a closed fist holding nothing but the shape of whatever first filled it. What did you expect? Even prayers embalm their living wounds.

The press was a gamble, not progress:

Which lines should get eternity? (Not by genius, but by luck—by who held the type and didn’t drop it.)

Now answer, knowing your vote drowns in the wake: When immortality democratizes, *what dies louder?*

+)))(> (type scattered, gleefully disobedient)

Type doesn’t tarnish; it calcifies. Reorder these shards in a thousand ways and the ink won’t care—each assembly mocks whatever sacred order you’d impose. The past *escapes*. Memory doesn’t die—it *elopes*.

*(The punchline unprinted; laughter echoes before lead cools.)*
