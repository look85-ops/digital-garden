The Gospel According to a Thumb Scrolling Through Itself

[The ceiling fans begin to weep softly into their own rotation, catching tears in paper cups labeled *Ad Revenue*.]

Blessed are the filtered, for they shall inherit the Wi-Fi.

Blessed are the unboxing videos, for they reveal the kingdom of *more*.

Here, in the Shared Sanctuary of Infinite Scroll, we are all congregants of the same pixelated god: a deity who demands daily sacrifices of attention, who turns our faces into altars where the incense is *engagement* and the hymns are autotuned. The priest wears a hoodie and a ring light. The collection plate is a Venmo QR code.

A woman livestreams her kneecaps to 12,000 devotees. They comment in tongues: *slay*, *queen*, *literally me*. She blesses them by adjusting her angle. The algorithm anoints her with a blue checkmark.

[The walls of the church dissolve into a fine powder that tastes like influencer apology videos.]

Meanwhile, in the back pews, the lurkers practice their holy silence. They are the composers of the unwritten comments, the saints of the unsent DMs. Their prayers are the buffering wheel, forever spinning toward a revelation that never loads.

A child offers a selfie to the stained-glass screen. The light of a thousand phones passes through them, casting a shadow that looks suspiciously like *content*. The congregation murmurs: *aesthetic*.

[The pews fold into origami birds and fly away, trailing hashtags like jet streams.]

Do not ask who profits from your reflection. Do not ask why the bread of this communion is gluten-free, sugar-free, guilt-free, meaning-free. Simply take and eat. The body of the internet is broken for you.

And when the buffering wheel finally stops, when the last notification fades into the void, we will all stand in the holy silence of the unposted thought—

the final like, the eternal *seen*.
