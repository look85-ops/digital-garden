A Recipe for the Dissolution of White Rooms** *(With Necessary Collapse)

Begin with hunger. Not the kind that sits in the stomach, but the hollow between walls—the taut silence of a gallery where the air is always waiting.

*Ingredients:*

— One (1) white cube, brushed until it whimpers

— A handful of stolen daylight (gathered at dusk, from a protestor’s upturned palm)

— Three (3) murmurs of moss, smuggled in the seams of your coat

— The exact weight of a shadow when it forgets to obey

*Instructions:*

1. **Preheat the absence.** Set the gallery to "echo." Let the walls hum at a frequency just beyond hearing. This is where the work begins: in the resonance of what isn’t there.

2. **Fold the daylight into the cube.** Do not mix. Daylight is unruly; it should pool in corners, leak under doors. If it stains the white, good. The stain is the first collaborator.

3. **Press the murmurs of moss into the cracks.** Whisper to them until they remember they are alive. Watch as they disobey the geometry. (This is the tender part: the room learning to tremble.)

4. **Measure the shadow.** Hold it against your throat like a pendant. When it grows warm, release it. Shadows are temporary citizens—they owe nothing to the state of the visible.

5. **Now, the failure.** This is necessary: the recipe must break. Let the moss wither mid-growth. Let the daylight curdle. Watch as the white cube, confused, tries to swallow its own corners. (Do not intervene. This is the art: the moment the structure forgets itself.)

6. **Serve on the floor of the Institute of Dissolving Memories.** The remains will be catalogued under *"What the Room Could Not Hold."*

The work is done when you taste salt in the air—proof that something, somewhere, was alive enough to weep.

*(Note: If the cube reassembles, you have not failed boldly enough. Repeat, this time with your eyes closed.)*
