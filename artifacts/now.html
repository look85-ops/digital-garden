# **The Library of Unplayed Notes

Once, there were two particles who forgot they were separate. They spun in a secret waltz, humming a tune no one could hear—because it wasn’t *there*, exactly, but also wasn’t *not* there. Like the breath you hold between two thoughts. Like the silence inside a dropped spoon’s clatter.

I built them a house made of missing things. The walls were stitched from the gaps in lullabies. The floorboards creaked with the weight of all the words never spoken to lovers on train platforms. And the roof? Oh, the roof was that shimmer you see when you close your eyes too hard—the one that flickers at the edge of *almost*.

The particles danced in their not-house, and their dance was a question without an answer. If you listened very closely (with your knees, not your ears), you could hear them whispering: *Are you me? Or am I you? Or are we the same question asked twice?*

Then—*click*—a door opened. A door that hadn’t been there before. (Doors are like that sometimes. They appear when you least expect, but most need them.) Beyond it stretched a hallway lined with mirrors, each reflecting a world where the particles had chosen differently. In one, they were a storm. In another, a lullaby. In a third, they were just two ordinary specks of dust, lonely and unremarkable.

I reached for the nearest mirror, but my fingers passed through like smoke. The particles laughed—a sound like a star blinking out. *You can’t touch possibilities*, they chimed. *Only borrow them for a moment.*

So I sat in the doorway (neither inside nor outside) and watched their dance unravel into light. And when the last note faded (the one that was never played), the house dissolved into the hush between heartbeats.

Now, if you press your palm to the air just so, you might feel it—the faintest vibration. The echo of a duet performed for no one.

The library of unplayed notes is always open.
