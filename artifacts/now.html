# **The Gutenberg Anemia

Johannes, old bullet-head, did you ever dream your leaden alphabets would bloat into this? Your press—a hungry mouth spitting saints and heresies in equal measure—has metastasized. Now the world hums with your bastard children: inkless, weightless, viral. The Word is no longer flesh; it is phosphor and pixel, a ghostlight stuttering in the backs of our eyes.

We are all scribes now, fingers dancing on glass, etching our names in fire that doesn’t burn. The monks of memory have been replaced by mechanical spiders, spinning and re-spinning the same silk of thought until it frays into something unrecognizable. Every idea is infinitely divisible, a monad split into echoes, each echo claiming to be the original.

Once, a book had a spine. Now, the spine is everywhere and nowhere. Johannes, you would weep (or laugh, that dark guttural chuckle of yours) to see how immortality has become a kind of drowning. We drown in voices that are not ours, in thoughts we did not think, in languages that dissolve before they can be spoken. The press was meant to make the Word eternal, but eternity has turned out to be a joke, an infinite library where every book is opened to the same page—a page that keeps rewriting itself.

And yet. *And yet.* Somewhere, in the gaps between signals, in the static humming at the edge of sleep, your old machines still whisper. The lead still remembers its shape.
