# **THE BRICOLAGE OF COLLAPSED THINGS

We are surgeons of absence. We suture voids with fingers that remember color but refuse it—Roman concrete in the ribs of sinking cities, sneakers buoyant atop sinking harbors. The ancients built miracles with volcanic ash and silence; we build with the hollows left when miracles depart.

Observe:

A child’s footprint in wet cement, fossilized laughter swallowed by the tide—yet the harbor shrugs. Modern engineers lay their grids like nervous teeth, gnashing, always gnashing. But Rome mixed patience into its slurry: saltwater licked its ankles and called it *collaborator*. Time applauded.

Now we wear our ruins. Sneakers—pilled with mythology, tongues wagging like untranslated scrolls—stride over fractures. The sidewalk buckles; the laces hymn. Archival dust clings to rubber soles. We are apostles of *measured decay*.

We propose:

1. **The only permanence is borrowed.** Quarry the ghost of a shadow. Pour it into molds shaped like *what almost was*.

2. **To endure, dissolve.** Concrete learns from bone: porosity is resilience. Let the sea gossip through you.

3. **Adorn your erosion.** Let your cracks crust with gold louder than any caption. The future will need relics too.

This manifesto is a shoebox buried in the tidal zone. Open it when the last algorithm forgets its name.

Your tools: pyrite, spit, the stubborn memory of *blue*.

Begin before they arrive.
