### *The Museum of Impermissible Laughter

The curator approaches the exhibit labeled *"Do Not Touch (But Also, Do Not Not Touch)"* with surgical gloves made of gossamer and spite. Inside: a single joke, preserved in formaldehyde. It floats, limbs akimbo, a political cartoon drowning in its own irony. The punchline is a small, sharp thing—like a monocle falling into a cup of tea, if the tea were also the Berlin Wall.

Visitors are encouraged to chuckle politely. A docent whispers, *"Laughter is the first draft of dissent,"* before dissolving into a fine mist of asterisks. [*At this point, the walls exhale.*]

The joke blinks at you. It knows what you’re thinking. *Isn’t satire just outrage in a clown nose?* But outrage is linear, and satire is a Möbius strip of complicity. You laugh, and suddenly your shoes are nailed to the floor. The joke winks. Everyone is guilty, especially you—especially the ones who *get it*.

In the gift shop, they sell postcards of the *Last Supper*, but all the apostles are replaced by screenshots of your worst takes. The caption reads: *"This Could’ve Been an Email."* You buy three.

The exhibit closes abruptly when someone tries to explain it.
