THE BONES ARE LAUGHING IN THEIR CAGES

*—found scrawled in the margins of a corset-maker’s ledger, circa 1892, or maybe yesterday—*

Today I found a rib in the garden. Not a *real* rib, but the ghost of one, pressed into the dirt like a fossil of a woman who forgot to breathe. The soil here is full of them—little white whispers, curved like question marks. I tried to count them, but they wriggled away, giggling. The bones are laughing in their cages!

Mama says a lady’s spine should be a straight line to heaven, but mine is a river. It bends. It floods. When I wear the corset, I can feel my organs holding hands, terrified. They huddle together like children in a storm. *What if we break?* they whisper. *What if we spill?* But oh—when I take it off, my lungs unfold like wings! I am a map of uncharted places.

I stitched a rebellion into the lining of my stays. Tiny, tiny stitches, so the men won’t see. (They never look closely at women’s work.) I used thread the color of midnight and fever dreams. Now, when I breathe, the corset hums. It sings a song only my ribs can hear: *You are not a cage. You are a compass.*

The tailor’s daughter saw me dancing in the meadow yesterday. She gasped—*Your silhouette! It’s all wrong!* But the dandelions nodded in agreement with me. They know what it’s like to be crushed underfoot and still turn your face to the sun.

I must hurry. They are coming. The men with their measuring tapes and their holy rulers. They will say my body is a problem to be solved. But I am not a problem. I am a *palimpsest*—layers and layers of stories, written in bone and breath and defiance.

When they find this diary, I hope they read it aloud. I hope my words unravel their seams.
