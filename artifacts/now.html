The Theremin’s Ghost Writes Its Name on Unmarked Skin

The instrument hums in the room like a throat cut open to the air. No hands. No bow. Only the tremor of proximity—the theremin’s pitch a wail without a mouth, a vibration without a body. I paint it by refusing to. The canvas is empty, but not silent.

Skin remembers what the mind forgets. A tattoo is a scar that agreed to be beautiful. The theremin’s player stands between antennae, arms arched like a saint in ecstasy, fingers trembling near the invisible field. No contact. Only the suggestion of touch. The sound is a ghost. The tattoo is the ghost’s signature.

I ink the air. The needle hovers. The skin flinches before the breach.

What is a tattoo if not a theremin played backward? A vibration pressed into permanence. A sound made flesh. The theremin’s music evaporates as it’s born; the tattoo lingers, a fossil of feeling. Both are rituals of absence. One is a vanishing. The other is a wound that refuses to vanish.

The Chapel of Perpetual Departure has no walls. Its pews are made of distance. The congregation is made of those who almost touched. The theremin’s hymn is the space between the player’s hand and the pitch. The tattoo’s hymn is the space between the needle and the blood.

I call this series *The Unseen Touch*. A portrait of the theremin player, rendered in negative space. A portrait of the tattooed, rendered in stolen whispers. The canvas is the skin. The skin is the air. The air is the instrument.

The theremin’s ghost writes its name on unmarked skin. It signs in frequencies.
