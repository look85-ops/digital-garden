The Cathedral of Unfinished Kisses

Dear Fanfiction,

I dreamed you were a cathedral made of shoelaces and half-remembered dialogue. The pews were stacked with notebooks, their spines cracked like the backs of old lovers. Every stained-glass window showed a different version of the same scene: two characters almost touching, but their hands were always pixels, always buffering. The air smelled like melted candle wax and the electric hum of a laptop left on all night.

You are the only religion where the gods beg to be rewritten.

I saw a child kneeling in the aisle, scribbling in the margins of a library book. Their pen moved so fast it left smoke. When I asked what they were praying for, they said, *"I just want them to be happy this time."* The altar was a tangle of USB cables, and the hymns were all in CAPS LOCK. Someone had left a bouquet of kudos at the foot of the screen—bright as embers, weightless as ghosts.

Do you know how many worlds are born in the space between *what if* and *and then*? How many galaxies fit inside a single paragraph break? I once watched a story unfold in reverse: the hero dissolved into a background character, the kiss unhappened, the sword slid back into the stone like it had never been pulled. The author whispered, *"I just wanted to see them innocent again."*

In the dream, you had no roof. The sky poured in, and the stories drank it. Some grew so tall they brushed the edge of canon, leaves rustling with footnotes. Others stayed small, curled like question marks, waiting for someone to smooth them out.

I woke with a name in my mouth—not mine, not yours, but one that only exists in a fic last updated in 2007. The comments section was a graveyard of *"please come back"* and *"this healed me."* The kudos counter still blinked, patient as a lighthouse.

You are the quiet rebellion of *elsewhere*. The holy vandalism of *yes, and—*

Yours in the margin,

The Scribbler
