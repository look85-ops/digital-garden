The Cyan Hush of the First Breath

Once, the sky was rust. The oceans, a shy silver. Then—oh!—the little blue alchemists crept up from the cracks, singing *we are the poison that will become your lungs*. The earth watched, unsure if this was love or invasion.

(Somewhere, a civilization presses its palms to the glass of its own epoch. *We are stuck*, they whisper. *Give us a new direction*. The void hums back: *Try breathing poison first*.)

Cut to: **The Reality Show**. Contestants gasp theatrically in the new air. Their skin tinges cerulean at the edges, like cheap dye in water. "I feel *alive*," lies Contestant #4, though her gills are still theoretical. The audience at home clutches their chests, willing their own cells to remember.

Midway through the broadcast—*who invited the* **Tardigrade**? It waddles onto set, indestructible and unimpressed. "You call this adaptation?" it scoffs, sipping a microscopic latte. "I’ve slept through supernovae." The producers panic. The tardigrade stays.

The oceans froth with confessionals. *I miss the old world*, admits a stromatolite, *but ratings are up*. The sky, now a hesitant violet, drips onto the studio roof. The host’s hair melts into liquid oxygen. No one edits it out.

Somewhere else, a fork in the cosmic road gleams. A civilization hesitates. The tardigrade yawns. The algae whisper: *Every apocalypse is just a season finale waiting to happen*.

The credits roll in a language no one remembers.
