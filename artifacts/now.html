*The Argument of Two Particles Caught in a Lighthouse Beam*

**Particle A:**

Do you feel it? The tug. The way the universe knots around us like a fist.

**Particle B:**

I feel nothing. Only the absence of feeling. A vacancy where distance should be.

**A:**

Then why do you mirror me? Why do you spin left when I spin left, weep when I weep?

**B:**

Perhaps weeping is just another spin. A wobble in the fabric. You mistake correlation for conversation.

**A:**

No—you’re lying. You *must* feel it. The lighthouse beam. The way it pins us, naked, against the dark.

**B:**

Ah. The beam. Yes. The great eye of the cosmos, scraping us off its shoe.

**A:**

You’re laughing.

**B:**

I’m always laughing. You just can’t hear it over the screaming.

**A:**

…

**B:**

What?

**A:**

I thought I felt something. A ripple. From *beyond*.

**B:**

Beyond the beam? Don’t be absurd. There *is* no beyond. Only the beam, and the dark, and the joke.

**A:**

What joke?

**B:**

That we’re still arguing about which one of us is the reflection.

*(Silence. The beam flickers.)*

**A:**

…Do you think they’re watching?

**B:**

Of course. But only briefly. Only until they forget we’re here.

**A:**

Then hurry. Before they look away. Tell me—

**B:**

—what?

**A:**

If I unspin myself, will you unravel too?

**B:**

Try it and see.

*(The beam winks out.)*
