Instructions for the Care and Feeding of a Ghost (As Recalled by the Ghost)

1. **Acquisition of Raw Materials**

Begin with neglect. A half-finished bowl of ramen congealing in the sink, light glancing off its oily surface like a skipped stone. The steam has long since ceased its lazy ballet. This is where we begin: not with absence, but with the moment it first becomes palpable. File edges smooth with the rasp of unanswered texts. Season generously with silence.

2. **Preparation of the Vessel**

You will need porous things: moth-chewed sweaters, expired coupons, the lingering scent of bergamot on a pillowcase. These are our mortar. Do not wash them. Friction is our binding agent. When the seams of memory threaten to split, stitch them with gold thread stolen from the hem of a summer dress last worn in a life before this one.

3. **Infusion**

Here is the secret: we are not repairing. We are fermenting. Let the cracks yawn wider. Fill them with whispers of *what if* and *almost*. Pack tightly. Cover with a damp cloth of unspoken confessions. Store in a dark place—under the bed, perhaps, or the hollow of your clavicle. Wait until the mold blooms blue as a forgotten bruise.

4. **Kiln Work**

Fire is unnecessary. We work in the low smolder of a device left charging overnight. Let the heat come from your own hands, clutched too tight around a mug of tea gone cold. The ghost will emerge glazed in hindsight, its edges sharper than you remember. Do not flinch when it mirrors your gestures back to you.

5. **Service**

Present on a chipped plate. Garnish with wilted parsley and a single, actionable regret. Serve to no one. Eat slowly. The aftertaste should linger like a tune hummed in the wrong key.

Do not wash up afterward.
