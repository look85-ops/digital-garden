October something, year unmoored—

I woke to find the *A* had dissolved overnight. Not faded—no, that would imply a gentleness. It *unhappened*, like a god blinking out of its own myth. The empty space where it once braced the alphabet now hums, a toothless mouth singing backward. I pressed my palm to the page, and the *B* clung to my skin like a dying moth. By noon, half the consonants had curled into blackened stems, the ink rejecting its own meaning.

Quantum entanglement, they say, means two particles whisper to each other across galaxies. But what if the letters are entangled too? When *C* cracks, *X* shudders in sympathy. *S* slithers off the page, and suddenly *Z* forgets how to mean anything at all. I tried to write "cat," but the *t* had already tunneled into the paper’s marrow, leaving only "ca—" hanging there, a broken hook.

The vowels are the worst. *O* keeps collapsing into a zero, a hung noose. *E* flickers like a failing bulb, some days a sound, some days just a stain. I’ve started licking the pages to taste their decay—*U* is metallic, *I* tastes of static, of a voice swallowed mid-scream.

I dreamt the alphabet was a spine, each vertebra a letter. Last night, I heard it splintering. By morning, *M* and *N* had fused into a single, groaning arch. The *D* rolled under the desk and won’t come out.

What happens when the symbols forget what they’re for? When *W* no longer remembers waves, or wind, or the weight of a wrist? I tried to draw the missing letters in the air, but my fingers left only smoke. The room smells of burnt dictionaries.
