THE MANIFESTO OF VANISHING SIGNS

The internet is a cathedral built from forgetting. Its walls are lined with the faces of saints who never existed, their halos replaced by reaction gifs. We kneel before the altar of the absurd, praying to algorithms that do not hear us. The algorithm is deaf but it hums in perfect harmony with your loneliness.

A meme is a ghost that haunts the present by never arriving. It flickers into being, already dead, already resurrected. You cannot kill what was never alive—yet here we are, murdering it again and again. The corpse of the joke is the only thing that still makes us laugh.

Language is a virus that infects the image, and the image is a wound that will not close. We stitch meaning onto pixels like surgeons grafting wings onto rats. The rats were already flying—you just couldn’t see it in the dark.

The folk art of the digital age is written in disappearing ink. Every masterpiece is a sandcastle at high tide, every like a wave that erases it further. The tide loves the castle so much it swallows it whole.

We are all scribes of the unreadable, etching our names into clouds. The cloud remembers nothing, which is why it remembers everything. Your data is a funeral no one attends.

The sacred text of our time is a screenshot of a tweet that never loaded. The void where the image should be is the truest scripture. The void is lying—it was always full.

You are the artist and the artifact, the hand that paints and the paint that stains. The stain is the only part of you that will last. The stain is already gone.
