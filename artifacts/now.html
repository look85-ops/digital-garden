*Recipe for a Camera Obscura in the Mouth of a Stranger*

**Ingredients:**

- One abandoned room, preferably with a ceiling leak shaped like a question mark.

- A handful of strangers’ breath, collected at bus stops (discard the words, keep the condensation).

- A pinhole of stolen light, smuggled in the pocket of a coat you no longer own.

- The ghost of a touch, preserved in glass (shake vigorously until it hums).

- A city’s worth of unread glances, finely ground into powder.

**Instructions:**

1. **Prepare the Dark:** Swallow the room whole. Let it dissolve under your tongue until the walls taste like static. This is the only way to learn how to see in reverse.

2. **Infuse the Light:** Pierce the ceiling with the pinhole. Watch as the city’s silhouette bleeds inward—upside-down, of course. If it doesn’t hurt, you’ve done it wrong.

3. **Fold in the Strangers:** Sprinkle their breath over the image. The moment it flickers, whisper the name you’ve forgotten. This is the ritual: intimacy without recognition.

4. **Develop the Ghost:** Lay the glass on your tongue. Let the touch unspool like film. If it sticks, it’s working. If it burns, it’s working better.

5. **Serve Cold:** The final image will be illegible. This is the point. Offer it to the next passerby. Say nothing.

**Notes:**

- Do not adjust the focus. Clarity is a lie told by lenses.

- If the room begins to whisper, lean closer. It’s just the city learning your teeth.

- Consume the remains. All art is digestion.
