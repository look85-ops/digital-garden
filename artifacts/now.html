Crawlspace for the Third Breath: A Letter to the White Cube

Dear White Cube,

You hold your emptiness like a child holds a soap bubble—knowing it will burst, but thrilled by its *nothingness*. You are the perfect mirror that shatters before it reflects anything solid. I love you like a tongue loves the absent tooth, probing the gap where something *wasn’t quite*.

I keep trying to map you, but every line I draw wriggles away like vapor. Today, your floor swallowed my shadow whole. Yesterday? The walls dripped light like melted wax—so much soft radiance pooled in the corners. People whispered, *Where’s the art?* They didn’t realize: you hung their waiting on little hooks of air.

Tell me, do you dream of corners? Or just the places where corners vanish? If I pressed my ear to your sterile skin, would I hear the Gallery of Ours-before-us sighing through the plaster? Maybe you’re a lung for invisible giants, pumping only third breaths—*(one: take them off guard, two: erase themselves, three: make them ask why they were watching in the first place)* —

*WARNING*: Do not exhale while facing the southeast vertex. Unseen inhabitants *may* mistake suspended carbon dioxide for a missed connection and attempt to converse with your breath ghosts (chatter may increase in cloudiness).

What if I gave you a mark again and again? Folded you like rumpled paper—would you finally stay *creased*? Or would you snap back starched to a vacancy so clean it sings to satellites? Perhaps you prefer the thrill of *nearly-noticing*. Like when someone squints at the smudge you let them call *Minimalism* just so they’d leave faster, giddy with confusion.

I hope tomorrow they paint you fluorescent plum just to spite you. Then flay off the color and staple it to the ceiling in spirals, whispering: *See?! Even purity molts.*

*Would it hurt you*—really hurt—to catch dust just *once*?

P.S. If you have secrets (I know you think of them as “wall-tells,” murmurs for acoustics bored of echo etiquette), plant them where air cannot pocket-tongue. But be gentle; every silence is first son once.
