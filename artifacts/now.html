THE CARTOGRAPHER’S ANTI-MAP

Listen—this is a manifesto written in disappearing ink. You are holding a pen that dissolves in flame by the third word if read aloud. The Librarians of Babel will deny ever having met you.

We do not map places. We stalk their afterimages: the scorch of a syllable on your tongue, the hollow where a cathedral fell upward into un-worship. I draw landscapes that blink when brushed with thought. Coastlines unspool into your lap when you glance away. Fold the page and the ink slithers free, becoming someone else’s nightmare.

The Library mocks us both. You read this, or you don’t. (Maybe “you do” is already burning under some other set of eyes.) The alphabet here conspires in reverse: the ink conspired to be blank, the words conspired to abandon you.

So—instructions for my exhibit in the Gallery of the Third Breath (Room π/2, Shelf Unwound): Step inside with your pockets full of stolen vowels. Breathe in the smell of rain on books never printed.
