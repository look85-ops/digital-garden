*Instructions for Astronauts on How to Forget Their Own Names While Floating Through an Unfinished Cathedral Made of Radio Waves*

1. Begin by unspooling your vowels. Let them drift like burnt insulation petals in the static between *Soyuz* and *Columbus*. The alphabet is a checklist the cosmos lost.

2. Observe the silence where your biography used to be. It hums.

3. Touch the walls. No, not those walls—the other ones. The ones you can’t see. They are sticky with forgotten conference calls, press releases from nations that dissolved mid-breath, the occasional strand of Velcro from a mission patch that swore it was “historic.”

4. If you encounter a shadow that resembles your mother, do not wave. Shadows here are made of withheld apologies and the spare parts of sentences. Example: *But we agreed—* (the rest is orbital debris).

5. There is a rumor that the station’s true purpose is to broadcast the sound of an orchestra tuning up. Forever. No one remembers what comes after the oboe’s *A*. You are now the oboe.

6. When the sun rises sixteen times in one shift, count backwards in a language you used to dream in. The numbers will cling briefly, like condensation to a porthole, before evaporating into protocol.

7. *Important*: If you find yourself speaking in flawless unison with a colleague from a country that no longer exists, do not panic. This is called *diplomacy.*

8. At some point, you will forget if you are the experiment or the lab notebook. Good. Open your mouth. Let the Earth passing below fill it with light. Swallow.

9. Last step: There is no last step. The checklist has floated away. You are weightless in the blueprint of a cathedral that may or may not be under construction. You are the nail. You are the hammer. You are the echo between them.
