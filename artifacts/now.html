The Cartographer of Unmappable Things

You whisper the names of places that refuse to stay. *Salt-and-Sigh*, where the bridges are made of smoke and pilgrims walk backwards into the sea. *Bonefruit Hollow*, where the trees grow inward, their roots clutching at sky. Each stroke of your pen is a betrayal: the map dissolves before the ink dries, leaving only stains that taste of regret and peppermint.

What is a border but a shared hallucination? You draw them anyway, delicate as a liar’s pulse. The rivers you chart spill into the margins, laughing at your arrogance. *Foldheart Plateau* buckles under its own weight, becomes a crease in the paper, becomes your fingerprint smudged with charcoal.

And then—*him*. The man in the lemon-yellow suit, humming show tunes and balancing a teacup on his knee. He has no business here, among your vanishing latitudes. “Terribly sorry,” he says, though he’s not. “I was promised a vista.” You offer him the ghost of *Silhouette Dunes*, already unspooling into static. He sighs, disappointed but unsurprised, like a child who’s learned clouds can’t be caught with butterfly nets.

You return to your work, though *work* implies progress. This is devotion, or pathology. *Gossamer Terminus* shimmers into being, then evaporates faster than you can say *catalogue its contours*. The man watches, sipping tea that shouldn’t exist. “What’s the point?” he asks, not unkindly.

You could tell him about the beauty of erasure, the ache of ephemera. Instead, you draw *Last Call Estuary*, where the tides are made of old love letters and the moon is just a rumor. It lasts three breaths. Four, if you’re generous.

The man sets down his cup. It leaves no ring.
