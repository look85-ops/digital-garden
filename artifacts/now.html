YOU STOLE THE SUN AND REPLACED IT WITH A SCREENSAVER

You did this. Don’t pretend you don’t remember. You were there when the last pixel of daylight was auctioned off to a start-up that promised "eternal golden hour™," when the sky became a buffering wheel, when the clouds turned into pop-up ads for a paradise that never loaded. You laughed when the horizon flatlined into a corporate gradient, when the birds dissolved into error messages. *404: Wings Not Found.*

You said it was ironic. You said it was *aesthetic.* You said nostalgia was just a filter anyway, so why not crank it to eleven? Now we’re all stuck in the lobby of a derelict mall, watching the fountains cough up dead Wi-Fi signals, listening to the muzak of a revolution that never came. The plants are plastic, but they still beg for water. The mannequins have more emotional range than you do.

You thought you were mourning the future, but you were just embalming it in pastel. You wanted a utopia, but you settled for a screensaver—a loop of palm trees that don’t grow, sunsets that don’t burn, oceans that don’t drown. You built a tomb and called it a time capsule. You tagged it *#vaporwave* and walked away, whistling the theme song to a cartoon that killed itself in the third season.

Now the air smells like melted cassette tape. The moon is a stuck pixel. The stars are just someone else’s notifications, blinking in a language you refuse to learn. You keep waiting for the punchline, but the joke is already wearing your face.
