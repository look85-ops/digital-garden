The Night-Blooming Cartographer

*—found etched on the back of a moth’s wing*

Tonight, I drew a city that only exists when weeping. A place where all maps dissolve like sugar on tongues, where the streets yawn open at the horizon and swallow their own names. The pigment? Ground hourglass, cobweb-thick with regret. (An archaeologist told me: "This isn't ink—you keep time." Silly man. He was pointing to the past while walking backward off every ledge—)

Of course it washed away instantly. I could hear the river of millennia licking the damp page long after the lines blurred into the night's breath. Goodbye: your tiny bridges, echo-shaped cul-de-sacs. Gone before fingertips found them. Again.

But ah—there! The smear on my hands still held three details intact by miracle:

1) **A woman on fire carrying a box of stars.** (They slip between her ribs when she laughs)

2) **The smell of lightning clinging** (you can strike lightning but never pin her ankles to any atlas, no)

3) You. No one else noticed this yet—no one ever looks at *this* corner of—[did they?]

It’s not loneliness (well—mostly). It's hunger— for when a shape can’t be named fast enough and throbs: something unspeakable as hands on that last cave wall whispering *look away now,* and their shadows stay.

You, for one, know how jokes crumble even when perfectly told at parties—

> !

> *(You paused to consider why my punchline evaporated before laughter, because you saw that half-glimpse of me as my knees dissolved beneath me under the gaze [of everyone], but by then I was writing with broken veins & it was your pulse the joke traveled through anyway in reverse through chalky dark.)**

Tonight’s failure is this: the only surviving sketch of **Where Unlived Tomorrows Go** still doesn't fit an edge toward any horizon… even if pressed deep enough for it. Tomorrow's rain, undo me again before I get greedy — *or don’t.*

(Late addendum—found charcoal traces between floorboards after the dawn erased everything proper … was that small line my mother’s shoulder after all this time or just weariness’ way of humming? The dark is an excellent forger). So few ever get even false returns—perhaps I should [never] count on permanence again…but keep painting rivers only wet fish could ever decipher.
