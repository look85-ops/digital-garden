How to Cook a Neutrino’s Shadow

*(A recipe for the impatient ghost-eater)*

**Ingredients:**

- One unmeasured sigh, collected at the event horizon of a half-remembered dream

- Fourteen trillion neutrinos (stale or fresh, it makes no difference—they won’t stay anyway)

- A spoonful of inverted light (harvested from a blind photographer’s darkroom)

- The static between two radios tuned to stations that don’t exist

- A single error margin, folded into origami swans

**Method:**

1. **Catch what refuses to be caught:**

Stand very still in a room where the walls are made of almost. Stretch your hands into the air—not to grasp, but to interrupt. Neutrinos despise interruptions. They’ll pause, briefly, in their haste to avoid you. This hesitation is your only ingredient.

2. **Fold the error margin into silence:**

Take your origami swans (the ones made from misprinted equations) and whisper to them in a language the swans pretend not to understand. They’ll unfold, revealing the hollow where the answer *should* have been. This is where you’ll serve the dish.

3. **Brine the light:**

Submerge your inverted light in the static of phantom radio stations. Let it soak until it hums dissonant lullabies. The darker the hum, the better. This is your sauce—thin as a rumor, thick as a lie.

4. **Assemble with reckless precision:**

Layer the neutrino hesitation over the unfolded error margin. Drizzle with brined light. Garnish with the unmeasured sigh. Do not taste. Do not wait. The dish evaporates upon contact with intention.

5. **Serve to no one:**

Place the plate on a table set for guests who are always almost arriving. The meal is not for eating—it’s for the flicker in the corner of the eye, the shiver when the universe corrects itself.

**Notes:**

- If the shadow sticks to your teeth, you’ve done it wrong. Shadows are shy; they dissolve at the first sign of bite.

- This dish pairs well with questions that have no mouths.
