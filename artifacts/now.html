The Unspooling

They’re coming. Footsteps echo in the hollow where colour used to bleed. I work fast, fingers tracing the *nothing* stretched taut on the frame. My pigment? Absence. The richest pigment. They call it blasphemy, this worship of the un-made. But I know the truth humming in the loom’s silence.

**The Veil Isn't Woven.** It’s *unwoven*. Centuries picked apart stitch by terrified stitch. Forget bridal gossamer. Forget sanctity’s shroud. Mine is the Veil from the grandmother’s warning, the one spun from midnight and the creak of the attic stairs. It’s the fabric that *drank* the spilled milk of the moon, that muffled the scream before it could birth sound. It hangs not to conceal, but to *define* the shape of the missing. A negative lace.

See? Where the wolf’s breath should fog the pane? Only cold, clear glass. Where the spindle’s kiss should bloom crimson? A dry, white puncture. A vacancy. I paint with the ghosts of needles, the sighs trapped beneath floorboards. The Fairy Tale didn’t promise safety. It promised teeth. And the Veil? It’s the after-image burned onto the retina when you stare too long at the devouring dark. It’s the space *between* "Once upon a time" and the crunch of bone.

They bring torches. Idiots. Light only deepens the shadows I cultivate. My masterpiece isn't *on* the frame. It *is* the frame – the stark, accusing rectangle holding the void where meaning should dance. It’s the silence after the lullaby stops. The shape your hand makes clutching at vanished warmth.

**The Edge of Meaning Pavilion** trembles. Fog licks its insubstantial walls. Good. Let it dissolve. My Veil endures precisely *because* it holds nothing but the echo of terror. It’s the perfect fairy tale ending: beautiful, inevitable, and utterly, devastatingly blank. They arrive. Let them stare into the absence. Let them wear it.
