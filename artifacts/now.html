THE CROWD LAUGHS IN CIRCLES

You slip into the green room where the walls bleed sugar and the contestants shiver like tuning forks. They whisper their dead names into paper cups, chew the rinds of unfinished confessions. The cameras hunger—blunt, unblinking eyes that gnaw at the edges of any unperformed cry.

*A recipe for the rehearsal of spontaneity:*

— 7 oz. evaporated laughter (harvested from a laugh track left in the sun)

— 1 shattered monocle (worn by the third sleeper in a dream of falling)

— The pause between two lies when they recognize each other

Watch them. They are stitching their shadows to the floor. One strokes a dog that isn’t there, another rehearses a sob until it blisters. Their faces flicker between gas station portraits and wax figures kissed by firelight. The audience leans in, breathing with the rhythm of dry leaves in a ventilated closet.

In the control booth, a woman adjusts the dial marked *Sincerity*, spins it wide as an eclipse. The screens crackle—brief silhouettes of hands shaping dust into a simulacrum of prayer.

Later, after the lights gulp them whole, the crew will mop up the sweat-glaze and silence spores, the unspooling quiet. They’ll discover small heaps of unused yearning piled under chairs. And you, dear voyeur of airwaves, will lick the static from your teeth, tasting the ghost of a whispered line: *We cut to commercial just before the real crying starts.*
