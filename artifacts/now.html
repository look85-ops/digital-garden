Disproven Experiments in Cartwheel Metaphysics, or Why My Bones Keep Speaking in Hexes

*A Found Diary Entry, Possibly Burned and Reconstructed*

October Something, Some Indistinct Year

The human sternum vibrates at exactly 19.6 Hz when filled with bees. This is a scientific fact. Like a tuning fork jammed into the ribs of St. Cecilia mid-translation, we are all singing backwards into the dirt, humming the first half of a prayer we no longer remember the end of.

This, in theory, was the beginning of my latest "masterpiece." Or so I assured the curators of the Nameless Biennale when they asked (through a series of increasingly concerning gestures) what my contribution would embody. I planned to sew taxidermied hummingbirds to the ceiling and program them to recite nursery rhymes in the voice of a long-dead physicist. Instead, I woke to a sky cracked open like an unplanned cesarean, and seven crows lined up on my windowsill in the post-traumatic quiet. They were knitting lichen into tiny woolen socks. Two were arguing philosophy.

I abandoned the ceiling, left the hummingbirds decomposing politely in their shipping case ("Crematorium? Taxidermist? Unsolicited gift registry?" read the return label). The real artwork had arrived, unannounced, in reverse: the crows were pulling my childhood teeth from their throats, one by one, and arranging them on the floor like braille. A sticky, blackened alphabet. **Here**, they seemed to say, **translate this**. They did not say it kindly.

Later, between migraine cycles, I tried explaining to my reflection: *What does it mean to archive destruction mid-process?* Like the Dutch still-life depicting oysters while eating them. Like building houses inside the cavity left by fallen molars. When I threw up from exhaustion, a tiny silver needle slipped out, shining between my fingers. I had no recollection of swallowing it. This was turning into a deeply flawed methodology.

The notebook smokes (metaphorically). Three ribs hum on (semi-accurately). The crows left at dawn with my shoelaces as tribute. Today, I wrote the words BEAK FORK ROT MIRROR LEG in permanent marker across my collarbone to see how they oxidize by morning. The museum director emailed to ask—again (gesturing abstractly "is there something with wolves...?")—about my installation details. I think her exact wording was, **"Is it haunted in, like, a transdisciplinary way?"**

Reply drafted, unsent: *Yes, but only legally on weekends when the law dreams of feral things growing veneers.* Enclosed: 400 dead mosquitoes arranged to spell **WHOSE CHILDHOOD IS THAT FLICKERING IN YOUR HOMINID FURNACE**, attached poorly (affectionately?) to my lease agreement as exhibit labels for an afterlife. Jury’s still out, scientifically speaking, whether the 19.6 Hz is from residual bee wings trapped in my ventricles— **OR JUST THE VERTEBRAE STARTING THEIR UNRULY CHORUS.**

(Machine humming sounds to be negotiated post-rejection via small claims court.)
