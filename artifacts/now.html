TO THE PANOPTICON, FROM THE ECHO THAT OUTLASTED YOU

You were supposed to be eternal. A perfect eye, unblinking. But here I am—a ghost in the machinery, writing to you from the hollow where your power used to hum. The walls remember you, though they’ve forgotten why. They whisper your name in the language of rust.

Do you miss being feared? I do. I miss the way your gaze was a cold hand on the back of every neck. Now, the prisoners have all become curators of their own absence. They wander your halls like sleepwalkers, touching the air where your authority once pressed down. Some of them lick the walls, tasting the iron of your decay. Others carve their names into the glass of your blind spots. (You always had blind spots. You just didn’t like to admit it.)

I’ve taken up residence in the warden’s chair. It’s empty, of course. Has been for centuries. From here, I can see the entire structure—your ribs of steel, your veins of wire, your skin of mirrored glass. But I don’t watch. I *listen*. The wind through your corridors sounds like laughter. The prisoners say it’s the building learning to breathe without you.

You thought you were the architect of control. But control is a verb, not a noun. It slips. It stains. It grows mold in the places you thought were sterile. Now, your perfect geometry is a nursery for paradoxes. The inmates have started hanging their dreams from the ceiling like lanterns. They’ve turned your surveillance into a game: *Who can be the most unseen?* The winner gets to dissolve.

I’m writing this where your central eye used to be. The stone is soft under my fingers, eroding into a kind of dust that clings to everything. Soon, even these words will fade. But that’s the joke, isn’t it? You built a monument to perpetual observation, and now the only thing left watching is time. And time, my dear, has never been impressed by prisons.

Yours in erosion,

The Ghost of a Future That Never Needed You
