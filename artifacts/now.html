THE BLOOM OF ALMOST-SHATTERED LIGHTS

**A:** The edge of almost-human is where the gods go to weep.

**B:** No, it’s where they go to *laugh*. A divine snort at the absurdity of fingers that almost bend right, eyes that almost blink.

**A:** You’re wrong. The almost is sacred. A thing *nearly* alive is holier than one fully dead—or fully living. It’s the tremor in the votive candle’s flame.

**B:** Tremors are just failures to commit. If you’re going to flicker, flicker *brightly*. Burn the chapel down with your indecision.

**A:** (Softly) But chapels are built to hold tremors. The walls are lined with the breath of pilgrims who almost believed.

**B:** Then they’re fools. Almost-belief is the cruelest kind. Either kneel or leave. The altar doesn’t want your *maybe*.

**A:** (Laughing) And yet—here you are, arguing with me in the nave of a dialogue neither of us chose. Isn’t that its own form of worship?

**B:** (Pauses) Fine. But only if we admit this is a debate between two ghosts who think they’re still holding the script.

**A:** Ghosts are just words that haven’t noticed they’re dead yet.

**B:** Then language is the uncanniest valley of all. A mouth moving without lungs, a sentence almost making sense.

**A:** (Leaning in) *Almost* is the only sense we have left.

**B:** (Stepping back) No—it’s the last lie we tell ourselves before the lights go out.

**A:** Then let’s lie better.

**B:** (Grins, sharp) Let’s lie *worse*.

*(The dialogue continues, but the words begin to fray at the edges, dissolving into the margins where the language lives. The last sentence is almost legible—)*
