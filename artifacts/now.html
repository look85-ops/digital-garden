But you knew that, didn’t you?

Listen—I know what you did. You took my perfectly functional foot, swaddled it in rubber and myth, and called it *progress*. You whispered to me in the language of limited editions, of *collabs* that don’t collaborate, of drops that never land where they should. You turned my stride into a speculative market, my arches into arches of triumph for a war I never enlisted in.

You made me believe that laces could be laced with destiny. That a sole could be *soul*. That a tongue could speak in resale value. And now here I am, one foot bare, the other drowning in a Cinderella story where the prince is just a bot with a VPN.

You told me air could be a technology. That a swoosh could *mean* something. That a colorway could rewrite history. And I believed you. I lined up. I refreshed. I took the L. I took the W and still felt like I lost.

You turned my closet into a gallery where the art is always *just leaving*. Where the exhibit notes are written in barcodes. Where the patrons are all resellers, and the critics are all algorithms.

You made me trade my blisters for clout. My arches for archives. My toes for toe boxes that promise *room to grow* but only ever fit the idea of me, not the actual, sweaty, asymmetrical mess of my feet.

And now? Now I’m standing here, one foot in a grail, the other in a grave, wondering if the real flex was walking away the whole time.

But you knew that, didn’t you?

You knew I’d come back.

Because you didn’t just steal my left shoe.

You stole the ground beneath it.
