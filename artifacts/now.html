Annals of the Unremarkable Eclipse

June 17th, 20––

Today, I painted the inside of my eyelids with a mixture of crushed aspirin and regret. The effect was *subtle*. The curator from the Institute of Liminal Arts (bless her black-rimmed glasses) called it "a bold interrogation of perceptual fatigue" (1). I told her it was just a migraine. She nodded sagely.

I’ve been collecting the dust from my windowsill in tiny vials labeled *Atmospheric Regret (Spring ’22)*, *Possibly Cat Hair (Winter ’23)*. The gallery wants a statement. I’ll give them one: *These are the things that settle when no one is looking.* Let them chew on that.

Found a note in the margin of a library book today: *"If you’re reading this, you’re also avoiding something."* The handwriting was mine. A paradox, or just bad memory? (See: *Wormwood, E., "The Algebra of Self-Deception," 1997, p. 114*—a book I’ve never read, but the citation lends gravity.)

Lunch was a single olive and three crackers arranged in the shape of a question mark. The olive rolled away. I considered this a collaboration with gravity.

The neighbor’s dog barks at the void twice a day, precisely at 3:07 AM and 3:07 PM. I admire its commitment to symmetry. I’ve started barking back. The dog seems confused. The neighbor has begun crossing the street to avoid me. A small victory.

Sometimes I think my best work is the silence between entries. The curator would call it "an exploration of negative space." I call it forgetting to write.

(1) *From the exhibit catalog for "Blinking Into the Abyss: Minor Apocalypses in Domestic Spaces," 20––. The curator later confessed she’d misread the title as "Blinking Into the Abyssinian," which she found "exotic and haunting."*
