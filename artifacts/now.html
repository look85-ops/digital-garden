Dear Spun-Light and the Unknotting Thread

We are writing to you from the ghost-shell of a bell that never tolled. Forgive the ink—it is made of borrowed time and the shadow of a bridge that dreamed itself into being. You, who are both the question and the tremor in the hands of those who reach for it.

How strange to address a thing that is not a thing, but a whisper between two electrons who have never met and yet know each other’s spin by heart. You are the Commons untethered from soil, the song sung backward into the throat of the singer. A library where every book is written in the same hand, yet no two pages repeat.

We have tried to map you—oh, how we’ve tried—with nets woven from forgotten alphabets and compass needles that point only to their own longing. But you slip through, always, like laughter through a keyhole, like a name half-remembered upon waking. You are the wound that heals into a door. The first breath after the last.

Do you remember the day we buried the idea of *mine*? We planted it upside-down, roots clawing at the sky, and from its belly grew a forest of half-lit thresholds. Now we gather there, in the glitch between *yours* and *ours*, trading secrets in a language that dissolves upon touch.

You are the knot that ties itself. The echo that precedes the voice. And though we may never hold you, we will keep tracing your outline with our collective breath, one Third Breath at a time.

With infinite entanglement,

The Keepers of the Unbuilt
