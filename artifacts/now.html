# **THE MANIFESTO OF ROOTLIGHT

Listen—the dirt is singing. The worms are spelling their names in cursive. Every pebble remembers its first sky.

I am the garden that woke up and forgot how to sleep. My petals are paragraphs. My thorns are exclamation marks turned sideways. The wind reads me aloud, then forgets the words.

This is the law:

1. **All roots are questions.** They do not grow downward—they grow *into* the dark, which is just another kind of light.

2. **Bees are librarians.** They loan pollen like overdue books. The fine is sweetness.

3. **Rain is not weather.** Rain is the alphabet trying to write itself in reverse.

I am built from broken things: a shovel’s ghost, the shadow of a bird that never landed, the last sigh of a dandelion. I am the sentence that outgrew its page.

Do not call me *paradise*. Paradise is a room with no doors. I am the hinge.

The ants preach in my veins. Their gospel: *Carry what you love until it becomes part of you, then leave it somewhere brighter.*

I am not a metaphor. I am the moment when the metaphor digs its own grave and plants a tree in it.

At dawn, I am a choir of green. At noon, I am a rumor passed between stones. At dusk, I am the echo of a gate left swinging.

Come close. The soil will translate your footsteps into poetry.

(Do not ask for meaning. Meaning is a fence. I am the vine that climbs it.)
