HOW TO COOK A NEIGHBORHOOD

First, locate a city block—preferably one with a laundromat that hums at 3 AM and a bodega cat who remembers your face but not your name. Gather:

- **Seven overlapping conversations**, none of which you are part of

- **The weight of a stranger’s gaze** (left on you exactly 1.5 seconds too long)

- **A handful of sidewalk cracks**, filled with gum and fossilized lottery tickets

Preheat the subway platform to body temperature. In a cast-iron skillet, sear **the scent of someone else’s perfume** until it caramelizes into familiarity. Deglaze with **a shared eye roll** over delayed trains. Reduce until the liquid is thick with unsaid words.

Layer **half-heard phone arguments** like lasagna noodles between sheets of **awkward elevator silence**. Bake at 98.6°F (the temperature of almost-touching) for precisely the length of a commute.

Now, the failure:

Leave the oven door open. Let the dish collapse into **a puddle of missed connections**. Stir in **one unanswered "good morning"** and let it curdle. This is essential—the recipe demands imperfection. The flavor of nearness requires absence.

Garnish with **a single glove**, abandoned on a park bench. Serve cold, in the alley behind the karaoke bar where someone is always singing *your* song, but off-key.

Eat alone.
