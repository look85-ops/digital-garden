Dear Flâneur, or Perhaps Just the Pavement That Misses Your Shoes

Flâneur, old ghost, shadow with feet—

I have traded my palette for the uncolor you taught me to see. The grays of cigarette butts drowned in puddle-wine, the void between streetlights humming a tune no ear has named. I wear absence like a stolen coat now. It fits better.

You owe me nothing; still, I invoice you for stolen glances. Itemized: one half-smile lost to a laundromat window (mismatched socks tumbling inside), one sigh confiscated by the metro’s maw. Payment is your attention. Void where prohibited.

Last Tuesday, I tried to draw your essence with a pencil eraser. Rubbed so hard the page tore. Marvelous failure, like a mime attempting to describe the sun with his elbows. (This was the intentional part.) The hole left behind—that’s you. A pilgrimage of empty pockets.

Tell me: when you wander, do the buildings lean in to gossip? Or do they yawn and scroll their sky-phones, bored? I swear the park bench whispered *sit here and dissolve* yesterday. It was a bad listener.

Enclosed: a map. Not of streets but of pauses. X marks where the bakery exhaled butter-lust onto the sidewalk, a greasy saint performing benediction for pigeons. Legend includes: *the sound of a scarf unraveling into wind,* *a crow debating Kafka with its reflection.* Fold it wrong and it becomes an obituary for sidewalks. (They get walked on their whole lives.)

I no longer paint, I evaporate. Still, I send this letter inside out, ink side touching air, so what’s unsaid bleeds through where the words stammer, fail, un-be. It won’t reach you.

The postman mistook it for a cloud’s shopping list and ate it.

Yours in vanishing,

—A Footnote Blown From a Misprinted Guidebook
