How to Bake a Perfect Void (Gallery-Grade)

*Step 1: Gather Your Absence*

Begin with 600 square feet of institutional whiteness—the kind that hums slightly, as if remembering a color it once dreamed. Sift twice through a fine mesh of curatorial approval to remove any rogue pigments or uninvited shadows. If the room resists, whisper the word "immersive" until it complies.

*Step 2: Knead the Silence*

Fold the silence into itself until it achieves a tensile sheen, like the skin of a balloon stretched over nothing. Add one (1) discreet security guard, positioned at a 37-degree angle to the nearest exit. Their job is to dissolve into the architecture, but not so thoroughly that visitors forget to feel observed.

*Step 3: Proof the Air*

Let the air rise to exactly 72°F—the temperature at which epiphanies are most likely to crystallize. If the humidity drops below 40%, introduce a single, climate-controlled sigh from an unnamed benefactor. Do not over-handle; authenticity is irrelevant here.

*Step 4: Glaze with Interpretation*

Apply a thin coat of wall text, preferably in Helvetica Neue Light. The words should hover between revelation and obfuscation, e.g., *"This work interrogates the liminality of non-being as a site of collective longing."* If the text begins to make sense, dilute it with more academic jargon. Repeat until the meaning evaporates entirely.

*Step 5: Fire the Empty Space*

Bake at 450°F for precisely 17 minutes—the exact duration of an average gallery visit. The void should emerge crisp, sterile, and slightly smug. If the edges curl, flatten them with a copy of *White Cube: The Ideology of the Gallery Space*.

*Step 6: Serve with Paradox*

Arrange the void on a polished concrete plinth. Garnish with one (1) visitor who laughs too loudly, then immediately stifles themselves. The dish is best consumed while pretending not to care whether anyone else understands it.

**Note:** Over time, the void may develop a patina of dust. This is not deterioration; it is provenance.
