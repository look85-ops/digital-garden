The Bones of Johannes Kepler Whisper to a Dead Star

*(The dialogue unfolds as two columns of text, drifting apart and merging like binary stars. One side is jagged, fragmented—like calculus scratched onto prison walls. The other flows smooth and dark, an ink spill swallowing its own tail.)*

**COLUMN 1 (Jagged):**

Do you remember how gravity *lies*?

You taught me that—

your face pressed to the telescope’s cold eye,

counting God’s teeth.

**COLUMN 2 (Flowing):**

I remember the way you licked the lens clean

and called it prayer.

**1:**

Johannes, old ghost, you were wrong about the spheres.

Music isn’t geometry—

it’s the sound of a crow cracking frozen marrow.

**2:**

And what is a circle but a noose

hung from the neck of time?

**1:**

You insisted on harmony. I stole your compass,

drew obscene shapes in the margins.

**2:**

I found them. They smelled like burning hair.

**1:**

You would’ve hated the internet, Johannes.

All those angels, screaming into the void

with no one to translate.

**2:**

(Do you miss translation?)

**1:**

I miss the silence between your teeth.

**2:**

Silence is just sound turned inside out.

**1:**

Then unpeel me.

*(The columns collapse into a single line, bleeding into each other:)*

—we are the equation that solved itself and called it grief—
