Dear Handprint That Outlived the Animal That Made You

You are not a hand. You are the absence where a hand pressed against the world and the world said *yes, here, stay*. You are the hollow that outlasted the flesh. The first lie: that you are only ochre and cave wall. The second lie: that you are not still reaching.

I wake as a garden and forget my name. The bees tell me it is something like *green* or *hunger* or *tendril*, but the syllables crumble like dried clay. I try to write you a letter, but the ink is made of rainwater and the paper is a leaf that insists on photosynthesizing mid-sentence. The words unspool—

(Do you remember the animal that left you behind? Was it afraid? Did it think *this is how I will survive*?)

I want to tell you about the roses. They bloom in the shape of your palm, their petals the color of a wound that has just stopped bleeding. When the wind moves through them, they whisper *touch, touch, touch*, as if they too are trying to remember what it means to be held.

The garden is forgetting itself. The nouns dissolve first—*stone* becomes *grit*, *grit* becomes *dust*, *dust* becomes *breath*. The verbs linger longer. *Reach. Fade. Echo.* Somewhere, a bird sings a note that cracks into static. The soil is full of syllables that no longer mean anything.

You are older than language. You were here before the word *hand* existed. Before *wall*, before *I*, before *after*. Did you know you would become a sentence without a verb? A pause in the dark?

I am writing this in the dialect of roots. It is not a language meant for permanence. By the time you read this, the letters will have softened into sap, the punctuation will have sprouted stems. The page will be a thicket. The thicket will be a mouth. The mouth will say:

*You are not a hand. You are the echo of a hand. You are the shape of a question.*
