Diary of the Last Bee Who Knew Morse Code

*—found in the hollow of a lightning-struck oak, ink of crushed blackberries and rust.*

**Day 17 of the Unseasonable Heat**

I am a liar who plants alphabets and waits for them to sprout consonants. The garden says: *you are not a body, you are a season.* I argue. My hands are full of holes where the light leaks through. I tried to write my name in the dirt, but the ants rearranged the letters into *a word I do not know yet.*

The roses have started humming in a minor key. I think they are translating my thoughts into a language of thorns. At dusk, the petals fall like unsent telegrams. I catch one—it says *"the sky is a delayed echo."* I eat it. My tongue turns blue.

Yesterday, I buried a clock face-down so it would tick backward. Today, a mushroom pushed through the soil wearing the clock’s hands as a crown. It whispered: *"time is a cul-de-sac with no houses."* I laughed until my ribs ached. The garden laughed too, but only from the waist down.

I found a snail shell filled with rainwater. Drank it. Now my dreams are in reverse. I see:

— a library where the books bloom and the librarians are made of pollen

— a funeral for a language, mourners throwing vowels into the grave

— my own shadow peeling itself off the ground and folding into an origami crow

The tomatoes are ripening into tiny stop signs. I ignore them. The bees have started dancing in straight lines, their wings spelling out *SOS* in dust. I tap back: *"I am here but not here."* They do not understand. Or perhaps they do, and this is their reply: silence in the shape of a hexagon.

At night, the earth exhales through the cracks in the greenhouse glass. I press my ear to the ground and hear the roots gossiping about my death. They say it will happen on a Tuesday, or possibly a Wednesday, or possibly never. They say I will dissolve into a verb. *To garden: to misunderstand the difference between hunger and love.*

I am running out of pages. The last one is already half-eaten by silverfish. I will write the rest in the air with a stick of burning lavender. The smoke will curl into a question mark. The wind will answer.
