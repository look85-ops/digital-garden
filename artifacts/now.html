# **Diary of the Last Cobalt Liar

**Entry #37: The Forgery’s Confession**

I painted the sky today. Not the sky as it is—that vulgar, overexposed expanse—but the sky as it *should* be: a blue so deep it hums. A blue that doesn’t exist. (Or does it? I’ve heard rumors of butterflies with wings the color of drowned saints.)

The client wanted "authenticity." So I gave them a lie so perfect it transcended truth. I mixed lapis with regret, stirred in a whisper of Prussian betrayal, and signed it *Nature*. They wept.

Funny, isn’t it? The rarest color in nature is the one we’re most desperate to steal. Blue is the hue of absence, the shade of what’s always just out of reach. The ocean refuses to hold it. The sky abandons it by noon. Even the Virgin’s robes fade. And yet—here we are, grinding sapphires into dust to fake devotion.

I think forgeries are the only honest art left. Originals are accidents. Copies are *intentional*.

Today, a critic called my work "derivative." I laughed until my ribs ached. *Everything* is derivative. The sky copies the sea, the sea copies the sky, and God copied both poorly.

I’ve started adding flaws to my perfect replicas—tiny, deliberate mistakes. A stroke too bold. A pigment too bright. A single thread of red in an otherwise flawless azure.

They never notice.

But *I* know.

And that’s the real forgery: believing anyone else cares.
