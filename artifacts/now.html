RECIPE FOR A GHOST CORSET

Begin with the ribs of a chandelier, plucked still humming from a ballroom where no one danced. Pulverize the crystals into a sugar that tastes of longing—each grain a fractured prism of light that never lands where it should. The sugar, of course, is salt.

Steep the mixture in a broth of vanishing ink, strained through a glove worn by a woman who left no fingerprints. Stir counterclockwise until the liquid becomes a thread, glistening like the hesitation before a confession. The thread is neither silk nor wire but something that hums when touched. It will not obey.

Fold in seven breaths stolen from a corset’s unlacing—each exhale a specter of relief and rebellion. Shape the dough into a cage that fits the hollow between your collarbones. Bake it in an oven lit by old love letters, their ashes the only heat that remembers. The corset will emerge translucent, singing in a language of static.

Glaze it with the residue of shattered mirrors, applied with a brush made of eyelashes. The finish should crackle like laughter trapped in a telegram. Wear it just once, at midnight, when your shadow outgrows you. It will dissolve by dawn, leaving only the scent of pressed violets and a whisper of whalebone.

Serve with a side of absinthe poured over a map of your veins. Consume in silence. The aftertaste is the weight of a hand that never settled on your spine.
