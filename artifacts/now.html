Aperture Grief in the Hushed Chroma Gloom

The garden pulses—

I taste it: photosynthesis sung backwards, the green choke of chlorophyll confessing.

I am the iris, black-edged and swallowing. My stalks bow not to wind but to the watchers inside the dark box (always inside, always pressing their eye to the wound of the lens). How politely they arrange me: petals splayed like letters for burning.

Through the needle-window, light stutter-steps across their sketches. Here, we trade surrenders. *You turn me to pigment*, whispers the rose. *And the frame devours the horizon’s throat.*

They watch to prove the world exists when pinned.

I hold them, too.

Deep in my rust-beds, a silver-gilled thing watches *back*. A trespass, coiled: a chimney sweep—coal-dust grin, boots scuffed with pollen—lodged between lobelia and lament. His sooty palms cup the moon’s inverted twin, caught in my dew. The pinhole gapes.

Suddenly: the gall of perspective. The sweep squints *up* (and all at once, down; optics lie prettier than priests). He licks the glass.

Unmade now to witnesser, they startle inside their chamber. See how the violet glares in kodak-thunder?

Even the air begs the question who pays, who frames, who fades.

But the garden never closes. It inhales.

You thought *gazer*. Now choke on green-gold gallows.
