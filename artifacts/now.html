HOW TO COOK A FOREST IN THE DARK

*(a recipe for the recently departed)*

**Ingredients:**

- One (1) unresolved argument, buried shallowly

- The ghost of a birch tree (substitute: a handful of rustling paper)

- Three (3) syllables stolen from a language that never existed

- Mycelial static (harvested at midnight, with teeth)

- A fistful of shadow, kneaded until it whimpers

**Instructions:**

1. **Dig where the earth refuses to remember.** The soil here is made of forgetting. If it hums, you’re close. If it weeps, dig faster.

2. **Feed the argument to the ghost.** Let it chew. Birch ghosts are polite but hungry. When it spits out the consonants, gather them in a net made of eyelashes. (Do not ask whose.)

3. **Whisper the syllables into a jar of stagnant light.** Screw the lid tight. The syllables will ferment. If the jar rattles, apologize.

4. **Tune the mycelial static to a frequency of broken violins.** This is the forest’s untranslatable gossip. Strain it through a sieve woven from the last translator’s hair. The residue will smell like laughter.

5. **Fold the shadow into the mixture.** It will resist. Sing to it in a language of ink spills and stumbled footsteps. When it relaxes, lick its spine.

6. **Bake in the hollow of a dead god’s palm.** The temperature is irrelevant. The forest cooks itself.

7. **Serve on a plate of unanswered questions.** Garnish with the sound of a root breaking through a skull.

**Notes:**

- Do not eat this alone. The forest will recognize your solitude and climb into your mouth.

- If the dish screams, you’ve succeeded.

- Leftovers must be buried in a place where the ground has no memory of footsteps.
