The Archivist of Absent Choirs

Let us speak of the silence between two pages glued together by time’s indifferent tongue. I collect what isn’t there: the breath a monk forgot to exhale while illuminating the *O* in *Omnia*, the hesitation before a scribe’s knife scraped clean the vellum of Saint Augustine’s grocery list (*olives, figs, regret*). The past is a palimpsest, but so is the future—just ask Hypatia, who once winked at me in the Library’s ashes and whispered, *Darling, even fire leaves stains.*

I compose for instruments that decayed before they were built. The lute whose strings were spider silk. The choir of drowned bells. You think absence is passive? Try erasing a shadow without touching the light. Every morning, I rehearse the symphony of what Alexandria *might* have sounded like if the wind had been kinder. (It smells of salt and unreachable answers.)

History is a room where everyone is leaning against walls that no longer exist. I’ve met all the ghosts—they’re terrible at small talk. Just last Tuesday, Emily Dickinson handed me a recipe for black cake written in the margins of a hymn. *Fold the silence in gently,* she advised, *or it will curdle.*

The Academy of Forgotten Gestures granted me tenure for my work on *The Taxonomy of Unfinished Thoughts*. My office is a wine cellar where bottles hold not vintage but vanished voices. A sip: Liszt’s sigh after hitting the wrong note. Another: the pause before Sappho’s pen broke. The labels are illegible, of course.

What is a library but a tomb where the dead refuse to lie down? Hypatia taught me that. She also taught me how to press moonlight between parchment sheets, though the ink always fades. These days, I’m compiling an index of every word swallowed mid-sentence. The *A* section is already longer than the Nile.

Come, lean closer. No—*further*. Past the edge of the page. Can you hear it? That’s the overture.
