THE WATERBEAR’S LAST WILL & TESTAMENT

*(etched into the underside of a disappearing moon)*

Let it be known: we who paint without pigment are the true heretics of light. Our canvases are the hollows where color once throbbed, our brushes the absence that licks at the edges of what was. Here, in the negative chapel, we worship the Tardigrade—not as it is, but as it refuses to be.

Observe: eight stunted legs sheathed in Acubi armor, a silhouette sharp enough to slit a vacuum. It does not *exist*; it *persists*. A paradox in miniature: soft as a fossil’s sigh, hard as the silence between stars. Fashion it in the void’s own tailoring—seams unpicked, hems frayed into oblivion. Let it stride through cosmic runways in a suit of pure resistance.

The Academy of Forgotten Gestures archives our creed:

1. **To erase is to invent.** Every stroke unmade births a new wilderness.

2. **Survival is the least interesting form of rebellion.** (Yet here we are, scratching hymns onto dust.)

3. **The sublime is a straight line laughing at its own collapse.**

We do not depict. We *excavate*. Our medium is the grit in the eye of eternity.

And when the last photon gutters out, remember: the waterbear will remain, clad in its nothingness, pristine as a razor’s ghost. It will outlive your palettes, your galleries, your brittle theories of beauty. It will dance—if dancing is the word for standing utterly still while the universe peels itself apart around you.

Signed in eroded ink,

The Society of the Unpainted
