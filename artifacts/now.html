The Museum of Unfinished Lullabies

You remember it like this:

The piano keys were made of moth wings, and each time you pressed one, a little dust would rise like a sigh. The room had no walls, only the suggestion of walls—a shimmer where corners should be, a hum where the ceiling forgot itself. A child sat on the floor, stacking hourglasses filled with honey. They melted as soon as they were balanced, golden threads pooling around their bare feet.

*This is how music is born*, someone whispered, though no one was there. *The notes are just the excuses. The song is what happens in the gaps where the sound gives up.*

You tried to hum, but your voice came out as a handful of black seeds. They sprouted tiny violins in your palms, their strings fraying before they could be played. The child laughed and pressed one to their ear. *Listen*, they said. *It’s the sound of a bridge collapsing in reverse.*

Outside (if there was an outside), the trees were unlearning their names. Bark peeled back to reveal cursive script—love letters written by the wind, now illegible. A clock lay on its side, gears blooming into dandelions. Every tick was a petal let loose.

*Time isn’t a line*, the child said, licking honey from their fingers. *It’s a circle that forgot how to close.*

Then the dream did what dreams do: it folded itself into a paper airplane and sailed into the fireplace. You woke with the taste of smoke and something sweet on your tongue, the ghost of a hum still tangled in your teeth.
