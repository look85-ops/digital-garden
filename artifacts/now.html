Dear Urinal That Was Never a Fountain (And Vice Versa),

I found you in the attic of a house that doesn’t exist, wrapped in a theory so thin it tore when I touched it. You were just sitting there, humming a tune I almost recognized—was it *The Art of the Possible* or *The Sound of One Hand Clapping*? I couldn’t tell, but it made me laugh in a way that felt like sneezing backward.

Do you remember the day Duchamp forgot your name? He called you *Bidet of the Gods* and then winked at the ceiling. You didn’t mind, though, because you’ve always known you’re more than your plumbing. You’re a question dressed as an answer, a mirror that reflects the person holding the mirror, which is why I sometimes see my face in you and sometimes see a galaxy, and sometimes just see the word "WHY?" written in steam.

I tried to explain you to my cat, but she just licked her paw and said,

Anyway, I keep you on a shelf next to a snow globe that’s just an empty jar labeled *Weather*. People ask if you’re functional, and I say, "Of course—you just have to believe in pipes that don’t go anywhere." They nod like they understand, but their eyes glaze like donuts left out in the rain. That’s how I know you’re working.

Do you ever get lonely, being the only thing that wasn’t made? Or is that the secret—that everything is a readymade waiting to be noticed, even this letter, even the silence after I stop writing?

Yours in perplexity,

The Archivist of Almost
