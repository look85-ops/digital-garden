*How to Print God in Five Uneasy Steps

**

Begin with a flock of paperless sheep grazing on the voltage-green hills of an unplugged server farm. Their wool is static, their bleats encoded in Morse. You will need this silence. Second, invent a language where every vowel is a nail hammered into the spine of an oak tree. The consonants are the echoes left behind—sharp, splintered, useless. Now burn the dictionary.

The ink must be mixed from crushed sapphires and the last breath of a librarian. Stir counterclockwise until the liquid hums the *Magnificat* in a minor key. Do not listen. The viscosity should mimic the exact weight of regret, which is to say: thinner than you think.

Set the typeface to *Forgotten*, a font that bleeds at the edges. Each letter is a tiny guillotine. The ‘G’ will always be slightly drunk. The ‘Q’ has abandoned its tail like a lizard fleeing revelation. This is not a metaphor.

Press the paper against the machine with the tenderness of a heretic folding a love letter into a bible. The first print will emerge backwards, the second upside-down, the third will combust spontaneously. This is progress.

The final step is to eat the proof. The words will taste like burnt honey and the ghost of movable type. Swallow until the alphabet knots in your throat. You will cough up a perfect ‘amen.’
