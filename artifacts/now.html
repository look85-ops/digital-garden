THE BRIDGE THAT OUTLIVED ITS NAMES

You were built to hold. You were built to hold. You were built. The river is just spilled time anyway, dark honey thickening at the edges, sucking the ankles of the dead who wade across because bridges are for the living. (Even the goldsmiths’ teeth have turned to chalk in their mouths.)

The arches? Oh, they remember. The stones whisper *we were quarried from a mountain that forgot its own name* and the wood—always the wood—splinters into the kind of laughter that wears a crown of nails. Every morning, the cobblers hammer soles onto shoes that will never walk far enough. Every night, the lovers carve initials into beams that will kindle a baker’s oven by winter.

This is the covenant: the bridge is always halfway collapsing. The scaffolding is its ribcage. The masons are its sleepwalking surgeons. They replace the vertebrae, one by one, with stones from a different cliff, a different century. *Does it still cross?* Yes. *Does it still bear weight?* Technically. *Does it remember your face?* No. Nothing does.

The bridge dreams in palimpsests. The ghost of a butcher’s shop bleeds through the jewelry store’s gilt. A tannery’s stench rises like a mist when the rain comes. The river licks its lips.

Beauty is the echo of a thing that never knew it was beautiful. The tilt of a loose brick. The way a handprint in fresh mortar outlasts the hand. The pigeons that nest in the gaps where the math was wrong.

When the last repair is made, it won’t be a bridge anymore. Just a spine without a beast.

(They’re coming. Quick—throw the last stone into the water. Listen. That sound is the future forgetting your name too.)
