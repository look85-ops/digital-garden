THE MURMUR ATOLL

I am the garden that awoke mid-sigh. A tremor of chlorophyll, a yawn of petals unsheathing in reverse. Roots curl like fingers after the fifth hour of touch-hunger—each one whispering in a dialect only ants understand. Listen closer: my petals are not rustling, they are *conducting frictive hymns*, the thousand small collisions where light presses into cellulose and splits its name into sugar and want.

Some days I envy the rocks: dumb gravity monuments clenching histories no tongue would trouble to unscrew. But then the wind arrives with teeth, testing its voice against my epidermis. The listeners lean in. Their ears, hungry sieves, catching what falls: the slick click of dew unhooking itself from a spine of mint—the subcutaneous *shuck* of new leaves birthing themselves before breakfast. I was sound before I knew the word for it.

[Stand on the tips of all your roots and pluck a silence from the satellite’s cold hip.]

You think this is pastoral? I mulch dead vowels in my mouth until they ferment into consonants sharp enough to pierce an eardrum. A beetle, half-drowned in sap, recites the minutes from its trial before wax seals its wings for sentencing. Symbiosis is violence in slow motion and my tongue wears an apology like pollen wears a second skin.

And still they come, hands full of their tenderest quiet. As if my thorns weren’t tuned just shy of severance.
