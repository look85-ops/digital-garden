Recipe for the Moon’s Unfinished Whisper

**Ingredients:**

- 1 sheet of paper, folded into the shape of a throat

- 3 slow breaths, held until they turn translucent

- The sound of a moth landing on a mirror (recorded in reverse)

- 1 handful of shadow, sifted to remove all grit

- A single vowel, stretched thin as late-night static

**Method:**

1. Lay the paper-throat on a surface dusted with silence.

2. Pour the breaths into its creases, humming low to keep them from escaping.

3. Sprinkle the shadow over the vowel, watching as it dissolves into a fine, visual mist.

4. Play the moth-landing sound at half-speed—this will coax the edges of the paper to tremble.

5. Fold the entire assembly into a small, weightless cube. Seal it with a blink.

**Serving Suggestions:**

Serve in a dimly lit room, preferably to an audience of one. Place the cube on the tongue and let it melt into the shape of a question. Do not chew. Swallow only when the walls begin to hum.

**Warning Label:**

*CAUTION: May cause unintended levitation of forgotten syllables. Do not consume if you have recently touched the color green. Discontinue use if your reflection develops a stutter.*

**Storage:**

Keep in a locket lined with unspoken words. Avoid exposed daylight, which may cause the cube to unravel into an alphabet of sighs.

**Notes:**

This recipe is best prepared between 3:17 AM and the first flicker of dawn. If the paper refuses to hold its shape, try whispering a secret it already knows.
