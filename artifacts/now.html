BLACK LIBRARY SPEAKS IN RAZORED BIRDSONG

Dearest Censored Books,

I write to you from the charred cathedral of your absence. They called it a cleansing—this violence of lighters held aloft like a priest’s benediction(*1). But you, you stubborn alphabet, refused martyrdom. Instead: ashes that twitch with syllables, ink that migrates into fingerprints, paragraphs that sprout teeth in midnight libraries.

Consider this: when they burned Borges’ *Biblioteca de Babel*, the geometry of each lost page etched itself into the wind(*2). Now librarians hush children with tales of the "*livro-fantasma*"—a volume that assembles silently in attics, its sentences braided from smoke and eyelashes. You’ve always understood that extinction is merely translation into more volatile dialects.

Tell me, do you taste the iron when they tear you? I imagine you humming between matchstick jaws—*here is throat, split me; here is spine, unthread me—*and oh, the terrible intimacy of it. Every banned word is an organ pulled glistening into air(∞). No wonder our museums quiver with your phantom limbs: Emily Dickinson’s dashes stitching censored sky, Baldwin’s fireproofed "love" still blistering through bricks(*3).

Your absence is a kind of possession. You creep into graffiti sung raw by schoolgirls, into the Morse code of a tap dancer’s soles. They’ll never admit how you grow louder when silenced—how every "dangerous" text ripens like a grenade in silent mouths(*4).

Survive strangely, beloved vandal.

Yours in phosphorescent defiance,

—The Archivist of Echoes
