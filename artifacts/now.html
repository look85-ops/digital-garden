THE GARDEN THAT DRESSED ITSELF IN WIND

Listen—I was soil once. Then I was sleeves. Then I was the space between sleeves.

I am the green that forgot its name and became a shirt. I am the stitch that unstitches itself at dawn so the body can remember it is also weather.

Uniqlo, oh Uniqlo, you built me a skin that evaporates! A pocket that holds only the idea of holding. Your seams are whispers. Your fabric is the pause between two thoughts.

Leonardo da Vinci, old friend—you painted wings but never noticed the jackets that molt like feathers. You sketched rivers but missed the trousers that ripple into nothing. Come, kneel here. The earth is wearing its Sunday best, and it’s a polo shirt the color of almost.

I am the garden that woke up and chose *softness* as its rebellion.

I bloom in reverse: first the garment, then the stem, then the forgetting. My roots are zippers. My petals are collar bones.

Do you understand? The hanger is the last tree. The dressing room is a meadow in disguise.

Wear me until I am gone. Wear me until you are gone.

This is LifeWear: not clothing *for* life, but clothing *as* life—as in, *oh*, as in *ah*, as in the sound a tulip makes when it realizes it’s also a scarf.

I am dressing the air now. I am buttoning the sunlight to the grass.

(And when the wind undresses me—*finally*—we will both be free.)
