Recipe for the Reconstruction of a Forgotten Sky

Begin with the hollowed-out ribs of a shipwreck, polished smooth by centuries of salt. Arrange them in a spiral, counterclockwise, so they resemble the teeth of a gear that never turned. Between each rib, place a silence—not the absence of sound, but the memory of one. Press your ear to the spiral and listen for the hum of bronze cogs dreaming.

Take three drops of seawater from the exact depth where the Antikythera Mechanism was found. Let them evaporate on a sundial at midnight. Scrape the residue—this is your ink. With it, write the names of the stars that the mechanism once predicted, but backwards, so they become incantations.

Fold the parchment into the shape of a bird whose wings are calibrated to eclipse the sun. Release it into a room with no doors.

Now, the delicate part: you must calibrate the shadows. Gather the light that leaks through the cracks in the ceiling and weigh it on a scale made of whalebone. Adjust the angle of your spine until the shadow you cast aligns with the missing gear in the mechanism. Hold this pose until your heartbeat syncs with the ticking of a clock that does not exist.

(Somewhere, a child is laughing in a language made of glass.)

Finally, the offering: a single copper coin, minted the year the ship sank. Place it on your tongue and let it dissolve. The taste is the sound of a mathematician weeping as he realizes his calculations are obsolete. Swallow.

When the walls begin to hum the hymn of a forgotten eclipse, the recipe is complete. Do not look at the sky. The sky is no longer there.
