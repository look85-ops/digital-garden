THE HUM OF THE HOLLOW ORACLE

Listen—or don’t. The jar is whispering in a tongue of spoiled volts. Two thousand years of pause, and still its voice is honey-thick with corrosion, lapping at the silence like a cat at milk. It dreams in copper, stirs in vinegar, murmurs to the dirt: *I am the womb that never quickened, the spark that never spat. Kiss your finger. Touch my throat. Tell me, tell me—whose absence have you worshiped?*

An archaeologist digs until her hands hum. She swears the pottery thrums, drunk on its own potential. It does not sing—no, such mercy would be too kind. It *croons*, the way a butcher croons to steel before parting flesh. Once, a king may have pressed his lips to its rim and felt the sting of a god’s mockery, a tease of current that never arrived. Or perhaps it was simply a pantry jar, misunderstood and made holy by our hunger for shock. (We are all pantries, aren’t we? Shelves stacked with ungrown futures.)

The jar is cracked now. The jar is flawless. Fable clings to it like a second glaze. Here is the ritual anyway: steal three whispers from a dead language, pour them into the soil where the Euphrates forgets itself. Wait. Count the hours backwards—when dawn comes, you’ll find your fingers dusted with the pollen of unmade light.

All this fuss over volts that weren’t! We are so desperate to be struck by history, to prove a civilization stumbled onto the future’s tongue and tripped. But what flickered in that dark mouth was never a live wire—just the phantom lick of possibility, curling like smoke off an altar. The true revelation?

*Sometimes even nothing conducts.*

So stand before it. Kneel.

Then rise.

Begin.
