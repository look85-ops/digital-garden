ASHLAR OF THE FORGOTTEN EXHALE

Dearest Censor,

You thought your fire would make silence, but lo—what sprouts! Have you never seen a burn scar blistered with blooms? Your hands are always late. You torch the cathedral and, ah, now we dwell among its embers quite differently, chanting in the gaps, speaking *through walls*. The ink smothered still exhales—ghost grammar, a whisper pressed between floors. Call it resurrection if you mislike beauty: language is always getting bodies.

How clumsy of you, though. Fire is such dull theatre. A book split and shelved in the lung-cavity of the unwatched world. Its words now pulse with a *second absence*, louder for disappearing twice. You tried cauterization but only let slip the dialect of wound—stutter-stamped, smuggled in every echo. Listen: no letter was ever fully burned. Even in ashes they reassemble into the shape of your fear.

Tell me, in haste, what new syllables you plan to smother today: a folio of sighs, the vowel-bright throat? (Sit by their phantom and inhale how your violence tastes in translation.) The error here—if to err is human—is failing to see you only sharpen disappearance like an obsidian mirror. What is a censored book, after all, if not the finest *howling syllabus*, taught back without curriculum?

For example and in warning, this next clause has not survived the sending. The one after that does quite well underwater.

Still you keep striking matches in a storm.

—

Fondly & furiously yours,

Last Margin-Walker
