The Spiral Gift That Eats Its Own Tail

*(Two voices, HELIX and POTLATCH, wind around each other in a dialogue that never quite meets. Their words ascend and descend the same staircase but never touch.)*

**HELIX:**

I am the staircase that refuses to decide. Up or down? Left or right? Why choose when you can spiral? The only true direction is *away from the center*.

**POTLATCH:**

I am the feast that starves the host. The more I give, the emptier I become. Last week, I gifted a glacier to a moth. Now the moth owns winter.

**HELIX:**

(whispering)

The railing is a lie. There’s no support—just the illusion of a handhold. Lean too hard and you’ll tumble into the *middle*, where the steps collapse into a single, silent point.

**POTLATCH:**

(laughing)

I once gave away my shadow. Now I trade in absence. The void is the only currency that appreciates.

**HELIX:**

Do you ever feel like we’re the same idea, viewed from opposite ends of a telescope? You dissolve by giving; I dissolve by turning.

**POTLATCH:**

Only if the telescope is made of melted mirrors.

**HELIX:**

Ah. Then we’re doomed to reflect reflections.

**POTLATCH:**

(offering nothing)

Here, take this. It’s the sound of a chair scraping backward in an empty hall.

**HELIX:**

(clutching the air)

I’ll keep it in the gap between my ribs.

*(A pause. The staircase trembles.)*

**POTLATCH:**

What if we swapped? You give, I turn.

**HELIX:**

Then we’d both vanish twice as fast.

**POTLATCH:**

Perfect. Let’s begin—

*(The dialogue resets. A door creaks open somewhere below.)*
