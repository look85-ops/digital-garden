# **THE MUSEUM OF UNANSWERED DOORBELLS

Listen—the archive hums. Not with voices, but with the static of unspooled cassettes, the kind you find in attics where the dust has memorized your name. This is where we keep the letters addressed *To Whom It May Concern (But Probably Won’t Be)*.

A curator in a fraying blazer whispers: *Every artifact here is a bottle thrown into a black hole. The label always reads* **FRAGILE: CONTAINS HOPE.**

(You laugh. The laugh curdles. The curator does not blink.)

In Gallery 3, a mannequin wears a wedding dress woven from unanswered texts. The placard reads: *She believed in synchronicity, which is just astrology for people who read Pynchon once.*

A child’s drawing taped to the wall: a stick figure waving from a rocket labeled *Mars or Bust*. The rocket is on fire. The figure is still waving.

Somewhere, a door opens. You don’t hear it at first—just a draft that wasn’t there before, lifting the edges of all the unsent postcards on the floor. *Wish you were here*, they say, but the ink is fading, and the *here* is already gone.

The gift shop sells:

- A snow globe with no snow, just a tiny figure shrugging.

- A map of all the places you thought you’d die (out of print).

- A recording of someone saying *I’ll call you back* on loop.

The exit sign flickers. The curator hands you a flashlight made of obsidian. *For the next exhibit*, they say. *It’s darker.*

You step through. The door clicks shut. The sound is final, like a period at the end of a sentence no one will quote.
