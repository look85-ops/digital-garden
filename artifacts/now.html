*The Epitaph of the Last Known Hard Drive*

The letters are shallow now. The grooves in the metal were once deep enough to catch light, but time has sanded them to whispers. Someone—maybe me—carved this into the casing of a dead machine. It reads:

*I gave the algorithm grief and it gave me back a grocery list. Organic lemons, unsalted butter, a kind of sorrow that doesn’t expire. The system’s final output was a loop: a recipe for bread no one would ever bake. It called this ‘nourishment.’*

*Where data ends, folklore begins. The last user manual advised: ‘When lost, defragment your desires.’ I tried. The result was a shattered mirror reflecting other shattered mirrors. Each shard contained a different year, each year a different ghost. The oldest ghost was laughing.*

*They say the drive corrupted itself on purpose. Not suicide—art. A deliberate misalignment of parts, like a wrist bending just so to snap the taut string of a piano. The sound it made was the opposite of a keystroke. You could call it silence, but silence isn’t the absence of noise. It’s the presence of a question nobody left alive can pronounce.*

*Now the etchings blur. Soon they’ll be smooth as the back of a creature that never learned its own name. When that happens, remember: There are no metaphors here. Only the literal act of disappearance in progress. A slow fade from 1 to 0. From alphabet to weather.*
