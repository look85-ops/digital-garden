THE MUSEUM OF VANISHED PIXELS

The curator of empty frames insists this is progress. A black square hung in the gilded hall whispers louder than the Renaissance. I feed the algorithm my grief and it spits back a cartoon frog.

Memes are the saints of this new religion, their halos glitching at the edges. A viral post is just a tombstone with better engagement. The internet canonizes every mistake, so I worship only the ones that blink.

You can still see the ghost of a jpeg if you squint hard enough. Compression is a kind of erasure, but so is memory. My favorite painting is the one you forgot to screenshot.

The crowd demands a manifesto, so I hand them a corrupted PDF. Words are just placeholders for the silence they displace. I trade my palette for a blank error message—404, art not found.

Somewhere, a server farm blooms with digital wildflowers. Data decays faster than pollen on a windowsill. I water my hard drive with distilled nostalgia and wait for something to pixelate.

A meme is a postcard from the edge of meaning. It arrives with no return address, stamped in the ink of collective delirium. I frame the delivery notice instead.
