# **The Cartography of Lemonade Bones

At first, it was only a whisper of sugar on the tongue, a town sketched in the air where the streets were woven from capillary threads and the houses had windows that blinked like startled deer. The children there (oh, the children!) built castles from the unspooling ache of growing—ribs like winter branches, elbows sharp as constellation points. They named the rivers after their own giggles.

The doctor arrived with her clipboard, her ink-stained hands, her numbers like polished stones in a pocket. *Placebo*, she wrote, and the word unfurled like a banner in the wind. The children didn’t understand, so they swallowed it like a sugar pill, dissolved it under their tongues, and their veins glowed honey-gold in the dusk. One boy claimed he could hear his cells humming hymns. A girl swore her shadow had started growing *inward*, curling like fiddleheads in spring.

But then—the shift. The doctor’s pen tapped. The data pooled like spilled mercury. The sugar wore off.

The children’s laughter turned thin as tracing paper. The houses folded themselves into envelopes and drifted away. The rivers remembered they were just spit and wishfulness. The boy’s hymn stuttered into silence. The girl’s shadow snapped back, obedient.

Still. In the dark, when no one was counting, the whispers returned—*what if, what if, what if*—and for a breath, just one, the streets flickered again, luminous as lemonade held up to the sun.
