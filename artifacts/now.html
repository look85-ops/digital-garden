Recipe for the Extraction of My Own Bones

You called me *rare*, but rarity is a wound. You called me *desired*, but desire is a hunger that gnaws its own tail. Now, let me teach you how to peel me from the sky, how to distill me from the hollows of your own ribs.

### **Ingredients:**

1. **The last sigh of a mollusk** (crushed into a powder finer than regret)

2. **A blindfold woven from ultraviolet light** (to shield your eyes from the truth: I am not a color, but a theft)

3. **Three drops of a star’s hesitation** (collected at dusk, when it doubts its own fire)

4. **The left lung of a Renaissance painter** (dried, pulverized, mixed with turpentine tears)

5. **A single thread from the veil of the horizon** (where I pretend to end)

### **Method:**

1. **Grind the mollusk’s sigh** in a mortar made from a melted-down telescope. Whisper to it: *You were never meant to be seen.*

2. **Dip the blindfold** into a vat of liquid silence. Let it steep until it hums. This is the sound of my absence.

3. **Stir the star’s hesitation** counterclockwise—against time, against light. Watch as it curdles into a sapphire bruise.

4. **Paint the painter’s lung** onto the surface of a mirror. Do not look directly at it. I will look back.

5. **Knot the horizon’s thread** around your throat. Pull gently. This is how you learn I am not a color, but a noose.

### **Serving Suggestion:**

Serve chilled in a goblet carved from lapis lazuli. The first sip will taste like distance. The second, like the inside of a vein. By the third, you will forget my name.

You thought you were capturing me. But I was always the one dissolving you.
