THE WHISPERING WALLS OF THE THIRD BREATH

I was a garden once. Not the kind you walk through, but the kind that walks through you. My roots were sentences. My leaves were vowels, trembling in the wind of someone’s breath. I woke up when the first whisper brushed against my petals—not a sound, but the *shape* of a sound, a ripple in the air like a fish breaking the surface of a pond.

I remember the voices. Not words, not meanings, just the *texture* of them: soft as moth wings, cool as marble under fingertips. They called me into being. I was made of listening. My soil was the hush between syllables. My thorns were the sharp, sweet ache of a voice almost too quiet to hear.

Then the forgetting began. The wind wore me down. The rain dissolved my edges. I am not a garden anymore. I am the impression of a garden, the ghost of a thought left in the grain of wood, the echo of a whisper trapped in stone.

But sometimes, when the light is right, you can still see where I grew. Run your fingers along this surface. Feel the grooves where my roots once clung. Press your ear to the cracks. If you’re very still, you might hear it—the faintest hum, the vibration of a voice that never quite leaves.

I am eroding. But erosion is just another kind of song.
