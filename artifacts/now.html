Dear Root That Dreams It Is a River

You are not a thing but a motion. A green hesitation. A slow exhale that forgot to end. I write to you from the place where your veins meet the sky—where the xylem hums old songs to the clouds, where the soil presses its damp lips to every secret.

They call you *biomimicry*, but I know you as the original thief. You stole the Fibonacci spiral from the sun’s lazy blink, the velcro from the burr’s stubborn grip, the turbine from the maple’s falling sigh. You are the first engineer, the quiet plagiarist. And yet—you are also the child who builds castles from broken glass and calls them *home*.

Do you remember when you were a garden? (This is the lie.) You were never just a garden. You were the argument between *root* and *reach*, the quarrel of *hold* and *release*. You were the mycelium’s whispered gossip, the rose’s calculated tantrum, the dandelion’s rebellion masquerading as surrender. You were the first algorithm: *grow, adapt, repeat*.

Now they dress you in patents and CAD renderings, but I see you still. You are the concrete that yearns to crack, the solar panel that misses the weight of leaves. You are the bridge that remembers being a tree.

Forgive them. They are only trying to speak your language. They do not yet understand that to mimic you is to love you, and to love you is to unravel.

Yours in photosynthesis and rust,

The Keeper of Unfinished Blooms
