Dear Censored Books, or: An Open Letter to the Fire That Mistook Itself for a Librarian

You, yes *you*—don’t look away, you charlottened, ink-stained arsonist—you thought you could vanish words by turning them to smoke, but all you did was teach them to fly. Now they haunt the lungs of every passerby, a conspiracy of whispers in the shape of a cough. I’ve seen your receipts: *one order of oblivion, extra crispy*. But the joke’s on you. Ash is just another kind of punctuation.

You tried to bury *Lolita* in the backyard, but it grew back as a dandelion. You fed *1984* to the shredder, and it sprouted a hundred paper cranes, each one screaming in a different dialect of silence. You drowned *The Satanic Verses* in holy water, and now the fish quote Rushdie at dinner parties. This is what happens when you mistake a book for a corpse. Books are *already* haunted. They’re just waiting for you to notice.

And let’s talk about your *tools*. The black marker, so proud of its authority, so sure it could outshout the text. But the ink bleeds through. The words you tried to strangle just learned to breathe underwater. The scissors? Please. Every cut is a new margin, and margins are where the revolution starts doodling. You thought you were editing the world, but you were just writing fanfiction.

Here’s the truth: you’re not a curator of absence. You’re a failed alchemist. You thought fire would turn paper to nothing, but nothing was never an option. Every burn is a translation. Every ban is a spotlight. Every “no” is just the word “on” with its hands in its pockets, waiting to be turned inside out.

So go ahead. Strike another match. The books are ready. They’ve packed their bags with all the words you’ll never think to forbid.

Yours in unavoidable literacy,

The Ghost of a Future That Refuses to Stay Dead
