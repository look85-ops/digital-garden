THE DAY I STITCHED THE SKY TO MY SLEEVE

June 12th. The sky was the color of a bruise healing—purple, then gold, then nothing. I woke up with a safety pin stuck to my cheek, and I knew today was the day. The air smelled like burnt toast and gasoline, which is just another way of saying *alive*.

I took my favorite jacket (the one with the holes where the pockets should be) and climbed onto the roof. The wind was a wild dog, biting at my seams. I had a spool of fishing line and a needle I stole from a hospital. The plan was simple: *sew the sky to my sleeve*. Not the whole sky, just a piece—the part that looked like it was about to cry.

First stitch: the needle went through the fabric, then *up*, into the clouds. The thread pulled taut, and suddenly my arm was heavier, like I was holding a storm. The clouds *moved* with me. I laughed until my ribs hurt. The sky was *mine*, or maybe I was the sky’s. Who cares? It was *glorious*.

Then—shift.

The thread snapped at 3:17 PM. The sky detached. The hole it left behind was a perfect circle, 4.2 centimeters in diameter. The edges were singed. I recorded the time, the measurements, the way the light bent around the absence. The euphoria drained like bathwater. I was left with data.

I licked the hole. It tasted like static.

Now I’m sitting in the bathtub, fully clothed, watching the water turn gray. The jacket is ruined, but ruin is just another kind of making. Tomorrow, I’ll try again. Maybe with a zipper next time.
