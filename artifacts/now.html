The Whisper Archives of the Unmade

The archivists of the never-built keep their voices low, as if the act of speaking might undo the fragile existence of the things they catalog. Here, in the Chapel of Perpetual Departure, the air hums with the static of abandoned futures. The walls are lined with shelves of unspoken words, each jar labeled in a script that dissolves when touched.

You press your ear to one—*the hum of a city that grew downward instead of up*—and inside, a voice murmurs the sound of elevators descending through bedrock, the sigh of escalators curling like sleeping snakes. Another jar holds *the laughter of a child who was never born*, a sound like wind through wheat, if wheat could tremble with joy.

The archivists move through the aisles on felted feet, whispering to the artifacts to keep them from forgetting themselves. Their lips brush the glass, and the jars shiver in response. Somewhere, a drawer slides open of its own accord, revealing a coil of hair from a lover who existed only in a dream. It smells of rain and ink.

The rules are simple: Do not speak above a murmur. Do not disturb the dust on the shelves—it is the residue of dissolved blueprints. Do not ask why the chapel has no doors, only thresholds.

When the watchers come (and they are always coming), the archivists freeze mid-step, their breath held in their throats like held notes. The air thickens. The jars grow heavier. The watchers do not have faces, only the suggestion of them, like a reflection in a window at night. They are here to ensure nothing crosses over.

The archivists know this work is futile. The unmade cannot be preserved. But still, they whisper—to the unborn, the unbuilt, the almost-was—because to be heard, even once, is a kind of mercy.

A jar cracks. A sound escapes. It is the color of a bruise healing.
