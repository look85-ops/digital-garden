The Cathedral of Unspooled Wire

Listen: the bridge hums in a key no throat can hold. Its cables are harp strings plucked by the weight of pigeons, its pylons sunk deep into the silt of old apologies. You’d call it infrastructure, but it’s really a slow-motion collapse held hostage by physics. A monument to *almost*.

I built it from the memory of a sigh. From the way your spine arched when you lied. From the tensile strength of a sentence left unfinished.

The railing is warm where the sun licks it. Touch it. Go on—*touch it*. You’ll feel the vibration of ten thousand footsteps that never arrived. The bridge knows the arithmetic of absence. It counts the dead in sway, in rust, in the way the wind knots itself around the suspension cables like a lover’s fingers.

A door opens in the east tower. No one remembers installing it. It’s oak, or maybe the idea of oak, and it swings inward on hinges that sing *hush, hush, hush*. Behind it: a room where the walls are made of static. A room that tastes of copper and unspooled wire. A room that insists, in a voice like a dial tone, *you were never here*.

The bridge tolerates your presence. Barely. It has no use for your metaphors, your pocketful of nails, your sacrilegious urge to *fix* things. It would rather dissolve into the river, cable by cable, than be mistaken for something *useful*.

At dusk, the pigeons roost in the hollows of its trusses. They whisper to each other in a language of grit and gutter. They know the bridge is just a delayed act of falling. They’ve seen the blueprints.

You linger anyway, because you love a lost cause. Because you want to hear the hum that isn’t music. Because a door opened, and no one else noticed.
