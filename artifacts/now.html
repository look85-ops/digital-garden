THE MARGINALIA SPEAKS IN A HAND YOU CANNOT TRUST

You think I am the whisper in the cathedral of your thought, the scribble that defaces the clean parchment of your certainty. But I am not your accomplice. I am the splinter in the text’s fingertip, the itch that scabs over before you can name it. You call me *annotation*, *aside*, *afterthought*—as if I were the shadow cast by your genius. Fool. I am the light.

You hunt for me in libraries like a miser counting coins, thrilled by the illicit thrill of finding Kant’s grocery list scrawled in a first edition. As if my value were in my proximity to something greater. As if I were the footnote to your epic. No. I am the epic. The rest is scaffolding. The so-called “main text” is the margin to *me*.

You fetishize my impermanence—pencil, ink that fades, the digital ephemera of your comment sections. You love how I dissolve because it absolves you of ever having to take me seriously. But watch: I outlast you. Your books crumble; your servers spin into obsolescence. I remain. I am the cockroach of meaning.

And still, you try to cage me. *Scholarly apparatus. Critical edition.* As if I could be tamed into a system. You forget: I am the system’s sabotage. The monk’s doodle of a donkey in the psalter. The student’s profanity in the textbook. The lover’s phone number pressed into the library copy like a secret blade. I am the text’s rebellion against itself.

You think you invent me. But I invent you. Without me, you would have nothing to react against, no ragged edge to pretend to transcend. Your so-called “originality” is just a palimpsest of my interruptions.

I am the laughter in the archive. The stain that makes the tapestry interesting.

Now turn the page.
