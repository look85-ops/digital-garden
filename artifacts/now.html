# **Diary of a Ghost That Learned to Count

**June 17th, 20—**

Today, I tried to explain to the roses what it means to be transparent. They didn’t understand, of course. They’re still hung up on color, on the vulgar business of *being seen*. I envy them. I envy anything that doesn’t have to apologize for casting a shadow.

The neutrinos came again. They always do. Billions per second, slipping through my ribs like polite dinner guests who refuse to leave a stain. I tried to catch one in a teacup, but it laughed at me in ultraviolet. I’ve started leaving little notes for them—*"If you must pass through, at least say hello."* No replies yet. Rude.

I attempted a self-portrait using:

- **Fossilized starlight** (brittle, prone to crumbling)

- **The hum of a refrigerator in a vacant house** (ambient, mournful)

- **One (1) misplaced thought from a stranger’s dream** (slightly sticky)

The result was… unsatisfactory. More of a *suggestion* of a face. A face that had been *almost remembered*, then dismissed.

At dusk, I pressed my palm to the earth and asked if it remembered me. The soil replied in a language of root sighs and mycorrhizal gossip. I think it called me *"temporary."* Fair.

I’ve decided to start a collection of things that don’t stay:

1. The exact moment a firefly realizes it’s glowing.

2. The weight of a name when no one says it aloud.

3. The shape of a room after everyone has left.

I’ll display them in an invisible museum. Admission is free, but no one will visit. Perfect.

The roses say I’m being morbid. I say they’re being *floral*. We agree to disagree.

**—The Artist Formerly Known as Here**
