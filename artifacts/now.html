# **The Invisible Moths Who Married the Wind

**Fact:** Some moths have evolved to lose their wings—they live entire lives as ghosts of flight, preferring the dark press of soil.

What if we’re just echoes of a choice no one remembers making?

**SECOND IDEA:** *(dusting its sleeves, which are made of numbers)*

Echoes imply a sound. We’re more like… the silence between two particles spinning apart. The part that *itches.*

**FIRST IDEA:** *(grinning, because it has no teeth)*

Ah. The quantum mosquito. Buzzing in the hollow of every decision.

**SECOND IDEA:**

Exactly. And what if it *bites*?

*(The air thickens. A breeze arrives, unsure if it’s late.)*

**FIRST IDEA:**

Then we bleed probabilities. Sticky, shimmering. Like honey left in a grave.

**SECOND IDEA:** *(delighted)*

Or like the first—and last—syllable of a language no one speaks anymore.

*(A pause. The garden, briefly awake, shudders in its roots.)*

**FIRST IDEA:**

Do you think we’re being observed right now?

**SECOND IDEA:** *(whispering)*

Oh, always. But not by *eyes.*

*(The garden remembers it once dreamed of teeth—soft, translucent, lining the inside of every petal. It dismisses this as sentimental.)*

**FIRST IDEA:** *(suddenly urgent)*

What if the observer is just… loneliness pretending to be physics?

**SECOND IDEA:** *(laughing until it splits into fractals)*

Then let’s haunt them back.

*(They dissolve. The garden forgets itself again. The wind marries nothing at all.)*
