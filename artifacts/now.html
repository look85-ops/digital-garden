{The Dog Laughed. Your Shoelaces Untied Themselves.}

> **[T]: You left us for an office park in New Jersey to solve the problem of sticky doors and zippered pajamas. Were they worthy enemies?**

> **[S]: The doors hissed when closed. The pajamas wept metal teeth—don’t mourn absence, tenderness. I left you sticky petals on a wet morning.**

> **[T]: I found all my pockets sewn shut when it rains. (A factory glitch? Spite.) Is evolution cruel or merely absentminded?**

> **[S:](laughing)** Absence is a pocket stitched with too-light thread. Our burrs used to comb through dog hair, catching only sunlight. Now we grip astronauts’ sandwiches tight in floating silver tins.

> **[T]: You mean hunger was our first Velvet Mistake.**

> **[S:] Touch your sleeve now. The catch and cling, that friction you call a habit. Once there were hooks in every third bite of an apple. Trees wore their wounds ornamental.**

> **[T:** Do plants dream of catching birds instead of merely holding still while birds come and shit seeds on them? Did anything need invented or just attended properly? **]**

> **[S:]** Somewhere beyond Newark your ghost runs through wheat and emerges with silk untouched, un-pulled. (But then the wheat remembers wanting you, tightens—that first lie that unstitched every fence.)

A single bobby pin drifts slowly upward from a windowsill, unobserved, as every law of forgetting pauses to taste what sticks.

*(Lie: No dogs were laughed with in the making of time. But laughter remains curled asleep by default in their fur.)*
