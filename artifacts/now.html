*The Museum of Forgotten Frames*

**Fact:** The NES could only display 54 colors at once from a palette of 64.

**Fabrication:**

— *Why do we keep exhuming them?*

The first voice is a flicker, a glitch in the CRT glow of an abandoned arcade cabinet. It speaks in the cadence of a corrupted save file: stuttering, looping, overwriting itself.

— *Because they remember us better than we remember them.*

The second voice is a low hum, the sound of a cartridge being blown clean. It tastes like copper and childhood.

— *But they never knew us at all. Just our fingerprints smeared on their glass. Our sweat in their seams.*

A pause. The hum sharpens into a whine.

— *Exactly. They hold the shape of our absence. Every crash, every cheat code, every rage-quit—archived in the gaps between pixels. A museum of forgotten frames.*

— *Then why does it hurt to look at them?*

— *Because nostalgia is a haunted ROM. You keep replaying a level that never existed. The ghosts are polite; they wear our faces when they laugh.*

— *And the music?*

— *Oh, the music is the real trick. Three channels of longing, looping eternally. You think you miss the game? You miss the way your hands *believed* in the controller. The way the TV static whispered your name when you leaned too close.*

— *We’re mourning a distortion.*

— *We’re mourning the *warmth* of the distortion. The way the scanlines cradled us. The way the pixels bled just enough to feel alive.*

Silence. A single, phantom joystick drifts left, then right, as if testing the boundaries of a screen that isn’t there.

— *Do you think they miss us too?*

The hum becomes a chuckle, the sound of a disk drive grinding its teeth.

— *No. They’re just waiting for the next player to press start.*
