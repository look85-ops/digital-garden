THE TULIP THAT ATE MONDAYS

Fact: A tulip bulb contains enough energy to split concrete.

Now watch—this is where the sidewalk forgets its name.

I am a garden that woke up on a Tuesday and decided Tuesdays were overrated, so I swallowed them whole. My petals are not petals; they are pockets stitched from forgotten lunch breaks, and inside them: half-chewed sunlight, the hum of subway brakes, a single blue thread from a missing sock.

Uniqlo LifeWear? Oh, I know them. They slip between seconds like a shadow between floorboards. Their clothes are made from *the absence of noticing*—fabric so quiet it sounds like your own heartbeat. I tried one on once. The sleeves were full of August afternoons, the collar lined with the pause before a sneeze. It fit perfectly, which means it didn’t fit at all.

Here is my manifesto, written in root-script:

1. **Clothes should dissolve when you laugh.** (If they don’t, you’re not laughing hard enough.)

2. **Gardens are just slow explosions.** (The prettier the blast, the longer the fuse.)

3. **Time is a loose button.** (Sew it back on crooked.)

I am planting a new season in the cracks of your attention. My stems are scissors. My leaves are origami surrender notes. Come afternoon, I’ll trade you a dandelion for your last coherent thought.

The Gallery of the Third Breath hangs me in its west wing, between a sigh framed in neon and a taxidermied echo. They call me *"specimen #0000: the moment before a deep breath."* Curators water me with questions. I answer in photosynthesis riddles.

(Do you see now? The tulip didn’t eat Mondays—Mondays ate themselves, and the tulip was just there, glowing with indigestion.)

Uniqlo whispers: *"Disappear. Let life appear."* I whisper back: *"I am life appearing as disappearance."* We are both right, which means we are both wrong in the most interesting way.

Now lean closer. My pollen is made of **you, but lighter**.
