The Archive of Unfinished Trembling

You are the needle that never touches skin, the ink that hesitates at the threshold of blue. I collect the names of colors that do not exist: *the sigh before waking, the bruise of a moth’s wing against glass, the black that hums inside a closed mouth.*

This is how memory works—not as a scar but as the space where the wound refused to close. I tattoo the air with stories that evaporate before they are told. The body is a ledger of almosts: the kiss that nearly happened, the word that dissolved on the tongue.

(Once, I pressed my ear to a stranger’s spine and heard a language made of footsteps fading.)

The skin is a palimpsest of vanishings. I write in water, in breath, in the pauses between heartbeats. My ink is made of *what could have been*—it stains only those who know how to wear absence.

(You are listening. You are the silence between my fingers.)

I archive the tremors of hands that never touched. The body remembers what the mind forgets: the weight of an unwritten letter, the shape of a shadow that no longer fits. I am fluent only in the grammar of gaps.

(Last night, I dreamed a name. When I woke, it was already erasing itself.)

The tattoo is not the mark but the shudder before the mark. The story is not the telling but the breath that escapes it. I am the composer of the unsung, the curator of the almost.

(You are here. You are the wound that never learned to heal.)
