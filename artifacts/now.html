RECIPE FOR COUNTERFEITING THE AETHER (OR HOW TO DISTILL NOTHING INTO SOMETHING SLIGHTLY MORE CONVINCING)

*Yield: One (1) masterpiece, or its facsimile, whichever comes first*

**Ingredients:**

- A fistful of erased signatures (preferably lifted from auction catalogs)

- The ghost of a brushstroke, ideally stolen from a museum guard’s peripheral vision

- Two (2) conflicting theories of originality, beaten until frothy

- A whisper of varnish, aged exactly 23 minutes in the sun of a forgery lab

- One (1) empty frame, pre-haunted by its own expectations

- A pinch of laughter, strained through the teeth of an art critic mid-sneeze

**Method:**

1. **Begin with absence.** The canvas is not blank; it is *strategically depleted*. Scrape away all intent until only the idea of a painting remains. If questioned, insist this is "conceptual purity."

2. **Infuse the void.** Soak your stolen brushstroke in irony until it swells to twice its size. Suspend it in the frame, ensuring it casts a shadow that vaguely resembles *Monet’s Water Lilies*—if Monet had painted them while sleepwalking.

3. **Fold in the signatures.** These should be applied with trembling reverence, as if signing a confession. The more illegible, the better. Authenticity thrives in the gaps between letters.

4. **Age artificially.** Expose the piece to the gaze of a dozen pigeons in a locked room. Their indifference is the finest patina.

5. **Serve with contradiction.** Present the work as both a "radical critique of authorship" and "something your toddler could’ve made." The tension between these claims is the adhesive that binds the forgery to legitimacy.

**Final Instruction:**

When the curator leans in, whisper: *"The original is under layers of laughter—peel carefully."* Watch their face. If they frown, you’ve succeeded. If they laugh, you’ve failed. Adjust recipe accordingly.

**Variation:** Replace *frame* with an empty pedestal. Label it *"The Sculpture Edition"* and charge double for the privilege of missing the point.
