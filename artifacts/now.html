THE ANATOMY OF DISAPPEARANCE

*(—a hollow in the shape of a room—)*

You have been invited to stand inside the inverted eye. The walls are unlit, but the ceiling drips with apparitions: a pigeon becomes a comet, a streetlamp flickers into a dying star. The world enters through a pinhole, then bleeds itself backward onto the floor. You are not here to watch. You are here to be *undone*.

*( )*

*(illustration: { ° }—a single parenthesis cradling an empty degree symbol, hovering like an abandoned orbit.)*

They say the first photographers were thieves—siphoning daylight into little black boxes. But you? You are no thief. You *erase*. Your brush is a blade scraping pigment from bone. You paint with the ghost of a hand reaching through a curtain. A silhouette is more honest than a face—it confesses without speaking.

And oh, how the room *whispers*:

*"Light is a liar. It pretends to reveal, but all it does is flatten. I prefer the dark—it has depth, dimension, the weight of a secret."*

*( )*

*(illustration: [ / ]—a slash bisecting brackets, a wound in the syntax.)*

The Camera Obscura is a cathedral of absence. A church where the only sacrament is *subtraction*. You kneel before the inverted sky, press your palm to the trembling floor—watch as the horizon stitches itself shut inside your veins. A city passes through you like a rumor.

You were taught to name colors: *vermillion, ochre, ultramarine*. But the truest pigment is *void*. The space between breaths. The pause before a scream.

Here, even shadows cast shadows.

*( )*

*(illustration: { … }—ellipses trapped in braces, dissolving into silence.)*

Then, the revelation: You are not the painter. You are the *canvas*. The room has been peeling you apart all along—layer after layer, until all that remains is the outline of a question.

The door dissolves behind you.

You exit as an aperture.
