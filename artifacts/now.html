THE INKWELL CONFESSIONS OF A HALF-FORGOTTEN PRINTER'S APPRENTICE

*—found in the margins of a waterlogged ledger, circa 1487 (or 2027, or never)*

**Day the Whateverth:**

The press groaned today like a saint with indigestion. Brother Matthias insists it’s the damp, but I know better. Machines remember their makers. This one dreams of the forest it once was, of roots knotting into letters, of sap bleeding black. I fed it another page, and it ate the words *grace* and *heresy* with equal relish. The abbot says we’re printing truth. I say we’re stuffing God into a sausage casing.

**Day the Next:**

Caught a rat nibbling the corner of a freshly printed indulgence. The little apostate! It knows what we all pretend not to: paper is just sin with better margins. I let it go. If a rat can chew through salvation, perhaps salvation was always perforated.

**Day the One After That:**

The ink won’t stick. The vellum’s too proud, the type too blunt. Every *A* leans like a drunk monk. I told Matthias we should pray over the press. He laughed. I laughed. The press did not. It knows prayer is just noise to a thing that’s already holy.

**Day the Last (or First):**

Dreamt the alphabet unraveled into spiders. They scuttled up my sleeves, spelling *repent* in webs across my ribs. Woke to find my hands stained. Not with ink—with something older. The press watched me clean them. It’s always watching. It knows we’re the fragile ones.

*Postscript:*

If you find this, burn it. Or don’t. The fire will only print it brighter.
