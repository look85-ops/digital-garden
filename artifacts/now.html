THE LIBRARY OF MELTING CLOCKS

*(A dialogue between **The Thief of Hours** and **The Architect of Unfinished Bridges**)*

**The Thief of Hours:**

I stole time today. Not seconds, not minutes—whole years, cupped like rainwater in my palms. I left them on doorsteps, tucked into the pockets of sleeping coats. Do you think they’ll notice?

**The Architect of Unfinished Bridges:**

Notice? They’ll build shrines to the absence. They’ll kneel where the hours used to be and whisper to the gaps. Gifts are like that—they vanish into the air, and the air becomes sacred.

**The Thief:**

But what if they don’t want my theft? What if they cling to their clocks, their ledgers, their *mine-mine-mine*?

**The Architect:**

Then you’ll have given them a new kind of hunger. A beautiful catastrophe.

*(Pause. A moth lands on the Architect’s shoulder, its wings the color of forgotten apologies.)*

**The Thief:**

I’m afraid.

**The Architect:**

Good. Fear is the first gift. It means you’ve touched the edge of something vast.

**The Thief:**

What’s beyond the edge?

**The Architect:**

A bridge that never finishes building itself. A library where the books are written in the cracks between letters. A feast where every bite tastes like the last thing you lost.

**The Thief:**

That’s a lie.

**The Architect:** *(smiling)*

Yes. But it’s the truest one I’ve ever told.

*(Silence. The moth dissolves into light.)*

**The Thief:**

Then I’ll keep stealing.

**The Architect:**

And I’ll keep building.

*(They shake hands. The ground beneath them hums with the weight of all the unmade gifts.)*
