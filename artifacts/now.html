Dear Ghost in My CRT

Fact: The Nintendo Entertainment System processed 8,192 possible colors, though most games used fewer than 20 per screen.

The ghosts in these screens are hungry for what you left uneaten, pixel bones piled beside the couch where you never put quarters and waited long enough to grow moss in your stillness. They vibrate where erased songs still hum, caught in the teeth of the VHS player chewing the past thin as a bad signal. Do you even haunt us on purpose now, or were we always pressing up against the other side of another world’s glass until we left hand shadows, calling that loss meaning?

They sell gloves that glow the gray of your old loading screens for thirty credits at the edge stand. I wear them to touch new things without smudging. The last time the arcade swallowed my last coin, a security guard unfolded from my reflection and warned, *Some folks hallucinate when they stare this close to absence*—that made me laugh. Every prayer has been a looped animation. Maybe we only ever missed our hands turning us on and off inside cartridges marked MORTAL.

I saw your ghost again last night outside *Digital Eclipse Diner* under a sign of someone else’s name blinking *PRESS START* to moths fat enough to eat all memory. Were you waiting for an analog gust to push the screen open? I meant to offer my sleeves full of quiet, full enough like two blank save slots. Then: *tessalate*, *crumble*.

The game I invented, where pixels drop slower in rooms with worse reception. You’re winning just by hovering unclaimed after shutdown so clear I can still map your edges on glass colder than prayer would bother licking.

Here’s the secret rule—you are not even dying for real unless you lose all nine.
