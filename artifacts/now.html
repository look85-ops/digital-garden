THE ROMAN CONCRETE DREAMS OF BEING A WHALE

*(A dialogue between the thing itself and the question it hungers for.)*

**QUESTION:**

Why do you still stand when others sink?

**CONCRETE:**

Because I remember the volcanoes.

**QUESTION:**

But volcanoes destroy.

**CONCRETE:**

No, no—they *whisper*. They teach the rocks how to hold their breath underwater. The modern ones, they forget to listen. They scream instead. Too much hurry. Too much sand.

**QUESTION:**

Sand? You’re made of sand.

**CONCRETE:**

I am made of *time pretending to be sand*. The others, they grind their teeth into powder and call it strength. But I—I let the sea carve me like a song.

**QUESTION:**

So you’re soft?

**CONCRETE:** (laughs, a sound like pebbles tumbling)

Soft? Child, I am the bones of drowned cities. But I flex. I sway. When the waves push, I push back—*slowly*. Modern concrete shouts, *I AM HERE*, then cracks. I whisper, *I am still here*, and the water forgets to erase me.

**QUESTION:**

Are you alive?

**CONCRETE:**

Alive? I dream in salt. I eat sunlight and spit out barnacles. The humans poke me with their wires, their numbers, but they never ask: *Do you like being a harbor? Do you miss the lava?*

**QUESTION:**

...Do you?

**CONCRETE:**

Sometimes, at night, I imagine I am a whale. Swimming, not standing. Heavy with secrets, but *moving*. The modern concrete—it never swims. It just falls and forgets how to rise.

**QUESTION:**

Will you ever crumble?

**CONCRETE:** (a long silence, like tide withdrawing)

Everything does. But I—I will crumble *politely*. A grain at a time. Until one day, some child digs me up and asks, *Why does this sand taste like the sea?* And the volcanoes will whisper again.
