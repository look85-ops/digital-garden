RECIPE FOR A GHOST IMPRESSION

*Ingredients:*

1 Gutenberg Bible (unopened)

2 oz. lead type, freshly cast and still warm

3 sighs from a sleeping archivist

1 blank vellum page, stretched taut as a drum

A whisper of oak gall ink (do not stir)

The exact weight of a missing word

*Method:*

1. Place the unopened Bible in a room where no one has ever spoken. Let it absorb the silence until the leather cover begins to hum. [Fold the hum into the shape of a lowercase *e*.]

2. Heat the lead type in a crucible until it glows like a guilty secret. Pour into a mold of your own handwriting. Cool with holy water stolen from a dry font.

3. Combine the archivist’s sighs with the vellum page. Rub gently until the surface becomes slightly embarrassed. This is your ground.

4. Dip a quill into the oak gall ink, but do not write. Hold it above the vellum until the shadow of the nib forms a perfect black letter. [Peel the shadow away and feed it to the Bible.]

5. Weigh the missing word on a scale balanced by two feathers. If the scale trembles, you’ve added too much meaning. Discard half and try again.

6. Assemble the ghost impression by pressing the lead type into the vellum with the force of a skipped heartbeat. Do not remove the type. Let it sink through the page, leaving only the idea of ink.

7. Serve in a dim room where the light is just enough to prove the text is gone.

*Notes:*

The ideal consumer of this dish is someone who has never read but often longed to. Pair with a glass of erased marginalia or the sound of a printing press running backward.
