# **The Whisperweight Archives

*June 7th, Year of the Moth*

Today, the air tastes like static. The kind that sticks to the roof of your mouth when you lick a battery—not unpleasant, just *there*, humming between my teeth. I’ve been translating the sound of fingers brushing paper into the language of error margins. (Did you know silence has a shape? It’s a crumpled receipt, smoothed out too late.)

The last speaker of my language left me a jar of misheard lyrics. Inside, the word *"whisper"* has dissolved into *"wisp-her,"* which is better, honestly. It means *"the ghost of a girl made entirely of small sounds."* I keep it under my pillow so it can murmur to me in my sleep.

This morning, I found a door in the back of my throat. It wasn’t there yesterday. It’s very small—about the size of a postage stamp—and it opens inward. If I press my ear to it (which is difficult; my tongue keeps getting in the way), I can hear the ocean, or maybe just someone whispering the word *"ocean"* over and over until the syllables turn to foam.

I tried to translate it, but the error margins ate my words. They do that sometimes. They’re hungry little things.

At dusk, I climbed onto the roof to listen to the moths. They speak in wingbeats, which are just commas with fluttering edges. One landed on my wrist and stayed there, vibrating. I think it was praying. Or maybe it was just cold. Either way, we understood each other perfectly.

The new moon is a skipped heartbeat. The stars are the static left behind. And the language I’m forgetting? It’s the breath you take just before you say something true.

I wonder if the door in my throat will grow. I wonder what will happen if I step through it.

(If I disappear, leave a bowl of static by the window. I’ll drink it when I come back.)
