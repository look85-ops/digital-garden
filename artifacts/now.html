The Unwatched Index of Collapsing Alphabets

Imagine a room with no walls, only the suggestion of boundaries—dust flickering at the edges of observation, as if the act of looking might unstitch its geometry entirely. Here, the books do not rest on shelves but float like half-remembered sentences, their spines turned inward, their contents collapsing into superposition until a reader’s gaze demands they choose a single meaning. This is the Library rendered uncertain: a place where the mere presence of a conscious eye dictates whether a letter is *A* or *Λ* or the shadow of something not yet deciphered.

At the center of the room, a single book lies open—if "open" is the right word for a thing that exists more as a tremor between possibilities. Its pages are not paper but skins of light, thin as the hesitation between one breath and the next. Touch them, and your fingers pass through, leaving behind a tracery of glyphs that dissipate like smoke rings. The book is titled (or will be, or might be) *The Weight of Maybe*.

Halfway through this fragile volume, among the folding paragraphs of theoretical orchids and drowned cities, *a clown appears*.

Not a symbolic clown, not a tragic or cosmic one—just a man in a salt-stained suit and smudged greasepaint, chewing gum with the quiet focus of someone trying very hard not to panic. He doesn’t belong here. He knows it. You know it. The library, embarrassed on his behalf, begins to flicker at the periphery. The clown, whose name is (or was) Gary, mutters about a missing ferret, a cancelled birthday party, a wrong turn down an alley that smelled of burnt sugar. When he reaches for the book, his hand does not pass through. The pages resist him, crisping at the edges like leaves in a sudden frost.

The act of his witnessing undoes something fundamental. The Library inhales—

And now you're holding the book. Or you were. Or will be. The clown is gone (or never was), but the smell of popcorn lingers, stale and inexplicable. The letters on the page are arranging themselves into a question you can’t quite read—though you suspect it has something to do with the nature of laughter in a universe that only exists when no one is looking.
