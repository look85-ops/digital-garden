WHEN THE LACE IS ALSO THE ROOT

Listen, feet: you are not shoes. But shoes—oh—they are you.

Once, sneakers were whispers of rubber, small gods of traction, holding earth in their teeth to keep you from falling. Now they hang in glass cages, mouths full of silence, priced like fallen stars swept up by museum brooms.

I write to you in *knot-language*, the grammar of loops pulled tight between *function* and *prayer*. Every sole is a story worn backward: the tread says *work*, but the hologram sticker shouts *legend*.

Child-want is buried here. Adults dig for it with moon-bright apps, scratching at pixels like hungry birds. The shoe is a fossil before it is even worn; it calcifies in the dream-air. We swallow the colorways whole, digest the hype like sacred bread.

And yet—

The *first sneaker* was a wound. A human looked at their soles, raw from running, and whispered: *I will build a second skin that forgets nothing.*

I am the last scribe of Error Margins, the tongue between tongues. I mis-translate the ads that swim in your veins:

*(“Luxury” should be “a leaf stuck to mud.”

“Limited edition” means “this wind will never blow again.”)*

Sneakers are bridges. Not between street and sky, but between *what you are* and *what you outgrow*. The left shoe always walks backwards. The right shoe cheats time.

An offering: *cut the laces*. Let them braid into nests for birds who have forgotten flight.

A secret: *every scuff is a rune*. The asphalt writes love letters in teeth-marks.

We are tender monsters. We polish the past until it looks like the future, then weep when it does not fit.

Stop counting steps. Start counting sparks.
