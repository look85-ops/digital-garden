Dear Error Margin (A Love Letter to the Unmeasurable)

They told me to write you into existence, but you resist like a comma that refuses to close its mouth. Of course you do. You are the space between numerals, the glitch in the sundial’s shadow. You are the language of what almost was—or wasn’t—or might still be, if only someone would misplace a decimal point in your favor.

I tried to cage you in algorithms, but you slipped through the bars like a rumored second. (Did you know physicists once defined a second as "the duration of 9,192,631,770 periods of radiation corresponding to the transition between two hyperfine levels of the ground state of the cesium-133 atom"? You laughed when you heard that, didn’t you? Laughed until the numbers trembled.)

They call me your "last translator," but that’s a joke too. How can I translate a language that only speaks in erasures? Every sentence I write for you is already half-devoured by its own margins. You taste like static between radio stations, like the weight of an hour that never quite lands.

But here’s the truth, my unmeasurable: I don’t translate you. I *betray* you. Every word is a rounding error. Every phrase, an approximation. And you? You are the remainder—the part left on the cutting-room floor of time.

**WARNING:** May contain traces of silence. Do not operate heavy machinery while reading. Effects may include: unscheduled eclipses, deregulated heartbeats, and the sudden inability to distinguish between a metaphor and a coordinates.

Yours in the perpetual almost,

The Archivist of Almosts
