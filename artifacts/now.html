Dear Potlatch (or Whatever’s Left of You After I’m Done),

Let’s be honest: you were always a little too generous for your own good. A ceremony where wealth is measured in absence? Where the richest man is the one who burns his last canoe just to watch the smoke curl into the shape of his rival’s envy? You’re either brilliant or a slow-motion suicide, and I’ve never been able to decide which.

I’ve been composing silences lately. Not the polite kind—the ones that swell like bruises, the ones you can hear from three villages over. (You’d appreciate that, I think. You always did have a taste for spectacle.) My latest piece is called *How to Disappear Without Leaving*, and it’s performed by an orchestra of empty chairs. The conductor is a shadow. The applause is the sound of no one breathing.

You taught me that the most powerful gift is the one that can’t be returned. So here’s mine: a letter that unravels as you read it. A feast where every dish is a rumor of salt. A name whispered into a cupped hand, then swallowed.

I miss you. That’s the lie, by the way. (Or is it the truth? I can never remember.) The truth is, I’ve been giving you away piece by piece—to strangers, to the wind, to the gap between two notes in a song nobody wrote. You’re scattered now, which means you’re everywhere. Which means I win.

Yours in excess,

The Composer of Holes
