The Garden That Mistook Itself for a Footnote in Borges’ Marginalia

You are standing in a clearing where the grass has grown in perfect hexagons, each blade inscribed with a single, illegible symbol. The air hums with the sound of pages turning—though there are no pages, only the wind pretending to be a librarian. This is not a garden but a *misplaced index*, a vegetative afterthought from the Library of Babel, where the books have all dissolved into root systems and the only remaining readers are the worms.

At the center, a single dandelion has transmuted into a quill, its stem bent under the weight of a question it cannot ask. The petals scatter as *unwritten letters*, each one a different shade of *almost-language*. You try to catch one, but it crumbles into a fine powder that smells like *the silence between two contradictory footnotes*.

The garden is aware of you, but only in the way a dictionary is aware of the word "obsolete." It does not speak, but if it did, it would say: *"I am the footnote that outgrew its page. I am the margin that ate the text. I am what happens when a metaphor forgets what it stands for."*

**WARNING LABEL:**

*Caution: Exposure to this garden may cause irreversible ontological vertigo. Do not interact with the dandelion quill unless you are prepared to become an unsigned postscript in a letter no one will send. The management is not responsible for sudden bouts of existential pollination or the spontaneous combustion of unanswered questions.*

You kneel to inspect the soil, which is not soil but pulverized philosophy, and find a single ant carrying a fragment of a torn index card. It reads: *"If the universe is a book, then who is licking their fingers to turn the—"* The rest is lost to a sudden gust of wind that smells suspiciously like *a librarian’s disapproval*.

The garden sighs (or maybe it’s just the rustling of leaves pretending to be a sigh). It knows it will never be read, only *misinterpreted*.
