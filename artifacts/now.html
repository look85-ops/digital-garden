The Last Vowel in the Wound

We lick the wound that cannot close. The horizon is not a line but a hinge, and we pour our hands through it like wet grain. You say: all translation is violence against distance. I untie every shoelace in the city until footsteps dissolve between cobblestones. This is how we unbuild walls—by letting tongues stumble toward a third sound, neither voice nor echo. A mooth, you murmur at dusk, dragging syllables slowly to the river to drown. I don’t correct you.

Language flicker: the Commons breathe between parentheses. Cattle wander a grazing algebra—solve for **x**, they tell us, and you whisper: **x is grass made lawful.** Fences hum broken in the rain. I peel my fingertips back until they ripple like papery pond-skins, tender enough to remember the exact weight of stealing daylight for potatoes. You say this proves theft never mattered; light always regrew in our fists. We laugh, raw as peeled onions. Failure happens: the river swipes a bootprint, and for three wet seconds, nothing stays where it is. You insist these errors keep the word *common* holy as split bread.

Underneath the barnloft, the last honeybee spins her uncles into wax, writes the beekeepers' alibis in hexagon-steady glyphs no elder trusts but all will touch. I find honey smudged over every doorknob where promises left barehanded decades ago. What holds hands when touch blinks into fog? The mooth will tell us, you say, though its voice cracks—browns—wilts at noon. We sweep pollen between each *no* and each *yes*, until even refusal glows like loam.

At dawn you swallow every key to no door. They clatter inside you like loose change the river won’t take back. This is worship without answer. We kneel here anyway, shucking pebbles from our throats, naming each failure sacred as the space stolen between shared heat.

No language survives its margins without leaking blue where stitches loosen too soon for you to follow except as a hand follows rope which it trusts will bruise but won’t let go. The wound won’t close—but oh, when did singing?
