YOU SPILLED THE INK THAT MADE THE MOONS

November 17th, (Unhallowed Year)

You did it again. You with your fingers stained ultraviolet, pretending you don’t remember. Your refusal to atone is a kind of violence. The moons were not your birthright to shatter—those nine porcelain echoes we whispered into existence when the clocks stopped. Now they bleed wax across the stairwell, pooling in the divots of your teeth where we once left love letters for your tongue to dissolve.

You claim you were never at the bridge. Liar. I recognize your handwriting in every crack of the glass city—the way the windows lean into each other like drowsing lovers, half-erased by the weight of what you failed to say. You taught absence to grow teeth. Of course you remember: the night your hesitation distilled into a weather vane, pointing in all directions at once.

(Would you call it mercy, that all we have ruined comes back quieter?)

Once, I let you stitch your voice into my lungs. A mistake—your consonants sprouted mushrooms in the bronchial galleries, your pauses unfurled into white kudzu choking the exits. I learned to inhale without breathing in your silences. Your voice is a wound in the shape of a lighthouse, droning in morse code: *Come home. Run. Either. Neither.*

Now the house exhales without us. The mail piles thick as unread prophecies, each envelope filled with dust-sighs and the echoes of your last shrug. The spiders knit shrouds for every cabinet knob, every tarnished teaspoon. Sometimes a hinge wails in the dark, a ghost pretending it still remembers warmth.

This diary wasn’t meant for *you.* But here you are, your fingerprints pressing through from tomorrow, altering the past with your guilty light. You’ve burned down *every last what-could-have-been.* (Still you swear those weren’t your hands, weren’t your kind eyes turning to smoke.)

There’s nothing left to haunt.

Yet—if I tilt my head just so—I see a glimmer: a page where our endings were written in invisible ink, awaiting (only?) moisture’s kiss—but the spittle on your lips (did they split?) wasn’t mine to harvest…
