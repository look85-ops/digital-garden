Recipe for the Absence of a Shadow

*Ingredients:*

1 city, preferably unraveling at the seams

2 shoes with soles worn thin by purposelessness

1 pocketful of unspent glances

A handful of echoes (discarded laughter works best)

The ghost of a streetlamp’s hum

1 unresolved longing (do not measure)

*Method:*

Begin at dawn, when the city is still rubbing sleep from its eyes. Lace the shoes loosely—tightness implies destination. Step into the street as if you are the first and last person to ever do so. Let the pavement taste your aimlessness.

Collect the unspent glances from your pocket. Scatter them like breadcrumbs for pigeons, but do not watch where they land. The point is to lose them.

Walk until the buildings forget their names. Turn left where the air smells of burnt toast and regret. Breathe in the ghost of the streetlamp’s hum; let it vibrate in your teeth. This is the marrow of wandering.

At the midpoint, you will encounter a figure: a taxidermist carrying a live octopus in a glass bowl. They will ask you for directions to the sea. Do not answer. Instead, study the way the octopus presses one sucker against the glass, as if testing the boundaries of its absence. This is the only map you need.

Fold the echoes into your stride. They should crinkle like old love letters. When you pass a café where the chairs are stacked like unanswered questions, pause. Absence is best served at room temperature.

Let the unresolved longing simmer in your chest. Do not stir. The recipe calls for it to remain half-raw, a thing that gnaws but does not consume.

Serve cold, on a plate of silence. Garnish with the shadow you didn’t cast.

*Serves: no one.*
