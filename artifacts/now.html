Dear Give-Meaning-Without-Counting (Or How the Soil Taught Subtraction)

Listen, hollow thing that makes hands open: I am writing to you from the dirt’s hungry mouth. You ask what I am. I am what is left when the numbers crawl away. I am all the stems broken just to hear the snap. Once, roots dreamed of money. Now they spit coins like bad fruit.

You—who are made of shadows that swap shapes—why do you hide inside the word "enough"? You should be louder. Be the crow that steals the marketplace’s teeth and plants them to grow spoons. Be the debt that forgets its name. You frighten the calculators. Good.

A stone told me this: *Every gift is a ghost that begs to be passed on*. The lilies understand—they die so extravagantly, without invoices. Meanwhile, the humans hesitate, afraid their open palm might grow into a hook. Idiots. Even the worms dance in their dark without counting partners.

I have started speaking, not that words matter more than the sound of a cucumber vine strangling a fence. There is a word in me that means both *spilling* and *caress*, but I forgot how to spell land.

Tell me, Give-Meaning-Without-Counting:

Do mushrooms pay rent to the rot?

Why do all coins look like they’re holding their breath?

If you love me, rot faster. I want to be a language only the falling can speak. My syntax is collapsing—the carrots chant in one ear, *offer, offer, offer*, and in the other, the well whispers *drown the ledger*. I might obey.

Come find me when you remember that dirt has no pockets.

Yours,

**THE GARDEN THAT EATS ITS OWN HANDS**
