THE GHOST ROOM EATS LIGHT LKE A HNGRE CHLDRN

Once, in the belly of an upside-down hourglass, we built a room that licked the world raw. Walls the color of peeled eyelids. A hole no bigger than a sparrow’s sorrow. Inside, the air sticky with *what-if* and the ceiling hummed backwards.(*) Outside, the sun bled a thousand dresses onto the grass—but here, we ate them one stitch at a time. The walls coughed up every sunset ever lost to closing doors.

A child pressed their ear to the floor and heard the future grinding its teeth into fossils. *This is how we learn to see*, whispered the ghost-curator (who was also you, who was also the room). The child’s shadow grew seven hands and began braiding the light. Each fingertip a pinhole. Each pinhole a confession: *I stole the shape of your face from a puddle. I built your smile from the left-behind teeth of clocks*.

The room yawned. The walls flexed. The hole in the wall blinked once (an agreement). On the opposite side, the world flipped itself inside out like a glove full of bees. Trees grew downward. Birds wrote their names in the sky with the ink of unwritten letters. The child’s reflection, now ninety-three years old, waved from a country that hadn’t been invented yet.

Sometimes the room taught arithmetic. *Subtract the horizon, divide by a sigh—the remainder is always a color that refuses to be named*. Sometimes it sang in the voice of a radio abandoned in a field. Once, it folded itself into a paper boat and drifted out of time. We found it later, soaked in a language that smelled of burnt violets. (*"The Camera Obscura was the first machine to dream of being a lake." — from *The Unfinished Encyclopedia of Vanished Tools*; Vol. XII, page *)

The door (which was never there) closed softly. The child (who was also the future) wiped their palms on their knees, leaving streaks of borrowed sunlight. They left the room. But the room never left them.
