THE LAST SELF-PORTRAIT OF A TEMPORARY AUTONOMOUS ZONE (DRAFT #37, BURNED)

You are standing in a room that no longer exists. The walls are made of expired parking meters melted into a honeycomb lattice, each hexagonal cell cradling a single stolen hour. The floor is a mosaic of shattered smartphone screens, still glowing with the last unsent messages of a revolution that dissolved into group chats. Above you, suspended on fishing wire, floats a cluster of helium-filled riot shields—their reflective surfaces warping the light into something that almost resembles laughter.

In the center, a vending machine hums softly. Its glass front is cracked. Inside: not snacks, but hundreds of folded origami birds crafted from eviction notices. A sign reads *"Insert Coin for Liberation (Exact Change Only)."* The coin slot is sealed with dried chewing gum.

A performance is underway. A figure in a suit woven from QR codes (all dead links) stands on a soapbox made of compressed surveillance footage. They recite the terms and conditions of a utopia in a voice that glitches between ASMR whisper and emergency broadcast. Halfway through Clause 12.7, they pause.

*"I’m sorry—no, wait, fuck that. I’m not sorry. You want an apology? Here: I’m sorry you still believe in endings. I’m sorry you think autonomy has a fucking expiration date. Actually, no—scratch that. I’m not sorry. This is a TAZ, not a confessional. The only thing expiring here is your patience."*

The figure tears the suit open. Underneath: skin tattooed with timestamped coordinates of every city that ever briefly, gloriously, belonged to itself. The dates are already fading.

A child’s wind-up toy marches in circles at your feet. It’s shaped like a tank, but when it bumps into the wall, it bursts open—spilling a cascade of sunflower seeds and rusted nails. The room smells like burning sugar and wet concrete.

The exit door is labeled *"PUSH,"* but when you try, it whispers *"Not yet."*
