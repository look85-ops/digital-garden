The Cartographer’s Last Breakfast

Fact: The human tongue can detect five basic tastes—sweet, salty, sour, bitter, and umami.

Fabrication: The sixth taste, *cartographic*, is only perceptible when licking a map that is actively erasing itself. To prepare such a map, follow this recipe:

1. **Acquire a vanishing medium**: Steal the shadow of a bureaucrat’s briefcase at noon. Stretch it over an empty frame. (If the shadow resists, whisper Duchamp’s grocery list to it—*eggs, bread, a small urinal*—until it relaxes.)

2. **Draw with absence**: Dip a brush into the space where a landmark used to be. Paint the outline of a city that no one remembers. The streets should fray at the edges, like old cassette tapes.

3. **Season with paradox**: Sprinkle the map with powdered silence (harvested from the pause between a question and its answer). This will give the dissolution a pleasant crunch.

4. **Serve immediately**: The map is only legible while it’s falling apart. Invite guests to trace their fingers along its evaporating borders. When they complain that they can’t read it, nod solemnly and say, *Exactly.*

The dish is best consumed standing up, in a room where the walls are painted with expired passports. Pair it with a glass of melted snow from a monument’s plinth.
