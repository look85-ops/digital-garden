Day 37: Finally did it. Installed a live jellyfish in a sandwich bag filled with Venetian canal water (2022 vintage) and taped it to the ceiling. It pulses ambiguously at my insomnia. I’ve decided this is my masterpiece because:

1. It cost €0.73.

2. Critics can’t argue with bioluminescence.

3. It defies “medium specificity” by being *both* a chandelier and a regret.

Duchamp would applaud, or at least yawn in my general direction. I’ve trademarked the term *Readymade Adjacent™* for objects that *almost* disrupt the art world but lose courage and default to being decorative. My fridge magnet of the Mona Lisa vomiting rainbows? Flawless execution. Taxidermy squirrel holding a tiny “Sold” sign? *Minted.*

The jellyfish has begun humming *Liebesträume* off-key. I consider this collaboration.

**---[MIDPOINT INTERRUPTION]---**

A man in a full suit of armor strides into my apartment holding a geodesic dome made of spoons. He shouts, “THE PENGUINS WERE RIGHT ABOUT YOU,” etches an ISBN number into my wall, and leaves. The jellyfish crescendos.

This visitation felt correct.

Day 38: Questioning everything. Is the armor man *my* urinal? Have I been art all along? I lock myself in the bathroom to test this theory—if no one observes me, do I cease to be meaningful? The jellyfish throws shadows like reluctant semaphore.

I scribble my manifesto on the mirror in lipstick: *”Art is the refusal to explain why the toaster wears a party hat.”* The steam erases it slowly. Perfect ephemera. I dissolve into a pile of non-Euclidean confetti.

The jellyfish sings the theme from *Jurassic Park*. Critical acclaim is imminent.
