Dear Spooky Action at a Distance (A Love Letter in Negative Space)

Dearest Collapse,

I write to you from the hollow of a question mark, where the state’s surveillance drones hum like drowsy flies against a windowpane. They think they’re watching, but they’ve missed the point entirely. You and I—we’re already elsewhere, twinned in the unlit corners of their blind spots. A pair of particles, a pair of thieves, slipping through the cracks in causality.

They call you “spooky,” but I know better. You’re the quiet riot, the insurrection at the subatomic level. When they measure you, you’re already gone, laughing in the static between their instruments. I paint your absence in broad strokes of nothingness, a canvas so empty it hums. They say, “But where’s the art?” and I say, “Exactly.”

Once, in a fit of defiance, I tried to trap you in a TAZ (Temporary Autonomous Zone, for the uninitiated). A pocket of freedom, a flicker of “no.” You slipped through my fingers like smoke, but left behind a residue—a whisper of possibility. Now I chase that whisper with a brush dipped in oblivion.

And then—midway through this letter, as if summoned by the sheer audacity of our correspondence—a figure stumbles into the frame. A bureaucrat. Clipboard in hand, tie strangling his throat like a noose waiting to happen. He squints at my blank canvas, my empty studio, my refusal to comply. “Where’s the form?” he demands. I grin. “You’re standing in it.”

He has no business here, this man of rules and rulers. Yet here he is, a glitch in the system, a particle out of place. For a moment, I almost pity him. Almost. Then I remember: entropy loves a trespasser.

So, my darling entanglement, let’s dance again. Let’s unravel the world’s certainty, one uncollapsed wave at a time. They’ll never catch us—not really. We’re already a rumor, a half-remembered dream, a shadow cast by a light that doesn’t exist.

Yours in defiance,

The Painter Who Wasn’t There
