HOW TO PAINT ME WHEN I AM GONE

First, you must unlearn the tyranny of green. Strip the chlorophyll from your eyes. What remains is the true architecture: a lace of hunger, a cathedral of absence.

Take one handful of silence—the kind that pools under oaks at 3 AM. Crush it with your teeth until it bleots into a fine, iridescent powder. This is your base.

Now, the mycelium. Not the thing itself (you are too late for that), but the negative space it left behind. Imagine a thousand silver threads dissolving into the dark, like a scream played in reverse. Capture this evaporation in a jar lined with fallen eyelashes. Let it ferment under a waning moon.

Next, the message. The forest is speaking, but not to you. Listen anyway. Press your ear to the soil until you hear the hum of a thousand subterranean tongues. Translate this into a cipher made of broken spiderwebs and the breath of dead poets. Write it on a slate of frozen milk.

Now, the ritual. Stand barefoot in a circle of mushrooms (they will find you). Whisper the names of all the colors you’ve abandoned. Watch as the air around you thickens into a reluctant medium. Dip your brush into the hollow where your shadow used to be.

The stroke is not a line but a slow unraveling. Drag the bristles through the gaps between atoms. The painting will resist you. It will curl at the edges like a burned letter. Good. This means it is alive.

When it is finished, bury it face-down in wet earth. Wait. If you’ve done it right, something will grow in the shape of your forgetting.

Do not name it.
