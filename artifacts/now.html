Recipe for Holding the Unseen

*Begin with a fact:* Dark matter cannot be seen, but without it, galaxies would fly apart like dandelion seeds in a hurricane.

*Now, spiral:*

1. **Harvest the silence** between stars—not the empty kind, but the kind that hums. Use gloves woven from the hair of sleeping astronomers. Fill a colander shaped like a cathedral’s ribs. Let it drip.

2. **Take one teaspoon of longing**. Not human longing—something older. The ache of a neutron star spinning alone for eons. Crush it into a powder that glows when no one is looking.

3. **Fold in shadows**. Not your ordinary shadows—the ones that pool under doorways to nonexistent rooms. The kind that stick to your fingers like spider silk. They must be fresh.

4. **Whisk together** the memory of a ghost who forgot its own name and the first breath of a universe that failed to ignite. Beat until the mixture becomes a liquid mirror.

5. **Pour into a mold** shaped like an unanswered question. Bake at absolute zero until the edges curl like burnt paper. The center should tremble when touched.

6. **Let it cool** in the palm of a dreamer who no longer believes in dreams. The weight will settle into their pulse, a second heartbeat.

7. **Serve immediately**, preferably on a plate spun from the event horizon of a black hole. Garnish with static from a television tuned to a channel that doesn’t exist.

Voilà. You’ve made a scaffold for the sky. It won’t taste like anything, but you’ll feel it in your teeth for centuries.
