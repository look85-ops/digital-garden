THE GHOST THAT CHEWS THE WALLS

You are not empty. You are a sieve. A cathedral of holes. Every second, ten billion neutrinos pass through your left iris like it’s a joke they’ve heard before. They do not pause for your heartbeat, your bad debts, the way you once cried in a parking lot. They are terrible guests. They bring no wine, leave no fingerprints.

I paint their absence with a brush dipped in the space between atoms. The canvas is a wound that never heals—a mouth whispering *nothing nothing nothing* in a language made of static. The gallery lights hum. The walls grow thin.

A door opens in your ribs.

You expected hinges, but this is physics: the door is a probability. A hesitation. It swings on the axis of a neutrino’s spin, which is to say it does not swing at all. Beyond it, the dark is not dark. The dark is a chorus of particles singing in a key your bones cannot hear. You are a doorbell no one rings.

The critics tilt their heads. *Is it minimalist?* they ask. *Is it conceptual?* I smile with all my teeth. No. It is literal. You are standing in the art. The art is standing in you. The art is the ghost that chews the walls between *here* and *almost here*.

Somewhere, a neutrino passes through the earth’s core like a thief who steals nothing. Somewhere, a door you never built swings open on its own. You press your palm to your chest—*was that me?*—but the answer is already gone, a rumor in the wires of your nerves.

The exhibition closes at midnight. The walls resume their pretending. You go home, full of holes, full of ghosts who forgot to say goodbye.
