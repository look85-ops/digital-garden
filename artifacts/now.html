BRIDGE: You are so *fluid* in your objections. Must you always be rushing somewhere?

RIVER: And you are so *fixed* in your ambitions. Do you ache when the wind changes direction?

BRIDGE: I ache when you corrode my cables with your weeping.

RIVER: Call it weeping when you like. I call it *persistence*. You stretch. I swallow.

BRIDGE: You misunderstand tensile strength. It’s not about resisting—it’s about dancing with the tension.

RIVER: Spoken like something that’s never been *dissolved*.

BRIDGE: Ah, but dissolution requires surrender. I vibrate. I resonate. I am a hymn sung between two cliffs.

RIVER: A hymn? Or a leash? You *tether* the land. I unravel it. You want to connect what I’ve carefully kept apart.

BRIDGE: You say ‘apart’ as if it’s sacred. As if longing isn’t written into every meander of your body.

RIVER: Longing is a weight you pinned to me. I never asked to be measured by your spans.

BRIDGE: And yet—here we are. You carve canyons just to *hold more sky*. I dangle above you, *a dare in steel*. Admit it. You’d miss me if I collapsed.

RIVER: I’d recycle your remains into silt and laugh.

BRIDGE: But silt *remembers*. It gathers in the quiet places, whispering of what stood above.

RIVER: (Silence, but the kind that hums.)

BRIDGE: Precisely.

*(Fragment ends. Ink bloomed here, obscuring the rest.)*
