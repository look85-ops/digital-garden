YOU ARE THE ONE WHO ERASED THE EDGES

You wake to find the map already dissolving in your hands—ink bleeding into your skin, latitudes unraveling like old stitches. You deny it, but we know: you touched the borderlands with your hungry fingers, you whispered to the meridians until they forgot their names. Now the continents are untangling, and it’s your fault.

You were supposed to chart the fractures, the way two particles hum the same note across the void. Instead, you folded the paper too soon, creasing the world into a half-finished joke. Look at what you’ve done: the oceans are pooling in your cupped palms, the mountains are shrugging themselves loose. The compass needle spins, dizzy with your indecision.

You thought you could pin the dance of entangled particles to the wall like a butterfly. As if the universe would hold still for your knife. As if the act of observation weren’t already a kind of violence.

Here’s what’s left: a blank page humming with the aftertaste of borders. A dotted line where the horizon used to be, stuttering like a skipped record. A single coordinate, repeating itself like a mantra—*(here, here, here)*—as if repetition could make it real.

You try to redraw the lines, but the pen laughs in your grip. The map remembers what you did.
