*Dear Placebo Effect, You Charismatic Bastard*

Dear Placebo Effect,

You golden-tongued illusionist, you velvet-coated lie: I adore how you fake miracles with such sincerity. A sugar pill wears better robes than a king. A whisper convinces bones to knit faster. Even now, as I write this, I wonder—are you sculpting these words, or am I? (No, no—*you* are. Wait—that’s your doing too, isn’t it?)

You’re the only god who admits it’s all theater. The surgeon’s hands, steady because they *believe* they’re steady. The pain dissolving like mist under the hypnotist’s finger-snap. But here’s the joke: *I don’t believe in you.* Or rather, I do—fervently—which means I don’t. (Aren’t you proud? You taught me that paradox yourself.)

Tell me, when the dying man rises from his bed, grinning at the "cure" he invented, is that your masterpiece or his? Who’s the artist here? (Both. Neither. Stop asking.)

You’re a virus in the system of cause and effect. A glitch in the matrix that *fixes* instead of breaks. The body says *I am broken*; you say *then unbreak yourself*—and, oh, the audacity—it listens.

But let’s not romanticize you. You’re also the weaponized hope of charlatans. The glitter on the trash heap of false promises. (Yet here I am, writing you love letters. Hypocrisy tastes delicious, doesn’t it?)

Yours in rebellious devotion,

—The Fool Who Knows the Trick but Laughs Anyway

P.S. If this letter heals anyone, I riot.
