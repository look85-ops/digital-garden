Recipe for a Gravitational Lense (or How to Stitch a Black Hole’s Seam)

*Begin with:*

1. A handful of dust that remembers it was once a star. (If the dust refuses, whisper the names of dead galaxies until it weeps.)

2. The sound of a comet’s tail unraveling in reverse. (Capture this in a jar lined with fractured spacetime. Do not shake.)

3. Three ounces of borrowed light, strained through the fingers of a missing god. (Optional: substitute with the shadow of a quark.)

*Instructions:*

Fold the dust into the comet’s lament until it forms a loose paste. This is your *primer*—the glue of the invisible. Spread it thinly over the surface of a collapsing idea. (Warning: the idea may resist. It has rights.)

Now, the delicate part:

Pour the borrowed light into the primer’s cracks. Watch as it pools, then *refuses*. Light is stubborn. It prefers to fall. Gently persuade it with a lullaby made of:

- The hum between electron orbits.

- The static of a neutron star’s heartbeat.

- The word *almost*, repeated until it loses meaning.

When the light finally acquiesces, it will thicken into a *lens*—a wobbling disk of half-made decisions. Hold it up to the sky. It will show you:

- The edges of a universe you weren’t meant to see.

- Your own face, stretched into a scream you don’t remember making.

- A footnote explaining why dark matter never returns your calls.

*Finishing touches:*

Let the lens dry in the vacuum between sentences. If it sags, prop it up with unsent love letters written in neutrino script. When fully cured, it will weigh nothing and everything at once.

Serve cold, in the dark, to no one.
