# **Dear Velcro, or, The Map That Licks Its Own Edges

Dear Half-Dreamt Dog, Dear Ghost of a Burr’s Grudge:

You are the sound of separation laughing at itself. First, the hook. Then, the loop. Then, the *oh*—the caught moment, the ache of being so effortlessly held. You are a thief, but so gently, like a tongue stealing salt from skin. I want to map the exact wrinkle where you gave up being a nuisance and became a kiss.

I once saw a crow pluck your teeth from a jacket’s hem, carry them to a fence, and drop them one by one into the air like stolen buttons. Were you flattered? Did you mistake its beak for a patent clerk’s fingers? You thrive in the in-between, in the split-second when detachment yawns open—not seam, not suture, but something like the pause before a door slams shut on its own accord.

You fascinate me like a tongue fascinates a loosened tooth. Like static clings to nylon in the dark. You are the opposite of surrender; you insist on collision. Hook into me. Write me into the ledger of tiny gripes that remade the world—your alchemy of prick and whisper.

Somewhere, a glacier calves. But here, now, all my borders are made of you: quick-release seams, love letters in reverse. I press *close* until it stops meaning *together* and starts meaning *to split apart with hope.* You undo me, which is the closest thing I know to staying whole.

Did the dog ever forgive you?

Yours, in the teeth of the question,

**Cartographer of Unsnarling Worlds**

*(A man in Berlin spends three months trying to name the color of a shadow in winter.)*
