The Last Brushstroke Was a Whisper

*—[The artist’s shadow peels itself from the wall and kneels in the corner, stitching its edges together with a needle made of silence.]*

**October 23rd:**

Today, I sold the sky. Not the idea of it, not a representation—just the sky itself, folded neatly into a manila envelope labeled *"Void, Handle With Care."* The buyer, a collector of vanishing things, asked for a certificate of authenticity. I handed him a blank sheet of paper and watched his pupils dilate like ink dropped in water. He understood.

I’ve been thinking about Duchamp’s urinal again. Not the object, but the absence it left behind in the porcelain factory. The hole where it once was, waiting to be filled. Is that the true *Fountain*? The negative space, the ghost of a decision? I tried to exhibit the hole today, but the gallery said it was "too literal." Philistines.

Instead, I hung a frame on the wall. Inside: a single breath I’d been saving since Tuesday. The label read *"Untitled (Exhalation No. 7)."* A critic walked by and called it "derivative." I laughed until my ribs turned to glass.

*—[The artist’s reflection steps out of the mirror and begins erasing the diary entries with a sponge soaked in static.]*

**October 24th:**

I woke up to find all my brushes had dissolved. Not melted, not broken—*dissolved*, like sugar in tea. Their handles were puddles of vague intention on the studio floor. I dipped my fingers in and wrote *"This is not a painting"* on the wall. It dried into a stain shaped like a question mark.

The curator from the Institute of Dissolving Memories visited. She said my work was "too present." I offered her the concept of my next piece: a room where the walls forget their own geometry. She nodded, wrote me a check, and then ate it.

I’ve decided to stop signing my name. Instead, I’ll leave a fingerprint in the air above each piece. Let the wind authenticate me.

*—[The artist’s signature floats away, caught in the beak of a bird that doesn’t exist.]*
