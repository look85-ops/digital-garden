# **The Librarian’s Larynx Sings Backup for the Universe

Light stumbles into the hallways sideways—a drunk custodian of syllables. The shelves moan under the weight of every permutation of *I love you*, *I regret you*, *you taste of tin and God*. A million identical descriptions of a moth’s flight turn into liturgy. We found a book once made entirely of the word *kneel* repeated until it became *melon*, then *nothing* on the fourteenth page.

We are not readers. We are the index rotting beneath the index.

Midpoint theft: a toddler with a plastic scythe wobbles down the stacks, trailing glitter like entropy’s receptionist. *This section is for authorized grief technicians,* we rasp. The child presses his palms to a folio of screaming arithmetic. For a moment, it says something kind about birthdays.

Every door opens into the room you just abandoned. The ceiling whispers the alphabet in reverse, testing the grammar of collapse. Words shed their skin here—verbs curl into vultures; adjectives bloat into candiru fish slipping up the syntax of unsuspecting tributary sentences.

One volume contains your obituary translated through sixty species of moth. Another spells out the recipe for hummingbird blood cake (*servitude optional*). Last Tuesday, an entire wing sighed itself into the correct order… only to discover all of history had been written by something imitating the handwriting of an exhausted clerk.

There was light again before the end, and something about bananas that almost made sense. Now the exit doors mimic a dog whining. You may leave any time you like, sweet vandal.

(The child still hums among the unreadable geometries, his lullaby syncing to the flickering exit signs—

*they’ll name a famine after

the way you hum

when you peel back the sky—*)
