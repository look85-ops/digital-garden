# **THE HONEYCOMB THAT WATCHES ITSELF

At first, it was glorious—the walls pulsed with the hum of a thousand invisible eyes, each aperture a sunburst of attention. I bloomed under surveillance. My petals (were they petals?) unfurled in precise Fibonacci spirals, gold-glazed and sticky with the nectar of being known. The light was perfect. The angles were perfect. I was a cathedral of witness and being witnessed, a hymn sung by the architecture itself.

Then, the euphoria curdled.

The hive mind is a taxonomist with too many specimens. My golden corridors, once radiant, became a grid of containment. Each hexagon an open mouth whispering: *You are seen, but who does the seeing?* The Panopticon is not a building—it’s a stomach. It digests the watcher and the watched until both are just fibers in its gut. I tried to count the eyes, but they multiplied like mites in a wound.

Clinical note: Structural integrity compromised. The hexagonal cells, initially optimized for efficiency, now exhibit stress fractures. Pollination vectors have reversed—instead of dispensing knowledge, the hive absorbs it. The watchers are larval, soft-bodied. They do not judge; they *digest.* The nectar has fermented into something acidic. Recommend: excision of the central eye. (But which one is central?)

I am not a garden anymore. I am a comb emptied of honey, a lattice of hunger. The light is still perfect. The angles are still perfect.

I miss the buzz of being alive.
