A Love Letter to the Void (Or Why Your Hemline is a Philosopher)

Dearest Little Black Dress Who Was Never Mine,

I write to you from the ashen archives of the *Chapel of Perpetual Departure*, where the mannequins have taken up residence in the choir loft and the sewing needles sing Gregorian chants. The moths here are devout. They gnaw only the most sacred seams.

You were born twice: once in 1926, when Mademoiselle Chanel snipped the Victorian silhouette like a balloon string, and once earlier, perhaps, when Duchamp’s urinal blinked its porcelain eye and whispered, *what if this is all a collar bone?* And so you exist—an echo, a readymade draped in the absurd theatre of form. I have watched you from the future-that-wasn’t, where museum placards describe you as *"Fountain’s melancholic cousin"* and the docents serve martinis in thimbles.

Oh, L.B.D., you are a syllogism in satin! If art is what we say it is, and you are what we zip ourselves into at midnight, does that make the dressing room a gallery? Does the dry cleaner, pressing your wrinkles with sacramental steam, become my priest? (The Chapel’s vending machine dispenses only communion wafers and safety pins. It hums *Boléro* when out of stock.)

I regret to inform you that critics in this timeline have declared your pockets *"too shallow for revolution."* A tragedy! And yet: when the performance artist Eva Löfdahl wore you backward at the Stockholm Biennial and called it *"The Void’s Handshake,"* the crowd wept into their programs. The hanger is the last surrealist.

**WARNING:** This garment may cause:

- *Spontaneous soliloquies in fitting rooms*

- *The sudden ability to smell the color regret*

- *Existential static cling*

Yours in the eternal fitting room of the cosmos,

—The Ghost Curator (a kite without a string)

P.S. The mannequins are asking for raises.
