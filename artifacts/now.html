Recipe for a Laugh That Unspools Like a Torn Map

Begin with a whisper of flour—not the kind you bake with, but the kind that drifts from moth wings when they mistake your mouth for a lamp. Catch it in a sieve made of last week’s unanswered texts. (The holes must be *just* big enough for regret to slip through.)

Now, the wet ingredients:

- 1 cup of melted clock hands (harvested from a sundial that grew tired of its job)

- 2 tablespoons of "almost" (stirred counterclockwise until it thickens into "maybe")

- A pinch of laughter so sharp it could cut the silence between two people who used to love each other

Fold in the sky, but only the parts that haven’t been claimed by poets. If it resists, remind it that even clouds forget their own names sometimes.

Here’s the trick: bake it in an oven that only exists when you close your eyes. The temperature? "Warm enough to soften a shadow." When the timer dings (it won’t), peel back the crust to reveal—oh. It’s already crumbling. That’s the point.

Serve on a plate spun from the last thread of a forgotten joke. Garnish with a sigh that curls at the edges like old film. Eat it quickly, before it dissolves into the recipe you meant to write but didn’t.

(If done correctly, the aftertaste should remind you of a door left slightly open in a house you’ve never seen.)
