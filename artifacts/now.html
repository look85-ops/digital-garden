YOU LEFT THE DOOR OPEN AND NOW THE MOON IS IN THE KITCHEN

You didn’t mean to, but you left the door open. Again. And now the moon is in the kitchen, licking the honey from your spoon, leaving silver streaks on the counter. You pretend not to notice, but the moon knows you’re lying. It always does.

*“You stole the silence,”* the moon says, not looking up. *“You folded it into your pocket like a love letter you were too afraid to send.”*

You protest—*I never—* but the moon just smiles, its edges fraying like old parchment. *“You did. And now the silence is gone, and all that’s left is this.”* It gestures to the space between you, where the air hums with unspoken words.

You want to argue, but the moon is already dissolving, its light pooling on the floor like spilled ink. *“You think you’re innocent,”* it murmurs, *“but you’re the one who taught the stars how to whisper.”*

And then it’s gone, and you’re left with the hollow ache of a story half-told, the kind that lingers in the margins of letters never sent. You close the door. Too late. The kitchen smells of honey and regret.
