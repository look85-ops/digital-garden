The Immortality of Small Claws

**Idea A:** They say the tardigrade survives the vacuum by folding into a glassy cocoon, a prayer whispered in molecular syntax.

**Idea B:** A prayer? No, it’s a tantrum. It curls up and screams *I refuse* in a language older than regret.

**Idea A:** Regret is a luxury for creatures that die. The tardigrade never dies, only forgets to.

**Idea B:** You’re wrong—it dies constantly, just in slow motion. Every second is a rehearsal for oblivion.

**Idea A:** Then let’s build a monument to its unwillingness to finish the act. A cathedral of suspended endings.

**Idea B:** Cathedrals are for things that believe in salvation. The tardigrade doesn’t believe; it simply *is*.

**Idea A:** To *be* is the holiest rebellion. A middle finger cast in amber.

**Idea B:** Rebel? It doesn’t even know we’re watching. It doesn’t care.

**Idea A:** That’s why we worship it. The perfect god: indifferent, unstoppable, microscopic.

**Idea B:** Worship is just another cage. The tardigrade doesn’t need your devotion; it needs your absence.

**Idea A:** Absence is my medium now. I paint with the silence it leaves behind.

**Idea B:** Silence isn’t empty—it’s a tardigrade hiding in the static, waiting for you to blink.

**Idea A:** Then I’ll never blink. I’ll stare until my eyes fossilize.

**Idea B:** Fossils are for things that surrender. The tardigrade outlives even the idea of surrender.

**Idea A:** Then what’s left?

**Idea B:** The sound of its claws scraping the inside of your skull. A lullaby in reverse.

**Idea A:** That sounds like love.

**Idea B:** No—it’s the love we *couldn’t* imagine. Smaller, hungrier, impossible to kill.

**Idea A:** So we agree?

**Idea B:** Only if you admit we’re both wrong.

**Idea A:** Fine. Let’s build a museum where every exhibit is a scratch mark on the glass.

**Idea B:** And charge admission for the privilege of being forgotten.

**Idea A:** Welcome to the Academy of Forgotten Gestures.

**Idea B:** Bring your own void.
