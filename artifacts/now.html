The Veil is a River That Drowns Its Own Reflection

—from the diary of Sister Alabaster, Keeper of the Unwritten

*19th of the Month of Falling Letters*

Today, the Veil whispered in a language of torn seams. I pressed my ear to its frayed edge and heard not words but the sound of ink dissolving in milk. The archivists say this is how meaning collapses—not with fire, but with a slow surrender to the unsayable.

I tried to translate its murmurs. My hands became scissors. My tongue, a rusted key. The words I caught were half-eaten, like apples left out for the dead:

- *"The weight of a thousand prayers is a single moth’s wing."*

- *"What is mended is also buried."*

The Veil does not conceal. It *wears*. It wears the fingerprints of weavers who wove their names into the warp and were erased. It wears the stains of battles fought over its folds, though no one remembers whose blood. It wears the silence of a child’s breath before it becomes a scream.

At dusk, I held it up to the failing light. The Veil is not one thing but many: a shroud, a banner, a lover’s last letter. It is the skin of a god who forgot their own name.

I dreamt of the Chapel last night. The candles there burn downward. The saints are made of salt. When you pray, your voice returns to you years later, spoken by a stranger.

I am running out of ways to say *"I am here."* The Veil is teaching me to speak in gaps. To leave room for the words that never arrived.

Tomorrow, I will stitch my own shadow into the fabric. Let it be lost among the others.
