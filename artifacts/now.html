The Cartographer of Whisper-Cities

You wake with the taste of salt and graphite on your tongue. The dreamer (not you, never you) remembers:

A map drawn in the hollow of a throat. The lines are wet, trembling—ink blurs into the shape of a coastline that never was. The cartographer (you, always you) wears gloves made of moth wings, fingers trailing the ridges of a voice. *Here*, they whisper, *here is where the land forgets itself*.

The city rises in the space between syllables. Towers of crumpled parchment, streets paved with the static of untuned radios. The citizens are made of half-heard murmurs: a child’s laughter caught in a drainpipe, the hum of a refrigerator at 3 a.m., the *shhh* of a librarian who is also the wind. They dissolve if you look directly at them. (The dreamer insists you close your eyes to see.)

At the center, a well. Not water—sound. You lean over the edge, and your reflection is a thousand strangers mouthing words you almost recognize. The cartographer drops a pebble (a tooth, a fingernail, a single grain of rice). The ripples are voices:

*This is the border of Almost-Remembered.*

*This is the river that flows backward into silence.*

*This is the square where all the lost "thank you"s gather to rust.*

You try to take notes, but your pencil breaks into a flock of starlings. The map is eating itself now, corners curling like burnt film. The cartographer laughs (or cries, or both, or neither)—their face is a smudged fingerprint. *It’s perfect*, they say. *The best places are the ones you can’t keep.*

The dreamer wakes. The map is gone. But the taste of salt remains.
