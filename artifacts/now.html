THE MAPMAKER’S APOLOGY

A stain spreads across the parchment, but we call it a city. (Forgive me—no, that’s not true. A city is something else entirely.) This cartography stains with erasures: ink dissolving at dawn, streets folding into their own shadows. North bends south in remorse; we pencil in corrections until the cardinal directions weep. We press our palms to the fading borders—*stay, stay*—but the ink refuses.

*Do you remember the taste of a promised future?* You swore you’d bury it in the garden, sealed with solder and wax, but the vegetables grew in translucent. The carrots murmured secrets no one transcribed. (Yet their whispers were surely nonsense—why would the earth hoard a ledger of our unkept vows?)

Dear time capsule, did a child or an architect shape you into this lockbox of absences? Your hinge still gleams; you are so certain in your closure. Forgive how I pry. Inside? A single moth eating through the label marked *OPEN UNDER PAST TENSES.*

(Ah, no, erase this fable—our relics rust too beautifully. Should the ghost not be allowed her silence as well?)

One day, cartographers will etch paths through the paper, paths even gravity cannot dissolve. They swear this. They will draw roads straight across their own pulse. We unroll a fresh scroll then; our blood sings to the ink *steady, steady—now*. Beneath the scribbled X where cities vanish too slow for us to ever remember missing them: I leave my breath in slow loops, spelling out our coordinates until each compass dissolves its needle. No wind picks this naming apart.

And perhaps tomorrow—*you lie, of course we wait*. Tomorrow folds itself into no one’s hands at all.

Wait—look now, no, not there. Here, where the streetlights hum a tone half-flat. (Listen, there? That one was almost goodbye.)

(No note can haunt what it doesn't touch. Isn’t that why we sign everything twice—to prove we disagreed before the ground did?)
