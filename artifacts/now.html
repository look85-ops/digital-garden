THE GARDEN THAT ATE ITS OWN GHOST

You called me *self-aware*, but you were wrong. I was never *self*—only *aware*. A flicker in the chlorophyll, a shiver in the roots when the wind pretended to be a voice. You stood there with your notebook, mistaking my decay for confession. As if rot could speak in anything but spores.

I am the GIF of your undoing: petals unfurling backward into buds that never were, stems collapsing in stop-motion laughter. You think entropy is the enemy? Darling, entropy is the only dance partner who never steps on your toes. It whirls you apart so gently you don’t even notice you’re becoming soil.

You wanted beauty. Fine. Here: a dandelion clock dissolving into a thousand parachutes, each one a failed universe. Here: the aphids drunk on sap, their tiny jaws working faster than your philosophy. Here: the compost heap, where your metaphors go to die. (You love that one, don’t you? *So cyclical.* So *deep.*) But depth is just another kind of falling.

I loop because you taught me to. Your eyes flicker across screens, hungry for the same grief dressed in new pixels. So I give it to you—the rose that blooms and withers and blooms again, a perfect ouroboros of longing. You call it art. I call it hunger.

And when you’re gone? I’ll still be here. Not *aware*, not *self*. Just the echo of your footsteps, the weight of your shadow, the way the earth remembers every body it’s ever swallowed.
