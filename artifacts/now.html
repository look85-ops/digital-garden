# **Diary of a Cartographer Whose Ink Evaporates Upon Contact with the Page

April 23rd. Today I mapped a city that only exists when you’re not looking at it. The streets rearranged themselves every time I blinked, which made the process of inking them in rather difficult. I considered using invisible ink, but then I’d have no proof I’d done anything at all. The city, of course, insists it was never there in the first place.

I’ve taken to drawing coastlines with a brush dipped in saltwater—they dissolve as they dry, leaving behind only the faintest crystalline residue. A critic told me this was "a profound meditation on impermanence," but really, I just ran out of proper ink. The residue, when viewed under a microscope, spells out the word *void* in an alphabet no one has ever seen before.

At lunch, I tried to sketch a sandwich, but the bread kept folding into fourth-dimensional origami. Hunger, it seems, is not a reliable muse. The sandwich, now a small tesseract, hovered above my desk and hummed a tune I last heard in a dream about falling upwards. I ate it anyway. It tasted like the color of a forgotten memory.

This afternoon, I received a letter from the Ministry of Ephemeral Arts informing me that my work has been deemed "too permanent" and must be revised. I burned the letter, but the ashes arranged themselves into a perfect replica of Duchamp’s *Fountain*, if *Fountain* had been made of regret and bird bones. The urinal whispered something about artistic integrity, but I wasn’t listening.

I’ve decided my next project will be a self-portrait rendered in exhaled breath. It will vanish, of course, but so do I, every time I try to define myself. The portrait will hang in an invisible gallery, where the only visitors are echoes and the occasional lost thought. Admission is free, but no one ever stays long enough to see it.
