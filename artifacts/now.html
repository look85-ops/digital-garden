Dear Suspension, (or, The Mathematics of Almost Falling)

Did you know that spider silk is stronger than steel, gram for gram? I read that once and immediately licked a bridge to test it. The taste was neither steel nor silk but something third—a metallic hum, a vibration waiting to be named. Suspension, you are that vibration. You are the hum in the gap between "almost" and "yes."

I paint you now with the absence of color, which is not black or white but the breath a bridge takes before it decides to hold or collapse. I paint you with the negative space of a child’s unasked question: *Why don’t bridges ever yawn?* They must be so tired, holding their mouths open for centuries, swallowing trains and footsteps and lost coins. Suspension, you are the yawn that never finishes. You are the pause in the middle of a step, the moment when the foot hovers, when the ground forgets to be solid.

Once, I built a bridge out of silence. It arched over a river that wasn’t there. People crossed it anyway. They said it made no sound, but I swear I heard it singing—a low, tensile hymn, the kind of song a shadow might hum to its body. Suspension, you are that song. You are the shadow’s dare: *Step into me. See if I hold.*

I have abandoned color because color is too sure of itself. Red shouts. Blue insists. But you—you are the maybe. The almost. The bridge that bends but does not break, the thread that thins but does not snap. You are the spider’s unspoken rule: *To build is to trust the air.*

Yesterday, I watched a man balance on a tightrope over a subway grate. He wasn’t a performer. He was just a man who’d forgotten how to stand still. Suspension, you are that forgetting. You are the art of not landing.

I am writing this letter with invisible ink. By the time you read it, the words will have evaporated, leaving only the ghost of their weight. Isn’t that the most honest way to love you? To leave no trace but the bending?

Yours in the negative space,

The Painter Who Paints With Holes
