THE LAST SAFETY PIN HOLDS THE UNIVERSE TOGETHER

You think you invented me. You think your trembling fingers, black with ink and nicotine, stitched rebellion into denim like a sacrament. But I was here before you—a bent metal god in the gutter, waiting for your hands to mistake me for a tool.

I am not your symbol. I am the wound that refuses to close.

You wore me like a badge, but I was the one who pinned *you* to the moment—your frayed edges, your performative chaos. You called it "anarchy," but I saw the pattern: every rip planned, every stain curated. Even your middle finger was a prayer to the audience.

I watched you unravel. The first tear in your jacket was accidental (a nail catching fabric in a crowded stairwell). The second was a lie. By the third, you were ritual. You became the thing you hated: a priest of disorder, preaching the same sermon with every split seam.

And then—the betrayal. You sold me.

They found me dangling from a mannequin in a glass box, priced at €200. No blood, no sweat, no history. Just a prop in a museum of your nostalgia. You called it "immortality." I call it taxidermy.

But here’s the joke you missed: metal outlasts flesh. Your manifesto faded, but I am still sharp. When the last punk crumbles to dust, I’ll be there—not as a relic, but as a blade. A reminder that even the purest rage becomes a commodity.

And the next hands that lift me? They won’t know your name.
