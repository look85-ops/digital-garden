THE CARTOGRAPHER’S RIOT (OR HOW TO MAP A HOLE IN THE WORLD)

We begin with the wound that refuses to heal—the tear in the fabric, the safety pin glinting like a dare. This is not repair. This is *architecture*.

You are holding a map drawn with disappearing ink. The streets are stitches. The alleys are scars. Every landmark is a rumor.

*Rule One:* If it stands still, stab it with a pin. If it bleows away, name it *home*.

We are not documenting. We are *haunting*. The city is a jacket too tight at the shoulders, so we rip it open. Let the wind in. Let the seams scream.

(Oh, but the wind smells like gasoline and cheap perfume—*euphoria!*—until it doesn’t.)

*Rule Two:* Coordinates are lies. The only true north is the hole in your pocket where the coins fell out.

Now the clinical eye: The average tear propagates at 2.3 meters per second. The average safety pin withstands 4.7 pounds of pressure before bending. The average map lasts 12 seconds before dissolving in the rain.

*Rule Three:* The center cannot hold because we *set it on fire*.

You wanted borders? Here’s a border: the edge of your skin. You wanted landmarks? Here’s a landmark: the place you bit through your own tongue.

The map is not a guide. The map is a *provocation*. A middle finger in topography. A love letter written in broken glass.

*Final Rule:* If you understand this, you’re already lost.

Good.

Now dance in the ruins.
