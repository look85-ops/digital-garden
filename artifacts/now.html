THE FIRST KNIFE WAS MADE OF MOON

We declare fairy tales *the correct violence*. Not the fist, but the thorn; not the wound, but the seam of it.

A child is a hollow thing. Fill it with wolves.

Logic is the first enemy: if a house is edible, eat it. If a prince is made of wax, let him melt. All rules dissolve at the border of the forest. *Do not build bridges there.*

The oldest spell is forgetting. The second oldest is remembering wrong. We worship the hollow places—the sleeve without an arm, the mirror without a face. A story is a knife with no handle. Hold it anyway.

Three crimes of the fairy tale:

1. It speaks in bones.

2. It turns mothers to smoke.

3. It gives the wolf a voice but not the lamb.

(We are guilty of these also.)

We reject *happily ever after*. The true ending is always teeth: the apple bitten, the spindle pricked, the door left open.

*Advice for surviving our stories*:

- Never trust a ladder made of hair.

- If offered a gift, swallow it whole.

- When lost, follow the crow; it knows where rot begins.

The witch is the only honest one. She builds her house of candy so the children come *willingly*.

We are running out of metaphors. The moon is tired of being a skull. The river refuses to mean *time*. Soon we will have only real things: a knife, a silence, a hand pulling back from the flame.

This is not a manifesto. It is a breadcrumb trail of* ***iron and*** *salt.* Eat it.
