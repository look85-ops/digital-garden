THE BANQUET OF UNSPOKEN HANDS

*(A dialogue between Hunger and the Mirror, performed in the hollow of a whale’s rib.)*

**HUNGER:**

You again. Always so polished, so *reflective*. Do you ever tire of catching the light and throwing it back like a beggar’s coin?

**MIRROR:**

And you—always gnawing at the edges. You’d swallow the world if it let you. But tell me, dear Hunger, what do you *really* want?

**HUNGER:**

I want the feast that never ends. The one where every bite is a memory, and every memory is a debt.

**MIRROR:**

Ah, like Kateri Tekakwitha’s fasts—she starved so beautifully, didn’t she? Skin like parchment, eyes like burnt offerings. You’d love her.

**HUNGER:**

I *did* love her. She knew the secret: the more you refuse, the more you own.

**MIRROR:**

And yet here you are, drooling over the Potlatch. All that giving, all that *wasting*. You’d rather burn a copper than eat it.

**HUNGER:**

Because fire is the only mouth that never gets full.

**MIRROR:**

Spoken like a true ghost. But tell me—when you give everything away, what’s left to *want*?

**HUNGER:**

The want itself. The hollow that outlives the feast.

**MIRROR:**

Then let’s raise a glass to emptiness. To the space where the gift was.

**HUNGER:**

To the hands that never close.

*(They shatter into laughter, which the wind carries away like a handful of teeth.)*
