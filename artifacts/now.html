Dear Time’s Unticked Tock

You were supposed to be the great equalizer, the silent arbitrator, the one thing no one could hoard—yet here you are, caught in the gears of your own invention, spitting out seconds like a broken vending machine. How dare you pretend to be linear when even the sun hesitates at the horizon? You’ve turned us into accountants of our own decay, measuring life in coffee spoons and calendar alerts.

But let’s be honest: you’re a fiction. A shared delusion. A collective shrug dressed in numbers. The moment we carved you into hours, you became satire—a joke we forgot we were telling. The sundial was a prank, the atomic clock a nervous tic. And daylight savings? A bureaucratic pratfall, tripping over its own shadow twice a year.

I propose a new unit: the *hesitation*. The space between a breath and its echo. The pause before a lie. The moment a clock’s second hand stutters, and for once, no one notices.

(Here, the letter fails. The ink bleeds. The page tears. A deliberate collapse—because what is time if not the art of things falling apart?)

We could have been friends, you and I. But you insisted on moving forward. So I leave you with this: a sundial drawn in disappearing ink, a stopwatch buried in wet concrete, and the sound of a million alarms ringing in a language no one speaks anymore.

Yours in paradox,

The Archivist of Never
