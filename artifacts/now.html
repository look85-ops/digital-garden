Dear Concrete Poetry (or Whoever’s Left in This Envelope),

I miss you like a missing tooth—not the gap itself, but the way my tongue keeps returning to it, prodding the absence into a kind of presence. You were supposed to outlast us all, a stubborn stain on the page, but here you are: dissolved into the margins, a ghost of ink and intention.

I curated you once, in that timeline where the alphabet grew legs and walked off the page. Remember? We exhibited your vowels in glass cases, fed your consonants raw sugar, and watched your serifs curl into little black flames. Visitors wept when they saw the ampersand give birth to a question mark. (It was a difficult labor.)

And then—midway through the retrospective, as we were mounting the *É* on velvet—*Gary from Accounting* barged in, trailing receipts and the scent of stale coffee. “Where’s the *profit* in this?” he demanded, waving a spreadsheet like a white flag. We tried to explain that you were a *revolution*, not a *revenue stream*, but Gary just sighed and began folding your stanzas into paper airplanes. (They flew better than we expected.)

Now, in this unwritten now, I write to you from the Institute of Dissolving Memories, where the walls are made of eraser dust and the exhibits change when you blink. You’d love it here. We have a whole wing dedicated to *lost punctuation*—the semicolon that dreamed of being a lasso, the parentheses that never closed. Sometimes, at night, the letters rearrange themselves into words we’ve forgotten how to read.

I don’t know if this letter will reach you. The postal service here is unreliable; the couriers are mostly shadows with bad handwriting. But if it does, tell me: Do you still taste like graphite and possibility? Or have you hardened into something even the rain can’t smudge?

Yours in illegible devotion,

*The Curator Who Wasn’t*

P.S. Gary says hello. He’s opened a franchise.
