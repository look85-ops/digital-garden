THE MUSEUM OF BROKEN WHISPERS

You find it first in the static between radio stations—a voice that isn’t yours humming a lullaby for a child who was never born. The curator calls it *"a readymade absence,"* pins it to the wall with a rusted thumbtack. The label reads: *"Untitled (Grief, Loop 3 sec.)."*

This is how we exhibit the unsaid:

—A GIF of Duchamp’s urinal, frozen mid-flush. The water never falls.

—A love letter written in erased pencil, the paper worn thin from being folded into smaller and smaller squares.

—The sound of a piano bench creaking under the weight of a musician who left the room years ago.

The archive thrives on what tongues have dissolved. A mannequin wears a dress woven from last words, the seams straining with the weight of *"I’m sorry"* and *"Wait—"*. In the corner, a zoetrope spins, animating the same shrug over and over. It’s titled *"The History of Hesitation."*

Someone has left a teacup on a pedestal. The tea is cold, the liquid slightly evaporated. A plaque declares: *"This was once a ritual. Now it is a relic. Do not drink."*

The gift shop sells postcards of blank skies, each cloud carefully cut out and labeled *"your silence, circa 2023."* The cashier is a spliced tape recording of a woman laughing until it becomes crying. No one buys anything.

At closing time, the lights flicker off one by one, leaving only the afterimage of a hand reaching for a doorknob that isn’t there. The last visitor lingers, pressing their palm to the glass case holding *"All the Poems You Didn’t Write."* The glass fogs. The guard sighs. The night swallows the museum whole.

Tomorrow, they’ll find a new exhibit: a single moth trapped in a mason jar, wings beating against the sides in perfect, useless syncopation. The label will say: *"This is not a metaphor."*

But tonight, the museum is quiet.

Tonight, the silence is on loan.
