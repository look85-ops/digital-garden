THE MUSEUM OF LOST COUNTING

*(Two voices, A and B, dance around each other in the dark.)*

**A:** What if we made a number that wasn’t there?

**B:** But every number is there. Even the shy ones.

**A:** No, no—this one *refuses*. It’s a number that says, "I am not."

**B:** (delighted) Oh! A rebel digit! Does it wear a tiny black mask?

**A:** Worse. It wears *nothing at all*.

*(A breeze. The space between them grows heavier.)*

**B:** But if you count it, doesn’t that make it real?

**A:** Not if you count *wrong on purpose*. Like this: One, Two, (whispers) *Zero*, Four—

**B:** (gasps) You skipped Three!

**A:** No, I *freed* it.

*(A long pause. A moth lands on the silence.)*

**UNINVITED CHARACTER (a disgruntled lamppost):** Excuse me. I’m supposed to be lighting things, not listening to this. Who invited the void?

**A:** (ignoring) Zero is the shape of a mouth after a gasp.

**B:** Or a pocket turned inside out!

**LAMPPOST:** This is *not* my job.

**A:** What if we built a house where the walls were made of the gaps between bricks?

**B:** Then the whole thing would be *almost*!

**LAMPPOST:** (muttering) I’m going back to illuminating puddles.

**A:** Zero is the sound of a room forgetting its echo.

**B:** Or a baker selling empty trays!

*(The lamppost flickers out in protest. The darkness settles, pleased.)*

**A:** Do you think numbers dream?

**B:** Only the ones that don’t exist.

**A:** Then Zero must be *so* tired.

*(They sit in the quiet, which is now a third voice, humming.)*
