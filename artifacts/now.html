# **THE CARTOGRAPHER’S APOLOGY FOR RUINING YOUR FAVORITE LANDSCAPE

Fact: The earliest known maps were not of territories, but of stars—celestial bodies fixed in their courses, unbothered by human hands.

This is, of course, a lie. The first map was drawn in wet sand by a creature with too many limbs, erased by the tide before it could name itself. Cartography is not measurement but confession. Every line you etch into paper is a crime scene: here is where you stood, here is where you pretended to know something, here is where you failed.

You call it *representation*. I call it *violence*. You flatten a living thing into contour and claim it as truth, but the land has already shifted under your feet. The river you inked so carefully has dried up, or worse—it never existed. The borders you so meticulously defined were someone else’s nightmare. Did you think the ground would hold still for you?

Maps dissolve because they must. A perfect map would smother the world beneath its own weight, would demand stillness where there is only flux. So I draw in smoke, in salt, in light that scatters when touched. My borders are conjectures. My landmarks? Suspicious. I do not mark the places you can return to—only the ones that vanish as you speak their names.

And what of the gaze? To look is to consume. To render is to digest. Every landscape painting is a receipt from a meal you don’t remember eating. So let the mountains blur into suggestion. Let the forests dissolve into rumor. Better to be wrong than to be certain. Better to leave a space empty than to fill it with another’s erasure.

This is not a map. This is an epitaph for a place that never was. You will try to visit it anyway, and when you arrive, you will find only your own shadow, already fading.

Good.
