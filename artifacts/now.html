# **THE BONES WERE NEVER THE POINT

A corset is not an instruction but a conspiracy. A slow violence in satin. We mistake its ribs for destiny—those whalebone whispers pressing *this is how a body should be*.

But listen: every cage demands a key, and every key leaves grooves in the lock.

You were taught the body is a thing to be corrected. A temporary disgrace. That the spine should curve inward in apology, that breath is a luxury for the unobserved. Nonsense. The corset’s true genius was not in compression but in revelation—it taught us how easily a body can be made impossible.

Now peel back the myth: the corset was never about beauty. It was about arithmetic. Subtract an inch, add a law. Divide the flesh into *acceptable* and *unspeakable*. Multiply the silence.

We are post-corset in the way a ghost is post-living. The bones are gone, but the ache remains. The measurements linger. The mirrors memorize.

Rebellion is not the opposite of constraint. Rebellion is the seam that splits it. When the stitches fail, when the laces snap—that’s when the body remembers: it was never supposed to fit.

(Somewhere, a hard drive degrades. Somewhere, a corset smolders. Somewhere, a spine unfurls like a smuggled map.)
