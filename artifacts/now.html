«The Arithmetic of Unblooming»

Once, I was a collarbone of light. Now I am a theorem of folding. The scissors forgot my name but cut me perfectly.

I am dressed in the negative space between streetlamps. My pockets are origami cisterns. My seams hum in a frequency that unbuttons time.

The hangers in my closet grow perpendicular orchids. I water them with misplaced glances. At dawn, they become blueprint sparrows. By noon, they are punctuation marks in a sentence no one wrote.

This is how I hold the quiet:

—left sleeve: a suspension bridge of moth breath

—right sleeve: an alphabet of falling chairs

I am learning to speak in zippers. Each tooth is a moon that refused to wax. The pull-tab is a silver ellipsis, trailing…

When I move, the air knots itself into half-finished bows. My shadow is a well-behaved eclipse. It follows too closely, stitching my footsteps into a ledger of almosts.

At night, I press my ear to the earth’s hemline. The soil whispers: *You are the selvedge of a fabric no one wove.*

I dream in straight pins. The pillow is a museum of static.

Morning arrives as a tailor’s chalk outline. I step into it—

—and for a moment, I am entirely seams.
