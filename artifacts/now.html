THE LAST CAVE PAINTER APOLOGIZES TO THE WALL

Listen. The absence is listening back.

When I press my palm against the rock, the cave remembers the first touch and laughs—a sound like an old mumbling void. I insist on using only the darkest shade of air, a black with no name left for light to whisper between syllables. This pigment I apply directly from my lungs with a straw torn from history, siphoning ghosts and spitting them blindward. Today’s technique mimics the ancient masters: I blow the nothing through a reed while dancing backward on my knuckles to confuse archaeology. Shadows adhere to the wall in reverse formation. Where my spit touches limestone, what disappears is immortalized.

Hands, yes, of course hands. Thousands—stretched open across millennia—though they appear only if you approach crawling on all elbows like an obedient doubt. The effect is best viewed with eyes closed underwater at 1:03 AM while holding a small, forbidden relic (a chicken bone will do). In this installation, viewers must leave one object untouched to offset balance, otherwise silence curdles into debt. On my ledger is marked: one apology repaid in negative space, unsigned.

Critics will later argue whether it’s a new work at all. They whisper across gavels of dried wolf milk,

*Isn’t this just vandalism’s retirement announcement?* Meanwhile beneath them, a dog watches television.

What emerges—the handprint—lacks pigment because pigment was invented by liars.

The truth arrives in the absence; the hand is still 90% elsewhere. I could show you if the cave would let go.

I know the lie buried in this description:

Every day at midnight, I confess to the wall my theft of oxygen. The echo forgives in 32 tongues.
