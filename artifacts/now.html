★ *They Say These Burn with Unicorn Tears; Also, Please Feed the Goats* ★

They tasked me with rendering an atlas of all absent things, the black-spots left behind when words flee mid-sentence. I thought it odd—I still think in ink blots, each stain wicking a missing verse upward—but they had a quota. Some clerk, high on vanishing dust, slid me an illustrated pamphlet: *Mapping Hisses in Parchment, Section 2(b), Subheader 'Do Not Use this Header—See Suppressed Preface (Disregarded 1937).'*

For context: every censor is allergic to daylight and requires a sacrificial goat, even an ornamental one. The department gave ours three little ballet costumes (sequined—why sequins?). Each evening a librarian chants backward at it in Dutch until the air smells of cardamom and erasures grow audible, hissing low around ankle height. That was the smell this morning.

Someone set fire to an allegory! Not *all* allegory—just three very stubborn verbs—before realizing nothing catches the way it’s intended. Scatology melts like cheap candy. A treatise becomes a sugarcube dissolving on the tongue of, let me check my notes—*ah yes*, the aforementioned goat, who apparently knows French folklore but won’t translate under threat. Bad business.

What remained: *a small country shaped like guilt* (the phrase itself had fled; I stitched its impression inside a parenthesis.) Some argue it’s the other way around, that absence pulls words from the tongue like milk teeth, but theory’s worse in person. One survivor, left by the pyre to document: he wrote it backward—not to evade scrutiny, just to upset syntax and then apologize to syntax with tea.

*Why goats?* Because they refuse myth and will eat any god you set beside them unprompted. Also, they hum in C-sharp, possibly to spite oboes. That’s irrelevant (unless notes become censored next).

Procedure: you smear erased ink across bread. You butter silence into geography. *Here, be very faint monsters of omission*.

Here be dragons would work in any other situation—but dragons are never actually censored*, or possibly that’s what remains whenever a metaphor dies screaming inside the mouth of a surprised official who clearly should have planned this better.

* * (*Except Brussels in 1902; allegedly one incinerated on contact after being listed in Appendix ?—*which proves my point but is beside the point in at least two other manuscripts.)

Sign posted at their suggestion: *If You See This Sentence Again After Tuesday,* DO NOT PANIC. Please water whichever void seems unkempt.

It’s a good gig if you can stomach absurd repetition —but tell the goat we have beef* with nihilists (*literally: he knows things.)

(We think.)
