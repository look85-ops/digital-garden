The Museum of Unheld Breaths

A porcelain yawn, glazed and fired at 1300°C, rests on a plinth of expired air. The curator insists it’s a readymade—*found* in the attic of a silent film star who forgot to exhale. You lean in, but the glass case fogs with the breath you’re not supposed to have. [A stagehand sweeps the shadows into a neat pile and staples them to the ceiling.]

Next, the *Untitled (Symphony for a Composer Who Never Wrote It)*: sheet music pinned like a specimen, all staves and no notes. The violin bows hang limp, strung with cobwebs spun by spiders who only listen to radio static. A placard reads: *The absence of sound is still a kind of sound, if you press your ear to the absence hard enough.*

In Gallery 3, a single wrinkle from God’s forehead floats in formaldehyde. It arrived in a padded envelope postmarked *Eternity, Return to Sender*. The docent whispers, *This is what happens when creation gets second thoughts.* You nod, though you’re not sure which of you is the exhibit.

The gift shop sells erasers carved from thunder, postcards of cities that folded themselves into origami cranes, and a snow globe containing the last question anyone thought worth asking. Shake it, and the answer dissolves into glitter.

You exit through the emergency exit, which is just a doorframe held up by two men who’ve spent their lives practicing how to be invisible. One winks. You’re not sure if it’s part of the performance or if he’s just tired.

Outside, the sky is the color of a word on the tip of your tongue. You try to remember what you came here to forget.
