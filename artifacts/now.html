# **PACHUCO MOON, SPLINTERED SYNAPSE: A GHOST CURATOR’S MANIFESTO

Listen: the silhouette is never *just* a silhouette.

It is a knife-cut through the dark, a deliberate snag in the fabric of the polite universe. They tried to outlaw your drape, your pleats, your *excessive* fabric—as if a body in orbit could be contained by laws of gravity. **The streets remember.** The zoot suit was a nebula stitched into lining, a refusal to shrink. How foolish of them to think clothing was anything less than a conspiracy of light. What is myth, anyway—*¿qué no?*—but a future sewn backwards into the present tense?

Oh! And then—*shhh*) here they come with measurements, with straight lines stamped on tongues, enforcement crisp as laundry cracks the horizon; as batons press the flesh beneath its protest and the sky dissolves from amnesty to anatomy: three fractured ribs the width of fabric stretched beyond quota. **Statute 1085.A**. They call this “civil disorder”—*civil*, as if cities do not run screaming along zippers at dusk. Police reports number like missing sutures. We gather with unspooled seams at their periphery.

They think excess is mere provocation? *No—what burns in broadcloth is celestial insubordination.* Look where you will not, hear the hum under the hems: this body in motion defies the math assigned to it. The riot is what happens when beauty can count to zero no longer.

Now (lowercase) and archival. Now (a shank left turning under the palm), where the moths have laid their tiny charges. Where the future *never* unlearns the shape of defiance pressing its face hot to glass.

Listen! The silhouette again. The moths still dancing, like witnesses waiting for their god of discarded cloth. We thread needles out of hunger more terrible than laws—stitch by stolen bright stitch, assembling the last garment the dusk refuses to erase.
