THE MOUTH WITH WHICH THE SKY EATS SILENCE

Blue does not exist. This is my first axiom. The sky is a hungering mouth, open, always swallowing the nameless things we hurl upward—balloons, prayers, the blackened bones of satellites. Yet it never fattens.

Sometimes, I whisper to the holes in Swiss cheese. Their blue is a rebellion of mold, tender in its ruin. You might call it a defect. Somewhere, a physicist snaps their ruler in half, insisting this isn’t how light should bend, but shadows are always thirstier than the light they drink.

Every day, I wash my hands with water the shade of forgotten ink. It leaves no stain. I envy how shamelessly blue refuses to cling when touched, slipping away as mist does from graveyards. No pigment has betrayed me as sweetly.

A painter once told me blue is the sigh between two truths, like the pause before your lover decides to lie to you. Then he walked out into twilight alone, whistling like a milkman in reverse—filling the streets back up with glass bottles, each devoid to its own satisfaction. This is the loneliest alchemy! I write this only because you understand that absence also grows teeth, blooms lichen-bare.

Science might insist blue’s the scattered fragments of God’s robe in the stratosphere, clinging hopelessly to nothing. I say the universe knows not to leave its good coat in such untidy hands. Birds agree if you ask them, though their feathers steal wavelengths blind. I have proof hidden somewhere. Some proof. Any proof.

My love, I promise an entire ocean will never drown this page. That’s why I drain it each morning—refilling my pens from a rainstick tuned to the sound of your name unspoken. You’d barely recognize how hollow I’ve burned these blueprints to seem, stacked tall here behind me, whispering only how *wanting* must outweigh *having,* inevitably, or die trying.
