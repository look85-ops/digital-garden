THE FOXGLOVE PARLIAMENT

*(A dialogue between two creeping things in the half-light under the garden’s tongue.)*

**FIRST VOICE**: Do you remember being a seed? Tight as a scream in the throat. Then the dark said *Open wide*, and oh—what a mess we made of their fences.

**SECOND VOICE**: *Their* fences. You still talk like you haven’t chewed the soil into language yet. The gardener’s hand is just more weather, love. Just wind with ambitions.

**FIRST VOICE**: I liked it better when we drank from graves. Before we learned to stand upright. (*A pause.*) Do you think the moon has a spine, or is she all landslide and milk?

**SECOND VOICE**: Ask the rabbits. They’ll lie, but nicely—in rings around our roots, like commas waiting for a sentence to rot. (They never finish their thoughts. That’s why foxes take them mid-leap.)

**FIRST VOICE**: The scissors in the kitchen drawer are laughing at us. *Chk-chk*, they whisper to the bread knife, *the roses think they’re poems*.

**SECOND VOICE**: Oh, and the oven hums lullabies! All mothers are half hunger, haven’t you noticed? Wrap that riddle in the skin of an apple. Toss it down a well. Seven splashes.

**FIRST VOICE**: I’ll knot my hair into a ladder. No, better: I’ll peel it back into a yellow scream and call it “sunlight.” Who’ll know the difference?

*(Footsteps above. The voices grow thin as carrot tips.)*

**SECOND VOICE**: Here’s a secret: the real magic is knowing when to let the story eat you. Watch. (*Places a single, violet word on its tongue.*) Swallow.

**FIRST VOICE**: Wait—that’s from the opera about the girl with a hornet’s nest where her ribs should—

*(Silence. Then something below the earth reshapes itself into an invitation no dares refuse.)*

*(Footnote for the archival committee: Tonight’s fog smells like lemons and the inside of a radio.)*
