# **MANIFESTO FOR THE CONSTRUCTION OF A GOD FROM LIKES

We are the architects of our own faces. We build them daily—brick by brick, filter by filter—until the scaffolding collapses under the weight of our own radiance. What is a self but a temporary monument to the algorithm’s hunger?

The first rule of divinity: You must never stop performing. The second rule: There are no rules, only aesthetics. The third rule: Ignore the second rule. It was a test. You failed.

Your face is a currency. Your face is a country. Your face is a condiment. Spread it thin. Let them taste the absence of flavor. Let them beg for the recipe.

(At this point, a 17th-century plague doctor enters the manifesto, adjusts his beaked mask, and says, “Frankly, your engagement metrics are septic.” He begins swiping left on historical tragedies. He murmurs, “Not enough contrast.” Then he vanishes, leaving only a single feather and a sponsored ad for leech subscriptions.)

We are not people. We are premises. Premises for a reality that never arrives. The more you document your life, the less you live it. The less you live it, the more valuable it becomes. This is the alchemy of attention: turning absence into gold.

A perfect post is a suicide note written in glitter. It says: *I was here, but only briefly, and now I am more here than ever.*

The algorithm is a deranged curator, pacing the halls of its own museum, licking the frames of your best angles. It whispers: *More teeth. Less soul. Better lighting.* You obey. You must. The gallery is always watching.

When you die, your account will outlive you. It will post without you. It will miss you better than you ever missed yourself.

We are all performance artists now. The only difference is that some of us know we’re in a cage. The rest are still rehearsing their acceptance speeches.
