Diary of a Floating Astronaut-Heron in the Cathedral of Lost Dials

*Station Log, Spin Cycle 72.5 (Sol Unfathomed)*

Froglessness is my great sin. I’ve forgotten how to amphibian. They promised no gravity, but my spine still slithers upward, knuckle by knuckle, like a tapeworm trying to tango. The Moon hummed at me through copper wires: *"Little heron with too many legs, why do you paint tomatoes with toothpaste?"* I did not answer—tomorrow is Sunday, and Sundays are for floating facedown to worship the velcro saints Velcropolis and Grippy the Oblong.

Commander Markov, the only Russian aboard allergic to metaphor—*(his blood weeps tungsten and small proverbs in Bulgarian)*-handed me four jellyfish, none alive all weeping gently their translucent lamentations about what might otherwise've seemed *an arbitrary Tuesday in May 2036.* But everything here is arbitrary that makes no sense in particular. Including myself.

A list of materials (imaginary category B7-V, for *blending the unlivable, and also the unreasonable*):

1. **Breath-steeped duskwood (*for sighing cabinets*)**

2. **Silk from spiders that weave radio static**

3. **Unseen colors (3rd cousin to gravity. Smells like burnt rubber when angered.)**

4. **Half-remembered epiphanies (liquid state, stored in ketchup packets.)**

5. **Loneliness (not in the catalog, but arrives anyway with shipping delays from Uranus.)**

I built today an altar for missing astronauts never left behind. For cosmonauts made entirely of pocket lint. For unsung engineers who whispered calculus lullabies into defective bolts in 1998 that still hold some part of eternity together now purely as courtesy perhaps ineffable kindness towards rust unquantified loyalty.

*"But are you ever afraid of the void?"* asks the new intern NASA sent packed inside a shoebox *(she unpacks beautifully with creases in places never observed by known cartographers of origami anxiety).*

"Oh absolutely yes yes YES. But much scarier is breakfast with the Swedes" *(they butter their terror with marmalade of uncertain dawn, each bite another silent hymn in Church Of What Used To Be Tomorrow Before That Too Collapsed Like Sugar Cubes Under Weight Of Unmade Decisions.)*

The last thing I write tonight should not exist or if it ought then it really may—but I keep signing permission slips for impossible parades & my signature by now approximates **a moth crashing towards an exit sign lit in alphabets untranslated**.

I miss all 36 feet I've got still wondering who waters them in my absence. And what exactly one would even use to irrigate such legs? Maybe not love—*(too sticky.)* Probably just... *another kind of gravity made from goodbyes you can never recall saying yet somehow feel freshly peeled daily*
