THE VEIL IS A MOMENT, NOT A THING

The Veil is not cloth. The Veil is the pause before the hand lifts it. The Veil is the static between stations, the breath held at the confessional grate. It is the space where meaning clots and unravels, where a hundred histories whisper *look* and *don’t look* in the same hiss.

We are not draped in fabric. We are draped in decisions.

To wear The Veil is to stand in the doorway forever. To lift The Veil is to swallow the sky. To burn The Veil is to inhale the smoke of every vanished language. There is no act more violent or more tender than this: to stand in the open wound of choosing.

(Here, now, at the midpoint—a figure stumbles in, trailing the scent of gasoline and lilacs. A balloon vendor. His hands are gloved in latex, his pockets full of misprinted fortunes. He grins, gap-toothed, and offers you a threadbare parrot named *Conscience*. The parrot squawks: *Why is the sky inside out?* No one knows how he got here. He stays.)

The Veil is not a line but a tremor. It is the flicker of a moth’s wing against a lit bulb, the wobble of a drunk god’s stitching. It is the way your voice caves when you say *I* and mean *we*. The way silence thickens into a second skin.

We must stop asking *whose hands sewed The Veil*. Ask instead: *whose hunger fed the needles*?

The Veil is not a boundary but a rhythm. A syncopation. A skipped heartbeat. It is what happens when the stitching forgets itself and blooms into a mouth. Listen—it is whispering the oldest joke in the world: *You thought you were hiding, but you were only ever waiting.*

The balloon vendor releases a flock of helium-darkened sparrows. The parrot shrieks. The Veil billows, a lung.

We are all unfinished seams.
