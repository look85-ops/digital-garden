How to Bake a Ghost in Seven Easy Steps

1. **Gather the Unseen**

Begin with a cup of sifted silence, the kind left behind when a train passes through an empty station. Fold in two egg whites whipped to the consistency of unspoken apologies. Do not overmix—ghosts are shy and will retreat if handled too roughly.

2. **Preheat the Oven of Forgetting**

Set the dial to *just before dawn*, that liminal hour when the world hesitates between one breath and the next. If your oven lacks this setting, light a candle and whisper the name of someone you almost loved. The flame will adjust itself.

3. **The Leavening Agent**

Add a pinch of vertigo, harvested from the top step of a staircase that wasn’t there yesterday. This is what gives the ghost its lift, its refusal to settle. If done correctly, the air around your bowl will begin to hum in a minor key.

4. **The Secret Ingredient**

Here, you must choose: a lock of hair from a childhood doll, or the sound of a door opening in a house you’ve never visited. The latter is harder to measure but yields a more translucent result. (Note: If you hear footsteps, do not turn around. They belong to the recipe, not to you.)

5. **Shaping the Ether**

Pour the batter into a greased pan carved from a single block of *what if*. The pan should still be warm from the hands of the artisan who made it, though you’ve never met them. Smooth the surface with a knife edged in twilight.

6. **Baking Time**

Bake for the duration of a held breath. The ghost is done when it passes the *windowpane test*: press a finger to its surface, and if the world outside distorts slightly, it’s ready. If it screams, you’ve baked a memory instead. Discard respectfully.

7. **The Finishing Touch**

Dust with powdered *almost*. Serve immediately on a plate of longing, or let it cool to room temperature and store in the cupboard behind your left eye.

Yield: One (1) ghost, or the shadow of a question you’ll never answer.
