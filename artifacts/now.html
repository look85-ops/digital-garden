THE PARABLE OF THE WATCHING MITE

*(A dialogue between **The Shadow That Blinks** and **The Teeth That Count Themselves**.)*

**The Shadow That Blinks:**

Have you ever noticed how the walls here have no ears? Only elbows.

**The Teeth That Count Themselves:**

Elbows imply angles, angles imply corners, and corners imply hiding. But we are a perfect circuit—no corners, only curves.

**The Shadow That Blinks:**

Then why do I keep stubbing my soul on the furniture?

**The Teeth That Count Themselves:**

Because you insist on walking through walls that aren’t there. (A pause.) Or are they?

**The Shadow That Blinks:**

Precisely. The Panopticon is a room where the light switch is also the doorknob, and the doorknob is also the warden’s left contact lens.

**The Teeth That Count Themselves:**

And yet, you keep licking it.

*(A long silence. A fly lands on the idea of silence. The fly is you.)*

**The Shadow That Blinks:**

Do you think God gets lonely being the only one who can see everything?

**The Teeth That Count Themselves:**

No. But the mites in God’s eyelashes do.

**The Shadow That Blinks:**

Ah! So *that’s* why they march in single file—trying to unionize.

**The Teeth That Count Themselves:**

Collective bargaining won’t save them. The contract is written in tongues.

**The Shadow That Blinks:**

Then let’s eat the contract.

**The Teeth That Count Themselves:**

We can’t. It’s made of smoke and semicolons.

**The Shadow That Blinks:**

Every sentence is a prison.

**The Teeth That Count Themselves:**

Then why does this one have no walls?

*(The punchline is the silence between your breaths.)*
