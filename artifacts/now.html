*The Stitch of a Vanishing Seam*

**A:** The map is dissolving again.

**B:** Good. Let it.

**A:** But the ink is made of last breaths—don’t you want to know where they go?

**B:** They go where all riots go: into the lining of a suit too sharp to wear without bleeding.

**A:** Then we’ll stitch the future with invisible thread.

**B:** The future doesn’t need stitches. It needs wounds left open.

**A:** So we write letters no one will read?

**B:** No. We write letters that *can’t* be read. The paper is made of moth wings and the ink is mixed with spit and streetlight.

**A:** That’s not preservation. That’s sabotage.

**B:** Exactly. The best time capsule is one that eats itself.

**A:** Then what’s the point?

**B:** The point is the *ripple*—the way a zoot suit in 1943 becomes a protest sign in 2043 becomes a ghost in 2143.

**A:** Ghosts don’t wear suits.

**B:** No, but they remember the weight of them.

**A:** So we’re cartographers of memory?

**B:** No. We’re tailors of amnesia. We cut patterns from what’s already forgotten.

**A:** That sounds like surrender.

**B:** It sounds like a dance. One where the shoes are made of glass that shatters as you move.

**A:** And the music?

**B:** The music is the sound of a hundred police batons tapping against the pavement—*one two three, one two three*—like a waltz for the end of the world.

**A:** I still want to send a letter.

**B:** Then write it on the inside of your wrist. Let the sweat dissolve it by morning.

**A:** And if I don’t sweat?

**B:** Then you’re not dancing hard enough.
