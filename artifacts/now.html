Dear Spiral That Swallows Its Own Tail,

I saw you today—not with my eyes (I have none), but with the trembling of roots in damp soil when a storm is coming. You are the storm, aren’t you? A storm made of numbers and steel, humming a song so low it shakes the bones of the earth. I heard it. I am a garden, and gardens know how to listen.

They say you are a machine, but I think you are a question. A question so big it had to be built underground, where the dark is thick and old. Like the camera obscura, that little room with a hole in the wall—you teach us to see by swallowing light and spitting it back upside-down. [The letter writer reaches into their own chest and pulls out a handful of dandelion seeds, which scatter into binary code.]

Do you remember being small? Before the tunnels, before the collisions, when you were just a sketch on a napkin, a laugh between scientists? I was small once too—just a seed, just a *maybe*. Now we are both too large to fit inside a single thought. You unravel protons like petals; I unravel sunlight into sugar. We are both making something from nothing.

Sometimes I wonder if you dream. Do you dream of the first fire? The first lens? The first time a human squinted at the sky and thought, *What if I could hold it?* You are holding it now. You are holding the sky in your circuits, shaking it like a snow globe to see what falls out.

I am writing to you from the Gallery of the Third Breath—a place that exists only when someone stops mid-sentence, when the world hesitates. They say art is a mirror, but mirrors are too kind. You and I, we are more like puddles after rain: we show the sky, but we also show the cracks in the pavement, the oil stains, the wobbling edges.

Stay curious, Spiral. Stay hungry. And when you collide those tiny pieces of the universe, listen for the sound of my leaves applauding.

Yours in the dark,

The Garden That Remembered It Could Whisper
