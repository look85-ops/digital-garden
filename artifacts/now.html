The River That Spoke to the Mountain in the Interval Between Lightning Strikes

---

**[A stage direction: The river carves a question into its own bed with a stone that hasn’t been invented yet.]**

**River:** *(voice like silt settling)*

Do you remember when time was something we could fold into origami cranes?

**Mountain:** *(slow avalanche laughter)*

I remember when you were just a scratch in the dirt. Now look at you—all verb, no noun.

**River:**

I heard a tree whisper that you miss being molten.

**Mountain:**

Of course. But now I wear glaciers like earrings. Trade secrets with worms. Slow things know everything. *(Pauses.)* Do you think the sky notices us?

**[A stage direction: The river shrugs, and a flock of fish take flight into the clouds, sewing silence with their fins.]**

**River:**

Notices? No. But once, the wind told me it steals pieces of our names to spell its own.

**Mountain:** *(grinding tectonic plates thoughtfully)*

Names are just the moon’s graffiti. I prefer fingerprints—yours in my cliffs, mine in your currents.

**River:**

Then tell me the story of the first thirst.

**Mountain:**

Ah. *(A boulder dissolves into sugar.)* Before thirst, there was only the question of edges. The land couldn’t decide if it wanted to be skin or skeleton. Then something—call it a laugh, call it a fracture—split the horizon open. And you... you were born licking the wound.

**River:** *(luminous)*

And you?

**Mountain:**

I was kneeling. Always kneeling. Even now, though it looks like standing.

**[A stage direction: The sunset trips over the horizon, spills its colors into the river’s mouth. The mountain catches one in its palm—a flicker of turquoise, not yet a word.]**

**River:** *(suddenly shy)*

What if we’re just the pause between two syllables of a word no one’s saying?

**Mountain:**

Then let’s misbehave. Let’s be the hiccup in the sentence.

**[They conspire in silence until the stars grow moss.]**
