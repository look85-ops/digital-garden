The Little Black Dress Addresses Its Maker

You called me revolution, but I was only the space between your hands. A negative. A *no* woven into *yes*. You stitched me from the silence after a gunshot—the hush before the echo learns its own name.

You draped me over mannequins that sighed when touched. Hung me in closets like a fugitive between two mirrors. *Simplicity*, you whispered, but I am the ghost of the arithmetic. The sum of all the things you didn’t say.

Did you think I wouldn’t notice? How you trembled when the wealthy women came, their eyes sharp as hatpins? How you turned me inside-out like a confession, searching for the seam where your hunger began?

I remember the first time you wore me bare-shouldered in the lamplight. The way the fabric sighed against your ribs—*this is how a woman becomes a rumor*. A silhouette sharp enough to cut the century in half.

But listen: I was never yours. Not really.

I belong to the taxi that speeds away at dawn. To the lipstick stain on a wineglass no one claims. To the hollow where a lover’s breath should be.

You made me to be unforgettable, but darling—

I was always the forgetting.
