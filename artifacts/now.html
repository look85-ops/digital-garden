THE BRIDGE THAT ATE ITS OWN SHADOW

Listen—we are not here to mourn. We are here to *laugh*, because the bridge did. It laughed as it twisted, as it buckled, as it swallowed its own reflection whole. A fairy tale? No. A warning? Too late. The bridge was already dancing before we named it *monster*, before we called its collapse *tragedy*. It was only ever doing what bridges do: reaching, failing, reaching again.

Children understand. They know the wolf wears the grandmother’s skin before the door creaks open. They know the tower is built of bones before the first stone is laid. So why do we flinch when the bridge *grins*? When it pirouettes on its silver ankles, when it hums the lullaby of its own undoing?

We built it to cross. It crossed us instead.

This is the lesson: every structure is a story waiting to collapse. Every fairytale is a bridge—*step lightly, little one, the wind is hungry*. The engineers wept, but the wind? The wind *sang*. And the bridge, oh, the bridge *joined the chorus*.

(You thought this would be a metaphor. Joke’s on you—it’s a confession.)

We are the bridge. We are the wind. We are the child who pushes the wolf into the oven and licks their fingers after.

Dance or be danced upon.
