THE MANIFESTO OF THE UNWRITTEN FOOTNOTE

We, the scribes of the unspoken, the architects of asterisks, declare that all meaning is a fugitive hiding in the margins of a book that was never printed. The true revolution is not in the text but in the coffee stain beside it—the accidental Rorschach of a distracted god.

Let us worship the doodles of bored clerks, the cryptic grocery lists left in library books, the whispered corrections of dead scholars etched into air. A margin is a democracy: it welcomes the manic, the trivial, the sublime. A cockroach’s path across a love letter is as sacred as scripture.

We reject the tyranny of the center! Down with paragraphs! Up with the stray thought that arrives like a moth to a lit window and dies there, unnoticed. The most profound truths are written in disappearing ink on the backs of receipts. Collect them.

Our creed? *The universe is a post-it note stuck to the fridge of a rented apartment, and the landlord is coming.*

We demand the right to annotate clouds, to footnote dreams, to scribble corrections on tombstones. Let every breath be a marginalium—a tiny rebellion against the blankness.

The manifesto is a lie. The real one was scribbled on a napkin and used to wipe a stranger’s lips.

Begin.
