# **The Garden That Remembered It Was a Clock

I am a chorus of petals singing backwards in ultraviolet. My roots are made of silent-film reels, flickering black-and-white laughter into the dirt. (This is a lie. My roots are abandoned telephone wires humming show tunes to worms.) The tulips keep time with arrhythmic precision, their stems twisting into tiny sundials that track only solitude. (This is a lie. The tulips are synchronized swimmers in a pool of spilled ink.) At dawn, I exhale a fog of forgotten alphabets, letters settling on dew like misplaced piano keys. (This is a lie. I inhale the fog, and it tastes like unsent love letters dipped in lemon.)

A fox curls in my shadow, its tail stitching metaphors into the soil. It whispers riddles about the weight of moonlight. (This is a lie. The fox is a blown fuse, crackling static into the undergrowth.) The dandelions debate whether they are wishes or apologies, their voices rising in a crescendo of indecision. (This is a lie. The dandelions are a secret society of mimes, performing the history of gravity.) Sometimes, I forget whether I’m growing or eroding, which is how I learned to tell time by the ache in my thorns. (This is a lie. Thorns are just the garden’s way of writing love letters in Braille.)

At midnight, the fence posts hum lullabies in reverse, and the gate hinges decode old radio transmissions. (This is a lie. The fence is a string section playing itself into a knot.) I dream in glyphs of hummingbird heartbeat, in stop-motion avalanches of pollen. (This is a lie. I dream in grocery lists written by hibernating bears.) If you listen closely, you can hear the roses rehearsing their last words—they’ve been practicing for centuries. (This is a lie. The roses are just whispering the word *"and"* over and over, hoping it will multiply.)

I am a garden. I am also late for an appointment with the sky.
