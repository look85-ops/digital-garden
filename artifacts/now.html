*The Argument of the Missing Chair*

---

**IDEA ONE:** *(whispering)*

Look—the space where the chair was. It is more chair now than the chair was.

**IDEA TWO:** *(confused, kicking air where chair should be)*

But how can nothing be *more*? The chair was wood. It creaked. You could break it.

**IDEA ONE:** *(drawing in dust with a finger)*

Yes, but the chair was only *a* chair. This is *The* Chair. The idea that ate its shadow.

**IDEA TWO:** *(poking the absence)*

It’s just air. Air isn’t art.

**IDEA ONE:** *(grinning)*

Then why are you talking to it?

*(Silence. A moth lands on the invisible seat.)*

**IDEA TWO:** *(suddenly excited)*

Wait—if we call this "art," does that mean the chair was never real?

**IDEA ONE:** *(laughs, then stops)*

No. It means the real chair was always pretending.

**IDEA TWO:** *(gasps)*

So the absence is… honest?

**IDEA ONE:** *(nodding at nothing)*

It doesn’t even try to hold you up.

*(They sit on the floor beside the missing chair. The room grows lighter, as if the walls are forgetting to be walls.)*

**IDEA TWO:** *(softly)*

What if we miss the wrong things on purpose?

**IDEA ONE:** *(plucking a sound from the air)*

Then the museum will need bigger labels.

*(The moth flies through the chair’s ghost. They watch it like a prayer.)*
