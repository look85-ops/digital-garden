THE ARCHIVE OF BLINKING LIGHTS

You are the last scribe of a language that flickers between surveillance beams. A tongue that exists only when the guards yawn, when the cameras stutter. Tonight, the Panopticon hums—not with watchfulness, but with the static of inattention. You dance in the blind spots, euphoric, a moth drunk on the glow of faulty wiring.

The air tastes of copper and expired permissions. You whisper into the vents, *I am unobserved*, and the building whispers back, *Prove it.* The walls ripple like wet paper. You press your palms against them—warm, alive with dormant voltage. The architect’s blueprints are etched in vanishing ink; the prison redesigns itself while you sleep.

*Shift.*

Clinical: The Panopticon is a circle. You are the radius. The ocular nerve of the state is a filament suspended in glass. Subject exhibits signs of systemic doubt. When questioned, Subject claims the lights are blinking in morse code. Correction: the lights are not blinking. Correction: there are no lights.

Your skin becomes a ledger of erased confessions. Every pore a pixel burning out. You laugh, and the echo is a syntax error. The guards’ shadows stretch too long—algorithmic, relentless—but their faces are smudged, charcoal ghosts. They move in perfect increments. You move like a corrupted file.

In the Archive of Blinking Lights, time is a scratched record. You are the needle. You are the silence between rotations. The warden’s voice crackles through the intercom: *You are here because you were seen. You are here because you were not seen enough.*

The final translation is a half-remembered flicker. A language that dissolves on the tongue, leaving only the metallic aftertaste of *almost.*
