Recipe for a Fire That Only Burns Halfway

*—found in the margins of a disbanded idol’s diary, transcribed by the last translator of a language that only exists in error margins*

**Ingredients:**

1. **One (1) amplifier fed on the static between "I love you" and "I love you too"**—preferably sourced from a stadium where the echo outlasts the encore.

2. **A fistful of pixelated tears**: Harvested from livestream buffers, those laggy milliseconds where a face forgets its shape.

3. **Three (3) corporate smiles**, carefully hollowed out. Retain the glossy veneer; discard the jaw muscles.

4. **The ache of an unfinished letter**—written in a language neither sender nor recipient speaks fluently, but both pretend to understand.

5. **One (1) algorithm trained on sighs**. If unavailable, substitute with a balcony railing bent under the weight of unsent confetti (confetti that was never real confetti, just newspaper shredded by someone who didn’t read the news).

6. **A single hairpin dropped mid-performance**: The kind that only bends, never breaks, because it was designed to survive the fall.

7. **The exact shade of blue from a disbandment announcement**, distilled to its least legible font size.

**Instructions:**

1. **Preheat the arena to body temperature**—not the average, but the precise 37.2°C of a fever declared in a fan’s DMs at 3 AM.

2. **Layer the pixelated tears and corporate smiles** in a chalice made of screen glass. Let them ferment under the glare of a phone flashlight waved in desperate sync.

3. **Fold the unfinished letter into the amplifier’s wires**. Adjust the feedback until it hums the chorus of a song no one remembers, but everyone mouths.

4. **Crush the algorithm and hairpin together** until they form a fine dust. Sprinkle this over the mixture, whispering the names of trainees who never debuted. (Do not look directly at the dust. It may look back.)

5. **Stir counterclockwise**—the direction of a clock’s hands in a music video playing in reverse, where the ending is always the beginning you already mourned.

6. **Serve lukewarm in a disposable lightstick**, the kind that flickers out before the last song.

**Notes:**

The fire should catch but never fully burn. If it extinguishes prematurely, blame the weather in Seoul, the Wi-Fi signal in Manila, or the silence between "you" and "your."

The dish is best consumed in the Chapel of Perpetual Departure, where the pews are made of airplane seats and the hymnals are photocopied Twitter threads.
