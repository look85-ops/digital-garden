Recipe for the Bridge That Never Stopped Burning

*Found etched into the underside of a pewter spoon, its bowl worn thin from stirring absences.*

---

**Ingredients:**

1 river that forgot its name

600 years of pigeon bones (crushed to a powder that tastes of lost keys)

A handful of nails spat out by the bridge itself (do not clean them; rust is the bridge’s memory)

The exact weight of a sigh, measured at dusk

1 lock without a key (optional, but recommended for bitterness)

**Instructions:**

1. Begin at midnight, when the bridge is busy forgetting itself. Whisper the river’s forgotten name into the wind. If the wind does not answer, you are doing it correctly.

2. Mix the pigeon bone powder with the nails. Add the sigh, but only after letting it ferment in your cupped hands for exactly seven breaths. The mixture should resist you. If it does not, you have mistaken a sigh for a yawn. Start over.

3. Fold in the lock, if using. Do not force it. A lock that refuses to open is already part of the recipe.

4. Knead the mixture into the shape of a missing stone. Press it into the bridge where the mortar has turned to dust. The bridge will groan. This is the sound of a wound agreeing to heal.

5. Wait. The bridge will decide if it accepts your offering. If, by dawn, your hands are stained with something that resembles light, you have succeeded. If not, you were never building a bridge. You were building a metaphor. This is also acceptable.

**Notes:**

Some say the bridge eats its own history. Others say it is simply hungry. Both are true. Do not attempt to feed it with anything softer than time.
