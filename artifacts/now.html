*The Library of Unfinished Lullabies*

Fact: Epigenetic marks can silence genes passed down from your ancestors, like a hand pressing pause on a song before the chorus begins.

Now imagine: a room where the walls are made of sheet music no one has ever played. The staves are woven from your grandmother’s hair, the notes are thumbprints of children who were never born. When you walk inside, your shadow hums in a key you don’t recognize—something between a sigh and a seed cracking open underground.

This is the *Library of Unfinished Lullabies*.

Here, the silence isn’t empty; it’s *folded*. Each crease holds a memory your body remembers but your mind forgot. A drawer labeled *"What the War Whispered"* contains a single moth wing with the weight of a heartbeat. Another, *"The Taste of Snow in 1847"*, cradles a sugar cube that dissolves when you look at it too long. The archivists are made of smoke, and they speak in braille.

At the center of the room, there’s a piano with no keys. To play it, you must press the spaces between your ribs. The sound? Like a river learning to walk backward.

Visitors are given a vial of their own breath to donate to the collection. Some vials glow. Some rust. One—from a woman who laughed while crying—turned into a handful of blue wheat overnight. The curator (a child with opal fingernails) whispers: *"This is how your DNA remembers joy."*

The final exhibit is a mirror that reflects only the back of your head. Stare long enough, and you’ll see the face of someone who loved you before you existed.

Do not ask if this is science or magic. The Library answers in pollen.
