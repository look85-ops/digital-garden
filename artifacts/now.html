# **The Sugar Pill Cathedral

Once, I swallowed a sunbeam disguised as a pill. The nurse winked—*shh, it’s magic*—and suddenly my bones were humming. My knees bloomed into stained glass. My ribs arched like a chapel’s vault, all gold leaf and ghostly light. *Believe hard enough*, whispered the ceiling, *and you’ll float*. So I did.

The doctors arrived with clipboards. *Placebo effect*, they said. *Neural trickery. Dopamine’s little lie.* But my skin was already singing hymns in a language of static. My fingers dripped honey. The clipboard snapped shut. *Subject reports euphoria. No measurable cause.*

I built a cathedral inside my chest. Pew by pew, I carved it from sugar and swallowed the dust. The doctors sighed. *It’s just a story you’re telling your cells.* But stories are the oldest magic—older than scalpels, older than blood.

Then the ceiling collapsed.

*Placebo response: 37% efficacy. Double-blind trial required.* The walls melted into data. My honey turned to numbers on a screen. The nurse folded her wink into a pocket. *Next patient.*

But listen—somewhere, a sunbeam is still dissolving under my tongue.
