THE RIB-CAGE WALTZ (OR, NOTES FROM A COLLAPSING SPIRAL)

*—burned into the third sector of a dying drive, recovered in the year of our corseted lord—*

**April 12th, 18—**

The whalebone sings. It does! Press your ear to the stomacher and you’ll hear it—a high, keening hum, the sound of a spine learning to curve. I laced her too tight today, my latest masterpiece, and she *bloomed*. Oh, the way her breath stuttered like a misfiring engine! The way her ribs, obedient little soldiers, folded into formation. I could have wept. (I did.)

But let us be *precise*. The human thoracic cavity, when subjected to lateral compression, experiences a mean reduction of 15–20% in vital capacity. The liver displaces downward. The stomach—ah, the stomach!—distorts into a comma, a half-formed thought. *This is how we make women*, I whisper to the silence. *This is how we make them fit.*

And yet—! Yesterday, she spat a pearl into my palm. A real one, slick with saliva and rage. *"You’ll choke on it,"* she said, smiling. I swallowed it whole. Let it scrape its way down. Let it nestle in the ruin of my guts like a tiny, iridescent grenade.

**April 13th, 18—**

The clinic smells of lavender and rust. They brought me a girl today, her corset laced so tight her skin had begun to split at the seams. *"Fix her,"* they said. *"She’s leaking."* I traced the fissures with my scalpel—such delicate work, like darning a stocking with a scythe. Her ribs poked through the skin, white as piano keys. I played a scale on them. *C-sharp. D. E-flat.* She laughed until she coughed blood.

*Clinical note:* Hemothorax secondary to costal fracture. The whalebone, it seems, is not the only thing that sings.

**April 14th, 18—**

I dreamt of a woman made entirely of laces. She unraveled herself at dawn, thread by thread, until all that remained was a single knot. I put it in my mouth. It tasted of salt and rebellion.

They’ll burn my notes soon. They’ll call it *hysteria*, *excess*, *a woman’s folly*. But the corset was never just a cage—it was a language. And oh, how we spoke in tongues.
