How to Bake a Cartographer’s Ghost in Eleven Steps

**Ingredients:**

- One room without corners (preferably spherical, but a half-collapsed circus tent will suffice).

- A gallon of raw twilight, strained through lace.

- The slow unraveling of a whisper that once knew your name.

- A compass needle blunted by too many apologies.

- A silver nitrate sigh, harvested under protest.

**Instructions:**

1. Lay the room flat. Pretend its walls were never breached. Paint shadows back into place with a brush made of eyelashes. Let them dry. (This might take longer than mourning.)

2. Pour the twilight into a chipped basin. Stir counterclockwise until the mixture forgets its own weight. If it thickens to the texture of a vow broken in reverse, you’ve stirred enough.

3. Introduce the whisper gently. Do not startle it. A flinching whisper will curdle the emulsion. Whispering at full volume won’t help either. Adjust.

4. With a knife filed on midnight, carve the compass needle free of its casing. Plant the shavings in unmarked soil; say a rosary for whoever the compass once called home. Crush the needle’s dull point to a pale green dust across a sheet of paper you never intended to map.

5. Take your place in the room as a fixed point; allow the darkness to pin limbs like gauzy star charts tacked down with stolen dreams. Hold. You are learning to be the thing you track. You are already dissolving. (Ignore Step 5 if unwilling to proceed as more-than-human. If skipped, Step 8 will compensate cruelly.)

6. Burn everything. Let ash fall in the shapes of towns unmapped before your ink touched them—
