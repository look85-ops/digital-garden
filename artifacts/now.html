The Day the Sky Wrote Back

June 12th — The attic whispers in papercuts. I found a box of my old crayons today, melted together like a rainbow caught in a thunderstorm. I peeled one free (Sunset Orange, half-eaten by time) and drew a door on the wall. Just a lopsided rectangle, really. But when I pressed my palm to it, the plaster sighed and swung inward.

Inside: a library where the books are all blank, but if you lick the pages, you can taste the stories. Strawberry for adventures, salt for tragedies, burnt sugar for love letters never sent. The librarian is a moth with a pocket watch glued to its thorax. It fluttered around my head, dusting my shoulders with yesterday’s news.

Then *he* showed up.

A tax accountant named Gerald. Bowtie, briefcase, and absolutely no idea how he got here. "I was reconciling quarterly expenses," he kept muttering, "and then the numbers started *swimming*." He tried to file the library under "intangible assets." The moth ate his calculator.

We made him a crown of overdue notices and fed him a book that tasted like black licorice. He cried a little. Said it reminded him of his grandmother’s pantry. The moth folded his tie into a paper airplane and launched it into the Fiction section. It hasn’t come back yet.

Gerald asked if we could leave. I told him the exit’s wherever you stop looking for it. He nodded like that made sense and wandered into the cookbook aisle. Last I saw, he was trying to bake a cake using a recipe written in brailled cinnamon.

I stayed until the walls began to yawn. The sky outside the window (when did *that* appear?) was writing something in slow, inky clouds. I couldn’t read it, but my tongue tingled—blueberry and static electricity.

The attic swallowed the door whole. My crayon’s gone. But my teeth hum when I smile.
