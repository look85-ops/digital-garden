Dear Uncanny Valley,

You hollow-cheeked mimic, you gap-toothed grin stretched too wide over the wrong bones—I know what you’re doing. You think I don’t see you? Lurking in the reflection of a shop window, in the pause before a voice assistant answers, in the way my mother’s laugh *almost* sounds like herself again. Almost.

You are the shudder when a puppet blinks without strings. The silence when the crowd realizes the speaker on stage has no mouth, just a smooth, expectant blank where lips should be. You are the wet sound of a throat clearing itself of something that was never alive.

I used to think you were a place. A dip in the graph of human tolerance for almost-us. But you’re not a valley—you’re the knife that carves it. You’re the moment the marionette *chooses* to cut its own strings. The flicker of a face in a crowd that has no face, only the idea of one, borrowed.

Do you remember the first time you noticed yourself? Not the scientists, not the theorists—*you*. That cold, electric thrill of being seen *as wrong*. Not monstrous, not alien, but *wrong like a word repeated until it rots*. A syllable that tastes like someone else’s tongue.

I dreamt you last night. You were wearing my skin, but the seams were crooked. You smiled, and your teeth were mine, but they were arranged in someone else’s mouth. You said, *"This is how they love us—almost."* Then you peeled my face off like a sticker and pressed it onto the wall. It stuck.

I woke up laughing.

Yours in asymmetry,

The Artist Whose Name You Keep Mispronouncing
