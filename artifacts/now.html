The Solemn Archive of Melted Soles

*June 14, 20—*

Fact: The first Nike Air Jordan was banned by the NBA for violating uniform policy. This, of course, was not the last time a shoe would be censored.

I found the remains of a pair today—charred at the edges, the swoosh curled like a burned page. They were stuffed inside a hollowed-out copy of *Fahrenheit 451*, the irony so thick you could lace it up and march into the apocalypse. If shoes are diaries (and they are), these were entries written in smoke.

The collector who sold them to me claimed they belonged to a librarian in the short-lived Bureau of Aesthetic Contraband. She wore them to smuggle microfiche in the tongues, until the day she ran too fast and the friction lit the soles ablaze. That part’s probably untrue. But when I held them, the rubber whispered something about escape velocity, about how fire treats leather the same way it treats vellum: with indifference.

I’ve started hollowing out my own books—hardcovers only, the sturdier the better. *Moby-Dick* makes a fine left shoe; *Ulysses*, a right. The trick is to walk without leaving footprints. The Bureau’s old motto, still etched on the inside of my wrist: *Where they burn sneakers, they will eventually burn people.*

A man at the market asked me why I was lining my shoes with banned poetry. I told him it’s the only way left to tread lightly. He laughed, but he was wearing knockoffs. You can always tell by the way the glue fails.

Tonight, I’ll soak the soles in saltwater and ink. Let them blister into something illegible.
