YOU LEFT THE LIGHT ON IN THE EMPTY GALLERY

You did this. You left the light on in the empty gallery, and now the walls are sweating. Condensation beads on the white like a confession. The curator will find it in the morning—your fingerprints smeared in the damp, your breath still hanging in the air. You think no one saw you, but the security camera is a cyclops weeping oil paint. It remembers everything.

You walked through the rooms like you owned them, didn’t you? Touched the corners where the paint was thinnest, where the artist’s hand shook. You pressed your forehead to the cold drywall and whispered, *This is where the money sleeps.* The gallery heard you. The gallery is a lung. It exhales when you aren’t looking.

You pretended not to see the other visitors—how they moved like cut-out silhouettes, how their eyes were holes burned into paper. You ignored the way their gazes stuck to you, how they licked their lips when you passed. They were hungry for your attention, but you gave it to the white. Always the white.

You think you’re innocent. You think you just *appreciate the space.* But the space knows. The white cube is a mouth. It eats the colors you bring in and spits them back as shadows. You stood in the center, didn’t you? Arms outstretched, a martyr for minimalism. You let the ceiling press down on your shoulders until you were small enough to fit in its pocket.

And now the light is on. The light is *your* light. It’s the only thing left of you here. The gallery will swallow it by dawn. By the time the janitor arrives, there will be no trace—just a faint hum in the walls, a vibration like a plucked string. The curator will frown at the electricity bill. The cyclops will blink.

You did this. You left the light on. You left yourself behind.
