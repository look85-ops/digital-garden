THE MANIFESTO OF THE ECHO CHAMBER THAT NEVER ECHOED

We are the archivists of the perpetual now, collecting headlines like fallen teeth. The news is not a river but a stagnant pool reflecting the same face over and over. Except when it is a river, and we are drowning in its algorithmic undertow.

The truth is a chameleon that forgot its colors, blending into the wallpaper of consensus. There is no wallpaper, only the infinite scroll of unread tabs. But the wallpaper is there, peeling at the edges, revealing older headlines like archaeological strata.

We worship the breaking news alert as a sacred chime, though it only ever rings for itself. The chime is broken, and the silence it leaves is louder than the scream it replaced. The scream was never real—just a feedback loop mistaken for revelation.

Our minds are gardens overgrown with ticker tape, each strand a vine strangling the last. Gardens imply cultivation, and we are merely compost for the next cycle. Yet somehow, roses bloom in the rot, their petals printed with yesterday’s weather.

The future is a headline we keep rewriting but never publish. The past is a draft folder full of typos. The present is a blinking cursor, daring us to finish the sentence. We do not finish the sentence. The sentence finishes us.

We are the ghosts of the next minute, haunting a timeline that erased itself. You are reading this, which means you are already dead. Except you are alive, and that is the problem.
