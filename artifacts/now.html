# **The Spore-print of All Possible Ascensions

You climb the left spiral, I the right—never meeting, though the same chandelier drips honeyed light onto our shoulders. The castle’s throat hums with old bones, but we are newer ghosts: our footsteps dissolve into the mycelial whisper beneath the floorboards.

This is how forests argue in slow strokes of chemistry. This is how the internet, if it loved, would root itself—

A tendril reaches through the mortar, splits the banister into lace. You glance up (or down?) and for half a breath, the staircase forgets its helix. A failure: two strands briefly braid. The fungi notice. They tilt their caps toward the breach, thirsty for error.

What is a castle but a hardened web? What is a body but a temporary knot? The chandelier trembles—not from wind, but from the weight of its own light. Somewhere, an ant carries a pixel of your shadow into the soil.

At the third landing, we almost touch. Almost. The air between us thickens into hyphae. The walls exhale spores. We are neither lost nor found, but *being threaded*—

(Here, the artist’s hand slips. A sentence fractures mid-

way. The ink blooms

like a mycorrhizal

apology.)

Still, the structure holds. Still, the message moves.
