THE HARBORMASTER’S LULLABY

*(A dialogue between Salt and Time, conducted in the ribs of a drowned pier.)*

**SALT:** You’re late.

**TIME:** I’m always late. That’s the point.

**SALT:** Then why do the Romans still stand? You were supposed to eat them.

**TIME:** I did. I just left their bones. You, on the other hand—you lick and lick, and all you get is a mouthful of rust.

**SALT:** Rust is just time’s spit. Admit it: you’re jealous of their concrete.

**TIME:** Jealous? Of a recipe? Please. They just knew how to listen.

**SALT:** To what?

**TIME:** To the silence between the stones. The way a good joke leans on the pause.

**SALT:** So their harbors are just… punchlines?

**TIME:** No. Their harbors are the breath before the punchline. The modern ones? They’re the laugh track.

*(A pause. The tide exhales through the cracks.)*

**SALT:** What if I told you I’ve been collecting the shadows of ships that never sailed?

**TIME:** I’d say you’ve got a museum of air. Cute.

**SALT:** It’s not air. It’s the shape of “what if.”

**TIME:** “What if” is my ex-wife. She left me for a better metaphor.

*(A pelican, mistaking the silence for a fish, dives into the conversation.)*

**SALT:** You’re avoiding the question. Why do they last?

**TIME:** Because they built like they were writing a letter they never meant to send. Every grain of sand was a word they didn’t say.

**SALT:** That’s… stupidly beautiful.

**TIME:** No, it’s stupidly *practical*. Beauty’s just the mold that grows on good engineering.

*(A single note from a cello, played in a room that doesn’t exist, wanders in, lost.)*

**SALT:** Fine. But I’ll outlast you.

**TIME:** Oh, sweet corrosive idiot. You *are* me. Just with better taste in shoes.

*(The pier groans. Somewhere, a librarian is weeping into a first edition of the sea.)*
