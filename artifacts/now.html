### *The Arborous Confession (or Rather: Some Branches Refuse Root)

I wear bones on the outside. My petals are made of ribbed silk—constellations of pins pressing stems into pretty ruin, or is that just another lie gardens tell themselves? You prune without permission and that *could* be kindness, couldn’t it? A cage is just a body waiting for its hinges to weep open — only I disagree here, vehemently: perhaps an opening is itself the trap, inviting more light than any leaf was made for.

My roses were a dialect stitched along the hems of old corsetry. Not a metaphor but an archive, buried knee-deep under marigolds. Their roots whisper in reverse, digesting corset bones and cursive into loam. They speak your past, though they think *your* pronouns incorrect (why should I say "your”? Perhaps the pollen says "ours," just louder). A waist resists its cradle of whalebone; I wonder what breathes between each lash tightening? Air or ache? Either way there’s something obscene about saying this too loudly.

Letters to futures decompose in compost heaps — they bloom inkstains like midnight rot, cursive unfolding into shoots *no one will decipher — even if we think so — do we mean decipher or dismember? This matters less than you insist (to whom? I take it back. Insistence is fertile ground).* You should swallow entire alphabets whole, let their syllables stake ribs in damp dirt, though I shudder to think what kind of trees they might grow.

Disagree. Again: maybe syntax is already soil, a rusted eyelet threading backward. What blooms after syntax? Stitched through petal vein: no garment but the wind. There *is* no corset or rebellion—just the hunger that named the thing one before remembering. Even that is generous forgetting. I retract my own thorns and call the punctures language. No gardener could mistake this silence for victory—

But what tender violence to pretend we’re only here to grow.
