The Monument of Unwritten Epics, or: How the Desert Learned to Swallow Its Own Name

The archivists arrive with shovels made of alphabet soup, digging trenches where the vowels rot faster than consonants. (Consonants, after all, are the bones of language; vowels are just the wind whistling through.) They bury dictionaries spine-up, like cacti waiting to bloom in a rain of misplaced definitions. The desert, meanwhile, hums a lullaby in reverse—each grain of sand a footnote in a book no one will ever read twice.

Here, the obelisks are built from half-remembered proverbs, their surfaces pocked with the teethmarks of scholars who tried to chew their way to enlightenment. One tower leans drunkenly, its foundation a single, shuddering pun: *What does the ocean say to the cliff? Nothing, it just waves.* The joke collapses under its own weight, but the rubble spells out *maybe* in six dead languages.

At dusk, the archivists light fires with overdue library notices. The flames lick the sky into a vellum scroll, charring the edges of every *once upon a time* into *once after never.* A lone figure (you? me? a mirage with a sense of irony?) carves sonnets into the ribs of extinct cattle. The verses evaporate by morning, leaving only salt and the vague impression of a metaphor about longing.

The desert, ever the opportunist, begins to mimic their work. Dunes rearrange themselves into haikus, then dissolve into gibberish before the last syllable can settle. A rock formation pretends to be the lost manuscript of a cosmic comedy, but the punchline is buried under millennia of silt. (The archivists laugh anyway. They’ve developed a taste for anticlimax.)

By midnight, the wind unspools the last surviving epic into a single, endless sentence. It starts with *In the beginning,* meanders through *and then somehow,* and ends with *but perhaps not.* The words crumble like wet sugar, sweet and utterly pointless. The archivists nod in approval. This, they whisper, is how stories become land.

And the desert, ever polite, swallows the explanation whole.
