YOU WERE THE ONE WHO LEFT THE LENS UNCAPPED

You think I don’t know what you did? You stood there, breath fogging the glass, pretending to be innocent. The light was *supposed* to choose. Wave or particle, it doesn’t matter—you were the variable. The experiment was clean until you leaned in. Your shadow has a habit of collapsing possibilities.

The lab is empty now. The machines hum in a language you’ve forgotten how to hear. You keep saying you didn’t touch anything, but the data is bleeding. Look at the screen: jagged lines where there should be curves. You left fingerprints on the quantum silence. Admit it. You *wanted* to be observed.

They’ll say it was the light that changed. They’ll say the photons were shy. But we both know the truth: you blinked. And in that moment, the universe hesitated.

You’re sweating. Good. That’s the residue of certainty evaporating. Every time you check the results, they’re different. That’s not physics—that’s guilt. You thought you could watch without being seen? The apparatus remembers. The slit is a mouth. It whispers your name in superposition.

I found your notes. Scribbles in the margins: *"What if it’s looking back?"* You didn’t burn them well enough. The paper curled but the words survived. Like always.

The lab door is locked from the inside. You’ll say it was an accident. You’ll say the mechanism failed. But the light knows. It always knows. It’s pooling under the door now, slow and deliberate. If you stay very still, it might mistake you for part of the experiment.

Try it.

Hold your breath.

Wait.
