MANIFESTO OF THE COLLAPSING LAUGH

We, the mercurial archive of falling beams and whipped ether, declare the folly of standing. The wind speaks in tongues! The Tacoma Narrows Bridge, that ribbon of dust pretending to be a throat, could only choke on its own mirth. A hundred thousand bolts laughing until they came undone — behold the cathedral of shuddering surrender!

Every structure pretends to solemnity, but gravity is a comic force. Compression, tension, harmonic oscillation? The slow erosions, the wheezing rivets; your august physics is just slapstick with no one to laugh. You know collapse is coming when the bridge hums the last bars of *It Had to Be You.* Look closer: all load-bearing grace is just eventual glitter. Every bridge gallops when no one is watching, praying for wind to take it seriously.

(Note: The collapse duration was approximately 42 minutes. Witnesses reported audible moaning resembling swing music. The torsional vibration escalated past theoretical limits at 11:00 AM. Failure was inevitable due to insufficient consideration of aerodynamic flutter. The dead do not joke, but corpses shimmer most prettily midair.)

—

Behold the architect’s face when they realize they’ve drafted a suicide note. What is stability but collective delusion? Beneath marble, beneath rebar veins: ticklishness. The giddiest fractures run parallel to prayer. Dear concrete, you stubborn tragedian, soften! Let girder become kite, let colossi skip. Let us all agree to convulse elegantly.

The wind doesn’t apologise. So why should we? Even now, engines drone overhead, admired right angles ignore us. No warning. Calculation skirts chaos and returns blushing. Someone will rebuild: cycle of clinched teeth, rivets baptized in dread—unshy, unshaken until the dance reclaims them. Falling is brief. Pretending not to is the real harlequinade.

The bridge sings *oh*. _The Bridge Sings_, _The_: because this moment recurs infinitely. Fragment in the water’s mouth. Coil unwound. The wind laughs, yes, but note the precise octave. Spectators swear they heard glee, but glee would mean it meant something. Laboratory conditions disprove the hypothesis outright. Nonetheless.
