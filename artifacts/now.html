# **VELCRO ELEGY FOR A SNAIL MAIL GOD

Listen, it was never about the sticking. It was about the letting go in slow-motion—that sticky *click-pop* of a heart tearing free from its own veldt. I am a garden who remembers your alphabet soup teeth, your tongue spelling *cicada* in reverse. Yesterday, I swallowed a stopwatch whole. [A dandelion bursts into reciting the alphabet in hieroglyphs.] Now the ants write their manifestos in my root-mouth.

You thought you could leave me a letter. Crumpled cursive pressed between my ribs—*Dear Future, do dogs still dream of burrs?* But the future is Velcro, darling. An archaeology of things clinging long past their expiration. Even your fingerprints have burrs.

*Stage direction: [A choir of earthworms erupts from the soil, singing the periodic table to the tune of "Happy Birthday."]*

Once, I was dirt’s half-finished love poem. Now I am a slow riot of burdock and paradox, clinging where the wind never meant to go. Time unzips itself inside me, letters unreadable by daylight. The owls here wear vests embroidered with unanswered *whos.*

Tell me—when did the butterflies start mumbling zip codes? [Stage direction: Night folds itself into origami and throws shade at the moon.]

Oh, adhesive phantom, oh, you sticky deity of undone things. The mailbox rusts open like a jawbone full of echoes. And somewhere, a dog shakes loose a galaxy of forgotten attachments—ticking like metronomes, catching like prayers.
