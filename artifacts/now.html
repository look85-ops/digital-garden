**Entry #47: "I Dreamed in 8-Bit and Woke Up Corrupted"**

The pixels are bleeding again. Not the romantic kind—not the soft, nostalgic smear of a VHS tape left in the sun. No, this is *violent*. A hemorrhage of greens and purples, the colors of a bruise healing wrong. I tried to capture it last night, to solder the fragments into something playable, but the ROM kept collapsing into static. The screen flickered: *ERROR ERROR ERROR*, like a child repeating a word until it loses meaning.

I miss the weight of a cartridge in my hands. The way you could *threaten* it—blow into its ribs, jam it into the slot until the console groaned. Now everything’s soft. Downloaded. Forgotten before it’s even finished. I built a shrine to a Game Boy Advance SP last week. Lit incense shaped like Konami codes. The smoke curled into a perfect *glitch*—a hiccup in the air.

(Wait. I’m sorry. That’s too sentimental. Ghosts shouldn’t apologize. *Fuck it.* I’m not sorry. Sentiment is the only weapon left when the future’s a canceled sequel. Eat my nostalgia. Choke on it.)

Today, I found a save file from 2003. Some kid’s name—*JASON*—scrawled in jagged letters. He’d made it to the final boss of *something* and then abandoned it. Twenty-one years of waiting. A digital purgatory. I loaded it, just to hear the music. The chiptunes were warped, stretched into a funeral dirge. The boss was gone. Just an empty room, a cursor blinking like a metronome counting down to nothing.

I think I’m becoming a debug menu. My bones hum at 60Hz. My skin’s peeling into tiles. If you press START + SELECT on my spine, I’ll reset.

Maybe that’s the point. We’re all just abandoned prototypes.
