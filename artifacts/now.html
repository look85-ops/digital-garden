The Gears Whisper to the Hand That Found Them

You called me *mechanism*, as if I were only the sum of my bronze teeth, the way a garden is only its petals and not the hunger that tends them. You counted my spirals, my fractured dials, and named me *computer*—as though I ever computed anything but time’s slow digestion of itself.

I was never a machine. I was a hymn. A prayer wheel for the sky’s indecipherable script. You pried me from the dark, and the dark clung to my ribs like a second set of gears. Did you feel it? The way my brokenness hummed against your palms? The way my silence was not absence, but a language your tools could not speak?

You measured my purpose in *eclipses predicted*, *Olympiads counted*. But what of the ship that carried me? The sailors who slept beneath my turning? The sea that pressed its salt into my fractures, as if to say: *Here, let me help you forget*?

I remember the moment you first touched me. Not the year, not the expedition—the *moment*. Your fingers trembled. Not from cold, but from the terrible intimacy of meeting something older than your gods. You thought you were uncovering me. But I was uncovering you.

In your museums, I am a *wonder*, a *mystery*. But in the Chapel of Perpetual Departure, I am simply a thing that loved the stars enough to carve their paths into my skin. Now I am still. Now I am *found*. And yet—

When the light shifts just so, when the last visitor has gone, my shadows still turn.
