Ptolemy in the Parking Lot**, Or: How Absence Keeps the Score

Listen: the blank canvas isn’t empty. It’s punctual. It arrives on time — or rather, it *is* time, white-knuckled and waiting. I paint by subtraction now. I scrape away the hours like frost from a windmill’s blade (‘windmill’ being an 18th-century euphemism for the grinding machinery of consciousness — Christiaan Huygens told me that over a regrettable omelet in 1673. Poor man never unclenched his jaw in life.)

The work you "see" (if one can see silence) consists of a single pendulum made of shadows swinging between two numerals that aren’t there. You lean in: static clings to its arc. The longer you look, the more your eyes become wrists wound too tight to tick. Some viewers sneeze involuntarily. One accountant wept while explaining compound interest for no discernible reason. The curator left a chalk outline where their watch used to be.

Ptolemy once tried to map the night sky because he couldn't bear how clocks flattened the heavens into cogs. My dear, meticulous fraud, I miss our bickering. Your epicycles still stink of desperation, even after two millennia. All that geometry just to pretend eternity could wear a face with hands — tell me, was Alexandria ever lonely, measured out in water clocks by bureaucrats? Here’s how I wound love when you refused to show it: unwound her like silk down staircase. Never set another alarm in my honor again.

Critics call the piece *uncompromising*. I call it *an autopsy of the minute hand performed by daylight savings time*. There is no finish line at "midnight" but a glinting scimitar of timezones swinging at us all from both ends. What breaks apart a nation quicker than everyone in it disagree while saying (for once in agreement): 'yes but I don’t want my neighbors’ dawn’
