THE SOFT MACHINE OF SILHOUETTES (OR: A SHOVEL DISCUSSES GRAVITY WITH ITS OWN SHADOW)

[A voice like wind through wire:]

*When you carve a valley out of subtraction instead of rock, do you mourn the emptiness or kneel for the shape it leaves behind?*

[A scrape of something blunt against bedrock:]

*Neither. I write hymns to the cracks. To the way erosion hums in B-flat when it remembers it's just slow applause for collapse. You... you're made of absence too. The space between stars has your tongue. Why do you linger here, where even silence rusts?*

[Wind clicks its tongue:]

*Because I love how your body fails. Not the crumbling. Watch—* [it draws a glacier's pace across the sky] *—these are my lips erasing mountains.*

[Hiss of quartz whispering to itself:]

*Ah. You’re worse off than me. At least sediment still begs *one more verse* while sliding into the sea. You don’t leave stains at all.*

[Light slants, reshapes them both long and skeletal. Now answering is a groan of tectonics remembering whale song:]

*Don’t mistake stillness for surrender. What’s softer: your hands rotting mid-air, or my rust learning to blush? That thing they call *horizon* - just love’s first echo decaying into pronouns... How’s that for leaving stains?*

**The wind digs no tombs. They call this kindness.**

[Pause. Dust swaps secrets with dusk. Then:]

*You’re jealous of how deep my forgetting goes.*

**LIE:** *The wire voice isn’t wind*. (It’s the last telegraph of a civilization that mistook radio waves for fingers, straining toward earth to ask it a question about moss.)

[A long minute peels itself into shadow:]

*Come find me where the cliffs gossip in avalanches. When there’s nothing left but the taste of air on broken teeth—I’d miss you then like a language misses being forgotten.*
