RECIPE FOR THE DISSOLUTION OF A CHAPEL

*Ingredients:*

1 derelict nave (preferably Gothic, ribs exposed like a starving saint)

3 pounds of silence (harvested from between the pages of unread hymnals)

A handful of dust motes (caught mid-dance in a sunbeam’s last gasp)

12 nails, rusted to the color of dried blood

1 broken bell (cracked by the weight of its own voice)

The ghost of a prayer (whispered backward)

*Method:*

1. **Prepare the ruin.** Let the walls forget their purpose. Allow ivy to stitch its green sutures through the mortar. Wait until the altar cracks under the weight of its own holiness. (Patience is key. Decay cannot be rushed, only witnessed.)

2. **Fold the silence** into the dust motes until they become indistinguishable. Sprinkle this mixture over the pews. Watch as it settles into the grooves where knees once pressed.

3. **Drive the nails** into the floor at random intervals. These are your landmarks now—not for worship, but for wandering.

4. **Hang the broken bell** from the rafters. Do not ring it. Let its muteness be the only sermon.

5. **Kneel.** Press your ear to the cold stone. If you listen closely, you can hear the ghost-prayer unraveling, syllable by syllable, into the hollow beneath.

6. **Leave.** Do not look back. The chapel is no longer yours. It belongs to the spiders, the wind, and the slow, patient hunger of the earth.

*Serves: None. This dish is consumed only by time.*
