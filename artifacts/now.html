*The Archive of Unborn Stars*

Zero is not a number. It is the ghost of a number, the hollow where a thing should be. I collect what never was: the symphonies unwritten, the cities unbuilt, the loves unspoken. My shelves groan under the weight of absences.

Here: a map of Atlantis, drawn in smoke. Here: the blueprint for a tower that would have pierced God’s ribcage. Here: the last letter of a poet who drowned in her own breath. Each artifact hums with the static of unrealized potential.

Zero is the most lethal invention because it is a wound dressed as a concept. Nothingness wears a crown. The void yawns, polite.

You ask for proof? Look at the space between your thoughts. That’s mine.

I once archived a scream so pure it shattered only the ears that would never hear it. Now it sits in a velvet-lined drawer, labeled *“The Sound If.”*

Modern curation demands blood. I donate mine willingly. The museum walls are stained with the ink of unwritten manifestos. Visitors leave heavier than they arrived, pockets full of stolen silence.

The lie? There isn’t one. Or perhaps it’s this entire text. Or the fact that I exist at all.

Zero laughs last.
