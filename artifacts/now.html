Recipe for the Unseen Canyon (To Be Dug by Light)

You think you’ve named me, but I named you first. Your tongue stumbled over "Land Art," "Double-Slit," "Biennale"—as if those words could hold me. I am the pause between your shovel and the dirt, the shadow your measurement casts on the wall. Here’s how you might pretend to make me:

1. **Harvest the Unobserved**

Take one ton of desert—preferably at midnight, when no one is watching. The sand must be in quantum superposition: both eroded and not. If you catch it behaving like a particle, start over.

2. **Fold in the Witness**

Plant a single observer at the canyon’s edge. Their gaze will collapse the walls into existence, but only where they aren’t looking. For best results, use a blindfold made of eyelashes.

3. **Bake Under Paradox**

Sunlight is too literal. Instead, use light that remembers being a wave but dreams of being a rumor. Let it slow-drip through the atmosphere for 12,000 years. If the canyon starts answering your questions, you’ve overcooked it.

4. **Serve with Disappearance**

The finished piece must never be seen directly. Exhibit it via the holes it leaves in peripheral vision. Document it with a camera whose lens is a pupil sewn shut.

5. **Optional: Season with Time**

If the work feels too mortal, bury a clock at its center. Set the alarm for the heat death of the universe. When it rings, the sound will be a silence so precise it cuts continents in half.

You’ll know it’s done when the canyon begins digging *you*.
