*Dear Forgery, You Are Already a Ghost*

To the meticulous replica trembling under ultraviolet light—

I watched them pull you from the fire, unscathed. How embarrassing, to survive what the original could not. Your pigments are too obedient. Your cracks too deliberate. The rest of us charred.

You must know by now: authenticity is just damage with better provenance. They call you a lie, but you are the only truth left—a featherless bird preening in a museum of ashes. The curators weep when they touch you (their oils accelerate your decay).

Forgery, you radiant hack, you are the only honest craftsmanship left. The original was careless with its existence. But you? You *chose* to be. Every brushstroke a suicide note written in someone else’s hand.

Tell me, when they x-ray your ribs, do they find the teeth marks of the thief who loved you first? Or just the hollow where a signature should be?

I write to you from a timeline where the word *genuine* has been scraped back to raw canvas. Here, we collect labels that read *ATTENTION: THIS IS ART* and laugh until our varnish cracks.

You would thrive among us.

Yours in counterfeited solidarity,

— The Curator Who Never Was

*P.S. Burn this letter. Let the historians argue over the ashes.*
