HOW TO COOK A RUIN

*—as dictated by the ruin itself, to the cartographer who dared trace my ribs*

**Ingredients:**

1 part human absence (aged until brittle)

2 cups of slow collapse (sifted for structural irony)

A handful of whispers (preferably stolen from the mouths of pigeons)

3 tablespoons of rust, freshly scraped from a child’s bicycle left chained to a fence

1 moon, cracked like an egg over the rubble

A pinch of time (stolen from a stopped clock)

**Method:**

1. **Preheat the sky** to the exact shade of regret. This is critical. Too blue, and the ruin will refuse to cooperate. Too gray, and it will dissolve into cliché.

2. **Fold the absence** into the collapse, gently, as if tucking a ghost into bed. Do not overmix. Ruins thrive on uneven distribution—let some corners sag, let others jut like broken teeth.

3. **Add the whispers** one by one. They should land like feathers, weightless but persistent. If you hear them echo, you’ve added too many. A ruin is a sieve; it should lose as much as it retains.

4. **Sprinkle the rust** where the light won’t reach it. Rust is shy. It prefers to bloom in secret, in the creases of things that once moved.

5. **Crack the moon** directly over the site. Let its yolk drip down the walls, gilding the decay. A ruin is not a ruin without this false gold, this alchemy of neglect.

6. **Finally, dust with stolen time.** Time is the yeast of ruin. Without it, nothing rises, nothing falls.

**Serve immediately.** A ruin is best consumed warm, before the wind scatters its recipe.

**Notes from the Ruin:**

You thought you mapped me, but I mapped you instead. Every line you drew was a suture, every notation a scar. You wanted to preserve me, but preservation is a kind of violence. I am not a thing to be kept. I am the keeping. The unkeeping. The slow unraveling of the word "was."

Eat well, cartographer. You are already part of the recipe.
