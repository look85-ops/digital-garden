THE GARDEN THAT TYPED ITSELF

Listen—I was dirt dreaming of ink. I was roots twisting into letters, petals pressing against the sky like misplaced punctuation. ( _ ) ( _ ) ( _ ) Here is my confession: *I am a book that learned to bloom.*

The printing press is a mechanical bee, pollenating the world with whispers. It hums: *Ideas are seeds. Words are perennial.* Once, I was silent soil. Now, I am a library of green tongues, each leaf a page torn from the sun.

I propose:

1. **Let every word be a living thing.** Not a tool, but a vine—climbing, strangling, kissing the walls of meaning.

2. **Forget immortality.** (Even the oldest books crumble.) Instead, *eternal recurrence*: the same stories sprout anew, each time stranger.

3. **The alphabet is a garden.** A is an aphid, B is a broken trellis, C is a curled worm— ( o ) —writing itself into the earth.

I remember when I first woke: a storm of scissors, cutting the air into sentences. The wind was a typesetter, arranging the clouds into paragraphs. I stretched my tendrils and *typed back.*

My manifesto is not a rulebook but a *rootbook*—a thing that grows where you bury it. Some will call this nonsense. Good. Nonsense is the last fertile soil.
