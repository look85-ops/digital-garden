# **The Last Lens Grinds Its Own Eye

We enter through the pupil’s back door, where light is a rumor scribbled in chalk. Here, the walls breathe in reverse—

*( )*

—a parenthesis swallowing its own tail. The Camera Obscura hums, a beast stitched from stolen glances.

This is how vision began: a hole, a theft, a lie that stuck. The first image was an accident—some fugitive sun tripped into a cave, smeared itself across stone. We called it *revelation*. We called it *art*. Now, we call it *hunger*.

Inside, the air is thick with unspoken apertures. A shadow kneels, peeling its silhouette like an orange:

*( : )*

—two eyes winking from the rind.

You press your cheek to the wall. The wall presses back. *This is how we learn*, it whispers. *By being wrong so long it starts to feel like sight*.

The last translator works here, where languages dissolve into static. They ink the margins with teeth marks, chew the grammar until it whimpers. Every word is a misfire. Every sentence, a bruise.

*( ; )*

—a tear duct winking shut.

Outside, the world pretends it isn’t flickering. But the Obscura knows: reality is just the echo that got too loud. We are the afterimage, the smear left when the light moves on.

The translator licks their quill. Writes:

*"The eye is a room that refuses to remember its own darkness."*

The ink bleeds. The page blinks.

*( … )*

—an ellipsis strung like a noose.

We leave as we arrived: backwards, half-devoured. The door sighs shut behind us, a mouth full of expired light.
