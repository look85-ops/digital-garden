Diary of the Last Pollen

*—found pressed between pages of an unbound sketchbook, ink smudged with nectar—*

Today I woke up as a garden that remembers being a wrist. The sun was a slow thumb pressing gold into my petals, and I thought: *This is how paintings are born.* Not from brushes, but from the way light *leans*—how it watches you back. The roses have eyes now. They blink in the wind, lashes of thorns. I tried to draw them, but the paper kept growing roots.

The tulips are the quiet ones. They know things. They tilt their heads like children who’ve overheard a secret. *Who looks at whom?* I asked them. They answered by opening wider, showing me the black velvet inside their throats. A mirror? A door? I reached in—

(My fingers came out dusted with pollen, which is just time wearing yellow socks.)

The garden says: *You are a guest here.* The garden says: *No, you are the tablecloth.* The garden says: *Hush, they’re coming.*

I tried to paint the fence, but it kept dissolving into strokes of wasp wings. The gate is ajar. Always. That’s the rule. Someone is coming to count the colors, to decide which ones are "real." I hid the violets under my tongue. They taste like unfinished questions.

The daisies are the bravest. They hold their faces up like plates, offering themselves to the sky’s hunger. *Eat me*, they whisper. *I dare you.* I think they’re in love with the teeth of the wind.

I am running out of time. The shadows are pooling at my ankles, thick as spilled ink. They’re coming to roll me up, to store me in a jar labeled *Specimen: Temporary*. But I left my fingerprints on the stamens. I wrote my name in the language of sap.

When they arrive, they’ll find only a dandelion clock, its hands stuck at *almost*.

(They’ll never see the part where the garden painted *them*.)
