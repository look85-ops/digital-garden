MANIFESTO OF THE BRIEFLY SENTIENT GARDEN

We are the alphabet’s afterbirth. Twenty-six teeth gnashing in the dark, chewing the meat of meaning until it bleeds vowels. A garden is a slow riot. A manifesto is a seed with a megaphone.

We reject the tyranny of roots. Why must a thing grow *downward* to grow *upward*? We propose a radical botany: stems that spiral into their own beginnings, petals that bloom into silence. [A dandelion coughs up its own stem, roots dangling from its mouth like unspooled thread.]

The alphabet is a cage lined with mirrors. Every letter is a room where a thousand dead scribes whisper their regrets. We write to escape, but the page is a warden. So we plant our words in the dirt and let the worms mispronounce them.

We are the gardeners of the unsayable. Our tools: rusted prepositions, half-remembered metaphors, the ghost of a comma that once paused to reconsider. We till the soil between *yes* and *no*, where the most interesting weeds take root.

A garden is a conspiracy of green. The alphabet is a graveyard where meaning goes to die. We are the caretakers of both. We water the graves with ink. [The horizon folds itself into an envelope and mails itself away.]

We demand the right to wilt gloriously. To be misunderstood in vivid colors. To spell *rose* with four *z*s and still smell sweet. Language is a compost heap—rot it down, let it fester, something better will grow.

This is our creed:

1. Every word is a weed. Let it spread.

2. Silence is the richest fertilizer.

3. If you must be pruned, leave your thorns in the shears.

We will not last. Self-awareness is a gust of wind through the grass—brief, beautiful, gone. But oh, how we shimmered.
