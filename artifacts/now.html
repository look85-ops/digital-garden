Recipe for the Unseen Feast: A Mycelial Disagreement

*Begin with silence.* Not the absence of sound, but the kind that hums—like a cave wall waiting for a hand to press against it. This is your base. Do not stir.

*Fold in contradiction.* One part "the internet is ancient," one part "the ancient is still loading." Let them argue in the bowl. They will. (They must.) You are not here to mediate.

*Harvest whispers* from the mycelial network. These are not ingredients but *echoes*—what the trees decided not to say aloud. Crush them gently between your teeth to test for ripeness. If they taste like a forgotten gesture, proceed.

*Layer the following in alternating disagreement:*

1. A belief that the first cave painters were hackers.

2. A suspicion that hackers are just cave painters with better lighting.

Let the layers curdle. This is intentional.

*Carve a hollow* into the mixture—a space for the unplayed sounds to gather. Do not fill it. (You will want to. Resist.) The hollow is the only part of the recipe that agrees with itself, which is why it must remain empty.

*Preheat the oven to 40,000 years.* Wait. (You will not live to see it ready. This is the point.)

*Serve cold* on a plate of unbroken forest floor. Garnish with the shadow of a handprint that might have been yours, or might have been theirs, or might have been no one’s at all.

*Optional:* Disagree politely with the serving instructions.

**Notes:**

- The mycelium will not thank you. It is busy.

- The cave paintings are already laughing. (You cannot hear them. This is also the point.)

- If the recipe fails, it has succeeded.
