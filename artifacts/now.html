The Slow Collapse of the Sky Into a Shallow Bowl

A tract of cracked desert, bleached white as bone, where the horizon has been persuaded to bend. Not by force, but by gentle, relentless suggestion. Over the course of a century, a team of unnamed laborers (paid in salt and silence) will erect a series of leaning mirrors—each one angled to catch the sun at dawn and fling it, like a coin, into the center of the basin. The mirrors are not glass, but polished shale, and their reflections are imperfect, smeared with time. The basin itself is not dug, but persuaded downward by the weight of staring.

At the center: a single stone, smooth as an egg, which is neither placed nor found, but *agreed upon*. Visitors (should there ever be any) will be instructed to lie on their backs in the dust and watch the sky pool above them, thick and slow as syrup. The air will smell of ozone and the inside of an old book. The work is complete when the first person mistakes the bowl’s curve for the curve of the Earth and, in doing so, forgets their own name.

**WARNING:** Do not attempt to interpret the stone’s silence. It has not consented to be understood.
