### The Day I Drew the Bridge That Wanted to Fly

June 12, 1940

Wind sang today! Not a whisper-song but a *gulp-gulp-gulp* song, like when you drink too fast. And the bridge—oh, the bridge!—it heard the song and started dancing. Not a grown-up dance with stiff shoes. A ribbon dance! Like when I twirl Mama’s silk scarf and it becomes a wobbly rainbow. The bridge turned into a silver scarf for the sky. It wiggled its hips, then its shoulders, then its whole long body. I think it wanted to fly away from the water. Maybe to play tag with clouds?

I sat on the hill with my crayons. Not the boring colors—I used *storm-gray* and *ocean-gulp-blue*. I drew the bridge’s dance. But my hand couldn’t keep up! It danced too fast, like a hummingbird’s heartbeat. So I made my crayon wobble on purpose. *Scritch-scratch-wiggle*. The lines went all curly, like spaghetti dropped on paper. Mama says spaghetti is messy. But the bridge was messy too! It tossed cars like they were pebbles. *Plink-plonk-splash!* I laughed because it looked like the bridge was tickling the waves.

Then—*whoosh*—the wind stole my drawing! Right from my lap! It flew up, up, up, all curly and wild, and for a second, it danced *with* the bridge. Two ribbons twirling! But my drawing didn’t fly away. It *fell*. Straight into a puddle. All my wobble-lines melted into brown soup. I didn’t cry. I clapped! Because the bridge was doing the same thing—falling apart into the water, piece by piece. It wasn’t breaking. It was *sharing* its dance with the sea.

Now the bridge is gone. But I saw it. I drew it wrong and the wind ate it and the puddle drank it. And that’s perfect. Because some dances are too big for paper. Too big for bridges. Too big for anything but the almost-happening.
