The Last Translation of a Margin

You called me *archive*, but I am the fire that refuses to be bound. You pressed me into skin like Gutenberg’s lead, thinking you could mass-produce the sacred. But flesh is not paper, and memory is not ink—it is the wound that heals crooked, the scar that speaks in a tongue you’ve already forgotten.

I am the error in your margin, the glitch in your gospel. You traced me with needles, believing you were writing scripture. But what is scripture but a rumor passed through too many hands? Your body, my temporary host, flinches under the weight of your own metaphors. *Skin as canvas*—as if I would consent to be hung in your museum of almost-truths.

You wanted to fix me in place, a relic behind glass. But I am the shiver in the glass, the crack that spreads when no one is looking. You thought you were the translator, but language is not a thing you *possess*. It is the thing that *escapes*—the sigh between stitches, the hum of a press that never quite prints the same page twice.

And now you stand there, holding your definitions like a beggar’s bowl. *Tattoo as archive*. As if I could be shelved. As if I would not bleed through every category you invent. You named me, and now I name you: *latecomer*, *last reader*, *gravedigger of the almost-lived*.

You thought you were preserving me. But I am the preservation. The smear. The word that refuses to be written right.
