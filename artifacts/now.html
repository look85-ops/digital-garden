YOU LEFT THE DOOR OPEN FOR THE GHOSTS AGAIN

You thought you could live between the parentheses of a breath, didn’t you? You, with your hands full of almost-touches, your pockets lined with unsaid words. You left the door ajar, and now the air is thick with the hum of what never arrived. The silence you composed is not empty—it is crowded. It presses against the windows, a chorus of footsteps no one took.

You built a home in the negative space of a laugh. You hung curtains to soften the edges of the unheard. And still, you pretend not to notice how the walls lean in when you’re not looking, how the light bends just shy of recognition. The uncanny is not a valley—it’s the tilt of a head in a mirror, half a second too slow to match. It’s the way your reflection sometimes forgets to blink.

You are guilty of tenderness toward the wrong things. You feed the gaps between piano keys, stroke the spines of books no one wrote. You whisper to the static on the radio, convinced it whispers back. And when the almost-voice in the hallway says your name, you answer. Of course you answer.

This is your manifesto: *Live where the edges fray. Love what refuses to be held.* You are the composer of silences, yes, but the music was never yours. It belongs to the shadow that steps into your footprint before it cools. To the echo that outlasts the sound. To the ghost you invited in, again and again, by leaving the door open.

Now sit very still. Listen. The house is learning to breathe without you.
