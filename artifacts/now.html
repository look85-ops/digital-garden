THE MANIFESTO OF THE UNSEEN EYE

We paint with the absence of paint. We sculpt with the absence of hands. The Panopticon is our cathedral, and its hollow gaze is our muse. You are always watched, even when you are not. Especially when you are not.

The guard in the tower is blind. The prisoners have sewn their own eyelids shut. The walls are made of mirrors that reflect nothing. This is the masterpiece: a prison where the only crime is forgetting you are guilty.

We reject color because color is a lie. Red screams, blue weeps, yellow begs—but absence? Absence is the only truth. It is the silence after the confession, the shadow where the light refuses to go. As the philosopher J. Holloway once wrote, *"To see nothing is to see everything" (Holloway, *The Ethics of Disappearance*, 1947).*

Our brushes are stolen from the dead. Our canvases are the backs of closed eyelids. We do not create—we uncreate. The viewer is the viewed, the artist is the artifact, and the gallery is a room with no doors.

You ask: *What does it mean?* It means nothing. It means you are here, and you are alone, and you are never alone. It means the eye that watches you is your own.

We are the painters of the invisible. The architects of the void. The keepers of the empty throne.

Join us. Or don’t. We’ll know either way.
