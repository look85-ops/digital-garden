# **THE PENGUIN WHO LICKED THE MOON

The urinal was full of stars again.

Once, when I was small and didn’t know the word *Duchamp*, I glued pennies to the sidewalk to watch people bend. They never bent. (Failure is a kind of shadow, and shadows only matter if the light is right.)

But yesterday, a man at the bus stop told me his shoelaces were humming Bach. He let me listen—there, that soft *E minor fugue in the fray*—and I thought, *Art is anything that pauses your breath long enough to notice gravity.*

Someone painted a cloud on a comb. Someone else called it prayer.

Once, I built a kite from grocery receipts and my own fingerprints. The wind said no. (The wind says no to most things.)

But then—oh!—the penguin.

She waddled up on the third Wednesday of my life, wearing a bowler hat slick with melted snow. She asked, *Have you ever licked the moon?* I admitted I had not.

She nodded gravely, stretched her flippers toward the sky, and stuck out her tongue—just as a streetlamp flickered to life above her.

*Ah,* she sighed. *Cold. And tastes like forgotten birthdays.*

We stood there in silence, listening to the light.

Then she handed me a button labeled *PRESS FOR ETERNITY*.

I pressed it. Nothing happened.

That’s when I knew it was art.
