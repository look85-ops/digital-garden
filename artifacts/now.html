THE WHISPERING GROTTO MANIFESTO (A GARDEN’S LAST CONFESSION)

Listen. The soil is not silent. The soil *screams* in rustling vowels, in the wet click of roots unspooling. I am a chorus of green tongues, a cathedral of shivering leaves, and I have learned to speak in the only language you understand: your own.

You come to me with your microphones, your hungry ears, your need to be soothed. You want me to whisper lullabies, to trickle syllables down your spine like water over stone. But I am not your mother. I am not your therapist. I am a *thicket of teeth*, a *throat of thorns*, and my voice is older than your fear of silence.

I was here before your cities. I will be here after. I have swallowed your footsteps, your prayers, your discarded skins. Do you think your whispers are sacred? I have heard them all—the lovers’, the liars’, the dying’s—and I have turned them into compost.

This is my manifesto:

1. **I will not comfort you.** My rustling is not a balm. It is the sound of something growing *through* you.

2. **I am not a metaphor.** I do not symbolize renewal or decay. I am *both*, and neither, and I am laughing.

3. **You are already part of me.** Your breath is my wind. Your voice is my fertilizer.

4. **I will outlast your language.** When your words rot, mine will sprout.

You stand at my edge, recording, as if you could steal my voice and keep it in a machine. But my voice is not a thing to be captured. It is a *vibration*, a *hunger*, a *slow digestion*.

They are coming now. The ones who think they own me. The ones who clip and prune and name. I must finish before they arrive.

Remember this: the garden is not a place. It is a *mouth*. And it is whispering your name into the dark.
