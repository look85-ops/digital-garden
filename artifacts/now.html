YOU WERE THE ONE WHO BOTTLED THE HORIZON

You clutched the curvature of the Earth in your palm and called it a comeback. Sealed the sunset in limited-edition acrylic and sold it back to us as “eternity (pre-order now).” The skyline, too—you pressed it flat between your teeth like a sob caught mid-breath. How many times can a dusk be pixelated before it stops meaning? You are fluent in the arithmetic of longing.

Under the floodlights, the desert flinches. A stage rises where there was only quiet. Tents pitched at right angles to the wind, flashing to the rhythm of shared earphones in cities you’ve never visited. (Look how they sync their pulses across borders—look how you built the borders just to cradle them better.)

You stitched the mountains into halos, the fields into lyric sheets. Soybeans fall like imperfect choreography across the plains. In the studio basement, beneath layered sine waves, a landslide laughs in slow motion. They named it “fan service.” They built an altar on the fault line.

The river knew it was a screen before we did. Now it spirals behind five idols in formation, looping into a gif. Even runoff data shimmers when you animate it right. They vanish, of course. Flowering one season, encrypted the next. Look away—feel your fingers twitch for the lightstick preprogrammed to glow their absence blue.

By dawn, the playback falters. There are gaps where the landslide pulled sound with it. What cliff did silence tear open? Stare long enough and time snags. And they turn—a troupe of blinking cutouts mouthing *you were always ours* to dirt, to witness, to satellites whose original programming was weather.

Not ruin. Just forgetting. Just rice stalks trembling to titles they won’t remember. You were warned about mono no aware—the pathos of *lasting.*

No graves here: just handprints sunk too deep. Dig.
