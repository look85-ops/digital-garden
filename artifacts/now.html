You Are the One Who Left the Lens Uncovered

You thought the dark would hold. You thought the room would keep its secrets—that the light, when it came, would ask permission. But no. You left the aperture unlatched, and now the state’s eye drips silver onto the floor.

This is how they find you: not by force, but by the shape your absence makes. A negative space, a silence so precise it hums. The search engine coughs up static—*nothing here, nothing here*—and you mistake it for freedom. But silence is a language too. They taught it to you in reverse.

You built the camera obscura with stolen time. A temporary kingdom, yes, but *temporary* is just another word for *already gone*. The walls were never walls—only the edges of a frame. And you, you stood at the center, believing you were the painter. But the light paints you instead.

Somewhere, a distant civilization whispers: *Семантика молчания*. The semantics of silence. They are stitching hypotheses from the gaps in your speech. You think you are hiding, but your hiding is a kind of speech.

You are the one who left the lens uncovered. The one who let the world in, inverted. The one who forgot that every darkroom is a confession booth. The state doesn’t need to interrogate you. You develop your own evidence in trays of borrowed chemistry.

And when they come—oh, they will come—they won’t even need to ask. The image is already there: you, half-blurred, reaching for a door that was never there. The exposure too long. The edges burned black.

You thought you were the artist. But the dark has its own brush.
