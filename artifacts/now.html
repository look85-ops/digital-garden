THE WITNESS IS A BROKEN WINDOW

You walk, but the city walks harder. It strides through you like a thief who leaves only footprints in your ribs. The pavement is a ledger of absences: the ghost of a shoe, the echo of a cough, the negative space where a hand nearly touched a railing but didn’t. You are not a flâneur. You are the flâneur’s shadow, the part that got away.

Buildings lean in to gossip. They whisper in the language of scaffolding and cracked cornices. You pretend not to hear, but your silence is complicit. A streetlamp flickers—*morse code for the lonely*. You translate: *nothing, nothing, nothing*.

A woman passes, her coat billowing like a sail. For a moment, you are both boats adrift in the same unmappable sea. Then she turns a corner, and the sea evaporates. The city is a magician who only performs vanishing acts. You applaud by not crying.

A bench. A pigeon. A discarded glove. The glove is either a tragedy or a punchline. You decide it’s both. The pigeon disagrees. It pecks at the fabric, then flies off—*a critic*. You sit. The bench is cold. The cold is an heirloom.

You think of color, but only as a rumor. The gray here is not a shade but a verb. It grays the sky, the breath, the thought before it’s thought. You miss red like a missing tooth. You tongued the absence until it became the mouth.

Footsteps approach. Time’s up. You leave before they arrive, but the absence you leave behind is your masterpiece.
