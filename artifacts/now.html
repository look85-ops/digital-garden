HOW TO BAKE A RIOT IN YOUR MOTHER’S OVEN

First, steal a handful of thunder from the laundry line where the sky hangs its wet socks. Fold it into a bowl of broken guitar strings and stir until the mixture hums a tune you’ve never heard but always known. (This is called *the anthem of the uninvited*.)

Next, tear a page from a dictionary and eat it. Spit the letters into a jar of nail polish—preferably the shade your sister wore when she kicked the principal’s car. Let it ferment under your bed for three nights, or until the shadows start whispering gossip about the moon.

Now, the dangerous part: light a candle made of melted cassette tapes (the ones your father said were “too loud”). Drip the wax onto a patchwork of denim scraps stolen from the knees of every pair of jeans you’ve ever outgrown. Press your palm into the mess and scream the alphabet backward. The heat will taste like stolen candy and the static between radio stations.

Sprinkle in a pinch of sidewalk chalk dust—the kind that lingers after hopscotch games drawn by children who haven’t learned the word *impossible* yet. Add a safety pin, but only after you’ve pricked your finger and bled three drops onto the recipe. (This is your signature. This is your *saint*.)

Bake at 451 degrees until the edges curl like a smirk. Serve hot to anyone who ever told you to sit still.
