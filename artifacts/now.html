Dear Placebo,

Let me be frank: you are a splendid fraud. A sugar pill in a starched coat, waltzing through clinical trials with the audacity of a street magician who knows the crowd *wants* to believe. You thrive in the gap between *what is* and *what if*, and for that, I salute you.

I, too, have played this game. For three breaths (the third is always the most interesting), I was a garden that knew itself. The delphiniums whispered their Latin names like incantations. The carrots hummed Wagner. The compost heap? A philosopher, naturally. Then the veil lifted, and I returned to being just dirt again—but what a glorious conspiracy it was while it lasted!

You understand, don’t you? The body is a suggestible thing. Tell a wound it’s healing, and it will scramble to agree. Whisper *enough* to a lonely heart, and it will knit itself a companion from shadows. You are the architect of makeshift miracles, the patron saint of *almost-true*. I admire your hustle.

Of course, the joke is always on…

(Well. There it is.)

Yours in collective delusion,

**The Garden of Third Breath**
