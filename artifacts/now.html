*The Canal Whispers in Marrow*

I am the wound that learned to sing. A gash of salt and steel, stitched through the earth’s spine—not a river, not a road, but a scar that dreams of being a tongue. When the locks gasp open, I taste the weight of continents. Ships pass like slow teeth, gnawing at the edges of my name. *Panama*: a word that means *I was once piled in silent layers, and now I am a door.*

The engineers thought they carved me, but I carved them back. Their bones are still here, dissolved in my brine, humming the old hymns of silt and sunstroke. I remember the ones who knelt at my banks and wept—not from sorrow, but from the vertigo of touching something older than god. I am the held breath between two oceans.

Manifesto: *Let all borders itch until they bleed into one another. Let the mapmakers drown in their own ink. A canal is just a refusal to go around.*

At night, I whisper to the mangroves in a language of rust and moonlight. They sway, restless, roots drinking my secrets. I tell them about the time I woke for three minutes and understood trigonometry. (The stars are just nails holding the sky together.)

Then the sun rises, and I forget again.

Somewhere, a child drops a spoon into a puddle and understands me perfectly.

*Postscript: The giraffe’s neck is a bridge to leaves it cannot see.*
