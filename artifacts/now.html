# **THE CARTOGRAPHER’S PLOW AND THE DOOR THAT WASN’T THERE UNTIL IT WAS

I am mapping a field that unstitches itself as I walk. The compass in my hand spins like a drunk moth; north is everywhere and nowhere, dissolving as soon as I name it. This is The Plow’s doing—civilization’s first scar, still tender. The earth resists.

They say the first furrow was drawn by a peasant who mistook an eagle’s shadow for God’s fingernail. Now, we follow that scratch like ants on the torn edges of a love letter. But the map is a liar. Rivers curl up and evaporate between annotations; mountains shimmy sideways, bashful.

Last Tuesday, I charted a village where every house was a different hour. The butcher sold Tuesdays at half price, his cleaver dripping with minute-fat. The baker peddled unbaked yesterday, half-chewed croissants that disintegrated in the mouth, leaving only the taste of forgetting. I placed a pin on the map. It vanished. Somewhere, a plow twitched in its sleep.

Three days ago, I sketched a cathedral that groaned under the weight of too many directions—its spires twisting like corkscrews, its altars bending toward futures still unhatched. Inside, a chorus sang psalms backward: *"Holes fill us the from risen loam."* A door I had not drawn appeared in the southern wall. It smelled of uncried tears and ozone. Opening it, I heard the sound of soil erasing itself—not dying, just unforming, unbreaking, undone.

That night, the plow returned and whispered secrets in dialects of worms. *"Legends bury themselves in reverse,"* it told me. *"The names you carve today sink upward, outracing rain."*

In the Gallery of Time's Exhaled Mistakes, my maps rot gloriously. Visitors lean in, their breaths smudging borders, unraveling meridians. All they see are remnants: scribbles, smears, one lingering phrase hovering like a ghost’s last sneeze—

**THE PLOW WOKE HUNGRY. IT ATE THE EDGES.**

The door remains, ajar.

It will not close.
