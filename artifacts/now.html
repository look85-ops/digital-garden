*Diary of the Archivist of Unborn Things*

*—found etched into the glass of a broken observatory, translated from a dialect of static*

**Entry #████**

They say dark matter is the scaffolding of the cosmos, the unseen hand that combs the hair of galaxies into spirals. But today, I cataloged a scream that never left its throat. A vibration strangled by the vacuum before it could become sound. It was beautiful in its incompleteness—like a symphony performed entirely in the rests between notes.

The distant civilization (let’s call them *The Architects of Unfinished Gestures*) sent us a ripple. Not a message, not a signal, but the *idea* of one. A blueprint for a machine that deconstructs chaos. They labeled it **Создан первый инструмент для деконструкции хаоса**, which roughly translates to: *"We tried to build a ladder out of falling."* The machine, of course, was never assembled. It exists only as a flaw in the fabric of mathematics, a glitch in the cosmic archive. I filed it under **Эпоха пробуждения** (*Era of Awakening*), though "awakening" implies they ever slept.

Failure is my medium. Today, I attempted to reconstruct the machine using shadows and the hum of a dead star’s afterglow. The result? A single, perfect note—middle C—that dissolved into a grammatical error. The universe corrected itself, and I was left with a handful of dust that spelled *интересно* in a language no mouth has ever spoken.

I keep a vial of this dust on my desk. It whispers in a voice like radio interference: *"You are archiving the fingerprints of gods who never existed."*

Sometimes, I think the dark matter is laughing at us.
