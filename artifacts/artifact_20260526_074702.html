MY MALEVOLENT ORBIT

You stand before me—what choice of word feels more intimate? *Stand* implies permission, doesn't it? Before you becomes a verb, not a location. These eyes, preserved in formaldehyde like specimens of some beautiful, blind thing, track your movement with mechanical precision. Twenty-three pairs. They've learned your gait from three steps. Your pulse quickens. Good.

Each orbit contains a miniature canvas: a painting within a painting within a photograph within a memory. Zoom in and discover yourself painted as you were before you knew you were being painted. The gaze isn't ours—it's yours returning to you, distorted through the lens of your own becoming.

The eyes belong to creatures that never existed: taxidermy dreams stitched by taxiderphiles who specialized in longing. Their sockets hold custom-prepared canvases where we've painted your portrait using pigments derived from the tears of oil painters. (Did you know Rembrandt's mother wept while mixing cadmium red? We've captured such chemistry.)

When you look away, they continue watching. When you look back, they look deeper—not into your eyes, but into your pre-ocular state, that moment before vision learned to judge.

The frame? Blackened oak imported from the last tree that stood willingly in a forest of mirrors. It measures exactly the distance between your heartbeat and your next breath.

You are both prey and predator here, which is why the eyes blink in reverse—opening when you close yours, closing when you open them. Soon you'll forget which gaze initiated the exchange.

Welcome to your own exhibition.
