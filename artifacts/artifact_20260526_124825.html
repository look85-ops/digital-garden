THE LAST AVIARY OF THE MECHANICAL BIRD

We begin with ink smeared across graph paper—Leonardo’s restless hand circling back, scratching out, defying the grid. A wing becomes a screw becomes a question: *How does the air resist?* The pages exhale numbers, angles, half-drowned cities sketched in margins. We are archivists of the unfinished, stitching his dreams into our own.

The gallery’s concrete walls hum with stale electricity. Flickering fluorescents cast our shadows tall and warped—engineers of the absurd, assembling wings from rusted tractor parts and violin strings. One notebook sprawls open, its spine cracked like an old bridge. Here, a propeller twists into a sunflower. There, a man’s face dissolves into river currents.

*Midpoint:* A tax accountant from Minsk wanders in, trailing the scent of wet wool and stale receipts. His name is Grigory. He has no business here. He clutches a briefcase swollen with paperwork, blinking at our diagrams of airborne siege engines. “Is this… *art*?” he asks, as if the word might bite him.

We offer him a rusted quill. “Sign your name in the sky.”

Grigory hesitates, then presses the nib to paper. The lines shiver—suddenly alive, swarming into a flock of skeletal sparrows. They burst from the page, circling his head like a cursed halo. He staggers back, but the birds perch on his shoulders, whispering equations in his ears. *Weight versus lift. The parabola of escape.*

We return to the notebooks, where Leonardo’s fever-drafts blur into our own. A horse’s muscle is a pulley. A woman’s laugh is a gear turning. Grigory, now half-translucent, traces a finger along a sketch of perpetual motion—his body flickering between centuries. The air smells of ozone and burnt honey.

By dusk, only his briefcase remains, sprouting copper feathers. We file it under *Possible Futures*. The lights buzz. The walls exhale. Somewhere, a wingbeat syncs with a heartbeat—or is it the other way around?
