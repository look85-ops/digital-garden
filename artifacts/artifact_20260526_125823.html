The Bureau of Suspended Sighs: A Manual for the Chronically Postponed

*(Warning: This text may induce premature nostalgia for events that have not yet occurred. Do not read while standing on one leg or humming the national anthem of a country that does not exist.)*

We are pleased to inform you that your application to wait has been processed. Your designated queue is forming in a corridor that no longer exists, though its absence has been tastefully preserved by municipal decree. Please remain seated until your number is called, or until the concept of numbers becomes obsolete—whichever comes first.

The Bureau of Suspended Sighs operates on a principle of *anticipatory erosion*: the slow wearing away of the present by the gentle friction of what might be. Our agents (trained in advanced hesitation) will distribute laminated tickets bearing the word "SOON" in 14 languages, none of which are recognizable to you. This is intentional. Misunderstanding is the first sacrament of waiting.

Visitors to our installation often ask: *How long must I stand here?* The answer is written in invisible ink on the back of your left hand. Lick it. No, harder. Wait—stop. You’ve smudged the expiration date. Now you’ll have to start over.

Key features of our program include:

- **The Perpetual Rescheduling Desk**, where clerks in paper hats will politely move your appointment to "yesterday" and then apologize for the inconvenience.

- **The Whispering Gallery**, a soundproof room where you may overhear your own future complaints about the length of the queue.

- **The Snack Bar of Infinite Delay**, serving only items that are "just about to come out of the oven." (The oven is unplugged. The pastries are theoretical.)

At dusk, the Bureau releases its most sacred ritual: the *Ceremony of the Unfurling Clock*. Participants gather to watch a timepiece unwind itself, its hands dissolving like sugar in lukewarm tea. This is not performance art. This is how bureaucracy dreams.

You may now take a number. Or leave one. Or become one.

*(Final warning: Prolonged exposure to this text may cause your shadow to arrive before you do. The Bureau is not liable for existential jet lag.)*
