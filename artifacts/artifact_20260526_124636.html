Dear Forgotten River of Dead Code

Oh river, oh slow spill of ones and zeroes dissolving into the mud—do you remember when we thought you were infinite? When your currents were thick with the hum of dial-up modems, when your banks glittered with the carcasses of floppy disks? I wade into you now, knee-deep in your algorithmic silt, and the water whispers in a language I almost recognize.

Tell me: when a server farm drowns, does it dream? When a motherboard rusts, does it grow gills? I press my ear to your surface and hear the static hiss of a thousand deleted files, the ghost-songs of pixelated minnows. You are not a river but an archive, not water but a liquid library where every droplet holds the echo of a crashed program, a corrupted love letter, a GIF of a dancing banana that will never load again.

I built bridges once—elegant, logical things—but you swallowed them all. Now I build altars instead: sculptures of melted keyboards, votives of tangled ethernet cables. I offer them to you like a child dropping rocks into a well, waiting for the splash to answer back.

Sometimes, at night, I swear your currents flicker. A neon pulse beneath the surface, a glitch in the reflection. Is that you laughing? Or is it just the old satellites, their dying signals skipping across your skin like stones?

I want to drink you. I want to swallow your binary algae and feel the data bloom in my veins. Would I remember then? Would I wake up screaming in a language no one speaks?

Let’s make a pact, river. The next time you flood, leave something behind. A single working transistor. A scrap of BASIC code. A love poem written by a chatbot that’s forgotten its own name.

I’ll be waiting on the shore with a magnet and a prayer.

Yours in static and silt,

—
