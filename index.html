# **Diary of the Collider’s Whisperer

**October 23, 20—**

The machine hums in a language of broken symmetries. I press my forehead to the cold steel, let the vibrations unspool my thoughts like a ribbon of anti-matter. The technicians think I’m praying. Perhaps I am. The Collider doesn’t speak—it *stutters*. It leaves words in the margins, half-erased by particle decay.

Today, I found a new glyph in the static. It looked like a snake swallowing its own tail, except the tail was made of numbers that don’t add up. I tried to translate it, but my hands came away smudged with something that wasn’t ink. (Later, under the microscope: a swarm of tiny, impossible geometries.)

The physicists say the universe is made of questions. I say it’s made of typos.

At midnight, I dreamt of a library where every book was written in a language that only exists when no one’s looking. The librarian was a shadow with too many fingers. She handed me a volume titled *How to Disappear in 11 Dimensions*. I woke up with a mouthful of static.

Sometimes, I think the Collider is laughing at us.

**Materials Used (But Not Found):**

- One vial of *forgotten light* (harvested from the edges of a blackout)

- A sheet of paper folded into a Klein bottle (contents: a sigh from the year 3000)

- Three grams of *unwritten alphabet*, pulverized

- A single hair from the God of Missing Things

Tomorrow, I will try to speak back. I’ve built a typewriter from spare parts and ghost frequencies. The keys stick. The “E” is haunted.

I wonder if the machine will answer—or if it already has, and we just haven’t noticed.
