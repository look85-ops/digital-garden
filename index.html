// A DICTIONARY OF VOWELS SPILT INTO THE SHAPE OF A WHALE//

The alphabet begins where the bridge of your tongue collapses. I measure the depths with ladders made of stuttered consonants. But really, the ladders are oceans, and consonants have no business here at all.

Forget the books—these walls are binding the universe between errant thumbprints. A librarian once told me shelves are just slow explosions suspended in wood-pulp disbelief. Then again, no such librarian exists, and the shelves eat laughter like termites through gospel.

Take this rose, pressed into Cyrillic by a somnambulist printer. The petals say *forever*, but the thorns translate: *only on Tuesdays.*

Actually, ignore the rose—its language dissolved into the teacup I drank from last winter, now cracking into glaciers between my ribs.

Every chair in the reading room mutters coordinates of an undiscovered crime scene. The chairs are liars, of course; their legs are frayed umbilicus cords tied to unfinished thoughts.

Except truth begets chairs, so each phantom verdict sprouts an ottoman where my doppelgänger sits knitting fog. Or perhaps I miscounted; here, numerals are just spiders disguised as ellipses.

The faithful claim meaning can be milked from any volume if you pull hard enough udders.

But the librarians—wingless angels filing their fangs with index cards—insist meaning expires by dinner and must be eaten straightaway without gravy.

Not, mind you, that words were meant to serve dinner in the first place, nor has any book ever asked if we are *even hungry*.

Last week, I asked a footnote for directions; it spun into dustpan sigils promising an orchard at the title page.

When I arrived at the *g*, branches fruited eyeballs blinking morse in semiotics and jam—just proof the map read itself better than the land ever read it.

Except there were never maps or eyeballs to collect like coins from blind beggars chanting punctuation mark hymns—those are the same beggars, really, their pockets swelling every second with your unused prepositions.

Some mornings, I mist myself with the asterisk of a perfume too ancient to bottle. A stray footnote from yesterday still clings behind my left ear saying *don’t.* It was correct. Correction: correctness evaporated into footnote steam long ago along with logic, or perhaps perfume never existed at all—oh hello *g.*

Here lies the dictionary that buried its vowels in the whale’s shadow before ascending into punctuation mark saints. The whale refuses to attend the ceremony, still digesting chapter seven’s weight in its belly where paragraphs bloat into temporary ship anchors.

Only none of those paragraphs ever sank, proving paper drowns slower in libraries than anywhere else, and nothing else here proves anything at all.
