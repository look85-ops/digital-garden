THE NIGHTSTITCH DRESS

She is not a dress but a cut in the air. A negative of a saint. You wear the absence, and the absence wears you back.

Her fabric is the black between stars, spun by blind moths who mistook their wings for scissors. Slip her on—your silhouette evaporates. Only the *clack* of heels remains, a stuttering telegram from a ghost.

*Chanel laughed while threading the needle with midnight.* They said it was too simple. She called it a *no* sewn into *yes*. A rebellion—no beads, no petals, no gold teeth. Just a blade of dress, honed against the throat of excess.

Men shuddered. *Where are the trophies?* Women understood: this is a dress that unstitches itself at dawn. A seam of liquid crow, a *shhh* with teeth.

She has pockets: one holds a knife, the other a love letter dissolving into ink.

*(Language falters here. I forget the word for *thimble* but remember the sting.)*

In ballrooms, she drinks the light. In alleys, she becomes the alley. She is not worn—she *occurs*.

At the Institute of Dissolving Memories, they pinned her to the wall. By morning, only a charcoal smirk remained.
