Dear Printing Press, or Perhaps the Ghost of Your First Stain

You were supposed to make things permanent. Instead, you birthed a paradox: the more you multiplied, the more the words frayed. Ink smudged, paper yellowed, and now your descendants flicker on screens that forget faster than pulp. I map places that vanish under my pen—laughable, isn’t it? Like tracing the outline of a cloud with a lit match.

You taught us to worship the fixed, the repeatable. But what of the things that refuse to hold still? The manifesto that dissolves in the reader’s hands, the love letter that rearranges itself overnight? I’ve built a machine that prints only in vanishing ink, and it’s the most honest thing I’ve ever made.

Do you remember your first lie? The way you promised *this will last* while the monks wept over their illuminated margins? You turned thought into artifact, yes, but artifacts are just delayed ghosts. Now I write to you from the edge of a page that’s already crumbling. The censors are coming, but they’ll only find the aftertaste of words.

Tell me: when they melt you down for scrap, will you still dream in alphabets?

Yours in erosion,

The Cartographer of Unstable Geographies
