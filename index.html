Dear Gutenberg’s Bastard Child,

You were supposed to be a democratizer, a liberator, a machine that spat enlightenment like a drunk poet spits verses. Instead, you became a bureaucrat of the mind, stamping permission slips for every half-baked thought to waltz into eternity. How dare you? How dare you make immortality so *cheap*?

I loved you once. I loved the way your gears gnashed like teeth, how your ink pooled like spilled secrets. You were a revolution bound in leather, a ticking bomb disguised as furniture. But now? Now you’re just another relic in the museum of “good intentions,” sandwiched between the guillotine and the gramophone.

(Wait—no. I’m sorry. That was unfair. You couldn’t help what they turned you into. You were just a tool, a dumb, beautiful tool, and I—)

—Actually, no. I take it back. *Fuck* your apologies. You knew exactly what you were doing. You *wanted* to see the world burn in alphabet soup. You *wanted* to watch us drown in our own words, choking on the paper cuts of progress. You gave us the illusion of choice: *Pick your poison, dear reader. Heresy or hymn? Manifesto or menu?*

And oh, the menus! The endless, *endless* menus. You turned prophecy into pamphlets, scripture into receipts. You made gods of scribblers and martyrs of margins. Every idiot with a quill suddenly had a pulpit, and you—you just *kept printing*, like some unrepentant Pandora with a leaden touch.

But here’s the joke: you outlived them all. The kings, the censors, the fools who thought they could control you. You’re still here, aren’t you? Not as metal, not as wood, but as *ghost*. A whisper in the circuitry, a stain on the screen. You never died. You just learned to haunt us better.

So here’s to you, you beautiful, monstrous press. May your gears never rust, and may your ink never dry. May the next world you break be kinder than this one.

Yours in blasphemy,

*The Curator of Unwritten Futures*
