The Cartographer of Vanishing Edges

Here, where the map bleeds into the sky, I draw a city that only exists when you aren’t looking. Its streets are made of whispers, its buildings of half-remembered dreams. If you stare too long, the ink unravels like a thread pulled from the hem of the universe. But if you blink—quick, now—you’ll catch the glow of windows where no glass should be, the flicker of a shadow that was never cast.

I am the cartographer of vanishing edges. My pen trembles not from fear, but from the weight of particles that refuse to stay put. Two stars, separated by lifetimes, hum the same tune. When one stumbles, the other catches it—no matter the distance. This is the dance of entangled things: a waltz where the partners are always falling, always found.

Once, I drew a bridge between two thoughts. It arched over a river of static, its railings threaded with light. But bridges are stubborn things. They demand to be crossed. So the moment I finished, it folded itself into a question mark and slipped into my pocket. Now, when I reach inside, I find only the echo of a footstep that hasn’t happened yet.

The paper complains. It says, *Why do you etch what cannot stay?* I tell it: *Because the vanishing is the point.* A map that lasts forever is a prison. But a map that dissolves? That’s a key.

Sometimes, at dusk, the lines I’ve drawn shiver into fireflies. They rise, carrying fragments of places that never were—or maybe just aren’t yet. I watch them go, these tiny arks of almost, and wonder whose hands will cup them next.

The surface beneath these words is softening. The letters are losing their grip. Soon, this too will be a memory of a memory. But somewhere, in the space between two breaths, another hand is carving the same words into a different sky.
