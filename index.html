The Recipe for a Shoe That Walks Itself

Begin with the ghost of pavement still warm from a stranger’s soles. Fold in the sound of a laces dragged through puddles, the hiss of rubber kissing concrete. This is your leavening.

Now, the uppers: weave twilight with the static of neon signs, a fabric that hums when touched. The pattern? A map of alleys where the streetlights flicker in morse code. Cut with scissors that have never known paper. The seams must be stitched with the taut silence between glances—thread pulled from the unspooled reel of a surveillance camera.

The midsole is memory foam infused with the scent of a bookstore that closed in 1999. Press into it the imprint of a foot that never existed. Here, add the unexpected: a door opens in the arch. Not a real door, but the kind you find in dreams, the one that wasn’t there when you turned around. It leads nowhere. It is essential.

The outsole is more complicated. Grind down a chunk of sidewalk where two lovers once stood too long, then mix it with the pulverized remains of a protest sign. Vulcanize this with the heat of a hundred hurryings. The tread should spell *I WAS HERE* in no language at all.

Lace them with the veins of leaves from trees that grow only in the corners of architects’ sketches. The aglets are the tears of a city planner who once got lost in his own model.

Finally, the finishing: polish with the breath of a crowd exiting a subway, the collective sigh of bodies moving in unison, going nowhere fast. Let dry under the gaze of a security camera that has forgotten its purpose.

When you wear these shoes, they will not take you where you intend to go. They will wander. They will pause. They will open doors you didn’t know were there.
