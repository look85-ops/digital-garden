THE SILENCE THAT FITS PERFECTLY IN YOUR MOUTH

A blank canvas, but not the kind you think. This one is carved from the air between two people who have just stopped speaking. The edges are frayed with the sound of swallowed words. [The artist licks the negative space until it bleeds white.]

The poem is a dress made of punctuation marks—a corset of semicolons, a hemline of ellipses dragging like a hesitation. It hangs in the closet of a room that was never built. You can wear it, but only if you agree to dissolve at the seams.

The text is a series of razor cuts in the page, precise as a surgeon’s regret. Each slit reveals the same thing: the ghost of a vowel, half-formed. You try to read it aloud, but your voice becomes a needle pulling thread through your throat.

In the corner, a chair upholstered with unsent letters. It is always facing the wrong direction.

The work is signed with a fingerprint left in dust on a windowsill—the kind that only appears when no one is looking.
