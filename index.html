THE LIBRARY OF UNFINISHED REVOLUTIONS

A hollow obelisk stands in the desert, its interior lined with shelves of blank books bound in expired passports. Visitors are invited to inscribe their own manifestos, confessions, or grocery lists—but only using ink made from dissolved border fences. The texts evaporate within 24 hours, leaving behind a faint metallic scent.

At the center of the room, a pendulum swings lazily, its weight a single bullet suspended in honey. The arc it traces grows imperceptibly wider each day, though no one can agree on why. A small plaque reads: *"This is a clock."*

The floor is paved with tiles cast from the melted-down coins of failed utopias. Some bear the faces of forgotten leaders; others show only the blurred imprint of a clenched fist. If you listen closely, the tiles whisper the names of streets that never existed.

The ceiling is an inverted hourglass filled with powdered legal documents. Every equinox, a hidden mechanism triggers a slow cascade, burying the latest inscriptions beneath a fine, bureaucratic dust. The process is silent, but attendees report hearing a distant choir singing *The Internationale* backward.

A single lie: The obelisk has no door.
