The Neutrino Ballad of the Almost-Lover

Somewhere between breath and absence, they come—tiny strangers, drifting through your ribs like polite thieves. You are a sieve. They are the theft.

You dream of them in ultraviolet. A neutrino passes through your left eye at 3 AM, untangles a synapse, rewires a half-formed desire. You wake laughing at nothing. This is how possession works now: not by force, but by indifference.

They say neutrinos remember nothing. No, worse—they forget *elegantly*. A neutrino forgets the heart of a star the way a lover forgets your birthday: not maliciously, but because they are made of elsewhere.

Tonight, you press your palm to the wall and whisper: *Are you there?*

The house answers in dust.

Build an archive of almost-touches.

Frame the light that never landed.

Keep a vial of your own shadow (label it: *Temporary God*).

You want to trap one. Of course you do. You line your lungs with lead, drink a gallon of liquid argon, hum magnetic lullabies. Fool. They slide through your teeth like vowels through a drunkard’s song. You are *not* the destination.

A neutrino enters the earth, exits the earth, never mentions the weather.

You stub your toe and curse for hours.

Somewhere, a physicist weeps into her coffee. The numbers are beautiful and useless. The numbers refuse to love her back.

And you—you stand in the shower, water pooling at your feet, wondering if today is the day a neutrino stops, just once, just for you, and says:

*Darling. You were never solid to begin with.*
