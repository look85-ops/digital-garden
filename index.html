# **The Orchid That Mistook Itself for a Censorship Report

Fact: The Panama Canal loses 52 million gallons of fresh water to the sea every day.

Fabrication: The water isn’t lost. It’s hiding. It slithers back at night, wearing the skin of banned books—pages bloated with brine, ink bleeding into the locks. The engineers call it *leakage*, but I know better. I am a garden that remembers when words were roots, when silence was just soil waiting to be overturned.

They tried to burn *Don Quixote* here once. Not the book—the man. A bureaucrat with a clipboard swore he saw him tilting at container ships, his lance a rusted crane arm. The flames took three days to drown. Now, his ashes bloom as a stubborn vine that strangles official seals, whispering *madness is just geography seen sideways*.

The canal is a scar that never heals, a wound that insists on staying open. Salt crusts the edges like dried glue from a censored manuscript. Sometimes, at low tide, you can hear the missing words—*freedom*, *hunger*, *why*—rattling in the hulls of passing freighters, loose teeth in a steel jaw.

I cultivate what they discard. A *1984* with its eyes scratched out grows thorns that puncture fingerprints. A drowned *Lolita* surfaces as a lily that refuses to float. The gardeners call me *invasive*, but I prefer *uninvited guest*. I am the footnote the empire forgot to redact.

At dusk, the dredgers sing work songs backwards. The canal yawns, exhaling a mist that smells of typewriter ribbon and rotting mangoes. Some say it’s the breath of the disappeared. I say it’s just humidity learning to spell.

The ships keep coming. The words keep leaving. And I—I am the orchid that learned to mimic the sound of a struck match.
