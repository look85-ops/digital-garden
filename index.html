The Diary of a Man Who Wasn’t There (But Left a Voicemail)

April 12th: Today, I painted the air. It was my finest work yet—a masterpiece of nothingness, stretched taut between two silence. The gallery owner wept when he saw it, then billed me for emotional damages.

I’ve decided to stop eating shadows. They leave a residue on the soul, like the aftertaste of a forgotten language. My therapist says I’m making progress, which is how I know I’ve failed completely.

The reality show contestants next door are rehearsing their authenticity. They practice crying in the mirror, perfecting the angle at which their tears refract the studio lights. I told them truth is overrated, but they just adjusted their lapel mics and nodded earnestly.

My latest sculpture is a hole shaped like God’s voicemail greeting. It’s interactive—you stick your head in and listen to the eternal hold music of the cosmos. A critic called it “derivative,” so I mailed him the void postage due.

The moon is just a prop, you know. I’ve seen the stagehands lowering it on frayed ropes when they think no one’s looking. Last night, I caught one of them smoking a cigarette behind the sky, his face lit by the flicker of a dying star.

I tried to explain my art to a pigeon. It pecked at my shoelaces, then flew away, trailing a string of bureaucratic forms behind it. This is why I prefer working with ghosts—they never ask for receipts.

Sometimes, I miss color. Then I remember it was just a rumor, a prank played by the retina. The world is grayscale, and we’re all just extras in a documentary about a film that was never made.

A producer called today. He wants to option my life for a reality series. I told him my existence is already a syndicated rerun. He offered me a raise in nonexistent currency. I counterproposed with a lower salary and a higher pension.

Tomorrow, I’ll paint the sound of a door closing in an empty house. Or maybe I’ll just leave the canvas blank and charge admission to the crowd’s disappointment. Either way, the reviews will write themselves in disappearing ink.
