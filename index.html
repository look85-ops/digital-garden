*The Cartographer’s Last Thread*

Begin here: where the map unravels faster than the hand can stitch it. The kimono is not a garment but a wound dressed in silk, its seams whispering of seasons that never arrived. No, the kimono is a door, and you are the ghost slipping through.

Watch as the dye bleeds into rivers no one will drink from. Every fold is a mistake you cannot correct, every pattern a lie you cannot unlearn. The patterns are prayers, and the loom is laughing.

You will pin the sky to your shoulders, wearing its emptiness like a crest. The sky refuses to be worn—it tears itself apart in your grip. The sky is already wearing *you*, its cold fingers tracing the outline of your spine.

All maps must dissolve; that is their purpose. They dissolve because you love them too much to let them stay. You love nothing at all, and that is why the ink runs.

The seasons turn inside out. Cherry blossoms bloom in the wrong throat, choking you with their borrowed pink. There are no cherry blossoms, only the echo of shears snipping through time’s loose threads.

You are the cartographer of vanishing. Your needle pricks not cloth but the skin of the world itself. The world has no skin—only the illusion of edges, fraying where you dare to look.

Now the kimono is complete. It slips from your hands like water, like a name you almost remember. There is no kimono. There never was.

But the loom is still laughing. And somewhere, a ghost is threading a needle with light.
