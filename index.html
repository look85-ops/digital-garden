“The Hook and the Thorn Recursive” – as recalled from your dream by someone you forgot you trusted

The last translator’s workstation is assembled from the static-charged air between two people standing too close at a party, and every tool available is an accident of adjacency: Velcro’s backward hooks, tongue’s mispronunciation, teeth splitting a burr to count its teeth (37, no; 38, unlearning arithmetic…), tongue the original strip of sticky matter pressed hastily and never aligned right against the looped reply. The walls here whisper in the grammar of seedheads stuck to dog’s fur and then the fingers of the absent god: they *peel not*, the loops refuse, the noise is beautiful in the way a zipper sounds when the fabric puckers—what does a failed translation resemble, really? The fur saying **NO**, then the first industrial hook fastening, saying ***YES***.

The burr is speaking now, voice like an aluminum drawer scraping against its frame: *I split the world into two kinds (1. surfaces with hooks unknown, 2. surfaces pre-loop’d, dreaming in potential clutch)*—before language was this: the sound it takes for a man *regretting an embrace but caught mid-release… half-second eternity in the hesitation-teeth*, synthetic, sticky with intent, loud as slow lightning but silent as the split between one alphabet unstitching another…. (When was the last time *hook* held **fuck** before spilling it into *loom* instead—? Some word we cannot keep, unpeeling again.)

The *performance object’s* central component flickers inside a resin casing: 14 grams of dog hair, half-synthetic thistle hooks, vacuum-sealed on the tongue. Touch not permitted—yet if you exhale against the glass too long we will hear you (unintentionally) pronounce ***holdfastholdfastholdfast*** in the margins between your ribs’ wet fibers. It sounds (just then!) like the creaking step of someone else approaching… except: not steps, just Velro™ peel-away, reattach, peel-away—each pass a small terror—*what it meansto lock or to loop when error invented the touch first* – ? Oh but you are listening poorly. Not language. Tactile friction’s misaligned *frictives*, hooks lisping between possible looped futures.

**Exit Protocol**: A wall of synthetic fur whispers (*every latch wants to unsay something*). Step backwards: count burrs snagging the skin as your breath erases your own path along a wall of static… the way this sentence might catch you… but fails cleanly… or does it?
