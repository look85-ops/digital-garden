*The Astronaut’s Dictionary of Missing Words*

1.

The station hums in a language of gaps. The cosmonaut folds her socks into perfect cubes, each crease a rebellion against entropy. She whispers to the airlock: *Tell me the word for when you miss a place you’ve never been.* The airlock says nothing. It is good at this.

2.

A single tomato floats through the module, red as a warning. The botanist chases it with the reverence of a priest. Later, they will find it pressed against a vent, softly fermenting. The crew agrees: *This is not a tragedy. This is a love story.*

3.

Night here is a falsehood. The sun rises sixteen times a day. The American engineer tapes a photograph of his dog to the bulkhead. The dog is dead. The photograph is alive. He invents a new tense for this: *The present-absent.*

4.

The Japanese medic dreams of rain. Not the rain itself, but the sound of it hitting a roof that no longer exists. She writes this down, then eats the paper. The station digests it slowly.

5.

The Russian pilot sings *Kalinka* into a vacuum-sealed bag. The melody condenses. When he opens the bag, a small galaxy drifts out. He names it *Nostalgia for a Future That Didn’t Arrive.*

6.

The Brazilian geologist maps the silence between two alarms. She calls it *The Ocean of Almost.* It has no shores. It is always leaving.

7.

At dawn (which is arbitrary), the crew gathers to watch Earth. They do not speak of beauty. They speak of the way the planet refuses to hold still. *Like a child,* someone says. *No,* says another. *Like a secret.*

The station rotates. The missing words multiply.
