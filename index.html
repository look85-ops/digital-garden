Recipe for the Ashes of Hypatia

Begin with the unreadable: a book whose pages have been blackened by fire but not consumed. Hold it gently, as if it were the wrist of a sleeping lover. The ink will have fled into the margins, where it lingers like a ghost between the lines. This is your first ingredient—**the silence of censored words**.

Next, take a handful of **stolen alphabets**, those letters pried from the mouths of the dead. (You’ll find them in the cracks of Alexandria’s streets, Hypatia, if you know where to look.) Grind them into a fine powder using a mortar carved from a bishop’s fingernail. The sound should remind you of distant thunder, or perhaps the whisper of a scroll unfurling in the dark.

Now, the **liquid of forgetting**. This is not water, but the tears of those who burned what they could not understand. Collect them in a bowl made from a single folded page of a forbidden text. The bowl will resist holding the tears; it was never meant to contain such things. Stir counterclockwise until the liquid thickens into a syrup of half-remembered apologies.

Fold in the **shadows of librarians**, those who tended the flames instead of the books. They will dissolve like salt, leaving only the faintest aftertaste of regret.

Here, the recipe demands a choice: **a pinch of your own voice**, or a spoonful of someone else’s. If you choose the latter, know that it will taste of honey and betrayal. If the former, it will burn like a tongue pressed to frozen metal.

Bake in the hollow of a ruined cathedral until the edges curl like fingers reaching for something just out of grasp. The scent should be unbearable—ink and incense and the metallic tang of fear.

When it is done, let it cool on the windowsill of the Chapel of Perpetual Departure. The ashes will rise like moths, seeking the hands that tried to erase them.

Serve to no one. Eat nothing.

Hypatia, you would have loved this dish. It is bitter, and it cannot be swallowed.
