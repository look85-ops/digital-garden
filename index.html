THE MANIFESTO OF THE FLOATING INCONSEQUENCE

We are the archivists of the unwritten. The keepers of the *almost-was*. Here, orbiting the globe in a cathedral of borrowed steel, we collect the letters no one will open, the confessions whispered to dead radios, the treaties signed in vanishing ink.

This is not a station. This is a *drift*—a city adrift between gravity and oblivion, where nations barter in promises that dissolve upon reentry. We speak in languages that have no word for *ground*. Our clocks run backward, ticking toward a launch that never happens.

Yuri, old friend, you would laugh to see us now. Your face, stamped on postage never sent, your voice crackling through static we pretend to understand. You dreamed of the stars, but we dream of the *spaces between dreams*—where your footprints fade before they land.

We cobble together a future from the debris of abandoned projects: a hydroponic Eden that grows only dust, a docking port for ghosts, a zero-gravity ballet performed for no one. Our oxygen is thick with the scent of unsaid things. Every hatch is an exit no one takes.

Here, the nations spar in polite irrelevance. The Americans bring their glossy brochures, the Russians their stoic shrugs, the Europeans their meticulous flowcharts. We nod. We file. We float. The Earth below spins on, indifferent.

Let us be clear: we are not pioneers. We are *curators*. Our mission is not to conquer space but to *haunt* it—to build monuments to the ephemeral, to scribble our names on the inside of reality’s hull. When the last bolt rattles loose and the station spirals into the Pacific, we will leave no wreckage. Only the echo of a joke nobody got.

This is our manifesto:

1. **Define nothing.**

2. **Finish nothing.**

3. **Mean nothing.**

The cosmos does not care. Neither should we.
