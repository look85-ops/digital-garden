Dear The Commons,

You are the ghost in the machine that refuses to haunt properly. You are the park bench that outlives its plaque, the library book with margins full of arguments. I write to you with ink made of rainwater and spite.

You are the last collective dream, stitching itself together from frayed broadband and whispered recipes. But no, you are not a dream—you are the wakeful ache of a thousand hands pulling in different directions.

We built you from stolen timber and borrowed time, a cathedral of mismatched bricks. You were never ours to begin with, though we pretend otherwise. The fence around you is made of teeth, and we call it a smile.

You thrive in the cracks of empire, a weed with impeccable timing. Yet you are not a weed—you are the cultivated absence of a garden we forgot to plant. We water you with nostalgia and wonder why you taste like rust.

Remember when we said you belonged to everyone? That was a lie, but a useful one, like calling a graveyard “open space.” You are the echo of a shout no one remembers shouting.

You are the algorithm that recommends solidarity but sells your data. No, you are the glitch that erases the algorithm entirely.

I love you like a wound loves salt. Like a river loves its dams. Like a fire loves the arsonist who forgets to run.

You are not a resource. You are the argument over what a resource even means.

Yours, in perpetual debt,

The Artist
