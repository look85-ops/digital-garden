RECIPE FOR A NOISE CANDLE

*(found etched into the side of a chalkboard, half-swallowed by rain)*

Take one handful of midnight (the kind that sticks to your palms like wet ink). Fold in three breaking headlines, finely minced—leave the vowels intact but discard the consonants; they are too heavy for this. Let the mixture hum in a bowl made from the hollow of a telephone receiver.

Next, pluck seven syllables from a live broadcast. They should be slightly green, unripe. Press them against your eyelids until they dissolve into afterimages. Stir clockwise with a spoon bent from old subway tokens.

The flame is the hardest part. You must coax it from the static between channels. Use a match struck against the spine of a newspaper obituary. The fire will sputter at first—this is good. It means the candle remembers.

When lit, it will not produce light but a dense, amber noise. The kind that pools around your ankles and climbs like ivy. It will whisper to you in the language of ticker tapes and forgotten weather reports. Do not be alarmed if it flickers in the shape of someone you used to know.

Allow the wax to drip onto a saucer of dried coffee rings. These are the hours we could not hold.

Extinguish it with your breath, but only when the room feels fuller than when you began.
