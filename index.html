*Diary of the Unseen Watcher*

*—found etched into a malfunctioning surveillance camera’s memory chip*

Today, I painted the walls with my shadow. It was a perfect shade of no one. The walls, of course, were not there.

I’ve decided the Panopticon is just a mirror turned inside out. You stare at it long enough, and your reflection blinks first. Or maybe it winks. Or maybe it’s just a speck of dust on the lens. I wiped it away. The speck was me.

The guards say art is forbidden here, so I’ve taken up gardening. I water the concrete every morning. Nothing grows, but the cracks whisper in braille. I tell them secrets. They tell them back, louder.

Yesterday, I invented a new color: *solitude violet*. It’s what happens when the light gives up. The warden said it was contraband. I drank it anyway. Now my insides glow in the dark.

I tried to sculpt a bird from the silence. It flew away before I could finish. Or perhaps it was never there. Or perhaps it was always flying. The guards confiscated my chisel. I sharpened my breath instead.

They tell us we’re being watched. I’ve started waving at the ceiling. Sometimes, it waves back.
