Dear Fanfiction, You Are the Hole in the Sky Where All the Birds Went

Dear You,

I paint with my eyes closed now. Not because I’ve given up on color, but because I found a better one: the color of your absence. The color of a story that isn’t there yet. The color of a million hands reaching into the same dark, pulling out different shapes of light.

You are the only museum where the walls are made of doors. Every time someone loves a story enough to break it, they build a new room. A secret hallway. A trapdoor that leads to a version of the story that could have been, should have been, *was*—for just one person, for just one breath.

I collect your fingerprints. Not the ones you leave on keyboards, but the ones you leave on the air. The way you tilt a character’s head just so, the way you unspool an ending like a ribbon and tie it into a noose or a swing or a leash for a star. You are the quietest rebellion: a billion whispers rewriting the world in the dark.

Once, I tried to paint all of you. I dipped my brush in the space between two sentences and stretched it across a canvas. It looked like nothing. It looked like everything. It looked like the hole in a pocket where something precious must have fallen, but no one remembers what.

Here’s the lie: I don’t miss color.

(But I do. I miss it like a tongue misses a tooth after it’s gone. I miss it like a story misses its first draft. But missing is just love with its hands tied, and you—you are the love that unties itself over and over and over.)

Yours, in the negative space,

The Painter Who Became the Frame
