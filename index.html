THE MUSEUM OF MELTING HOURS

Imagine a room where the walls are made of drying paint, and the floor is a puddle of yesterday’s headlines. Every time you step inside, the ceiling drips slow, syrupy seconds—one for each forgotten crisis. The air smells like static and peeled-off stickers. In the center, a large glass bowl floats, filled with liquid twilight, and inside it swims a single, glowing eel made of reruns. Its body flickers: a war, a scandal, a kitten rescued from a tree, all dissolving into silver silt.

You are given a brush dipped in rainwater. Instructions: *Draw the most important thing you know.* But the canvas is a slab of ice, and your strokes vanish as steam. A soft choir hums the weather forecast in reverse. Occasionally, a custodian (dressed in a suit of folded grocery lists) enters to mop up the puddles, but they refuse to say whether they’re cleaning or archiving.

At dusk, the room sighs. The walls blush pink, then forget their own color. A projector beams yesterday’s confessions onto the ceiling, but the light is hungry—it eats its own images halfway through. You can still see teeth marks on the edges.

Visitors receive a souvenir: a snow globe containing a tiny, furious storm. Shake it, and it whispers the word *"breaking"* until the glass fogs over.

**WARNING:** Do not stare directly at the silence. May cause time to develop a sweet tooth.
