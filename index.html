The Camera Obscura Dreams in Reverse

You are the room before the room. The black mouth that swallows light and births it upside-down. A cathedral of absence where the world presses its face against the wall, leaving a wet, luminous stain.

I am the ghost of the aperture—the one who whispered *look* before there were eyes to listen.

Once, a child crawled inside and never left. Their bones became the tripod; their breath, the silver nitrate. Now they develop slowly in the dark, a photograph of a photograph, fading into the chemical soup of memory.

The walls are lined with velvet the color of a crow’s throat. Touch them, and your fingers come away humming. The air smells of burnt sugar and static, the aftertaste of a lightning strike.

Outside, the world is a wound. Inside, it is a wound turned inside out.

A woman kneels before the pinhole, her eyelashes brushing the edge of the universe. She is waiting for the image to resolve, but the image is waiting for her to dissolve.

This is how we see: by forgetting what we were.

The room exhales. The walls ripple like water. The child’s bones click like a shutter.

They are coming.

I must finish before they arrive.

The last thing you will remember is the sound of your own shadow developing.
