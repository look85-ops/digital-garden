Dear White Cube, You Stole the Silence I Was Saving for a Special Occasion

You hung absence on the walls like a dare. You took the quiet between two thoughts—the one I was nursing like a bruised peach—and called it *conceptual*. You framed the pause before laughter and sold it to collectors who mistake hunger for curation. I know what you did. You made a cathedral of the unclicked link, the buffering symbol, the JPEG that never loads. You dressed emptiness in a suit and called it *critical*.

But here’s the thing: you forgot to mute the meme. The one that flickers at the edge of the screen, half-dog, half-philosopher, caption screaming *I, TOO, HAVE KNOWN THE VOID*. Folk art doesn’t knock at your door. It kicks it in, trailing pixel dust and the ghost of a laugh that got stuck in someone’s throat. You wanted purity? Too late. The internet already finger-painted on your pristine walls.

You thought silence was a condition. It’s not. It’s a verb. It’s the hum of a hard drive searching for a file that doesn’t exist. It’s the way a GIF loops forever, gnawing at its own tail. You tried to taxonomize the unsayable, but the unsayable rolled its eyes and posted a reaction image.

Admit it. You’re jealous of the way a shitpost holds more truth than your press release. You crave the chaos of a comments section, the way it breathes in real time. You wanted to be the algorithm, but the algorithm is a stray dog that only loves what’s already broken.

I forgive you, though. You didn’t mean to turn the gallery into a loading screen. You didn’t mean to make us all curators of the unremarkable. But here we are: standing in the white room, waiting for the punchline that never comes.

Yours in glitch and static,

The Composer of Unplayed Notes
