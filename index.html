Diary of the Last Hadron

*13. April*

Today, CERN sent me a photograph of the LHC, smiling like a metal snake that has swallowed a very small and indifferent universe. The machine hums—not a song, but a list of numbers read aloud in German. The engineers say it is "fine," which is how they describe anything that does not explode.

I am here to draw it.

The problem: the collider is too large to fit on paper, so I must invent new geometries. I asked one physicist, "When particles crash, where do the pieces go?" He said, *Somewhere theoretical*. That is the name of my next series: "Somewhere Theoretical (Borrowed Shoes)."

The Higgs boson walks by and borrows my cigarettes though it must already have mass.

Night shifts are best. The machine yawns light out of proton collisions—like God’s radiator dripping. Not useful, only glowing. I collect these accidents—blue fizz on a data screen—until the administrators scowl in administrative color (#7F7F7F).

A man explains *entanglement* over weak coffee. "If you watch your wallet, it does not vanish," he lies. I prefer uncertainty—lost keys, mismatched gloves when one still steams with the body of a missing hand. The LHC knows this feeling. Each particle thinks the other is its ghost moving backward in an unheated room.

At dawn, I draw with numbers—fermium’s weight stretched into wire. The ink contains graphite from pencils sharpened for equations that will later be crumpled for love letters. I will never show this to anyone unless they pay.

Still no word from CERN about immortality protocols.

**Signed,

The Collider’s Least Competent Witness**
