Tendrils of the Last Streetlamp Whispering to the Mice

Somewhere between the cathedral collapsing inward like a lung and the wet teeth of ivy gnawing its ribs—there, in that *almost*, we press our ears to rubble and strain to hear the absences hum. What is it we crave from derelict ballrooms, from gutted hospitals, from wallpaper curling like a saint’s tongue in flame? The stains where pictures hung?

Rot writes its own psalms.

Mold blooms in gospel.

Cobwebs consecrate the gaps where prayers faltered.

Decay is never still.

In the asylum’s atrium, a chandelier swayed long after they took the bodies—wind or ghost, indifferent witness. Glass teeth trembled, shedding prisms onto peeling linoleum: tiny, guttered suns. Once, orderlies mopped this fever-shine away. Now the light pools and crusts, anointed by dust.

When time forgets its arithmetic, the walls begin remembering backwards: first the peeling lead paint, then the wet slurry of plaster, then the sloshed bucket of a laborer whistling through bad teeth. Deeper, older—pocketknife carvings softened by centuries of breathing, children trading names like secrets across an attic beam; splinters holding the warmth of hands that never knew they were ruins.

I touch a doorknob warmed by noon, find my fingers tracing not metal but the vanishing-point of a thousand exits, hinges still whispering *go, go*. Who lingers for these echoes? Me, and the pigeons baptizing themselves in rainwater from a shattered ceiling. Them: wings scattering shadows like loose coins. Me: a wound disguised as a visitor.

This city inhospits us all in the end. The stationmaster’s ticket hole eaten by rust, the pharmacy scale dreaming of apricots in its brass bowl, chalk ghosts of grocery lists haunting the sideboards. But here—by this buckling staircase, fingers flaked brick from rot that tasted like—

—I mean to write *memory*.

I scribble **salt**. The page mulch-greedy, word-thirsty, crumbling into what? Footnotes for a burial rite? Silt. Silence. Syntax untangling: noun for the dead watch in the pocket beneath the rafters, verb for how mildew chants without needing a mouth.

We stand where the living once pivoted on their heels and departed, leave what we cannot carry to be measured *against* the sagging beams—

—measure *toward* the mice in their cathedral of chewed hymnbooks, their whiskers tuned to frequency—

of something too tender **a fracture** to name.
