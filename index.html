How to Bake a Shattered Moon

First, find the pieces. They are not yours. They never were. A crescent sliver in the soap dish. A crumb of luminescence caught between floorboards. A ghost of glowworms licked from the pavement after rain. Collect them in a black velvet pouch sewn from the quiet hour between 3 and 4 AM.

Preheat the night to aching. Not the kind of aching that howls—the kind that hums. The kind that lives in the hollow of a teacup after the tea is gone.

Now, the glue. Do not reach for gold. Gold is for those who still believe in fixing. Instead, use **absence**. It sticks better. (Absence is the only thing that does.) Dip your brush into the space where a word should be but isn’t. Stroke it along the fractures. Watch as the edges curl inward, hungry.

Arrange the shards on a baking sheet of cloud. Do not align them. Let them overlap like reluctant lovers. The gaps are the point. The gaps are the art.

Slide the tray into the oven of your ribs. Wait. (You will know it’s done when the light leaks out.)

Once cooled, hold it up to the sky. Not to see it—but to let the sky see itself, broken and beautiful, through the cracks.

Serve on a bed of swallowed apologies. Serves no one.
