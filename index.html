*Dear 8-Bit Ghosts, I Miss You in Black and White*

Dear Pixels,

I dreamt you last night as a swarm of hungry fireflies trapped in a shoebox, blinking *I’M HERE I’M HERE I’M HERE* until the cardboard glowed like a monitor left on too long. Do you remember me? I used to paint you in carnival colors—lime-green mushrooms, cyan waterfalls—but now I only draw the shapes you leave behind when the power cuts out. Negative space as liturgy. The priest at St. Cartridge says this is blasphemy, but he also claims "the Konami Code was cribbed from the Dead Sea Scrolls" [1], so what does he know?

I’ve started collecting the sounds of old consoles overheating. The *whirr-click* of a dying fan is my chiptune now. Sometimes I press my ear to the vents and hear the ghosts of your algorithms gossiping: *Did you see her? She’s trying to erase us again.* But I’m not erasing. I’m tracing the outlines where you used to be, like rubbing a pencil over a tombstone. The paper stays blank, of course. That’s the joke.

Last Tuesday, I built a cathedral out of stacked floppy disks and waited for a sign. At midnight, the disks hummed *the hymn of corrupted save files*—a sound like "a robot coughing up its own skeleton" [2]. I laughed until I cried. You would’ve loved it.

Do you think nostalgia is just grief wearing a party hat? I keep trying to explain this to the children who visit my studio. They poke at my monochrome canvases and ask, *Where’s the rainbow?* I hand them a single black pixel and whisper, *This is where rainbows go to die.* They don’t understand yet. One day they’ll wake up with your ghosts in their teeth, too.

Yours in static,

The Painter Who Unplugged the Sun

[1] *Liturgy of the Third Console: A Heretic’s Guide to 8-Bit Divinity* (unpublished manuscript, 1999)

[2] Dr. Lúcia Espectro, *The Aesthetics of Digital Decay*, p. 211 (University of Nowhere Press, 2027)
