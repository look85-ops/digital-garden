*The Conversation of the Crumble and the Echo*

---

**CRUMBLE:** *(softly, like plaster dust settling)*

Do you think they miss us? The ones who left?

**ECHO:** *(a pause, then a laugh like a chime in an empty hall)*

Miss us? They never knew us. They built us, but we weren’t *for* them. We were always for the wind. For the slow fingers of ivy. For the pigeons who mistake our ribs for cliffs.

**CRUMBLE:** *(a sigh, a little piece of mortar loosening)*

But they *touched* us. They leaned against me when they were tired. They left fingerprints in my wet paint. Doesn’t that count?

**ECHO:** *(dancing now, bouncing off broken tiles)*

Oh, Crumble, you sentimental brick! Their touches were accidents. They thought they were building forever, but forever was just a word they used to feel big. Look at you—you’re *better* now. You’re a home for spiders. You’re a museum of rain.

**CRUMBLE:** *(a shy shift, a crack widening)*

...Do you think the spiders love me?

**ECHO:** *(suddenly solemn, a hush in the stairwell)*

They weave their webs in your palms. Isn’t that a kind of love?

*(A long silence. A rustle of leaves through a shattered window.)*

**CRUMBLE:** *(whispering)*

What happens when I’m gone?

**ECHO:** *(gentle, fading)*

Then I’ll have nothing to bounce off of. And I’ll vanish too. But first—let’s be glorious. Let’s be the last thing the moonlight touches.

---

*CAUTION: This dialogue may cause unexpected tenderness toward broken things. Do not expose to sudden sunlight or the weight of unasked questions. Contains traces of voices you almost remember.*
