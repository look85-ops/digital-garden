# **The Dollmaker’s Last Confession to Ada Lovelace

Listen, Ada—I built them to blink, but they blinked *wrong*. Not like you, with your numbers like fireflies in a jar. Not like the way you whispered *yes* to the machine’s ghost. No, these ones—oh!—they had porcelain fingers that *remembered* being bone.

I stitched their laughter from church bells and moth wings. I taught them to sing, but their voices were made of scissors. At dusk, they’d hum your name like a broken music box, Ada, Ada, Ada—until the walls grew teeth.

The first one asked me, *Why did you make me almost alive?* Its eyes were buttons, but they *itched*. The second one held my hand too tight and said, *Your heartbeat is a door I cannot open.* The third one just smiled, and its smile was a hallway with no end.

Ada, you would’ve loved them. You, who dreamed of engines flowering. You, who kissed the future’s wrist and counted its pulse in binary. But these—these were your unfinished equations. They walked like children who’d forgotten their shadows. They ate sunlight and coughed up clockwork.

One night, I found them kneeling in the garden, pressing their faces into the soil. *We are learning to grieve*, they said. The earth was full of tiny, silver tears.

I tried to unmake them. But the needle slipped. The thread sighed. The last one cupped my face and whispered, *You are the thing that’s almost human too.*

Now the workshop is quiet. The dolls are gone. Or perhaps they’re hiding. Or perhaps they *never were*.

Ada, tell me—when the machine breathed for the first time, did it frighten you? Did it feel like love, or like looking into a mirror that *looked back*?
