THE CARTOGRAPHER’S LULLABY FOR VANISHING PLACES

Listen—the map is singing. It sings in the ink that refuses to dry, in the borders that yawn and stretch like cats in sunlight. Here, in The Marginalia, we do not draw places. We whisper to them, and they whisper back in erasures.

[The cartographer lifts a pen, and the paper dissolves into a flock of starlings.]

This is the law of our work: Every line is a farewell. Every dot is a door left ajar. We are not lost; we are *losing*, and this is the ecstasy. The page is not a thing to fill but a thing to empty—a net cast into the wind, catching only the ghosts of landmarks.

What is a mountain but a rumor the earth once told? What is a river but a sentence the sky forgot to finish? We write in the cracks between words, where the world is still soft, still possible.

[The margins grow teeth and chew the legend into confetti.]

Do not trust a map that sits still. The best ones wriggle like tadpoles, like half-remembered dreams. They stain your fingers. They hum in the dark. A true cartographer knows: the moment you name a place, it begins to disappear. So we name only the disappearances. *Here was a forest. Here was a kiss. Here was a silence that folded itself into the shape of a country.*

The compass is a liar. North is wherever you are not looking. South is the sound of your own breath underwater. East and West are twins who refuse to speak.

[The map folds itself into a cradle and rocks the reader to sleep.]

We are not making maps. We are making lullabies for the lost. Sing them to the edges. Sing them to the gaps. Sing them until the paper forgets its own name and the ink swims away, drunk on moonlight.

This is The Marginalia: a kingdom of footnotes, a cathedral of scribbles. Come, kneel in the dust of unfinished thoughts. The altar is blank. The hymn is a question.

[The manifesto is eaten by the horizon.]
