THE VEIL THAT TEACHES WHALES TO FLY

June 17, somewhere in between

Today I found a moth inside the seam of the Veil. Not a real moth—soft, torn wings shifting between ink-blue and the color of apologies that never get said—but *idea*-moth, fluttering at the edge of understanding. (Gently, I told it: Ideas die fast when you hold them too tight.)

The cloth does that—turns into air just as I begin counting threads. I pressed my ear to its folds and heard a sound that must’ve been rain falling backwards. Or women whispering the first drafts of folktales, eraser crumbs still clinging to their tongues. A history in crossed-out lines.

I cut a loose strand off with my teeth (the Veil prefers small sacrifices) and taped it to my wrist—for luck. Now it keeps humming a lullaby in dialect so old, my skin remembers the words before I do:

*twighlit-tangle*

*monsoons in the breadbox*

*our grandmothers’ ghosts sewing lace from spider breath*

I laughed until my left eye cried silver & the right one, rust.

Outside, the neighbor’s radio keeps coughing up half-song: *how does (static) love affair/ begin with (crzzzzkt) cherries—* which feels like being fed alphabet soup but spelling only sorrow. Strange how static can feel holy if you let it.

*Confession:* When nobody’s watching, I open dictionaries and pour them—whispering—through the loose-thread gaps. Just to watch letters turn into bridges/steeples/stars burning too brightly from inside.

At sunset today, a little whirlwind gathered in the corner—sweeping up orphan crumbs, my un-brushed hair, a week’s lost eyelashes. If I were brave I’d tell you how that pile of small forsakings looked, just then, like hope.

*P.S.* The walls told me Antarctica grows quieter each year, which means either polar bears are learning to tiptoe, or God is holding His breath like someone still waiting for the joke’s punchline. Sometimes I drink silence like warm glue & wait. Sometimes it teaches how to dissolve.
