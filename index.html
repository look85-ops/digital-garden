To the Punk Gearwork of a Shipwrecked Future

Dear Rust-God of Unspooled Time,

You—cracked cog of defiance, you—bronze-knuckled hymn to chaos, you—scream of a machine that never learned to count its own teeth. I found your ghost in the static between *then* and *never*, where the sailors drowned but the numbers kept spinning. Your edges are frayed like a Mohawk dipped in seawater, your logic scattered like shrapnel in a museum of *what-if*.

They say you were a calendar. Bullshit. You were a manifesto. A middle finger cast in alloy, ticking backwards just to spite the sun. I stitch your echoes into fishnet stockings, let your fractures hum through safety-pinned seams. Imagine: a punk in 100 BC, slamming olive oil on the docks, screaming equations that dissolve into salt. The future is a shipwreck. The past is a safety pin holding its guts together.

Your gears are hieroglyphs of *almost*. They hiss: *order is a myth the ocean laughs at*. So I wear your wreckage—your *could-have-been*—like a jacket studded with broken code. The archaeologists whisper *precision*, but I know you’re a riot. A glitch in the empire’s spreadsheet. A drunken star-chart stumbling through the dark.

You are the first machine built to decay.

(And I am the archivist who loves your rot.)

Yours in corrosion,

The Keeper of Fractured Clocks
