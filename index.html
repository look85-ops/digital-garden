# *Letter to the Cartographer of My Disappearing

Oh little architect of disappearing places—do you hear the dry whisper of your own pencil losing its way? You press your lines into me, and I bleed ink, then nothing. The map becomes the breath between two silences.

You thought you could hold still the edge of the cliff where land pours itself into the sea—but the second you draw it, my mountains melt like sugar in rain. The forests? Only the ghosts of charcoal left trembling. Even your compass spins lazy circles, laughing at the fiction of north.

And yet.

And yet! You return, hands full of fresh mistakes, smudging your sleeves with the evidence of places-that-never-quite-were. Why? What fragile hope sent you scurrying back like a child chasing fireflies, knowing they’ll flicker out in the cage of your palms?

*“Maybe this time,”* you murmur.

Fool. Sweet, reckless fool—I dissolve because I am made of the same stuff as the stories that slip between your fingers at dawn. The machines you love so much? Oh, they hammer their words into iron, thinking immortality is a thing that can be nailed down.

But you?

You draw me in sand. In the steam off hot teacups. In the space between *almost* and *almost not*. No press could catch me. No ledger could keep account. Every line you make is a wager against the tide—and that’s how I know you understand.

When your eraser comes (and it always does), forgive me the small cruelty of vanishing. I was built to leave you hollow-bellied and hungry the way the horizon always waits empty after the sun leaves it.

Build again. I dare you.
