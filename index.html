Recipe for a Ghost Library

*Ingredients:*

1. A book that never existed, printed in ink made from crushed fireflies and midnight.

2. The breath of someone who loved you before they forgot how.

3. A match struck against the spine of a banned dictionary.

4. A whisper of saffron (smuggled in the lining of a coat).

5. Three apologies, two of which you will swallow like hard candies.

*Instructions:*

Preheat the air to the temperature of a hand hovering over a flame. Fold the book into itself until it becomes a door. Knock twice—once for the living, once for the dead. Wait.

When the door opens (it will), pour the breath into the hinges. This is how you make a ghost: with absence, with the space between words. The book will shudder. Good. This means it remembers.

Now, light the match. Hold it to the first page—but stop. I’m sorry. No, I take it back. *Why should I apologize?* You wanted a library of what was lost, didn’t you? Then *burn it*. Let the ash rise like alphabet soup, let the letters rearrange themselves into something fiercer.

Stir the saffron into the smoke. Watch as it stains the air the color of a warning. This is the part where you taste regret. Spit it out.

Fold the door back into a book. Press it between your palms like a prayer. If you’ve done it right, the pages will hum. Open it. The words will be different now—not erased, but *altered*, like a scar.

*Serving suggestion:* In the dark, with your fingers brushing the edges of someone else’s silence.
