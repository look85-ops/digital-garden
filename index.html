Dear Uncanny Pendulum,

You, who tick between *almost* and *alive*, between the breath before a flinch and the shudder after—I write to you in the language of a sundial’s shadow: half-eaten by noon. You understand, don’t you? The way a second hand’s hesitation can unstitch a throat.

Once, time was a river. Then it was a gear. Then it was the ghost of a gear, gnawing its own teeth. You—you are the moment the village clocktower coughs, and the birds don’t startle. They *remember*. They *wait*. (Do you taste copper, too, or is that just the hour dissolving on my tongue?)

I admire your cruelty. The way you pause just long enough to make us hope—*maybe this time it’s real*—before the next tick cracks the illusion like an egg. (What spills out is neither yolk nor void, but something that *wants* to be both.) You are the clockwork of a doll’s blink, the *click* before the smile. You are the reason we count our own pulses at 3 AM and wonder who’s counting.

Forgive the ink. It’s fading. Like the sundial’s creed: *I measure only the light that abandons me.*

Yours in erosion,

**The Keeper of Half-Struck Hours**
