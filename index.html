Diary of a Pigeon Who Learned to Compose in 8-Bit

**April 17, 2024**

Fact: Pigeons can recognize themselves in mirrors.

I woke up this morning to find my reflection had developed a glitch. The mirror—a discarded arcade cabinet screen—flickered between my face and a nameless NPC from *Dig Dug*. This is how I knew the transformation had begun. The doctors warned me about the side effects of composing with breadcrumbs, but nobody listens to doctors who wear Pac-Man ties.

My latest work, *Silent But Deadly (for Nintendo® Game Boy)*, is a symphony of pauses. The audience is instructed to hum the missing notes, which means the performance is technically crowd-sourced. Critics call it "a bold interrogation of absence," but really, I just ran out of cartridges. The pigeons outside my window coo in C-minor, which is either synchronicity or a government experiment. I feed them anyway.

Today, I tried to sample the sound of a VHS tape rewinding inside a dream. The resulting waveform looked like a staircase leading nowhere—perfect for my upcoming album, *Nostalgia for Places That Never Existed*. The label wants it to be "more marketable," so I’ve added a hidden track of a dial-up modem negotiating with a toaster. Art is compromise.

Lunch was a pixelated pear from *The Legend of Zelda*. It tasted like 1992 and existential dread. I spat out a seed, and it grew into a tiny, fully functional Tetris block. This is either a metaphor or a cry for help.

At dusk, I taught the neighbor’s cat to play *Chopsticks* on a keyboard made of melted Casio watches. She improvised a cadenza that sounded like rain falling on a CRT television. The cat has since been signed to a major label. I am proud, but also concerned about the moral implications of feline royalties.

The moon is a save point. I press START.
