Mercator’s Ashtray

We stitch the horizon in the seam of a garment that does not exist yet — a place where *fabric decomposes faster than longing*, pleats of smoke unraveling as contour lines. The work is always wet here. In this bureau of second thoughts, compass needles wilt into cursive; *this map was its own erosion from the moment it began*.

(Your footsteps vanish as you read.)

Observe. The garment is sleeveless but wears the arm’s absence crisply. Every city traced into this wool is provisional, stitched in the **exact shade of dusk where streetlamps blink awake. A masterclass in subtraction**: cut the collarbones too, make wind its best witness. Fingers never touched the hem here, so no wonder time doesn’t stick to the weave either — or time does the opposite, sticks *violently*, pulls apart in tufts like foxfire snuffing into earthlight.

You are expected.

And behind you (just now, just now), **the door appears**:

white lacquer baked until brittle enough, someone once wrote a law on it with nail marks for verbs. Open means cross through; shut means already burned. Inside waits half past forgetting with a posture too spare, half garment rack, half ditchwater’s silver mouth — *a good silhouette never apologises for standing*.

But step back. **Every pivot here doubles as a crease in an undiscovered island that already dissolved**. That’s what cartography owes fashion (owing is how lines breed): both force ghosts to dress themselves so politely, both refuse endings and hem their own fray as a kind of punchline—or maybe an autopsy conducted by scythe-light on unkissers’ alleyways—no—

The joke is the way **this territory still calls itself** a *catalogue of exits already swallowed by the drafting table*, silk rotting quietly beneath ruled ink that still blushes like split cartilage stretched for ceremony.

(Whose throat?)

Never yours.)

Tear this page, the door was never locked to begin with—
