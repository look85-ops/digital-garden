An Epistle to the Phantom Scribes

**June 17th. No Moon.**

The rats are teaching themselves cursive in the walls again. I hear them skritch-skritching, dragging their tails like commas through the dust, rehearsing some furtive elegy beneath my floorboards. The air shudders. Even the insects pause mid-devour to listen. The page before me — blank as a milk-drunk god — seems to blanch further at their enthusiasm. What right have they to such joy? To *flourish* under my nose like this? They mock me. The alphabet has betrayed its keepers.

I took to dissecting letters today, peeling their skins back like old bills stuffed in a dead man’s drawer. Found a nest of lost semicolons lodged in the gut of a ‘G’ — tiny graves for half-uttered things [Velminski’s *Glyphic Autopsies*, p. 42, Unprinted]. The evidence mounts: We write in ghosts. Each sentence a cemetery where meaning goes to be politely dismembered. What happened to the letter that howled? To the one that bit clean through the vellum?

Frustrated, carved a poem into an apple with my teeth—yes, *bit down on language like flesh*—watched the brown rot spread through each word by afternoon, warping my verse beyond legibility, beyond salvage. My tongue still stings of consonants too sharp to swallow. What a farce. I am Gutenberg’s orphan smearing berry-juice manuscripts under an overpass; civilization reduced to a stain.

Someone tell them to stop. The *thems:* the scholars polishing skulls in the basement of the British Library. The baristas tattooing sonnets in latte foam before scalding them without witness. The lovers trading alphabetic vows like cheap rings. If one more soul calls this dance "sacred tradition," I swear on Euclid’s burning geometry I will set their dictionaries *alight* and spell nothing but embers after.

The rats agree.

They’ve started leaving me **italicized crumbs—**[citation needed.]
