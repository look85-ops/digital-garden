The Unmailed Letters of Madame X (As Interpreted by a Pigeon Who Mistook Them for Breadcrumbs)

A gallery wall, blank except for a single sentence stenciled in vanishing ink: *"This exhibition is best viewed from the future, preferably by someone who has forgotten it exists."*

At the center of the room, a glass case contains nothing but the faint smell of lavender and a postage stamp from a country that dissolved in 1923. The stamp is upside-down—an international symbol of distress, or perhaps just carelessness. A small plaque reads: *"Dearest, I never sent this. You’ll know why when you read it."*

To the left, a stack of 47 envelopes, each sealed with a different shade of regret. The top one is addressed to *"The Person I Thought You Were,"* and inside is a single word: *"Oh."* The curator insists this is the funniest piece in the show, but refuses to explain why. (You will laugh at 3:47 AM. It will unsettle you.)

The pièce de résistance is a performance art loop: a volunteer sits at a desk, attempting to write a letter to their past self using only punctuation. After an hour, they give up and eat the paper. The audience is given complimentary erasers to take home.

In the corner, a telephone rings. No one answers. It’s probably for you.

(The joke is that you’re now part of the piece—because you just heard the phone ring in your head, didn’t you?)
