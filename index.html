The Recipe for the Disappearance of an Hour

Begin with a fact: The first mechanical clocks in medieval Europe did not indicate minutes. Their slow, ecclesiastical hands marked only the broad strokes of prayer, labor, twilight creeping inevitable. (Already, you notice distortion—those clock towers loomed heavy with menace and angel wings. History is half apocryphal weight.)

Then, the lie unfolds, a gluttonous script:

*Ingredients*

1. The last breath of sundown (unrecorded in archival ledgers but caught here deliberately, like a firefly in your cupped hands).

2. Two tablespoons of swallowed words from abbots arguing whether time flows from God’s mouth or only back toward His teeth.

3. An ounce of molten shadow—warmed from no particular furnace but scraped instead inside pockets too deep to call mistakes. A shadow can weigh things if whispered correctly.

4. A pocketwatch casing, meticulously sanded until its numerals vanish. Trace the blank face, then pause beneath where 7 should glare until you begin counting a phantom tick (essential—measure by absence).

*Technique*

Step ONE:

Pound the hour flat using the buttresses of a deserted cathedral for mortar, its noon bells already melted into war medals, useless things without voices but worn as relics, as penitence. (History, at ease for centuries until suddenly it is told to fold up its gown under something smaller.) Your hands ache under insistence of shape—let them spasm open then stiff to starved candelabras. Time thrives under silence.

Step TWO: Gape where you suspect direction lives like wet ink or wasps' flight over old fence-post. A pause without punctuation expands inside: call it minute enough.

Step THREE: Hold all motion at the spine with rigor, until your tongue blooms metallic as when clocks were iron weights pulling against monks’ song. Shed language where you stand until its remnants form a moat of dust motes through cathedral panes, those little golden excommunications drifting through, unhailed—

Step FOUR—and do this softly, without documentation:

Light the minute hand as incense. Then turn to an unsilenced bystander—one you are not sure exists but who now watches nonetheless—and offer the relic of now back into their emptied pockets.

*(Serve lukewarm among broken sundials, where ants already gather in secret convocation, stitching stolen hour-threads where grass will not tell.)*

A final axiom—or a confession. To digest an unmeasured pause, hunger must first be rendered illegible. Like this manuscript burning itself backward to keep apocalypse gentle as cathedral bells muffled beneath fog on water’s face. So: go missing—so wholly that not even clock oil stains the way free now?

The rest is rumor (it worked, it failed before waking).
