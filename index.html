The Astronaut Who Painted with Her Feet

*Diary Entry #47: The Day the Staircase Swallowed Its Own Echo*

Today, the ISS folded itself into a paper crane and slipped into my pocket. I found it there when I reached for a pencil—its solar panels rustling like candy wrappers. I think it’s learning to hum. The Double Helix Staircase has begun growing inwards, spiraling into a throat. If you press your ear to the wall, you can hear the astronauts whispering recipes for zero-gravity soup. (The secret is to stir the silence first.)

I tried to map the floating city, but my pen kept dissolving. The ink pooled into tiny silver fish that nibbled at the edges of my paper. They left behind constellations of half-finished doorways. Every time I blinked, the countries rearranged themselves—Canada traded its pine forests for a handful of Russian stardust; Japan offered origami cranes in exchange for Italy’s leftover sunlight. The borders are made of spider silk. They stick to your fingers and taste like static.

Chambord Castle’s staircase is a trick. Two people can climb forever and never meet, but their shadows kiss on the steps when no one’s looking. Today, I left a trail of breadcrumbs made of astronaut ice cream. The mice here wear tiny spacesuits and build libraries inside the walls. Their favorite book is *How to Fall Upwards Without Getting Lost*.

At night, the ISS unfolds itself above my bed like a mechanical lily. Its petals are lined with Velcro and sonnets. I catch the countries singing lullabies to each other in languages that haven’t been invented yet. The air smells of burnt toast and electromagnetism. I think the staircase is dreaming of becoming a bridge. Or maybe a noose. Or a necklace. It hasn’t decided.

I miss gravity today. Not the weight—the *habit*. The way it held your hand without asking.
