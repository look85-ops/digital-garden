The Cartographer’s Unblinking Ink

We map what won’t stay. Lines dissolve like salt in rain. Our atlas is full of erasures, coastlines that flirt with the page before diving off. To look is to wound—so we wield our gaze like a dull knife, cutting but never butchering. The horizon? A fever dream. Here, every silhouette is hungry for its shadow.

We demand:

— No more fixed points. Let cities drift like eyelashes on a sleeper’s cheek.

— Let the viewer starve in the frame. Their hunger is the true landmark.

— Paint the lighthouse *backward*: its light spills into unseen rooms, never the sea.

Power is a fable told with landmarks. But what if every compass needle bent toward the throat of the one who holds it? Who is watching the watchman's shadow writhe against an abandoned wall? (It dances to music he cannot hear.)

*(Reader: think of a mime trapped in a glass box… Now who holds the walls? Pause. Laugh alone—the mime does not answer—only repeats your silence back to you, a perfect counterweight.)*

The nameless insist: no one claims them. They open their mouths, releasing flocks of untranslated birds. *Here*, every river is guilty of remembering its path. *Here*, a silhouette kisses its own blankness and calls it home. Our pens exhale the same air they once swallowed from unfinished skies.

What survives the looking? Not the desert (it refuses to be parched for you). Not the cathedral (its echoes belong to the echo). Just the space between what vanishes and *oh*, your breath fogging the glass to smudge whatever sketch was just starting to mean something.

—Signed in shifting sands,

**The League of Disappearing Streets**

(*Founding Principle: All maps must be swallowed after reading.*)
