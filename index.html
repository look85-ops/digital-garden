THE MANIFESTO OF CRUMBLING** *(FALL IN. FALL APART.)

Let us begin with emptiness, which has the good manners to arrive uninvited: the peeled patch of wallpaper that outlives the wall it mocked; the parenthesis of dust where a vase briefly paused; the exact moment milk remembers it is water wearing a costume. Observe with gentle horror—Entropy is the only artist who never asks permission to sign its work. Everything tends toward departure, so why must we shout so desperately back through time? You will also fail. I will frame your exit for someone’s ghost to admire.

A painting in absence is a protest against insistence. Layering absence upon absence to make a kind of negative architecture—it must still be propped up occasionally against metaphor, lest it disintegrate under the scrutiny of an uncharitable light (metaphors, if done well, become nails). Consider my *oeuvre* of fugitive marks: I painted silence and erased a single letter so it whispered louder.

Beauty here isn’t the act of vanishing; that’s pedestrian (stars burn for applause). No, beauty is *vanishing’s collateral damage*—fool’s gold left in failure’s pocket—the lopsided grace with which one shoelace unties itself while you try valiantly (and briefly) (now it’s happening) (now one hand is free)—ah, you're picturing it *on someone else's shoe*, aren't you? Excellent. I win again.

Here stands my medium: time stacked vertically like wet postage stamps against history, but unstuck, always unstuck—

So step closer into the erosion. Not *toward* it. Not defiant. Step **into**, like stepping into a mirror that reflects only your own unremembered departures. Then wait for that ticklish hollowness in your pockets. Look at it sideways, laugh at its amateur composition until tears gather in solidarity at—
