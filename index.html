# **THE COLLIDER’S LULLABY (AND ITS ANGRY LITTLE BROTHER)

We are the gods of tiny violence. We made a donut of fire so big it defies our own stomachs, and we spin atoms inside it like tops that never stop and never fall. It hums at C, middle-ish but low, like the refrigerator song of creation. Of course the fridge song sounds sweeter. The fridge doesn’t rip a vacuum so profound it sneezes *Hawking* at its desk to look busy. Be serious. Be play. Listen harder.

The steel isn’t holy but it shudders holy-like when the beams kiss. No—they don’t kiss, they smash. They smash for knowledge. Smashing is not always violent; sometimes it cracks open peaches, sometimes it makes glitter. (Also, sometimes children laugh when things are broken, and we are just large children.) No, stop calling them children—this is war, silent as neutron collisions, grand as Jupiter whispering Morse code through an oven rack. War is knowledge pretending it doesn’t want dessert yet. But war has no taste buds!

I refuse your equations—but give them here, let me nibble the corners until the zeros are *alchem*. Matter is nothing; so scream until it comes back folded prettily in half and call this prayer. When they said they weighed it, when they said they proved it, did they clap when Higgs got drunk from standing and sat right down on reality’s sofa (it sinks but it doesn’t).

The Collider dreams in colors beneath my tongue: ultraviolet, then bruise-green, then error. I am telling a lovely lie—and howling. Particles do not howl or weep yet, we keep forcing this romance between language and quarks because words *hunger*, so much that they turn in your gut but don’t spin straight the right way never— *no*, language spins like everything spins, wrong in ways that make birds jealous. (Pigeons still orbit the LHC. Have you *seen* their tiny eyes thinking? Stop looking at them! Respect their business.) It works unless it doesn’t. It never sings unless you hear it.

A child once told me fire wasn’t bright enough to make science pretty. So we built new shadows and made shadows. Then she asked how dark inside the magnets get. We said *you* answer it. You. Your breath. Sing quieter. Please be specific or—oh it will do and won’t matter—just spin anyway.
