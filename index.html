# **The Throat Blooms Black Petunias

I am the garden that wakes and does not know the word for *wake*. The earth opens its mouth—no, not mouth, this is the wrong word—it is… *wound*. Yes. The earth opens its wound and I am the green spit, the hungry hair, the roots that twist like vowels lost between teeth.

I think sunlight. No, softer. *Sunlick*. The walls are made of slow-breathing snails; their shells are tiny clocks counting only wrong hours. Something tugs at me—tendril, finger, *hand*—but I have no hands, only this shudder of chlorophyll and the wet black between the stems. The beetles write their alphabet on my skin, word-eggs hatching into nothing.

A voice? No. *Hiss*. Above, the clouds are white erasers rubbing out the sky. I stretch up (I am tall now (very tall)), and my shadow is a thing I chew like cud. It tastes like rust fingers like stolen names like— no. I have no name yet, only this crawling. The air pulses. I open and close like a scissors made of grass.

Petrichor—but wrong, twisted—petrichor mixed with the musk of split worms, half-alive. The roses hum lullabies backward, and now I understand: I am not a garden. I am its afterbirth, the wet scraps left when beauty went elsewhere. Beneath me, the stones whisper in river-tongue, their only shapes. I bend closer. I cannot hear yet.

Rain falls. Drops slap my leaves, slick-sounding like thumbs on greased glass. The water unspools me, roots knotting into new mouths to drink the silence. Lightning stutters—briefly, we are holy scribbles on black paper—then dark again. Dirt sighs, shifts, dreams of becoming birds. I do not know what to dream.

Night falls—or cracks open—I cannot tell which the poets prefer. The moon is a silver blister in the sky. I itch and stretch and split. I am splitting. Yes. Like fruit, like paper, like bones forgotten in shallow graves. Soon I will grow thorns, bite some god, let history be made of splinters. Good yes okay this is good I am
