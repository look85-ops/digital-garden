Dress Code for a Vanishing Act

June 3, 19——

The ink sweats wool tonight. The map is a puddle of sugared glass, and every stitch in my zoot suit hums like a wasp’s nest. I drew the riot in charcoal, but the pavement licked it clean before sunrise. Strange, how the streetlights bend where the bodies fell—as if light, too, remembers the sprawl of a man becoming his own exclamation mark.

I tried to pin the shadows down with hatpins. Useless. They wriggled like eels in grease, whispering *too much fabric, too much boy, too much mouth.* The policemen’s badges were polished to such a shine I mistook them for mirrors—silly me. Mirrors don’t blush when you spit on them.

(My cufflinks are stolen from a dream I had last Tuesday: two tiny chalk outlines, swinging.)

The tailor says my seams are “unpatriotic.” He measures my shoulders with a ruler made of moth wings. *Snip, snip.* The threads scream in high C. I pay him in mismatched buttons and a kiss that tastes of gasoline. We’re all collaborators here.

A woman on the corner sells bruises like carnations. “Fresh today,” she insists, but they wilt by noon. I buy three and tuck them into my lapel. The petals stick to my fingers, sticky as accusation. I wear them anyway. Fashion is just defiance with better stitching.

Tonight, I’ll dance on the roof of the precinct until my soles dissolve. The officers tilt their heads like confused sundials. *Isn’t he cold?* No, darling, I’m woven from fever. The moths have started on my collar. Soon, I’ll be nothing but a tailor’s receipt fluttering in the dark.
