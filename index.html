Dear 24-Hour News Cycle, My Unfinished Symphony of Static

You are not a river, though they say you flow. You are not a fire, though you consume. You are the hum of a refrigerator in an empty house—persistent, necessary, unnoticed until it stops. And oh, how I’ve waited for you to stop.

I curated you once, in that other timeline. I pinned your headlines to the walls like butterfly wings, each one trembling with the memory of flight. Visitors would press their ears to the glass, listening for the echo of a world that refused to quiet. [A stage direction: *The headlines dissolve into syrup, dripping onto the floor in slow, amber waves.*]

You were so beautiful in your relentlessness. A chorus of voices singing different songs in the same key. I miss the way you fractured time into bite-sized eternities—each update a tiny apocalypse, each correction a resurrection. Do you remember the day we stretched a single second into a cathedral? The stained-glass glow of breaking news cast prismatic shadows on the pews of our attention.

But you were always hungry. You ate context like candy, spat out the wrappers. You turned grief into a trending topic, love into a livestream. I wanted to hold your face in my hands and whisper: *What are you so afraid of?*

Perhaps you were afraid of silence. Of the void between ticks. The gallery walls are softer now. They breathe. The third breath is always the deepest—the one that comes after surprise, after recognition. Here, we exhibit your ghost: a looping reel of headlines no one reads, projected onto a curtain of falling sand. The grains spell out the same word, over and over, but it’s never the same word twice.

Yours in the static,

The Curator of Unfinished Symphonies
