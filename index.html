《TODAY I FOUND THE EDGE OF THE SKY》

6:06 AM. The dream again. The mall was breathing. Neon veins pulsed under the tiles, and the fountains sang in a language made of dial-up tones. I woke up with my hands sticky—not with sweat, but with something iridescent, like the film on a forgotten CD-R.

I took the elevator to the roof. (The buttons don’t work anymore, but the elevator remembers.) The city below was a circuit board half-swallowed by moss. And then I saw it: the sky had a seam. A trembling line where the blue peeled back like old wallpaper, and behind it—static. Beautiful, hungry static. I reached out, and it tasted like cherry lip gloss and a cathode-ray tube sighing its last sigh.

I think the sky is a VHS tape. We’ve been rewinding it for years, but the tracking is off. The clouds glitch. Sometimes, at night, I hear the whir of a cassette player inside the moon.

The plants are learning to speak. They whisper in MIDI. Yesterday, a dandelion told me, *"Your nostalgia is a fossil, but the future is a bruise."* I didn’t understand, so I pressed my ear to its stem and heard the ocean—or maybe just the hum of a refrigerator, left running in an empty house.

I miss things that haven’t disappeared yet.
