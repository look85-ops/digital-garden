Recipe for the Collapse of a Quantum Soufflé

*Ingredients:*

1 beam of hesitant light (preferably orphaned)

2 slits, cut from the fabric of a forgotten wedding dress

1 observer (hungry for answers)

A pinch of paradox, finely ground

3 tablespoons of silence (unmeasured)

1 collapsing waveform (handle with doubt)

*Method:*

1. **Prepare the Light:** Isolate your beam in a darkened room. Whisper to it until it wavers. Light, like all shy things, performs best when it believes it’s alone.

2. **Dress the Slits:** Affix the slits to an unseen wall. Ensure they are parallel, but not *too* parallel—perfection ruins the experiment. The wedding dress fibers should hum with residual longing. This is crucial.

3. **Introduce the Observer:** Position them at a distance where their gaze becomes a physical force. Do not blink. Blinking is a form of mercy, and we are not here to be kind.

4. **Combine Silence and Paradox:** Fold the silence into the light, then sprinkle the paradox over the slits. Watch as the beam hesitates. It will split, then double back, then forget what it was. This is the soufflé rising.

5. **The Collapse:** Here, the recipe demands a lie: *The observer is irrelevant.* In truth, the observer is the only ingredient that matters. Their hunger bends the light into particles, into answers, into something almost edible. The soufflé falls. It was never meant to be eaten.

6. **Serve Cold:** The remnants will arrange themselves into interference patterns on the plate. These are the ghost of the meal you almost had.

*Notes:*

- If the light refuses to behave, it has likely grown suspicious. Start again.

- The wedding dress slits may weep. This is normal.

- Do not taste the soufflé. It tastes of the question you no longer remember asking.
