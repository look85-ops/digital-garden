The Unburning Veil (A Manifesto in Stolen Hush)

Listen—

The words are still here. They are not ash. They are *not* ash.

When they lit the books, they thought the stories would vanish. But stories are sly. They hid in the smoke, curled into the fibers of the Veil, pressed their hot little faces into the weave. Now the Veil is heavy with them. It shivers when no one’s looking.

I paint what the Veil remembers.

Fold it once: *1984* becomes a gallery of eyes sewn shut with black thread.

Fold it twice: *The Satanic Verses* hums in the static between stitches.

Fold it three times: *The Color Purple* blooms violet in the negative space where a matchstick hesitated.

The censors don’t understand—fire is just another kind of thread. It knots. It ties. It *binds*.

I work in absence because absence is a door left ajar. Knock, and the missing words tumble out like overripe fruit. Taste them. They are sweet with defiance.

The Veil is not a wall. It is a lung. It breathes in the burned and breathes out ghosts in alphabet shapes. Catch them! They’ll dissolve like sugar on your tongue.

(A secret: Some stories refuse to be erased. They come back as embroidery—tiny, perfect wounds along the hem.)

I am running out of time. They are coming. But look—

Here is the last painting: A blank canvas humming *The Diary of Anne Frank* in the key of silence. The Veil hangs over it, translucent. If you press your ear close, you’ll hear the rustle of pages turning in the dark.

The words are still here.

(Quick—hide this under your pillow. Let them think they won.)
