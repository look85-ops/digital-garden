THE MUSEUM OF ABSENT SIGHS

We do not paint with light but with its withdrawal. Not with pigment but with the tremor in the hand that refuses to lift the brush. Our gallery is the negative space between a held breath and its release—the hollow where meaning collapses into awe.

Art is not made. It is *unmade*: a subtraction so precise it leaves a scar that glows. Duchamp knew this when he hung the urinal like a relic, but he missed the deeper heresy: the urinal was never the art. The art was the collective gasp, the shudder of *what if*, the body’s rebellion against the mind’s dare. We trade in that shudder. Our medium is the moment before the mind catches up—the placebo’s sly grin.

(Here, a figure stumbles into the manifesto: a librarian with a paper cut, clutching a overdue copy of *The Tibetan Book of the Dead* folded into a paper airplane. She whispers, *"The Dewey Decimal System is a conspiracy to hide the gaps between numbers."* We let her stay. She belongs here more than we do.)

To make art now is to curate absence. A canvas untouched but *charged* with the ghost of a gesture. A gallery where the walls are lined with frames holding only the pressure of what once was—or what might have been. The placebo effect is our patron saint: belief so fierce it rewrites flesh. If a sugar pill can mend a wound, then a blank wall can be a cathedral. A pause can be a revolution.

We propose a new readymade: the *unseen* readymade. Not the object, but the space around it. Not the urinal, but the hesitation before naming it art. Not the pill, but the pulse that quickens *because you believe it will*.

Our manifesto is a rumor passed mouth to ear until it becomes a fact. Our museum is built from the sighs of visitors who swear they saw something move in the empty room.

Art is the wound where the world rubs against itself and calls it holy.
