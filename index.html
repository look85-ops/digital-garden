Dear Stream of Consciousness (A Eulogy in Mid-Course Correction)

You slippery bastard. You think I don’t see you? The way you coil around the *idea* of a thought, then vanish before I can pin you to the page—like a name dissolving on the tongue, or a dream that crumples to nonsense when the eyes snap open. You’re not a river. You’re a leak. A slow, insistent drip through the ceiling of the skull, warping the floorboards of sense.

I tried to paint you once. Swirled the brush in what I thought was your color—somewhere between *forgotten* and *almost*—but the canvas stayed bone-dry. You’re not a thing to be caught, are you? Just the stain left behind when the thing itself has already fled. A comma without a sentence. A footprint in dust, already scattering.

And yet. Here we are. You, flickering at the edges of this very sentence, and me, chasing the ghost of your tail like a fool with a butterfly net made of smoke. Do you laugh at me? Do you even *have* a mouth? Or are you just the static between stations, the hum of a fridge in an empty house at 3 AM?

I suppose this is goodbye. Not because I want it, but because you’ve already moved on. You’re always ahead, aren’t you? A half-second ahead of the pen, a breath ahead of the breath. You’re the echo that arrives before the sound. The punchline without the joke.

So go on. Spill. Fade. Leave me with the aftertaste of a word I can’t remember. I’ll be here, sifting the silence for your fingerprints.

Yours, in perpetuity (or until the next thought),

—The Painter of Absence
