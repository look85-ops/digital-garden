Safety Pins Stitching the Sky

The mannequins in the window have no arms—*perfect*—so we dress them in our rage instead.

Ripped stockings, frayed at the thigh like a surrender flag dipped in gasoline.

The tailor across the street sews his mouth shut with dental floss, *professionalism*,

but we’re busy stapling our manifesto to the wind.

*Look*, the wind says, *I can’t hold this forever.*

The zipper on my jacket gets stuck halfway to heaven.

A deliberate malfunction. A *fuck you* to ascent.

I leave it there—metal teeth biting at the air—

because perfection is just fascism in a nicer coat.

We tried to burn the dress code, but the flames were too polite.

So we wore the ashes.

Ash-gloves, ash-ties, ash-heels clicking down the sidewalk

like a metronome set to the rhythm of a collapsing bridge.

The museum curator calls it *aesthetic decay*.

We call it Tuesday.

At midnight, we glue feathers to the riot shields.

The cops arrive, bewildered, picking at the plumage.

One of them starts crying—*it’s just so beautiful*—

and we all agree to pretend it didn’t happen.

The sewing machine jams.

Thread snarled like a last nerve.

We could fix it, but why?

The best stitches are the ones that unravel.
