The Seawater Conspiracy of Saint Brendan’s Filing Cabinet

They built it backward. Not the canal—the *hypothesis*. First, they dredged a wound, then called it a nation. The whole thing hinges on volumetric theology: what is the shape of a stolen ocean? You could say it’s a lock-step penitentiary for libations, a probationary maze for Leviathan’s conscience. Salt licks both walls.

But consider the inverse liturgy: what if the canal was always there, latent, and the engineers merely *unforgot* it? A scar remembers its knife. A debt remembers its hand. The water remembers nothing, which is why it’s perfect.

At the scandalous midpoint of this reverie, a minor character surfaces, clutching a rusted fire escape. It’s Gary from Accounts Receivable. He’s wearing an albatross necktie and muttering about postdated cheques. “I *told* them,” he insists, “you can’t invoice a metaphor.” He slides a spreadsheet across the brine—evidence that God’s liquidity ratios are suspect.

They’ll pension him off with a conch-shell severance. It plays a miser’s tango if held to the ear.

The canal is bored. Bored of being a miracle, bored of evaporating prayers, bored of syncopated nations tugging at its tide. It wants to unbecome—to spit back the 14,000 ships it swallowed like aspirin. Or perhaps dissolve entirely, reverting to its original blueprint: a rumor, a stutter between estuaries. A skip in the record titled *Colonialism*.

Gary misses his stapler. The ocean misses its density. The typist misses the categorical violence of the period key. Everyone is grieving an omission.
