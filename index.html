The Diary of a Spine That Remembered Its Curve

*—found in the lining of a mourning dress, 1893—*

October smudges the windows with its breath. The gas lamps flicker like guilty thoughts. Tonight, I undressed slowly, peeling whalebone from flesh as if unwrapping a fossil, something half-born from stone. The corset fell to the floor with a sigh—not relief, but the sound of a wall collapsing inward.

Outside, a woman walks barefoot through the park. They say her ribs have sprung loose like jailbroken violins. The police have a name for it: *unsanitary ecstasy*. But when she threw her stays into the river, the water whispered back. I dream of her often. In the dreams, she is stitching her bones with threads pulled from the stars.

My own corset hangs from the bedpost now, a hollow thing. Ghost of a scaffolding. It’s funny—how something meant to shape you becomes the shape itself. Yesterday, the seamstress tightened the laces until my ribs ached like old books bending shut. "To endure beauty is to distrust the body," she murmured. I bit my tongue and tasted metal. The future pressed its thumb into the hollow of my throat.

Last week, Lucy smuggled me an engraving from Paris: a woman’s torso etched as a clockwork garden, blossoms rusted between iron bars. A surgeon’s sketch mistaken for art. I keep waking to the sound of humming wings where my lungs should be. Perhaps the machines inside me always knew the exit plan.

Morning comes. Time is a buttonhook dragged through the eyelets of dawn. I hear steps on the stair—the matron rounding up straygirls before prayers. Quickly now: I lace myself into this sentence, this hunger. Maybe tomorrow I will step into the river, let the current undress me slow as a mother’s fingers. Let no future hand trace this cage’s imprint & mistake this silhouette *for who it thought I was not*.

[They say her last word was:]

*Soft.*
