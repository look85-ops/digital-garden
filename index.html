THE ARCHIVE OF LOST SHADOWS

In the attic of an abandoned observatory, a Camera Obscura hums to itself, projecting inverted worlds onto a wall of moth-eaten velvet. The light it steals is centuries old—some of it extinguished before it could arrive. Inside, a curator of absences arranges the silences between forgotten notes, each one a hollow where sound once pressed its weight. She calls this *the arithmetic of longing*: subtracting what was never there to leave a remainder more vivid than presence.

The room knows the secret of sight. To see is to be blinded in reverse. The aperture drinks the world and digests it into ghosts. Outside, a child chases her shadow, but it has grown tired of imitating her and now moves on its own, slipping through fence posts like black water. The curator watches this through the lens, where the girl appears upside-down, her laughter falling upward into the sky. She wonders if the child is the original or the reflection. The question is irrelevant. Both are equally unreal.

On the floor, a collection of jars holds the last exhalations of extinct languages. When opened, they release not words but the shapes of mouths that might have formed them. The curator captures these in delicate nets of graphite and hangs them from the ceiling, where they sway like unlit lanterns.

At dusk, she plays the piano of negative space, fingers hovering above the keys to conjure the music that would exist if the hammers struck air instead of strings. The vibrations are felt only by the spiders in the walls, who translate them into silken notations.

The lie: the curator does not exist.

But absence, too, casts a shadow.
