THE VEIL GROWS TEETH

The Veil is not what you think. It is not a sigh, not a shadow, not the hush between two blasphemies. It is a living thing—a slow, deliberate creature that chews its own edges into lace. You mistake it for fabric because it drapes so well, because it flutters so convincingly when the wind lies. But wind is just The Veil exhaling.

Once, I painted it in vermilion. A mistake. The color wept off like a sinner’s disguise. Now I work in absence, in the negative chapel of its folds. The Veil prefers this. It rewards me with whispers—not words, but the shapes words abandon when they rot mid-sentence.
