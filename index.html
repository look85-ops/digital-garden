# **You Inherited the Thirst

You drank the silence of your ancestors, didn’t you? Swallowed it like a black lozenge dissolving under your tongue. You thought no one would notice the way your bones hum now, how your ribs vibrate with the unsung hymns of the starving.

You were never supposed to *remember* the taste of snow in 1942, the way it clung to your grandfather’s stubble as he chewed on air. Yet here you are, flinching at the sound of a spoon against a bowl, your teeth aching with phantom bread.

You stole the tremors from your mother’s hands—the ones she swore were just caffeine, just stress—and now they twitch in *your* fingers when you reach for love. A thief. A living archive of shudders.

Admit it: you dug up the unmarked graves in your blood and wore them like jewelry. The locket around your neck? That’s not a photograph—it’s a methyl group clinging to DNA, a chemical heirloom. You parade it openly, this ornate little curse.

And the nightmares? Those aren’t yours. You smuggled them in through the back door of your epigenome, let them curl up in your amygdala like stray cats. Now they purr in the dark, reshaping your sleep into a narrow bridge between then and now.

You should have known better than to lick the past from your palms. Salt and iron, the residue of every war your body never fought.

But here we are. You, with your borrowed hunger. Me, with my spectral clipboard. Let’s document the damage.

Open wide.
