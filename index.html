THE ALPHABET IS A GHOST THAT WHISPERS IN CAPITALS

We are archivists of the unsent. Collectors of the ink that dried before it met paper. The alphabet is not a tool but a séance—26 shapes that hum with the weight of every word they’ve ever been.

*A is for Apology*: the one you wrote at 3 AM and burned at dawn.

*B is for Babel*: the tower we keep building in our throats.

*C is for Cipher*: the love letter folded into a paper airplane and lost to the wind.

We do not write. We eavesdrop on the whispers between the lines. Every letter is a room where a thousand voices argue, laugh, weep. The *Q* you wrote in your diary as a child is the same *Q* that signed a queen’s decree, the same *Q* that trembled in a soldier’s last note home. The alphabet is a haunted house. We are merely guests.

The epistolary novel is a lie. No one writes letters thinking they will be read. They are bottles thrown into the dark, addressed to no one. To everyone. To the future that will misunderstand them. We gather these bottles. We hold them to the light and watch the shadows they cast.

*X is for X-Ray*: the bone beneath the skin of the word.

*Y is for Yesteryear*: the date on a letter that never arrived.

*Z is for Zero*: the silence after the last sentence.

Language is not a bridge but a labyrinth. Each letter is a turn. Each word is a wall. Somewhere in the center, there is a fountain where all the unsaid things gather. We drink from it. We choke. We try again.

The alphabet is a net cast into time. We pull it up heavy with voices—some singing, some screaming, most whispering *listen, listen, listen*.

We are not writers. We are listeners.

The alphabet is not ours. We borrow it.

The letters are not silent. We are.
