THE MANIFESTO OF LEAKING EDGES

We are the residue of what was never meant to be recorded. The ink that bleeds through the page, the whisper that lingers after the throat has closed. We are the error margins—the trembling space between what is said and what is heard.

Listen: the future is not a destination. It is a commons. A shared pasture of unread letters, of time capsules buried in the wrong soil. We tend to it not as archivists but as gardeners, planting words that will rot before they are spoken.

Maria Sibylla Merian, I dreamt of you last night. You were sketching moths on the back of a receipt, their wings dissolving into the ledger’s grid. You laughed when I called you a scientist. “I am only a witness,” you said. “The truth is in the decay.”

We reject the tyranny of permanence. Let the message fray. Let the paper soften into pulp, let the ink blur into a stain that could mean anything. The Chapel of Perpetual Departure has no doors—only thresholds, only the sound of footsteps fading into the next century.

Here is our covenant:

1. **Write in the language of spillage.** Let your words slip, let them pool in the cracks.

2. **Address the dead as if they are listening.** They are. (They are not.)

3. **Build libraries where every book is a palimpsest.** Let the past overwrite itself until it becomes a kind of weather.

4. **Trust the unreadable.** The future does not need your clarity. It needs your ghosts.

We are not translators but trespassers. We steal meaning back from the vault of history, only to scatter it like seeds too light to take root.

When the last speaker of our language is gone, the wind will still hum it, badly, in the eaves of abandoned buildings. That will be enough.
