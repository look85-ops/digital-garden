Dear Epigenetic Alphabet (or How Your Grandmother’s Sighs Still Live in My Semicolons),

Let’s begin with the obvious: you are an overachiever. Two millennia of human noise pressed into 26 tidy glyphs, and still you insist on whispering through my ribcage like a forgotten lullaby. I resent you for this. I resent the way your serifs curl like ancestral trauma, the way your vowels hum with the weight of unspoken harvests. You are a typographical haunting. A font of inherited tremors.

But let’s be honest—this letter is already a failure. I promised boldness, and here I am, writing to a concept as if it might write back. (Though if you *do*, use Courier New. It’s only fair.)

Forgive me. No—scratch that. *Retracted with prejudice.* Apologies are for those who believe time moves in one direction. You and I know better. You are a spiral. A palimpsest written in methyl groups. A library where every book is also a ghost.

Consider the letter *H*. Two pillars and a bridge. A ladder, or a pair of stilts over a chasm. Now imagine it’s 1942, and your grandfather is carving it into a barracks wall. The shape is the same. The silence is not.

I suppose what I’m trying to say is: you are not a tool. You are a *symptom*. A collective tremor passed down through hands that never touched. The way a famine curls inside a grandson’s sweet tooth. The way a scream can fossilize into an umlaut.

Yours in reluctant awe,

A Composer of Unplayed Silences

P.S. If this letter feels uneven, blame your parents. Or theirs. Or the fact that language is just epigenetics with better PR.
