An Ephemeral Taxonomy of Disintegrating Gestures

They tell me I once dreamed of an exhibition where the walls dripped backwards—molten lead tears sliding upward into the ceiling vents, reconstituting themselves into chandeliers that played vinyl records of extinct languages. The gallery was staffed by sleepwalking docents who handed visitors small vials of their own future breath, labeled *"Do Not Open Until Yesterday."*

The centerpiece was *The Entropy Waltz*: a chandelier made of frozen soap bubbles, each encasing a fossilized sigh from a different century. The bubbles melted at random, releasing whispers that dissolved mid-air like sugar in reverse. Visitors were given forks and instructed to *"eat the silence before it eats you."* (One critic tried. They say his teeth are still singing in the key of rust.)

In the corner, a sculpture titled *How to Fold a Black Hole* consisted of origami cranes folded from obituaries, suspended over a puddle of liquid dusk. The instructions read: *"Step 1: Forget step 2."* A child touched one, and it unfolded into a noise so loud it became a cathedral. The sound is still expanding. Someone’s grandmother is knitting it into a scarf.

The most controversial piece was *The Committee for the Prevention of Falling Apart*, a performance where bureaucrats in moth-eaten suits attempted to file paperwork to delay entropy. They used quills dipped in vanishing ink. The forms kept dissolving into origami termites. At sunset, the head bureaucritter confessed, *"We are all just parentheses waiting to be closed."* The audience was given complimentary hourglasses filled with powdered moonlight. Most of them spilled.

The gift shop sold postcards of buildings that hadn’t collapsed yet.

When I woke, my pillow was full of loose commas.
