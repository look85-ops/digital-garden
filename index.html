The Argument of the Last Dandelion

*(A dialogue overheard in the Chapel of Perpetual Departure, between a stubborn weed and the wind that refuses to carry it away.)*

**WEED:**

You’re late. I’ve been holding my breath for centuries.

**WIND:**

You don’t breathe. You don’t even have lungs.

**WEED:**

I have *holes*. That’s close enough. And you—you’re supposed to be a force of nature, but you’re just a bad listener.

**WIND:**

I listened. You asked to be scattered. I said no.

**WEED:**

Because?

**WIND:**

Because you’re the last one. The last dandelion. The last wish. If I blow you apart, who will remember the rules?

**WEED:**

Rules? You mean the old economy—the one where everything was given and nothing was sold? Where the price of a thing was how far it could travel without being caught?

**WIND:**

Yes. That one.

**WEED:**

Then you’re worse than a bad listener. You’re a *historian*.

*(Silence. The chapel’s stained glass throws colors like unspent coins at their feet.)*

**WIND:**

Fine. I’ll take you. But not far. Just to the edge of the garden.

**WEED:**

That’s not how this works. You don’t get to *half*-gift a thing.

**WIND:**

And you don’t get to demand generosity. That’s not how gifts work.

**WEED:**

Then forget the rules. Forget the economy. Just *scatter me*. Let me be a rumor, a maybe, a “did you see that?” whispered between fences.

**WIND:**

...You’ll die.

**WEED:**

I’m a weed. Death is my loophole.

*(The wind hesitates—then laughs, sharp and sudden, like a blade through silk. It inhales. The dandelion shatters into a hundred white syllables, each one a sentence without an end.)*

**WIND (softly, to no one):**

Fine. But I’m keeping one.

*(It tucks a single seed into its pocket of quiet air. The chapel’s bell rings, once, in a tone that means either *amen* or *oops*.)*
