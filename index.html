The Dust That Dreams

Look. Look closer. No, closer still. See that? Floating? Not nothing. Dust. Ordinary dust. Kitchen-counter dust. Attic-forgotten dust. Dust that slept on library books no one opened. But *look*. Sunlight hits just so. Suddenly: galaxies. Each speck a tiny, trembling universe. Swirling. Dancing. Alive with impossible light.

I collect them. Not the dust. The moments. The instant before the sneeze. The breath held when the sunbeam lands just right. That exact slant of afternoon that turns motes into magic. I bottle them. Label them. Place them gently in my Archive of Almost-Art.

Some bottles hold the dust that remembers being stardust. Some hold the dust that sighed when the sculptor’s chisel *almost* touched the marble block. One holds the dust that fell from the moth’s wing as it hovered near the unlit lamp – the lamp meant for the masterpiece that wasn’t painted. That dust hums a faint, silvery tune, like an unwritten symphony.

Is it art? Duchamp said a urinal could be, if you *said* it was. But what about the dust *before* it landed on the urinal? The dust that drifted past the window while he was thinking about it? Is *that* art? Or just potential? A possibility hovering, waiting for a gaze? My Archive whispers: yes. Maybe. The art is in the noticing. The wonder-shock. The gasp when the ordinary becomes... *more*.

This dust? In Bottle #734? It drifted down from the ceiling of the train station where the poet *almost* met the love who *almost* wrote the poem that *almost* changed the world. Catch it in the right light? You’ll see faint, shimmering letters forming words that vanish before you read them. Words like *what if* and *almost* and *maybe next time*. They taste like static electricity and forgotten birthdays.
