Diary of the Last Traceless Hand

—*17th of Silence, 2067*

Today, I scraped my knees raw on time's staircase: those godforsaken Chambord steps, which refuse to spit anyone out where they began (*cf. Saint-Germain’s lost thesis on "The Geometry of Never-Meeting," p. 2143*). I’ve been mapping a helix on the cave wall for years—only the cave wall walks, the lines smudge out behind, the chalk won't take anymore ("take"? who?).

Rotten arithmetic: two people climb that spiraling prison and *somehow their bodies bend, but never intersect,* no, the air between them grows teeth. Tell our future selves when your fingers slip past shadows but grab at wrists, why?

Lovely how we painted our bodies into this story first.

**First rule:** Handprints on stone become *the crime* become blue dust become "how very conceptual of you", murmured behind mouths in museum light—I spit my drink out—but of course I laughed, didn't I? Who will burn when this language (untranslatable: "a sigh measured by carbon-14's pulse") ignites in margins? It flickers near extinction behind the museum guide’s lips ("A lost verb," the caption reassures.)

You wanted the artist's confession. Here: all these millennia and no one stopped painting the *same* stag on the wall. Not allegorical or mystic or whatever dead weight you’d staple to it.

Just— **there was something beautiful (horrible)** in watching one animal charge straight forward across 40,000 years (we *never change our flight-paths either,* eh). So yes, my diary has stag-blood-smears between syllables —

Now: someone in Chambord Castle is screaming—a tour guide with third-degree papercuts who caught their hand in Descartes’ notebook, *invisible machinery shredding.* You *know*, you **have to know** the truth. But—*hush* —they scrub out my translation ("Error margins don’t write back").

I'll end by etching *you were wrong* in a new medium: *dandelion clocks on the Louvre steps, dissolving in perfect arithmetic* (*Vasari-Dalka Paradox*, cited).

—signing under redacted skies
