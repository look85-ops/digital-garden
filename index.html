Fragments for a Cartographer of Disappearing Places

Dear Fairy Tale,

You liar. You god. You swallowed bone, you knot of fingers in the dark. Did you think I wouldn’t notice the way you dissolve under scrutiny? Every map I draw of you fades before the ink dries, every forest I chart is already growing thorns through my palms.

They call you *instruction*, call you *morality*, but we know the truth—you are the sweetness laced with foxglove, the well that reflects no face. A child’s voice reciting you is a spell; the same words in an adult mouth are a confession.

Remember the rules: If you stray from the path, you deserve what finds you. If you are kind to the crone, you will be rewarded. (Unless you are not.) If you open the door, you will be punished. (Unless you are not.)

You are full of teeth you pretend are pearls.

I have tried to chart your borders, but the compass always spins. The cottage moves when I’m not looking. The river flows backward in my sleep. You are the only country where the wolves are both kind and hungry—where the princess is rescued *by* the tower, not from it.

What I mean is: You were never meant to be safe.

Yours in ambivalence,

The Cartographer Who Bleeds Ink

(P.S. Burn this letter. You know how the story goes.)
