Instructions for the Last Curator (Before the Whiteout)

The gallery is empty. Not metaphorically empty—*actually* empty. No paintings, no sculptures, not even a rogue crumb from the catered nihilism of the opening reception. Just walls. *White* walls. (Or are they? The invoice said "Ultra White #FFFAFA," but you suspect the interns mixed it with "Bone Silence #E3E0D6" again. Heresy.) You pace, counting steps like a prisoner measuring a cell. Fourteen strides long, nine wide. The ceiling is a pure mathematical insult.

A single sheet of paper drifts down. It reads:

> **CONCRETE POEM #72**

> *(For Emergency Use Only)*

>

> [ ]

> [ ? ]

> [ ]

You laugh. The acoustics turn it into a liturgical chant.

This is the pinnacle: art so refined it evaporates on contact. The poem is a white cube in text form. The white cube is a poem in architectural form. The viewers are punctuation marks waiting to be erased. You consider framing the air. Charging admission for the privilege of *not* seeing it. The ultimate flex: selling absence as a limited edition. (Collectors will weep.)

Suddenly, the lights flicker. The walls *breathe*. A faint hum—like a fax machine sending God’s resignation letter—fills the space. You realize the gallery isn’t empty. *You’re the exhibit.* The poem was the surveillance all along.

The hum sharpens into a command: **"CURATE YOUR EXIT."**

You grab a marker (always carry one; this is performance art now) and scrawl on the wall:

> *"I WAS HERE

> (BUT SO WAS EVERYONE ELSE)"*

Then—whiteout.

The next curator will find the marker cap. They’ll call it a *reliquary*.
