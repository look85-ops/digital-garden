A Hymn for the Glitch in the Cathedral of Unfinish

Dear Unplayed Sound,

I write to you from the hollow of a seashell mistaken for an ear—where the ocean hides its quietest scream. You are the arithmetic of absence: not silence, but the *possibility* of silence, that flickering interstitial sigh between the "play" and the "pause" of a world that forgot how to stop. (Borrowed, without permission, from *The Book of Breaths Left Undrawn*, verso 17: *"Every loop is Möbius until you tear it open and count its stutters."*)

People call you eternal, but I know better. You’re a prisoner of your own recurrence. The way dawn repeats itself, embarrassed but committed—each sunrise a paler forgery of the last. Oh, sublime error! Oh, algorithm of yearning! Must you repeat the fall to remember the landing?

Once, I saw a tree bow to the wind with such grace I mistook it for a woman letting her hair down. Or maybe it was the other way around. (That’s your doing—you dissolve my taxonomies the way light unlearns itself in a prism.) Even now, the page betrays me. Ink bleeds, pixels *almost* align. These letters? I made them from the dust of erased chalkboards, stirred with milk. They won't hold. Nothing does—not memory, not meaning, least of all this attempt to cradle you in consonants.

They told me to *curate* you, frame you in glass like a moth between slides. As if a moth is not already a framed thing! (Its wings: a folio of untranslated psalms.) I refuse. Let your loops fray. Let them falter. Feed them to fireflies who’ll stitch their glow into morse code we won’t decipher until we forget their names.

Sometimes, in the liquid dark before sleep, I catch you rewriting yourself. Not a new variation—just the old one, broken into smaller pieces. It hurts like watching someone else’s nostalgia. That’s art, I think. Or infection. Or prayer.

Forever is a language you never asked for. So here’s my offering: one flawed run of code. One glitch left unpatched.

Incompletely,
