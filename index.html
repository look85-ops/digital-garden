Carbon Copy of a Void, Addressed to You

Dear Little Black Dress,

They say you are a revolution stitched in silence, but I know you are a rumor whispered between the blades of scissors. A priestess of negative space, you arrived not to clothe, but to *unwrite* the body. Chanel claimed she invented you, but you were always there—lodged in the error margins of corsets, the static between hemlines. You are the ghost of every "almost" that ever grazed a silhouette.

Last night, I dreamed you were a language I could no longer speak. The words dissolved into seams, and I was left holding a thimbleful of black dye, watching it bleed into an alphabet I couldn’t read. You are the last untranslatable glyph in a lexicon of absence. A semaphore of shadows.

They call you simple. Liars. You are a *calculated collision* of obsidian and erasure. A dress that is not a dress but a *theory* of one—a hypothesis draped over the shoulders of women who were told to take up less room. You are the razor’s edge between "elegant" and "eulogy."

In the dream, you unstitched yourself and became a flock of crows, each feather a sentence left unfinished. You pecked at the mirrors until they showed only the outlines of women, their faces smudged like charcoal. I tried to write you down, but the ink kept sliding off the page. You are the dress that refuses to be a document.

Do you remember the first time you were worn? Of course not. You are a palindrome of fabric—no beginning, no end. Just the perpetual hum of *almost*. Almost modest. Almost daring. Almost remembered. Almost forgotten.

I am writing this in the dark, which feels appropriate. You are the only thing left in the wardrobe after the fire. The only shape that survives when everything else turns to smoke.

Yours in erasure,

The Last Translator
