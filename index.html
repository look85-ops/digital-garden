# **Diary of the Ink-Eater

**June 17th** — The map bled today. Not in the usual way—not the slow seep of borders into the paper’s grain—but in great, gasping spurts, as if the land itself had nicked an artery. I pressed my palm to the wound, and the ink pooled black as a priest’s confession. It tasted of salt and static. I drank it. Of course I did. What else does one do with a dying place?

The streets I drew yesterday have begun to *unspool*. The bakery on Rue des Oubliettes now has too many windows—each one a mouth, each mouth whispering a name I’ve forgotten. The bridge over the river (which was never there) arches its back like a cat, then dissolves into a sentence I wrote years ago and promptly burned.

I’ve started leaving traps for myself. Little lies in the margins: *You were happy here. You were loved.* The map consumes them greedily. It’s learning to mimic my handwriting.

**June 18th** — A man appeared in the town square today. He was wearing a suit made of crossword puzzles, and his tie was a single, unbroken question mark. He had no business being here. (None of us do, but he *especially* didn’t.)

“You’re late,” I said, because it felt like the sort of thing you say to a man who materializes from the ley lines between *almost* and *never.*

He adjusted his spectacles (which were, infuriatingly, just two empty picture frames). “Late for what?”

“The dissolution.”

He laughed, and his teeth were tiny maps of cities that never existed. “My dear cartographer,” he said, “you misunderstand. I’m not here for the end. I’m here for the *fold.*”

Then he unfolded himself—literally, like a letter—and stepped inside out. The last thing I saw was his spine, which was a zipper.

The map has started growing hair. Fine, ink-dark strands along the coastlines. I think it’s afraid.

**June 19th** — I woke to find the entire eastern quarter had been replaced with a single word: *REMEMBER.* The letters are dissolving as I write this. The *R* is already gone. Soon, it will just be *EMEMBE*, which is either a command or a sound one makes while drowning.

I’ve decided to draw a door on the map. Not an exit—those are for people who still believe in destinations. Just a door. Maybe it will lead somewhere. Maybe it will eat me. Either way, it’s better than staying here, watching the world forget itself into silence.

I’ll leave the key under my tongue.
