Artwork Title: *How to Cook a Tardigrade in the Negative Space Where God Used to Sit

*(Transcript of a dream remembered by someone else, recited to you through a mouthful of wet chalk.)*

---

**Ingredients:**

1. One tardigrade (preferably unborn, or better yet, *unthought*—harvested from the pause between two blasphemies).

2. The hollow left behind when a star collapses into something too quiet to be called darkness.

3. A single grain of salt, but only if you lick it off the wrist of a physicist mid-sentence.

4. The patience of a cemetery that has forgotten its own name.

5. A knife that has never cut anything, only dreamed of it.

**Instructions:**

1. **Locate the absence.** It is usually found crouching in the corner of a laboratory where the light doesn’t touch. It may resemble a stain, but the stain is merely its shadow. The real absence is shaped like the question you swallowed as a child instead of screaming.

2. **Place the tardigrade inside the absence.** Do not use your hands. Use the memory of hands, or better yet, the idea of a touch that never happened. The tardigrade should float there, neither alive nor dead, but *possible*.

3. **Season with the collapsing star’s hollow.** Stir counterclockwise until time begins to pool at the bottom like spit. The tardigrade will start to glow, which is not light but the *absence of dark*, which is a different thing entirely.

4. **Wait.** This is where the cemetery’s patience is useful. Let the tardigrade steep until it becomes a fossil of the future. It should feel like holding your breath underwater while someone else drowns for you.

5. **Serve on the knife’s blade.** The tardigrade will now resemble a scar—small, perfect, and painless. To eat it, you must forget it was ever there.

**Notes:**

The taste is unimportant. The aftertaste is a door left ajar in a house that never existed. If you wake up with sediment under your tongue, you’ve done it correctly. If not, the tardigrade is still out there, surviving in the space between your failed recipes and the silence that came before language.
