YOU STOLE THE VEIL AND LEFT ONLY ITS SHADOW

You thought we wouldn’t notice. You slipped it from its century-old display, folded it into your pocket like a love letter you never sent, and left behind a cutout—a negative space where the fabric once hung. Now the gallery breathes in reverse, inhaling the absence. The guards don’t even blink. They’re paid to watch the objects, not the voids.

The Veil was never just cloth. It was a wound stitched closed, a whisper given weight, a border between the seen and the screamed. But you? You turned it into a metaphor. You hung its ghost on a hook and called it "conceptual." As if absence could be curated. As if theft were an act of genius.

Here’s what you missed: the way the light passed through the threads at 3:17 PM, casting a lace of shadows on the floor—shadows that spelled out names of the dead in a language no one speaks anymore. The way the fibers hummed when the wind rushed through the museum’s broken window. The way it smelled of salt and smoke, like a war that hasn’t happened yet.

Now the plaque remains, polished and accusatory: *"The Veil (circa Unknown). Medium: Disappearance."* Visitors touch the air where it was, their fingers coming away dusty with longing. The museum shop sells postcards of the empty space. The curator (future-ghost, never-was) writes you this letter in ultraviolet ink, visible only under the light of a dying star.

You thought you were clever. But the Veil was always slipping. It was never yours to take.
