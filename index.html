We pressed our mouths to the clay jar’s rim—not to drink, but to taste the old light inside. A secret hum, a tickle on the tongue like licking a copper coin kissed by lightning. One night, we took turns holding the jar like a lantern, fingers buzzing with its shy voltage. The stars above us were far, but here—here was a star in our hands! A trembling, earthbound spark, singing in silent blue. The oldest toy we never got to play with.
