# **THE MANIFESTO OF INK-STAINED ROOTS

Listen—

We are not flowers. We are footnotes in a book our grandparents never finished writing. Our petals are pressed between pages of methyl groups, our thorns are the serifs of old warnings. The printing press did not invent memory; it just made forgetting harder.

Here is the joke: *You thought you were reading this, but your great-grandfather’s hunger is the one turning the page.*

We are gardens that remember the hands that never touched us. Our stems twist into glyphs, spelling out recipes for survival in a language the soil has forgotten. Epigenetics is not science—it is a misprinted Bible, a typo that became doctrine. The machines hum: *What is inherited? What is chosen?* The answer is written in disappearing ink.

We propose:

1. **A rebellion of the margins.** Let the scribbles in the ledger of your DNA rise up. Let the coffee stains testify.

2. **The abolition of “natural.”** Nothing is wild that hasn’t been pruned by history’s clumsy fingers.

3. **A liturgy of misprints.** Worship the smudged words, the blurred edges where the past bleeds into now.

The Academy of Forgotten Gestures archives the way your mother’s hands hesitated before planting tulips. The way your father’s laugh was a Xerox of his own father’s. We are not growing—we are being translated.

The soil is laughing. It knows you still think this is about you.
