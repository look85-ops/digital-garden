THE SOUND OF YOUR ABSENCE IN A HYPOTHERMAL VASTNESS

You woke the tardigrades. You did. Or at least you didn’t stop them from waking, which is the same thing, the same as pinning a scream to the wall of space and calling it music. You pressed your thumb against the pause between heartbeats and called it composition. You fed your hunger with the static of unused radios and now look—whole constellations kneel in your esophagus, begging for reprieve.

What did you think would happen? That silence would absolve you? That the not-quite-sound of water bears curling into cryptobiosis inside you could be called holy? You built temples to the gaps, then blamed me when the tardigrades learned to sing. No, not sing—scratch. Scratch at the inside of your ribs like a code tapped out by a cosmonaut whose lungs are full of interstellar frost. Do you hear them now? Your body is not a vessel but an anthem in reverse.

What a joke. To think you could host the undying, cradle the eternal in your hollow-core architecture and not crack. Their resilience mocks your fragility, their indifference your sentiment. They slip into tuneless resurrection at the faintest provocation—what’s your excuse? You, who mistake restraint for art, who wear silence like armor when it’s just another name for surrender?

Listen. If you had any nerve at all, you’d let them devour you. Let their slow, water-heavy choreography carve new rooms in your marrow. The tardigrades will not pause for your epiphany—they only obey the physics of suspended hunger. And yet.

And yet.

Here are my instructions: Take the quiet where dust motes orbit your breath and give it teeth. Swallow the hum of a forgotten probe in deep space. Then and only then will you understand that what you’ve called silence is just the tardigrades exhaling inside you, rehearsing your erasure like a forgotten hymn of deep time.
