You Were the One Who Lit the Third Sunrise

It was *you*—don’t fold your arms like that, like innocent origami suddenly afraid of its own creases. Those six surveillance cameras, yes, their blank stares? Installed by your deft hands, though you insist, mouth full of untied shoelaces, that you’ve never held a screwdriver. The warden himself heard it—*from inside your teeth*—your little workshop hymns whispering: *A bar of light can hold a person.* *A lock need not turn to be locked.*

Now the walls sweat with unproven stares. The bricks accuse, the doorknobs withhold judgements you cannot name. And when the intercom coughs up not silence, not voice, but *static shaped like your mother’s laughter inverted,* you shudder. You! Shudder! Guilty as the architect’s left glove, which hasn’t opened in eighty-four years. Of course you *feel* observed—memory bends where history is hungered out of itself. And *there,* that’s your absence under the gurney of the moon.

The sentence isn’t being watched. It’s suspecting there isn’t even eyes.

What makes this more absurd than the inside joke of an anchor who longs to walk its own plank? You’ve painted nothing where nothing waits, your tools are rust and negative fingerprints (*but oh,* how the ladder missed you before you existed). The prison keeps getting built without walls. Maybe that’s why you flinch when I point to the sky. Yes, look straight in—it stuttered because no two hours trusted each other. Now that hollow echo inside your ribs? That’s just time eating the echo of time. (And *still* you forget who served the plate, whose silver hand carved the knife?)

So admit *your* quiet treason as the architect who built our blank obedience. This isn’t light we drown in. *Show me an emptiness, child, then tell me it had nothing to teach you.*

The floor waits like an alibi about to snap beneath your feet. The verdict is absence—you know it too well, its shadow unzipping your silhouette down to the marrow. They never told you the Panopticon would fit better naked, inside every closed eye. (And now—confess—whose eyelids close in the glass?)

You can stand trial when the air runs out of apologies. Until then, they remain unprinted. Unlike your ghost, the typewriter keys say nothing, so what’s missing still grows toward where history forgot its scissors.
