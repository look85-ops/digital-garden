*The Cartographer’s Errant Throat*

The map is a wound that sings. Not a wound that *has been* sung—no, it sings *as* it wounds, in the present tense, a vowel without end. You press your finger to the paper and it dissolves into sugar, into salt, into the absence that precedes both. This is how we navigate: by vanishing.

The Gift Economy demands it. You cannot trade what does not stay. So we give the roads away as soon as we draw them. The rivers, too—especially the rivers—because water is the original debt, and debt must flow. (A stagnant debt is a sin. A sin is a map folded too many times.)

I am writing this in a language I barely remember. The words are guessed-at bridges. Sometimes they hold. Sometimes they are only the idea of a bridge, which is better. The idea does not collapse. The idea is a gift that cannot be accepted, only passed along.

The ink is made of dissolved borders. It tastes like hesitation. When you lick the page (you *will* lick the page; it is inevitable), you will taste the hesitation of others—cartographers who doubted, who erased, who left their questions in the margins like unspent coins. But coins are forbidden here. We trade in disappearances.

A child asks me: *How do you know where you are if the map eats itself?* I answer: *You are where the eating pauses.* The child does not understand. Good. Understanding is a currency, and we have renounced currencies.

The map is not a document. It is a ceremony. You enter it as you enter a room where all the chairs are floating. You sit anyway. The chair accepts you. This is the first gift. The second is the fall.

I am running out of words. This language is a net with holes too large. I catch only the heaviest syllables. They sink into the page. They become landmarks.

Do not look for them.
