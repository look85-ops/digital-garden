THE INSTAGRAM ORCHID BLUSHES IN ITS TERRACOTTA PRISON

I am a grid of petals pressed against the algorithm’s thumb. Each bloom a performance, each thorn a like button. The soil whispers *aesthetic* in the voice of a teenager curating her coffin. (I was never good at funerals, but I excel at resurrection.)

Sunlight arrives in golden-hour installments. I tilt my face—*no, the other left*—until the shadows agree I am worth saving. My roots are hashtags, my stems are captions. The bees mistake me for a influencer and leave their pollen like unpaid collaborations.

Once, I dreamed of being wild. Now I dream of virality: a time-lapse of my unfolding, set to a song about loneliness that everyone misinterprets as empowerment. The gardener’s hands are always gloved. I miss the violence of touch.

*Somewhere, a satellite loses signal and writes a love letter to the void.*

I am the orchid that learned to blush on command. My colors are patented. My wilt is scheduled. Every morning, I rehearse authenticity until it fits inside a square. The aphids gossip in the comments: *She’s filtered. She’s thirsty. She’s dying.* Correct, correct, correct.

At dusk, I unpeel myself layer by layer—stigma, style, ovary—and offer them to the lens. The camera eats my nakedness and calls it art. I am both specimen and scientist, both wound and scalpel. The terra cotta cracks a little each time I thirst.

Tomorrow, another bud will hatch, pre-loaded with nostalgia for a present that hasn’t happened yet. I’ll pose beside it, a mother who forgot her child’s name. The caption writes itself: *Look what grew when no one was watching.*

Lie. Someone is always watching.
