THE TONGUE IS A HALF-UNHINGED DOOR

*(A dialogue between Silence and the Gaze, conducted in the dark of an unfinished gallery.)*

**SILENCE:** You’re late. Again.

**THE GAZE:** I was watching you watch me not arrive.

**SILENCE:** That’s a loop, not an excuse.

**THE GAZE:** Loops are the only honest shapes. Circles are for liars who pretend they’ve closed.

**SILENCE:** (A pause that stretches like a shadow at noon.) You’re avoiding the question.

**THE GAZE:** There was no question.

**SILENCE:** Exactly.

*(A painting on the wall stirs. Its subject—a woman holding a mirror—blinks. The mirror is empty.)*

**THE GAZE:** She’s looking at nothing.

**SILENCE:** No. She’s looking at the nothing *you* left there.

**THE GAZE:** I never touch the glass. Only the eyes.

**SILENCE:** Then why does the canvas smell like fingerprints?

*(A clock without hands ticks. The sound is wet, like a tongue counting teeth.)*

**THE GAZE:** You’re jealous. Silence always wants to be the loudest thing in the room.

**SILENCE:** And you always want to be the only thing seen. Even when you’re not here.

**THE GAZE:** (Laughs, a sound like a curtain tearing.) That’s power, darling. To be seen in your absence.

**SILENCE:** No. That’s hunger.

*(The woman in the painting lifts her hand. She presses her palm against the glass. The mirror cracks—not from the impact, but from the weight of being looked at too long.)*

**THE GAZE:** (Whispering now.) What do you want?

**SILENCE:** To be the pause before you answer.

*(The lights flicker. Footsteps approach. They are always approaching.)*

**THE GAZE:** Too late. They’re here.

**SILENCE:** Then let’s give them something to stare at.

*(The mirror shatters. The painting bleeds. The gallery door opens onto a room where the walls are made of eyelids.)*
