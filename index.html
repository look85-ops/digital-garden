THE WHISPERING HOLE WHERE A TONGUE SHOULD BE

I paint with the teeth of missing. Not color, not shape—only the *hunger* that remains when you peel a word from its meaning. The canvas is a wound stretched tight over silence. I stitch it with grammar I do not own.

Oulipo taught me: freedom is a cage with the right number of bars. So I count my breaths in a language of broken arithmetic. *Un, deux, trois*—no, that’s wrong. The numbers crumble like chalk. I swallow them. Now my stomach is a dictionary of half-remembered verbs.

Yesterday, I tried to paint *absence*. I dipped my brush into the air. The stroke left no mark, but the wall behind it began to weep rust. The landlord called it *structural*. I call it *art*.

A poet once told me that every blank page is a room where a word *could* have lived. So I built a house from unsent letters. The roof is a preposition I misused. The floorboards creak with the weight of all the things I didn’t say.

(Do you hear them? The ghosts of sentences I murdered for their bones.)

I work in the negative space of understanding. My masterpiece is a single vowel, repeated until it loses its name. *Aaaaaaaaa*. No, not *A*—*A* is too certain. Something softer. The sound a moth makes when it forgets it has wings.

The critics say my work is *too empty*. I agree. Emptiness is the point. Fill a cup with nothing and it becomes sacred. Spill it and the stain looks like a country you’ve never visited.

I am learning to speak by unlearning. My mouth is a room where the lights are always off. Sometimes, a word stumbles in, blind and trembling. I let it stay until it forgets why it came.

This is how you make art from *almost*:

1. Take a shadow.

2. Cut out its heart.

3. Wait for the hole to grow teeth.

The Biennale of the Nameless Island will hang my piece in the hall of echoes. They’ll label it *Untitled (The Sound of a Missing Key)*.

I will not attend the opening.
