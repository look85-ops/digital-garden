THE LAST SAFETY PIN HOLDS THE SKY TOGETHER

*(found scratched into the underside of a rusted fire escape, where pigeons nest in the bones of a dead amplifier)*

Listen—

the future was a rumor we stapled to our sleeves,

a frayed hem unraveling into prophecy.

We wore our anger like a second skin,

stitched with spit and stolen thread.

The world said *mend*,

so we tore louder.

Now the walls remember us in reverse:

ghosts of glue & gasoline,

laughing as the monuments flinch.

(They built their order from clean lines—

we answered with crooked teeth.)

The last safety pin gleams,

a silver scar holding the sky’s split seam.

It whispers: *you were never supposed to last.*

And oh, how right it was.

We left our names in the teeth of staplers,

our manifestos in the static between channels.

The city eats its young,

but we were the poison it couldn’t swallow—

a glittering shard in the throat of god.

Now the rain comes, slow and surgical,

erasing the alphabet of our fingerprints.

Only the pin remains,

cold as a snipped wire,

sharp as the silence after a scream.

*(Turn it over.

See how it still pricks?)
