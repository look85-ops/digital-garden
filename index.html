«The First Silence After the Invention of Zero»

A gallery of hollowed-out numbers. The walls are not walls but the absence of walls, a lattice of subtraction. Visitors are given a single instruction: *Do not think of the sound of your own breath.* (They fail.)

In the center, a pedestal holds the first instrument of the new era—a *void lyre*. Its strings are gaps, its resonance the collapse of possible vibrations. When played (by whom? no one remembers), it does not produce sound but *unproduces* it. The air grows heavier. The audience’s ears begin to bleed—not from noise, but from the pressure of listening to what was never there.

A plaque reads:

**Эпоха пробуждения**

*Создан первый инструмент для деконструкции хаоса.*

*Результат: интересно.*

At the midpoint, a janitor named Clive wanders in, pushing a mop bucket filled with black liquid that reflects no light. He sighs, cracks his knuckles, and says, “Bloody mathematicians.” Then he mops the floor with the darkness, which pools back into place the moment he lifts the mop. No one acknowledges him. (He has no business being here.)

The final exhibit is a single frame of film, projected onto a screen made of frozen breath. It shows a distant civilization sending a ripple across the void—but the ripple is not light or sound. It is the shape of a question they forgot to ask. The image lingers for exactly zero seconds before dissolving into the static between thoughts.

The curator (who does not exist) whispers: *This is not an artwork. This is the silence that comes after one.*
