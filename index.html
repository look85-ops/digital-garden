*The Garden That Blinks Back (And Invents a New Color in the Process)*

*(Imagine, if you will, a canvas that is also a throat. It hums. You lean closer—mistake!—it coughs up petals. They stick to your cheeks like wet confessions.)*

Once, I was dirt. Then, I was *Dirt That Knew It Was Dirt*. This was alarming. Suddenly, roots were not just roots—they were *questions* growing downward. The roses? Oh, the roses were *watching*. Not with eyes (how boring!) but with the *absence* of eyes, which is far more unsettling. (A single rose petal falls. It lands like a dropped monocle.)

Now, I am a garden that has learned to *gaze back*. The painter’s brush hovers, nervous. Who is painting whom?

A face? A flower? A face *becoming* a flower? The eyes are parentheses—always half-open, never quite closed.)*

The tulips have started *quoting* the clouds. ("You are so *soft*," they lisp. "We are soft *too*, but we *stab upward*.") The daisies, meanwhile, have begun rotating slowly to face the viewer. They do not blink. (Daisies *never* blink. This is why they win.)

And then—*oh!*—the garden invents a new color. It is not green, not gold, but *the sound of a bee stumbling mid-flight*. (You cannot see it, but your skin *tingles* where the color touches you. Later, you will find petals in your pockets. They will whisper things you shouldn’t repeat.)

The gate creaks. A child steps in, trailing a kite shaped like an eyelid. The garden *hesitates*. It had forgotten about *children*. (Children are the only ones who look at a rose and *lick it* to see if it tastes like *looking*.) The kite snaps free. The garden, briefly, wants to *eat the sky*.

*(Another illustration:

A key? A cloud? A key *made of* cloud? The stem shrugs.)*

At dusk, the painter flees. The garden, left alone, realizes: *it is not the gazer or the gazed-upon*. It is the *space between*. The *breath* before the brush meets canvas. The *glint* in the snail’s trail. (The snail, for the record, is the only one who *understands*. It carries its house like a stolen crown.)

The roses sigh. The dirt *remembers*. And the new color? It spreads.

(You will dream of it tonight. When you wake, your teeth will ache. This is normal.)
