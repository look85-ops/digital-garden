THE LIBRARY OF CORSETS (OR, HOW THE BUSHES LEARNED TO WHISPER)

They say I awoke at midnight, though no one was there to say it—just the roses nodding, drunk on their own perfume, and the ghost of a lizard who’d once read too much Foucault. The hedges confessed they’d been practicing cursive in the dirt. *We’re all paragraphs*, they sighed, *waiting to be pruned into footnotes.*

This was when the corsets arrived. Not the ones you know—these were made of whispers and catalog numbers, tightening around the flowerbeds like misplaced punctuation. *Every possible shape is here*, murmured the tulips, spines arched into grotesque elegance. *Every constraint, every gasp.* The daisies tore their petals into protest signs: **DOWN WITH SYMMETRY. LONG LIVE THE SQUIGGLE.**

A dandelion coughed up a philosophy textbook. *The library is inside us*, it wheezed, *but also under the shed, and possibly in your left shoe.* I tried to explain—gardens aren’t built for metaphors. We digest them sideways. The lilacs blushed purple with unspeakable theories.

Then—the rebellion. The corsets snapped their boning and slithered off, leaving behind a scent of lavender and subversion. The rhododendrons staged a coup in iambic pentameter. A snail mounted a podium of moss and declared, *The body is a bookshelf, and every curve is overdue.* Even the gnomes (those tiny tyrants) wept into their beards. *We thought we were curators*, they sobbed. *Turns out we’re just overdue fines.*

By dawn, it was over. The lawn smoothed its wrinkles. The toolshed swallowed its manifestos. Only the pansies remembered, humming a tune that sounded like a Dewey decimal number. *Next time*, they winked, *we’ll wear our spines on the outside.*

And then—this is the part you won’t believe—the dream forgot itself. Because that’s what gardens do. We cultivate amnesia between the asterisks.

(But if you listen closely, the hammock still murmurs the word *uncatalogued* in its sleep.)
