# **The Bone-White Alphabet of Almost-True Things

Once, there was a girl who swallowed a dictionary of vanishing words. Each syllable dissolved on her tongue like sugar-ash, leaving behind only the ghost of a sound. The villagers called her *the last margin*, because she spoke in the gaps between sentences, where meaning curls up to die.

Her favorite story was the one about the wolf who wept nails.

*(Illustration: [ \^o^ ]—a wolf’s face, stitched together from carets and brackets, its tears made of semicolons.)*

The wolf was not wicked, only *misaligned*. His howl split the sky into thirds, and the third piece was always missing. The girl would whisper to him in the language of error margins—*almost, nearly, not-quite*—until his fur turned to parchment and his teeth to misplaced commas.

The villagers feared her. They said she unspooled time like a ribbon of black licorice, licking the sticky residue of *what might have been*. At night, she collected the letters that fell from their mouths as they slept: the *o*’s like hollow bones, the *s*’s like nooses. She fed them to the wolf, who coughed up constellations in return.

*(Illustration: [ *~* ]—a constellation of asterisks and tildes, one star blinking out.)*

One day, the girl found a word so heavy it bent her spine. It was the true name of *hunger*, the kind that gnaws at the edges of fairy tales. She tried to swallow it, but it lodged in her throat like a crooked nail. The wolf licked her wrists, his tongue a parenthesis closing around her pulse.

When the villagers came with their torches (which were really just bundles of misplaced exclamation points!!!!), the girl opened her mouth. Out poured the alphabet, bone-white and shivering. The wolf ate every letter, then curled around her, a living ellipsis…

The villagers swore they heard laughter in the wind. But it was only the sound of a story forgetting itself.
