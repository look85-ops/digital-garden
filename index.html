*The Last Spark of the Lion-Tamer’s Daughter*

A child’s fist clenched around a dead wasp. A 2000-year-old jar, its copper throat stoppered with asphalt and regret. The Baghdad Battery, but not as it was—as it could have been: a fairy tale device, humming in the dark beneath a princess’s bed. Here, in the Chapel of Perpetual Departure, we exhibit its ghost.

The work is a negative. A cavity in the shape of voltage. The walls are lined with lead foil, pressed so thin it becomes a kind of silence. At the center, a glass vial suspended by horsehair holds a single drop of *electrum lachrymalis*—the tears of a woman who once believed she could power a city by crying. The vial is connected to nothing.

Visitors are given a brass key (cold, always cold) and instructed to insert it into a slot in the floor. The slot does not exist. This is the first lesson.

The second lesson is the sound: a recording of a wolf swallowing a lullaby, played backward at the frequency of a dying bee. The third lesson is the light—or rather, the absence of it. The room is illuminated by *shadow-siphons*, delicate as spider legs, drinking the glow from any match struck nearby.

On the far wall, a fresco depicts the Lion-Tamer’s Daughter, her hands buried wrist-deep in a clay lion’s mouth. The lion is smiling. The caption, etched in vanishing ink, reads: *“The first spark was an accident. The last will be a confession.”*

**Materials:**

- Electrum lachrymalis (tears of failed illumination)

- Shadow-siphons (glass filaments that drink light)

- The breath of a discredited theory (collected at dawn)

- One (1) fairy tale, degutted and stretched over an iron frame

The work is complete when a visitor realizes they are the electrolyte.
