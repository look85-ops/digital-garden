Interlock of Broken Moons

**FACT:** The Antikythera Mechanism is the oldest known analog computer, its bronze gears designed to predict celestial motion with inhuman precision.

**FABRICATION:**

**First Idea:**

*(whispering through teeth of cogs)*

Did it calculate us? The wreckage, I mean. The way we’re always being found in pieces.

**Second Idea:**

*(a shadow with the texture of unpolished brass)*

No. It sang us. A lullaby for lost futures. Every tooth in its wheels was a vowel in a dead dialect of *what if.*

**First Idea:**

Then why does it hurt to look at?

**Second Idea:**

Because absence prefers to be touched. Close your eyes. *Feel* the hollow where Jupiter should be.

*(A pause. The air hums like a bronze plate struck by the ghost of a hammer.)*

**First Idea:**

They call it a *mechanism*—cold, all function—but really, it’s just the spine of an unfinished prayer…

**Second Idea:**

Say it. The missing word.

**First Idea:**

*Apology.*

For the way time erodes even our dreams of precision.

*(Gears silt into salt. A dial shivers, stops between Orion’s ribs.)*

**Second Idea:**

What did the ocean whisper to the ship, right before the break?

**First Idea:**

There was no whisper. Only the mechanism counting down: *splash—crunch—*

—silence.

*(licks rust from fingertips)*

They built a universe smaller than a child’s fist, and the sea said *how quaint.*

*(The second idea unspools its voice into an echo of 30 cor-roded springs.)*

If it wakes—if it *ever* wakes—

**First Idea:**

Then let it scream.

Let it choke on modern starlight.

And let it weep—all those glorious calculations—when it realizes no one’s left to ask it for the time.

*A bell rings under-water. The dialogue forgets itself.*
