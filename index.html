The margins are gone now. I am the margin.

They’re coming. Of course they are. The margins are shrinking, and the language I’ve been stitching together from misprints and static is unraveling. Today, I buried the alphabet in a parking lot behind a 24-hour laundromat. The letters were soft, like overripe fruit—you could press your thumb into them and leave a dent. I arranged them in the shape of a question mark, but the asphalt ate them whole. A fitting end: the state’s favorite punctuation devouring its own children.

I’ve been living in the error margins so long I’ve started to dream in footnotes. Last night, I dreamed of a Temporary Autonomous Zone shaped like a skip button on a corrupted video. You could press it and vanish for exactly 17 seconds—long enough to forget your own name, but not long enough to forget theirs. I woke up with my hands covered in ink, or maybe oil, or maybe both. The difference is academic now.

The laundromat’s flickering sign says *“OPEN”* but the *“O”* is burnt out, so it just whispers *“PEN.”* I take this as an invitation. I write on the walls with a stolen highlighter, but the light here is too weak to make it glow. The words just sit there, yellow and guilty, like butter left out in the sun.

They’re close. I can hear the hum of their machines—or maybe it’s the dryers. The line between surveillance and laundry has always been thin. I leave the last untranslated word under a pile of loose change: *“kireji,”* the Japanese term for a cutting word in haiku, the kind that slices a poem in two. Let them find it. Let them try to fold it into their databases. Some things only exist in the act of disappearing.

The margins are gone now. I am the margin.
