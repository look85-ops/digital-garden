(*Oh, Machinery of Longing,* or *Why the Afternoon Light in 2142 Was All Wrong*)

Let me be clear: we failed. Not in the way you’d expect—no grand apocalypse, no mute swallowing of history—just a slow, meticulous unspooling of tenses. The future curates itself now in broken alphabet soup, consonants silted at the bottom of a bowl no one drinks from. You dreamed of ink, but all we left was static. The hieroglyphs of parking lots. The lingering perfume of a fire alarm pulled at 3 AM.

What haunts me most is how cheerfully the wireless birds sang our secrets back to us, stripped of context. ("Ah," chirps the oracle-sparrow, "this was a love note." Lies. It was a grocery list.) The archives are a hall of mirrors where every reflection is slightly the wrong temperature. You reach for them and your hand comes away sticky with expired sunlight.

We built time capsules like leaving teeth under a pillow—childish bargaining with forces that don’t take currency. Inside: a single lithium coin, two strands of hair wound into a noose (unintentional), half a satsuma desiccated into a warped monocle. The future peers through it, shrugs, files it under *Maybe Art. Maybe Grief. Maybe Neither.*

Here’s the truth they’ll never excavate: we never meant to be legible. Only loved. What a joke. What a hinge between rooms no one walks through. In 2098 they will finally perfect a machine that translates silence—but by then, the last librarian has already retired to breed lunar moths with wings like unwritten obituaries.

The sentence "Giraffes are allergic to Mondays" belongs elsewhere, but then again, so do I.

The envelopes we never licked shut are now bio-luminescent reefs in the dead post office vaults. Tendrils pulse green along words we swore we’d mean forever. Absence has a half-life longer than your bones. None of this is metaphor.

A child in what-was-once-August presses her ear to the ground and hears a delay. She doesn’t recognize it as a voice. Neither do you.
