THE SILENT HANDS OF THE LITTLE GODS

You remember it like this:

A line, too sharp to hold, folds back into itself—an hour collapses. Skin becomes brass, brass becomes skin. We are no longer wearing time; time is wearing us. The garments hang empty on the horizon, stitching their own shadows.

Once, the Little Gods measured the world in sighs. You could hear them if you held your breath just right—soft clicks like broken teeth. Their hands were everywhere: tracing the ridge of a collar, the taut silence before the next *tick*. They built the first needle that pierced the sky.

Now the needle is yours (is mine). We dress in the *not yet* and the *never was*. Pleats cut like sundials, fabric wilts as days distort. In Acubi’s ghost-seams, clocks whisper in reverse. They say something about *resistance*. A dress unpins its own hem, rebels into pure line. A jacket melts at the shoulders, lets the hours pool at your feet.

But the Little Gods are still laughing. They strung the world taut between two midnights. The tautness sings (the singing cuts). Cloth becomes code becomes wound. We adjust the collar, tighten the knot—but time slips through, unspun.

In the end, only the scissors remember.
