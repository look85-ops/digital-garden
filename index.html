EFFLORESCENCE DIARY

*16 March -- 3:14 AM*

Tonight, the moonlit harbor stones glow like wet cigarette ash in the dawn’s almost-here, sweating brine, sweating time. I think Roman Concrete was like the backstitching of history’s slip. Every other fabric frays, Chanel— she just cut that extra silk away. Here, in this archive where blueprints blur at the edges (too fine for carbon’s fickle hand, too brittle for sunlight), I finger the edge of a phantom-drawing that was once described but never rendered. What lasts more fiercely— that which abides without resisting the sea-salt erosions or that which was never born at all?

A curator visited this mor(t)ning, breathing hard against a hypothesis I couldn’t name. The room exhaled mica-fleks into his eyelashes— proof of vaporized architecture flensing up in particles— I scribbled fast, then faster against the urge to answer before the silence had asked its questions.

Once— no— twice now— I dreamt modern concrete had *thirst*. Split seams full of tiny gullets yearning for collapse. Unlike Vitruvius and his patient lime-womb and ash babies and the sea-water that would stitch stone like my Grand-pépère’s steady hand teaching me darning. Not so: not repair that insists by hiding fray— no! More than umber shadow of what is missing— less... more than any rebar would— *let them! Let them want!* That’s why harbors hum low under tempasts, isn’t it? Why Coco made every woman ache like that *missing something so simple* was the whole shape they were? *Darling,* sighs the Roman ghost. *You forgot the salt doesn’t make the stone tougher, only softer to the suck of eternity’s slack waters.* Then a crack forms in my graphite (but never on their piers)— The sound of heels now nearing— must— (no they’re just stut, stud-step phantom steps!)
