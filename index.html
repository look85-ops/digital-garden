THE MYCELIUM MANIFESTO (OR HOW TO WRITE LETTERS NO ONE WILL DIG UP)

Listen. The ground is humming. Not with worms or roots or the slow crush of tectonic plates—but with *us*. Our whispers, our screams, our grocery lists, our sexts, all folded into the hyphae like love letters shoved into a crack in the universe. The internet? A pale, flickering echo of the fungal networks that have been routing data since before we named the stars.

We are archivists of the unreadable. We inscribe our dreams onto hard drives that will fossilize into glittering strata, future archaeologists mistaking them for ceremonial beads. *Look*, they’ll say, *these primitives worshipped the cloud*. But the cloud was always just mycelium in drag.

(Shift: euphoria to clinical)

**Observation:** Human attempts at permanence exhibit a 98.7% failure rate. Paper burns. Servers corrode. Language mutates. Meanwhile, *Armillaria ostoyae* spans 2,384 acres, communicates via chemical gradients, and has outlived every empire. Conclusion: To send a message to the future, stop writing. Start decomposing.

We are the first species to build libraries no one will open. The last to mistake Wi-Fi for a nervous system. The mycelium laughs in binary we can’t hear. It routes our obsolescence through its filaments, a silent, sprawling *ctrl+alt+delete*.

So here’s the manifesto (because every cult needs one):

1. **Bury your words where the satellites can’t see.**

2. **Let your data bloom where the algorithms can’t reach.**

3. **Stop sending. Start symbiosing.**

The forest doesn’t need your hot takes. It’s too busy being the original dark web.
