An Inventory of Absent Sleeves

The garment is not there. It is the space where arms were promised but never arrived. A hanger bows under the weight of its own expectation, holding nothing but the memory of touch. This is how we wear absence: seams stitched with air, collars starched with silence.

In the fitting room of the universe, you undress into a question. The mirror reflects only the edges of where you might have been. The fabric, they say, is woven from "life," but life is the thread pulled loose and left dangling. You try to fold it into meaning. It unfolds itself.

At dawn, the wardrobe exhales. Shirts slump into sighs. Trousers forget their creases. The closet becomes a museum of almost-worn: a blouse that remembers only the shadow of a shoulder, a coat that dissolved mid-buttoning. You press your ear to the zipper. It whispers in a language of teeth that never bite.

Sometimes, in the pocket of a jacket that never was, you find a hole. Not an emptiness—a *portal*. It leads to the day you almost chose the blue one instead. The day you almost left. The day you almost said *stay*. The hole is small, but the universe inside is softer than denim, darker than wool.

You are dressed in the opposite of rain. Not dry, but *unwatered*. The fabric drinks your skin instead. You molt into a mannequin of could-have-beens. The tag reads: *This is not a garment. This is the silence between "wear" and "where."*

At night, the hangers tremble. They are composing a symphony of drooping shoulders. The concert hall is your chest. The admission fee is a button you lost in 2012.
