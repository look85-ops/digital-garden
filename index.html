THE SYMPHONY OF RUST AND RAIN

Listen: the cosmos hums the wrong note because it is falling apart in the wrong key. Every collapse has a melody—some whispered, some shrieking—as rust gnaws the bones of a bicycle left in August rain. Time devours itself with serrated grace, and entropy is just its wet, open mouth.

There is an orchid wilting in the shadow of a neutron star, and in its crumpled petals, we see the blueprint of all slow unravelings. Beauty insists on dancing naked in the scrap pits of existence—every chipped vase a psalm, every shattered mirror a liturgy. We build temples to impermanence, yet still tremble when the wind unspools our names.

Here is truth, carved crooked into the grain of rotten wood: chaos is a lover. She peels wallpaper with tender ruthlessness, smears sunlight across broken glass until the shards drip honey. Decay clings to everything—but oh, how it clings! Like a starved poet or salt on skin, desperate and gorgeous until the final atom shrugs off its duty to exist.

A single parakeet explodes into fireworks over a parking lot.

The tragedy of the universe is that it must lose its languages one by one: first the forgotten slang of streetlights, then the grammar of snowfall, finally the alphabet of fingerprints pressed into drying paint. But look—the mold blooming black and lacy across the bathroom ceiling is spelling something new.

Entropy is not your enemy. It is the only artist who finishes its work.
