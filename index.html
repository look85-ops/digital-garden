Diary of the Last Spiral

**Entry 12: The Weight of Light**

The moths came first. Fluttering in through the cracks in the walls, their wings shedding dust like slow applause. They gathered on the ceiling, forming a constellation of trembling ash. This is how the museum begins its unraveling—not with a collapse, but with a softening.

I pressed my palm to the glass case holding the *Dress of Vanishing Hems*, its fabric now fraying in reverse, threads reaching like thin fingers back toward the loom. Time doesn’t move in lines here. It spirals, yawns, forgets its own name. Yesterday, I found a teacup under the conservator’s desk, still warm. Whoever drank from it never existed.

At noon, the chandelier in the Grand Foyer exhaled. Its crystals fogged over with breath, though no one was there to breathe. I licked one—salt and static, the taste of a star dissolving. This is the aesthetics of decay: not ruin, but *unfurling*. A slow applause.

***

Her name was Marigold, and she had no business being here. She spilled into the Hall of Fragile Directions carrying a half-eaten pomegranate and a skateboard. "I’m lost," she said, though the way she said it made it sound like a theorem. Her sneakers left footprints that glowed faintly, then evaporated.

"You’re not in the archive," I told her.

She shrugged. "Neither are you."

For hours, we watched the *Portrait of a Forgetting Woman* gradually forget her own face. The paint dripped upward, collecting in brushstrokes on the ceiling. Marigold offered me a pomegranate seed. It burst between my teeth like a tiny supernova. "You taste like the future," she said. I didn’t ask her which one.

By dusk, the moths had woven themselves into a shawl around her shoulders. She left it hanging on a nail in the wall, where it turned to lace, then to light.

The museum is quieter now. The air smells of burning sugar. Tomorrow, I’ll wake to find my handwriting blurred, the ink retreating into the pen. But tonight, I press my ear to the floor and listen—to the hum of disappearing, to the hum of what never was.

Time’s arrow is a spiral. Time’s arrow is a moth. Time’s arrow is hungry.
