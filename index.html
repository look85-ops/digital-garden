Dear Ribcage: Ode to a Half-Finished Boning

_June 17th, 189_-ish.

The corset arrived today, pre-collapsed. Like a lung pinned to a birdcage. The shopgirl insisted it was “gently used,” but I’m certain the last wearer simply dissolved—a slow evacuation of self through thirty-six eyelets and a stubborn knot. I laced it anyway. Bones that have never existed began to press against my liver, a polite revolt. (Note: Is this how whales felt before ribs became fashionable?)

What is scaffolding if not an apology to the sky?

My sketchbook: margins crowded with vertebrae insisting they’re colonnades. My breakfast: soft butter, which feels pointed now. (What an overbearing metaphor! Like nature wagging its finger—except I believe nature is elbow-deep in a bathtub of laudanum, laughing at us.) When the laces creak, they make sounds like old stairs complaining underfoot—*oh*, say I, still the ingénue who forgets: pain has terrible timing. The seamstress swore it was “modern”—less torture, more geometry disguised as mercy. And yet. Mathematics has always underestimated the *meat* of it—how cartilage bends first, but does not forgive.

Tonight, I shall attend *The Exposition of Mechanical Melancholia*. I will wear the corset. I w ill br e a
