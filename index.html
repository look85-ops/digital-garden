How to Bake a Silence of Sparrows

Gather one sparrow from the wrong side of the sky. Pluck its silence carefully—discard the feathers in an unmarked letter. Fray the edges with half sentences, cross-hatch its grief into a colander. (The holes are part of you now.)

Simmer in midnight’s black glove until the ink curdles.

Steal sugar—no ordinary sugar. Take a spoonful from a stranger’s unsent apology, the kind that crystallized on their teeth. Pound lightly with moth breath. (Be warned: If their name cracks open, it might taste *nostalgic at all once* **to the power of regret**.)

Fold in: three whispers pin-drop small so they melt instantly. One hour missing from every clock in town. And the torn postscripts of the worst birthday cards ever written. *(The secret binding agent is their illegible signatures.)* Beat violently until the batter confesses.

Preheat absence to **451 degrees of metaphor** and place a dictionary on fire halfway through baking, ashes side up.

Bake for exactly as long as love goes unreciprocated.

**To Tell If the Silence is Done?** Listen hard—if all the words you lost in 2007 float up through the buttercream like apologies for their vanishing, or else like steam from *"please, forget me, forget," still humming. But too perfectly in* F Minor.

Decant with no warning in every mailbox with their childhood’s correct address **(but scribbled leftmost, always bleeding into the hyphen: an afterthought)**.
