# **THE GARDEN THAT DREAMED IT WAS A COAT HANGER

You were there—or someone was—digging through pockets of loam to find your misplaced selves, each one a folded origami of pollen and static. The garden had just discovered symmetry and immediately regretted it. What use are sharp lines to dandelions? What use are soft edges to the posture of midnight?

The manifesto:

1. **Wear shadows as dresses**, but only on weekdays when shadows molt.

2. **A collar is a theory gone sentient**. The knot in your throat? A failed revolution.

3. Acubi stitches silence into seams. Speak too loud, and your silhouette will fray.

Blades of grass rehearsed their elegy in morse code. The tulips blinked code violations.

You—or whoever it was—dug further. Found: a button made of cloud, a hemline fraying into dusk, wrist cuffs embroidered with non sequiturs (“The moon is a fainting goat wearing a tie”). Instructions sewn inside read:

- **Step 1**: Interpret the dress code as an existential rebuke.

- **Step 2**: Button up chaos until it resembles a vest.

- **Step 3**: Dissolve at sunrise.

Garden. Coat hanger. An identity crisis with excellent posture. The manifesto won’t hold still—it lunges at formalwear, steals adjectives from unsuspecting jackets, leaves lint in philosophy books.

Out in the meadow:

The scarecrow stripped of clothes finally felt naked. Perhaps fabric is ontological scaffolding. Or perhaps he just wanted pocket squares that taste of bergamot and unsent letters.

Someone narrated the entire ordeal in second person. Press releases fluttered like misplaced feathers, adverbs stuck in crevices, gnats formed focus groups. A singular headline later found crumpled in violet ink:

*KNOTS ADAPTING FASTER THAN WISDOM.*

By then the garden was sewing all sidewalks into waistcoats. Hedges wore suspicion like sleeve garters. Midmorning perfume: *petrichor and printer toner*. Tailored pulse to silhouette, *irregular heart in a garment bag*. Sloppy welt to cortex.
