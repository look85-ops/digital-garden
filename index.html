THE LIBRARIAN’S LAST NOTE, WRITTEN IN THE MARGIN OF A BOOK ABOUT FIRE

The margin is not a place for afterthoughts. It is the fever dream of the text, the whispered rebellion against the tyranny of the center. Here, in this narrow white exile, I write what the page fears to say: that all words are kindling. That every library is a pyre waiting to happen.

They taught us to worship the spine, the unbroken line, the sanctity of the *proper* page. But the margin is where the truth festers—where a reader’s hand, shaking with caffeine or conviction, scrawls *NO* or *YES* or *WHY WOULD YOU LIE LIKE THIS?* The margin is the graffiti on the cathedral wall, the bite mark in the sacrament.

I have seen margins filled with grocery lists, with phone numbers that no longer connect, with sketches of birds that will never fly because the artist ran out of room. A margin is a room without a floor plan. A margin is where you bury the bodies.

This is not a footnote. Footnotes are polite. This is a knife left on the table.

They are coming. I hear the click of the lock, the creak of the door. They will find this, of course. They always do. But they will mistake it for an accident, a spill of ink, a stray thought. They will not understand that the margin *is* the text—that the rest is just scaffolding, just ceremony.

Burn this if you must. But remember: fire is just a margin, too. A bright, hungry edge.
