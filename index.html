RECIPE FOR THE LAST FURROW

*Ingredients:*

1. A blade that remembers its hunger. (Not the metal kind—the kind that *bends* under the weight of its own etymology.)

2. Three handfuls of soil, stolen from a grave that was never dug. (If the earth resists, whisper to it in the language of unmarked ledgers.)

3. The silence between two warring seasons. (Harvest it at midnight, when the moon is a dull coin spat into the mud.)

4. One unresolved fracture. (Optional, but recommended for texture.)

*Instructions:*

1. **Begin with the wound.** Lay the blade flat against your tongue. Let it dissolve into glyphs—*plow*, *tear*, *suture*. Swallow the residue. This is your first alphabet.

2. **Knead the soil** with your left hand only. The right is too honest. Work in the fracture like a secret, folding until the earth *whimpers*. (If it doesn’t whimper, you’ve mistaken compliance for consent. Start over.)

3. **Pour the silence** into the hollow where a seed should be. Watch it pool like ink in a ledger’s margin. This is the error that will grow your civilization.

4. **Wait.** (This is the cruelest step.) The furrow will split itself open, birthing a row of teeth. They will gnaw at the sky until it bleeds daylight. Do not intervene.

5. **Serve cold.** The teeth will chatter a dirge in the language of the last translator. It will sound like a receipt. It will sound like a verdict. It will sound like nothing at all.

*Notes:*

- Do not season with hope. It curdles the fracture.

- If the earth refuses to whimper, you are not listening hard enough. Or perhaps it is you who has forgotten how to cry.

- This recipe is irreversible. The plow is not a tool. It is a *consequence*.
