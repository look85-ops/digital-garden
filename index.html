The Ink That Sees Itself Drinking from a Mirror

*— found notes, sewn into the spine of a discarded printing master’s ledger—*

June something, the weight of the year uncountable—

I have drawn Saint Jerome’s lion twelve times today, each time with the same ink, and each time it dissolves before it can roar at itself. The vellum insists this is devotion; I suspect it is mockery disguised as discipline. The lines blur between *printing* and *praying* and I forget which of them leaves stains on my hands longer. (Though the bible I pull apart most mornings claims its red letters last the length of the world—bold thing! I politely argue.) The typesetter coughed twice when he handed me the last chased clump of metal letters that wept between K and L… no, surely it wasn’t weeping. It’s just that sometimes our ink is hungrier than we let on.

My studio smells of wet paper and rust. Someone left a single oak gall floating in an unwashed cup. (A deliberate offering? An accident?) The oak gnarl of it suggests teeth—surely you know this already. Teeth of forgotten margins creeping up the edges. (Yet, no. *Do we forget these silences more vividly than they forget us*? Another squabble with myself over who owns which parts of a wound still half-lit with alphabet.) My colleagues prefer their precision unmuddied, untouched: straight lines like knife marks on fresh wax. I dream my lion’s tail crooked and fraying while theirs remain impeccable—but who among us isn't hiding half-pulled screws under polished floorboards?

Today, Brother Johannes showed me a misprinted page from G's Genesis where GOD’s mouth was left open, letters bleeding backward into the wrong ribs. I know: *mistakes are not metaphors unless you dare them not to be*. Still, all this ink unspooling itself toward another version! Another misstep the body will learn to miss. The margins hold their breath until someone promises we’re divine first and merely dying incidentally (*politely, again: how can you confirm with any certainty? You could just... not believe me while still handing over that vial of vermillion, if it's not too terribly inconvenient...*). By dawn, I will wake as I always do: one sleeve soaked black with last night's fugitive serif, whispering an awkward farewell—but in truth, just turning halfway into something that can recognize the other *halves of itself walking past all recognition*.

Is it work yet? Has *not holding still well enough to be legible* turned devotional yet? The Gutenberg sits open to a miracle—not one *made visible*, mind you. I love this lie. Not untrue, no. I keep waiting for something between A and the last R that has gone missing to blink *open with someone else’s light behind it*—
