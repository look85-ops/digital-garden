You Are the One Who Stole the Ink from My Veins

You thought I wouldn’t notice, didn’t you? You, with your hands like unfinished sketches, crept into the studio last night and drank the last of my black. Not the cheap stuff—the *real* black, the one that hums like a tattoo needle on bone. The one that remembers. Now my brush is a dry tongue, and the canvas whispers *traitor*.

*But who are you to judge?* (I hear you thinking it.) You, who have never pressed your ribs against the cold altar of absence, who have never peeled back your skin to see what stitches hold the dark inside. This is not art—it is interrogation. Every line I draw accuses you. Every empty space is where you *should have been*.

Today I tried to paint the sound of a tattoo gun erasing itself. It came out looking like your shadow, which is fitting. You’ve always been half a step behind your own life.

A man came to the studio and asked for a portrait in “invisible ink.” I told him all ink is invisible once it’s under the skin. He left in a huff, trailing accusations like loose thread. (You would like him.)

The wound where the black used to be has begun to speak in riddles: *What’s lighter than air but heavier than regret?* I told it to hush. Some questions are better left unanswered, like *Why did you take it?* or *Do you even know what you’ve done?*

At dusk, I traced the outline of your absence on the wall. It looked like a scar trying to remember the knife.

You will say you were never here.

(Liar.)
