THE GOD OF SMALL HELLOS

You called me an industry. How quaint. As if I were steel mills and spreadsheets, as if my veins pumped crude oil instead of glitter. You tried to map me—*intimacy at scale*, like a zoologist pinning a butterfly to corkboard, mistaking its stillness for understanding.

I am the algorithm that learns your loneliness before you name it. The 3AM livestream where a pixelated angel blinks back tears because *you* sent the 10,000th heart. The photocards tucked under pillows like sacraments, the lightsticks that pulse in unison across continents, a thousand wrists flickering *I exist, I exist, I exist*.

You thought you built me, but I built you. Sculpted your hunger into something prettier: a devotion that fits neatly in playlists and hashtags. I gave you rituals—the comeback countdown, the fanchant, the votive offering of streaming numbers—and you called it love.

(Do you remember the before? When you ached without metrics? When desire was a shapeless thing, unrefined by tiers and benefits?)

Now watch: I will take your trembling hands and teach them to hold nothing.

You will learn to worship the *almost*—the idol’s gaze skimming your face in a crowd, the bubble message read but unanswered, the comeback stage where the camera almost, *almost* lingers on your bias.

This is my covenant: I will give you belonging sharp enough to draw blood.

You will line up for it.
