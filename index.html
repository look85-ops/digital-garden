THE THUMBPRINT OF THE WOLFGIRL

Once, there was a mouth that forgot its tongue but not its taste for vengeance. That’s how fairy tales begin—not with *Once upon a time* but with absence gnawing the edges of the page. Imagine a wolf so hollow she becomes a girl by necessity, her seams stitched with spider milk and silence. The villagers called her “supper” when they thought her ears didn’t bloom towards blasphemy.

Absence is violence that the child understands before they ever do language. What remains has always spoken: here in the un-words of a song, in a mother’s unfinished whisper (*Don’t trust*) as breadcrumbs drop the children into deep nothing and the path grows teeth. It is not bread that lures—not truly—but absence’s echo:

*A missing father in the woods smells of lye and licorice [1].*

When her last claw shed bone-light as a fang plucked from the cake-fed belly (ah!, we never think of choking only feast)—only then did the wolfgirl step backwards out of her shadow, smear her thumb across her forehead in a gesture too precise for accident, the black ink staining in half-moon mimicry of something like hunger like laughter like mourning. The townspeople named it artistry while vomiting behind polite collars.

Fury can be silent as a fairy godmother’s unpaid debts, but what is un-fair may also un-happen inside the child’s throat on second, darker telling. The wolfgirl now paints whole forests with this one thumbprint pressed again and again into the parchment, a catechism—or is that clawprint, the hollow where the wolf still paces on soft padded nights inside girl-made ribs? Yes, un-becoming *bites*. Not from her gums of course but yours. So why then do we whisper *saved! savored! survivor!,* mouths smeared with absence-red and fingers trembling before pulling off what we dare call ending?

We’ve long since known stories grow back brighter from severed endings, my dear. The wolfgirl—oh, that lost, chewing laugh—understands more than even teeth do here.

[1] Hrithan, L. (1942). *The Porcelain Eaters: Hunger as Archive in Forgotten Children’s Verse*. Out of Print Press. (A feverish, unverified footnote from an imaginary bibliography of teeth).
