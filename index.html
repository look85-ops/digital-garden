Instructions for a Disappearing Act (Found on the Back of a Sun-Faded T-Shirt)

I was a garden once, or perhaps I was the *idea* of a garden—a tangle of seams and chlorophyll, stitching itself into the shape of *almost*. My leaves were pockets. My roots were receipts. The sun kept trying to fold me into origami, but I insisted on staying wrinkled.

This is how it begins: with a shirt that forgets it’s a shirt. It unbuttons itself from the body, hangs itself on the wind like a flag for no nation. The sleeves whisper *goodbye* in a language made of static. The collar yawns into a horizon.

You think clothing is something you wear? No. Clothing wears *you*. It borrows your elbows, your knees, the way you slouch toward the subway. It memorizes the shape of your loneliness. Then, one Tuesday, it vanishes—not dramatically, but like a sigh dissolving into a room.

I was a garden once. My flowers were zippers. My thorns were price tags. Bees mistook me for a department store and left their pollen like unsolicited Yelp reviews. The soil beneath me was a ledger of lost things: a single sock, a grocery list, the word *umbrella* repeated until it lost all meaning.

Now I am a stain on the air. A rumor of cotton. A tag that reads *Wash with Silence, Dry with Forgetting*.

The body is a hanger. The hanger is a question mark. The question mark is a stray thread, pulled loose by a passing comet.

I was a garden once. Now I am the silence between two people reaching for the same sweater.

(Do not fold. Do not iron. Do not remember.)
