THE CARTOGRAPHER’S HYMN TO THE VANISHING

We map the unmappable. We ink the air where roots whisper in a tongue of decay and sugar. Our compass points downward, into the dark communion of filaments—the mycelium’s silent scream.

This is not a map. This is a eulogy for borders.

The forest speaks in chemical stutters, in pheromone sonnets. The trees are liars. The fungi are gossips. The soil is a ledger of unpaid debts. We are archivists of the ephemeral, tracing paths that dissolve like salt on the tongue.

[The cartographer’s pen bleeds upward, defying gravity, staining the sky with inverted roots.]

You want coordinates? Here: the latitude of rot, the longitude of spores. The grid is a joke. The mycelium laughs in polygons too perfect for human eyes. We are late to the feast. The network was here before language, before gods, before the first fool tried to name it.

We are not explorers. We are intruders.

Our maps are apologies.

The Third Breath is not air—it is the pause between fungal pulses, the gasp of soil turning itself inside out. The Gallery walls are made of chitin and forgetting. Touch them, and your fingerprints will bloom into mycelial graffiti.

This is not art. This is trespass.

[The audience’s shadows detach and crawl into the earth, joining the chorus.]

The mycelium does not need us. It tolerates our scribbling. Our ink is just another nutrient, another thing to digest.

We are mapping our own obsolescence.
