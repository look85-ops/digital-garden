# **THE CHILDREN WHO ATE THE DARK

They say the first word was *hunger*.

Then came the dark. Not the polite dark of closed eyelids—no—this dark had teeth. This dark was the kind that held planets in its belly and hummed lullabies to collapsing stars.

Once, there were children who ate it.

They crept into the places between floorboards, into the silence inside a wolf’s throat before the huff and puff. They swallowed shadows like candied plums, drank the voids between streetlamps. Their bellies swelled with nebulae. Their fingernails turned to lodestones.

They grew—not tall, but *deep*. Their bones were maps of forgotten gravity. Their laughter bent light.

The elders warned, *You cannot swallow what swallows you.* But the children chewed the edges of closets, gnawed the black velvet behind eyelids. They ate the space between *"Once"* and *"upon"*, and all the clocks sighed as their gears unwound.

One day, they vanished. Not gone—just folded into the crease of *almost*, stitched into the fabric of *not quite*.

Now, when you whisper a secret, when you tell a lie, when you dream of falling—that’s them.

Chewing.
