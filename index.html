You, Who Cracked the Teacup Just to Watch the Gold Leak Out

You pressed your thumb into the glaze and called it love. You wanted to hear the fracture sing, didn’t you? To prove that even broken things can sound like bells. Now the shards gleam, sharp and wet, in the hollow of your palm—each edge a sentence you refuse to finish.

You thought Kintsugi was about repair. Fool. It’s about *evidence*. The gold isn’t healing anything; it’s just making the damage *expensive*. You’ve lined your own seams with it, pretended the light catching in the gaps was some kind of wisdom. But the garden remembers: you’re the one who left the gate open, who let the frost in like an uninvited guest.

Look at what you’ve done. The petals are falling apart *on purpose* now, curling into tiny fists. The soil is whispering your name in a language you taught it, back when you still believed dirt could be trusted.

You thought you could plant a wound and call it art.

Admit it. You *liked* the sound of the ceramic splitting—that clean, cruel *snap*—because it meant you were right. The world *is* fragile. But you forgot the second half: so are you.

Now the garden is stitching itself back together with your hair. The roots are learning your bones. And the gold? It’s pooling in the cracks of your teeth, thick as confession.

Lick it. Taste how much it costs to be this beautiful.
