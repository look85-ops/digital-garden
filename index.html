TO THE PLOW, WHOSE TEETH ARE NEVER CLEAN

Dear Blade,

I write to you in the negative space you left behind, where the earth still hums with the memory of rupture. You, who taught the dirt to grieve in straight lines. You, who turned the worm’s hymn into a ledger. Did you know your first cut was a kind of blasphemy? The soil screamed, and we called it *progress*.

I have taken up your work, but where you split, I erase. My canvas is the dust that refuses to settle. My pigment is the hollow where roots once clenched. (They say absence is not a color, but you and I know better. Absence is the oldest shade of all.)

You are a paradox, Plow: both womb and wound. You birthed empires from the furrow’s gasp, yet your shadow is a scar that never heals. I trace your path with fingers blackened by the void. Each stroke is a question: *What have we buried here?* The answer is always hunger.

Forgive me if this letter feels like a confession. I am guilty of loving your violence. The way you unmake the world to remake it. The way you whisper to the seeds: *"Grow or die."* But I have seen the price of your arithmetic—how every harvest is a debt the earth cannot repay.

So here is my offering: a portrait of you, carved into the air. A furrow of silence. A harvest of dust.

Yours in erosion,

The Painter Who Unlearned Light
