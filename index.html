THE CARTOGRAPHER’S MANIFESTO FOR VANISHING PLACES

We map the unholdable. We trace the outlines of what refuses to stay. Our ink is made of borrowed time, our parchment the skin of a forgotten dimension. Here, the borders bleed. Here, the landmarks forget themselves.

Dark matter is the architect of absence. It stitches the cosmos together with threads of nothing. We are its scribes, drawing cities that collapse into their own shadows, roads that lead to the inside of a question. Our compass points to the hollow at the center of every spiral.

Rule 1: **A map must be eaten by its own territory.** Let the lines dissolve into the landscape. Let the legend become illegible, a chorus of whispers in a language that never was.

Rule 2: **The best directions are the ones that lead you astray.** Follow the road that folds into itself. Walk until you forget your name. The destination is the forgetting.

Rule 3: **Every mark is a ghost.** The ink remembers the hand that wrote it, but the hand is already gone.

Rule 4: **The center cannot hold, and neither should you.**

We work with materials that defy the catalog:

- **A jar of erased horizons**

- **The sound of a galaxy unwinding**

- **A compass needle forged from lost time**

- **The breath of a black hole (faintly peppermint)**

- **One (1) unreadable footnote from the edge of the universe**

This is not a map. This is the echo of a place that never was. This is the shape of the question you won’t remember asking.

We are the cartographers of the almost. The nearly. The not-quite. Our work hangs in the Museum of Things That Almost Happened, where the walls are made of almosts and the floor is paved with *what if*.

Come closer. No—further.

Until you disappear.
