# **Ink Blossoms Siphoned Through the Holes in My Gloves

I soaked the calendar in lavender-scented mineral runoff, pressed it between pages of a first-gen e-reader (display permanently burned with the word "ERROR" in soft pink). The months bled into each other, slow as IV drips. Time is supposed to be linear here, they tell me. But I keep catching it pooling in the corners of the room, stagnant and sugared.

The studio smells like overripe synthetic peaches. I’ve been trying to reconstruct the way the shopping mall’s central atrium hummed at 3 PM on a weekday—that particular frequency of empty longing. My tools keep failing me. The samples degrade before I can pin them down. Yesterday, I played back a recording of escalator sounds spliced with dial-up tones, and the speakers sprouted tiny silver mold blooms. They pulsed faintly, like jellyfish.

At night, I dream in palette-swapped corporate logos. Last week, I woke up with a mouthful of translucent packaging peanuts. They dissolved before I could spit them all out.

Someone left a rotary phone in the alley behind the studio. I pressed it to my ear; no dial tone, just the sound of a fountain looped infinitely. I think it was the one that used to be in the East Concourse. You could throw a coin in and watch it sink forever. The water never touched the bottom.

I read once that certain brands of air-conditioning are encoded with subliminal apologies. I’ve been collecting them in glass vials, but they evaporate by dawn. The residue tastes like penny-per-gigabyte storage plans.

I miss the way cassette tapes hissed their secrets to you when you listened too close.
