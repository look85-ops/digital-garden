### **"The Spontaneous Combustion of Streetlamps

**Fact:** On average, a city streetlamp burns for 10,000 hours before it flickers out.

**Fabrication:** But listen closely—between the hum of failing bulbs and the shudder of pigeons taking flight—there is another kind of combustion. The slow, radiant disintegration of sidewalks into myth. The way a discarded glove on a bench becomes a relic before it even decays. The flâneur does not walk; they dissolve. Their footsteps are measured not in distance but in entropy—each one a small betrayal of order, a quiet rebellion against the tyranny of maps.

We propose an art of disappearing sidewalks. Observe how the cracks in the pavement trace constellations no one else remembers. How the graffiti on the overpass mutates into prophecy. The city is not a museum—it is an arsonist’s sketchbook. Every alleyway is a self-erasing poem. Every shadow, a fugitive.

**Rule One:** Never stay long enough to be named. The moment you recognize a face, it ceases to be part of the landscape and becomes part of the story. Stories are prisons.

**Rule Two:** Doubt every straight line. The shortest distance between two points is a lie told by engineers who fear the delirium of wandering.

**Rule Three:** Worship the stains. Spilled wine on cobblestones is more sacred than any fresco. The way rust consumes a fire escape is the purest form of devotion.

**Final Rule:** There are no rules. There is only the slow, inevitable unraveling of intention. The flâneur does not seek beauty—they are the wound through which beauty escapes.

By dawn, you will have forgotten this manifesto. By noon, the streetlamps will have forgotten their own light. This is how it should be. The city is not a place. It is the act of vanishing in plain sight.

*(Signed, in invisible ink: The Committee for the Liberation of Dust.)*
