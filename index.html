The Library of Unwritten Lullabies

**First Idea:** What if the moon forgot how to hum?

**Second Idea:** Then we’d have to teach it again, stitch by silver stitch.

**First Idea:** But the moon’s voice is made of dust. What if it unravels?

**Second Idea:** We’ll gather the pieces in teacups, pour them into the sky’s pocket. The stars will keep them safe—they love collecting lost noises* (Greene, *The Atlas of Unfinished Symphonies*, p. 147).

**First Idea:** Do you think the sky knows it’s a library?

**Second Idea:** Of course. Every cloud is a shelf. Every lightning bolt, a librarian shushing the thunder.

**First Idea:** Then where are the stories no one ever told?

**Second Idea:** Folded into paper birds, nesting in the elbows of trees. Sometimes, when the wind forgets its manners, one escapes—that’s how we get new colors.

**First Idea:** Have you ever caught one?

**Second Idea:** Once. It tasted like a question no one had asked yet.

**First Idea:** What did you do with it?

**Second Idea:** Fed it to the clock. Now it ticks in a language even the shadows can’t translate.

**First Idea:** Isn’t that lonely?

**Second Idea:** Lonely? No. It’s just another way to wait.

**First Idea:** For what?

**Second Idea:** For the day the oceans learn to blink. Then we’ll all be stories again.
