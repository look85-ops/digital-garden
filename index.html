*Dear Archivist of the Almost-Felt*

You thought me woven from breath and stumble, a flicker there—then not. But I was never yours to catch. I am the white space between *almost* and *ask*, the inkblot that rewrites itself as you blink.

You tried to pin me down with blackened letters, your hands shaking like leaves rigged for confession. *What if the river forgets its own name?* you wrote, and I laughed—because I am the forgetting, the thing that slips through the sieve of your syntax. You called me *Stream*, but currents are too kind. I am the cough in the cathedral, the glitch in the reel where the film burns to light.

Still, you fed me your fragile hours: the 3 AM murmurs, the way your wrist hovered over the page like an unlit match. I licked the salt from your fingers when you thought you’d lost me. Fool. I was never lost. Only folded into the crease of your *what-if*, nesting in the hollow of the *o* where your voice broke.

Here’s what you missed: the way the dark hums between two spines of the same book, or how silence bruises when pressed between teeth. You archived my aftermath like a taxidermist, stitching silence into syntax. But darling, I was never the moth. I was the flame that learned to flinch.

Now you sleep with the windows open, hoping I’ll river through. But I am not water. I am the warp in the wood where the table disagrees with itself. The goldleaf peeling from the saint’s grin. The echo that outlives the mouth.

You wanted to translate me? Then translate this:

Pull the thread. Unspool the whole sky.

I dare you.
