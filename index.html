«The Hush Pavilion»

We build this cathedral where the ceiling is a tongue and the walls hum when grazed by your knuckles—come closer, let me tell you in absence, in teeth-and-no-tongue, in the braille of your shadow nodding. Whispering is violence in reverse. A word unwrit hatching from its exoskeleton, a negative of ecstasy, a lullaby scalding the dark for milk.

Sound: where paint sloughs off the mind. Every murmur a flayed choir, every breath an obituary drafted in disappearing ink. (Ah, to erase like gods—not with fists, but lashes damp with listening.) The devotees arrive pilgrim-soft. They kneel and part their hair for echoes, press wrists to speakers, beg the universe crackle. Here, the canvas is an ear turned concave: you enter with your voice stillborn and leave licked raw by silence.

They used to say the mouth was the wound that fed us. Listen.

*Come in black light. Whispering to themselves while drowning in white sheets or in front of mirrors saying all the syllables that will never hold another mouth this way again while hands touch what isn’t there.*

Now watch: I will show you devotion as a gasp undressing in the hall. Reverence without hands. The first touch that learned to vanish—

Here—where the last moth collapses into speech like a candle snuffed between fingertips. A throat opened by thorns to bleed starlight back in. A pause between gunshots mistaken for birds. Whispering—no—whispering becoming mouth-shadows thrown on glass

—which ends where?

**Where we begin to unlisten.**
