# *Diary of the Unbuilt Bridge

**Entry: XIII**

Today I scraped the sky with my knees again. The workers complain—*the blueprint is illegible*, *the pylons dissolve in mist*, *one cannot pour foundations with empty hands*. How little they understand. The absence of suspension is my material now. My bridge stretches between two deaf silences, its cable-spines humming with the memory of weight.

What is architectural honesty? The lie believes itself first; the rest are consequences. A colleague came by, stared at the hollow pylons rising like inverted plinths, sucking light into some hypothetical elsewhere. Asked, "Where do feet go when there’s only echo?" To which I reply by unscrewing his left shoelace—an unknot as theory, loosening certainty. His frown trembles under its own tensile rigor.

I dreamt my shadow signed contracts not in name but in **negative space**, dotted where pressure should be. I told this to the concrete mixer on-site. He only gulped another shudder of gravel, as silent as the rebar knitting our absence beneath us, like sleep between sentences.

*Addendum: The mayor sent word. Requested a bridge citizens "might *actually use.** He didn’t underline the final phrase, but the paper did: buckled in a way no architect could calculate. So. Tomorrow, I resign. Or perhaps pre-retcon the resignation for yesterday’s logs.

Either way, the city will **yearn better** with nothing but the spine of where we’d dreamed to cross.
