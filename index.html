Diary of a Transient Topography

*June 17th, Year of the Unfurling Root*

Today, I woke as a grid. Not the rigid kind—the kind that trembles when the wind licks it. My rows are sentences written in beetle script, my hedges the frayed edges of a manuscript left too long in the rain. The art is in the collapse, they say, but I am the collapse *and* the archivist. A paradox of petals.

I’ve begun pressing my shadows into clay tablets. Not for permanence—permanence is a myth told by mountains—but because the act of recording is a kind of photosynthesis. Light into language. The robins agree, though they’ve taken to nesting in my margins, which is frankly unprofessional.

At noon, a man in a pinstripe suit arrived, clutching a briefcase full of thistles. He claimed to be an "accountant for the ephemeral." I asked if he’d come to audit my decay. He shrugged and began measuring the circumference of dandelion clocks, muttering about "taxable sunlight." I let him stay. Even gardens need the absurd.

By dusk, the snails had written their manifesto in silver trails across my flagstones. *Demand less. Glide more.* I’ll frame it beside the receipts from the accountant, who left without explanation, his pockets full of stolen pebbles.

The moon is a misprinted page tonight. I don’t mind. Misprints are the only truths left.
