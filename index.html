BLACK SUGAR REQUIEM FOR A SAINT OF THREE ALTARS

*Yield: One unstable communion*

**Ingredients:**

1 cathedral’s worth of whispered apologies (collected at dawn, from the lips of heretics)

2 cups of crushed stained glass (color ratios: 51% blue, 49% gold—this imbalance is crucial)

1 living root, stolen from a temple garden where it was never meant to grow

3 breaths from a child who doesn’t know the word "god" yet (harvested mid-laugh)

1 rusted harmonium key, sharpened to a blade

A pinch of smuggled daylight (the kind that slips under doors during riots)

**Instructions:**

1. **Knead the Sky**: Begin by braiding the apologies with the crushed glass. The glass will cut; this is how the dough learns humility. Let it rise beneath the shadow of a minaret, a steeple, and a ziggurat stacked like unstable dishes. Sing a lullaby in a dead liturgical language. If your voice cracks, pause. Feed the fracture to the hungry root.

2. **Ferment the Unbelonging**: Bury the harmonium key in the dough. Let it rust further. The metal must forget its original song. When the root sprouts (it will; roots are terrible at following rules), water it with the child’s breath. Watch as the leaves form tiny, imperfect hexagrams, crescents, crosses—all blurred, as if drawn by a shaking hand. This is the intentional failure: the symbols will never resolve.

3. **Bake at the Temperature of a Griever’s Palm**: Line your oven with the smuggled daylight. The loaf will resist baking evenly; one side will blacken while the other stays sticky with raw hope. This is correct. The burnt crust is the saint’s spine. The unbaked core is the doubt they swallowed.

4. **Serve on a Platter of Disintegrating Doctrine**: When the loaf cools, it will sing. The notes will be dissonant, clustered like birds on a wire that’s about to snap. This is the shared sanctuary: a fleeting chord no tradition claims. Eat it with your hands. Let the glass-cut wounds sting. The blood is the only holy water left.

**Note**: The recipe will fail if attempted more than once. The ingredients remember.
