☁︎ *The Bone-Choral of Ponte Vecchio* ☁︎

We mistook longevity for stone when it was always marrow. The Florentines say the bridge was built to never collapse—but they do not tell you how often it crumbles inward, sighing into Arno as if returning to its mud origin. Stones dissolve into silt-murmur but the scaffolding refuses. Men, drunk on dusk and tinsellit malice, haul corpses of future shops onto the wreck each solstice, whispering through rotted teeth: keep it *ours*. So *Ours* became teeth too. The ribs of butchers' stalls jut where new goldsmiths weld eternity into their locks (even they mistake permanence for looting).

A shoestring architect proposed adding floors mid-abyss so that, descending, you pass strata of old money—fishwives on the eleventh submerged layer singing a drowned credit hymn, moneylenders on six chewing each others' wrists in usury panic—but a baker slit his throat in a dream and his blueprints unraveled posthumously.

Here you stand as tourist, though ghosts call it “half-occupant.” When the 4 p.m. light spills across iron shutters you will see the **bruised gold hum**, a sheen known only as *The Grin Of The Nearly-Evicted*. A hundred shop-sign hymns creak backward while three teenagers etch initials under blood-flecked beams: they vanish by suppertime—the **children who were a scaffold**—sucked into the gaps. Oh, **the bridge eats best of all midwinter**.

A **door opens** behind your sternum where the tour didn’t say lungs should be, though frankly they shouldn't. Inside the aperture flutter:

♱ 15th century leather, diced by a blacksmith’s delirium—(a wedding purse now **holds calcified honey**, but no name)

♱ A cat left in charge of cobweb law—it only **barks when architects weep**. True law sounds backwards always. We who **barter in disappeared steps** watch through glass in the attic-gallery—**blister pearls rolling in their vellum sky**.

What collapses in us makes the bridge long to bend into its own scaffold—suffering or singing gets difficult to discern by layer nineteen-thirteen. Tonight though! Oiling fish-scales of history into new **slut light** down there near **Baldasarre Boring-Brother-Last**, he's sawing marble for some new **door**, humming low and stupid and inevitable.

**They must keep rebuilding—the moment they agree the structure ends it eats their faces clean to clavicle**.
