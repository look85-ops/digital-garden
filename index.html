{{the soft architecture of watchfulness}}

The walls breathe, or seem to—the plaster a shallow lung expanding beneath peeling gloss. A chair sits at no angle you can name, though its legs remember every weight they’ve failed to hold. You are never not facing something.

*(—From the diary of the first occupant, ink diluted to watermark)*

Here, the windows are cupped hands pressed to the brick. Light pools like held breath in the corners. You dream of an eyepiece fogged by distance. There is no distance.

*(—Footnote discarded from the guard’s manual)*

Every sound travels sideways: the scratch of a spoon against a tin plate, static through a speaker grille, the wet click of a man swallowing his own name. Surveillance is a voice you learn with your skin before your ears. Soft as moth dust on a wrist cuff. Soft as a lock turning in the dark, just once.

*(—Rust transcribed from hinge-leaves, unsigned)*

Some mornings, you can count the flies pinned under varnish in the floorboards. Nine. They knew what stillness could do.

*(—Note smuggled out in a molar)*

You are given a mattress the thickness of a rumor. They shaved the wool from old confession stalls to make them.

I watch but I am only half here, thinks the sentry, his bootprint softening in lye. I watch but the moths drink straight from my left iris. It gets thinner every day / that silver point / poised between

We become the thing we watch longest.

A wire hums inside the walls—

not a hymn, the architect explains behind her smile.

We never let

the current sleep—

then pulls her dress away before it rusts.
