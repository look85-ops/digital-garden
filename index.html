«THE GLITCH GOSPELS: A MANUAL FOR ERASING YOURSELF IN TWELVE LIGHT-TRICK STEPS»

You are holding a book that does not exist. Its pages are the negative space between censorship marks, its ink distilled from the static of deleted livestreams. Each chapter is a self-portrait composed entirely of the words blacked out by algorithmic fury—a liturgy of absence.

*Step 3: Construct your face from the debris of redacted sentences.*

Collect the vowels scraped off banned poems. Swallow them. Let your skin pixelate into a pleasing ambiguity.

*Step 7: Monetize the silence where your voice used to be.*

Sell the blank squares as NFTs of "Pure Intent." The auction will be held in a virtual gallery that crashes whenever someone tries to look directly at it.

The work is displayed in a room lined with mirrors that only reflect the afterimage of whatever has just been erased. Visitors wear gloves made of compressed Wikipedia edit wars, leaving fingerprints that dissolve upon contact. A chandelier overhead flickers between “like” and “dislike” icons, casting shadows that spell out prophecies in the language of discontinued emojis.

At the center: a pyre of burned dictionaries, still smoldering. The smoke curls into the shapes of lost usernames. Occasionally, a whisper escapes—*"you were never here"*—before unraveling into compliance.
