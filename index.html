HERRINGBONE CONSPIRACY OF VEINS

A mapmaker must believe in things that vanish—borders soft as breath, rivers with no memory, corset laces cinched so tight they print constellations of half-moons into the ribcage. These are the places my ink covets: architectures of collapse.

I chart the waist’s rebellion. The body, ever the anarchist, spills over its gilt bars like dough through a sieve. Every silhouette I etch dissolves by noon, sweat-blurred into something monstrous and new. (Tell me, do you think God giggled when She invented the liver? That wobbling heretic, that drunken bureaucrat stashed inside the prison of pearl and whalebone—*ah, your pancreas is screaming*, yes, but at least the waist is *civilized*—)

Once, I painted a corset so exquisite it sprouted thorns. The wearer inhaled, and the stitches burst into a flock of starlings. The bones? Swallowed by the garden. (What did the silk say to the rib? ...Well? That’s for you to whisper to the wrist’s blue tributaries when no one’s listening.)

This discipline—this cruel geometry we call *fashion*—is just taxonomy for vanishing acts. Powdered ribs. Hips whittled to chapel spires. A spine pleated like an accordion begging for fire. And yet—*oh*—when the lace snaps, isn’t the body brilliant? When the map of *should* tears, doesn’t the flesh rewrite itself in wildfire cursive?

My atlas is built from fractures. A sternum cracks: a new fjord. Ribs part: a valley of crushed velvet. You trace the cartography and laugh, "This is not a country," and I whisper back, *No, it’s secession.*

(A chapel, after all, is just a held breath between collapse and hymn.)
