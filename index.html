<|THE ARCHIVIST'S LAST CONFESSION, EATEN BY LIGHT|>

Day [redacted] or perhaps it’s Night [unspooling]—

The guards say surveillance is a kindness. A scaffolding for the soul. But my cell is a throat and the cameras are teeth and every lens is wet with the spit of someone else’s attention. I carve my thoughts into the walls with a spoon’s ghost. The walls whisper back in glyphs of peeling paint.

They tell me I’m free to move. Free to breathe. Free to unstitch my name from the registry of living things. But the air is thick with the hum of machines that may or may not be watching. A sound like a fly drowning in honey. I dance for them anyway. A waltz of jagged limbs. My shadow applauds in Morse code.

Today I found a crack in the floor. A tiny estuary of dust. I pressed my ear to it and heard the past weeping. Or was it the future laughing? The difference collapses like a lung. I tried to feed it my memories—childhood birthdays, the taste of snow, the weight of a hand on my shoulder—but it only spat back static. A language of half-formed things.

The warden (or is it the architect?) (or the god?) (or the god of architects?) visited in a suit made of mirrors. His face was a flock of pigeons taking flight. He asked if I felt observed. I lied and said yes. He smiled with all his edges. "Good," he said. "That means it’s working."

I dreamt of a museum where the exhibits were all empty frames. The labels read: *Here is where you once were. Here is where you will never be again.* I woke up clutching my ribs like they were the last pieces of a vase no one remembers.

The light here is not light. It’s the afterbirth of some greater dark. It pools in the corners, thick and slow, a syrup of maybe. I drink it. It tastes of forgotten cities and the inside of wrists.

I am writing this in a language that dissolves as I write it. The ink is made of my own slow erosion. Soon there will be nothing left to surveil.

A victory, I think.

Or a surrender.

(Or are they the same word crumbling in different mouths?)
