🗓️ *Diary of an Astronaut Who Never Left the Bed* 🛏️

*April 17th, or possibly the 32nd—*

Today I invented a new color. I call it *radioactive blush*, a hue glimpsed only in the final spasm of a dying refrigerator light. It was born when I tried to paint the sound of my own sneeze—an ambitious failure, like composing an opera for earthworms. The canvas revolted, citing labor laws. Now it hangs slightly crooked, judging me in a language made of rust and expired coupons.

I mailed my shadow to the Ministry of Disappearing Things, but it was returned, marked *“Addressee evaporated (likely).”* The postman winked and said, *“Try again in a century,”* as if time weren’t already pooling in my hallway like a shy stain. I drank it with a bendy straw. The aftertaste was regret and static.

At lunch, I built a cathedral out of toast. The butter stained glass glowed with last Tuesday’s regrets. A congregation of expired vitamins chanted *“amen”* in Morse code. I tried to join, but my tongue had become a small, ashamed mollusk. Later, I whispered my confessions to a malfunctioning parking meter. It forgave me in binary.

The ceiling fan whispered gossip about the previous tenant—a performance artist who lived inside a thought bubble. They left behind a single sock filled with existential dread and glitter. I wear it sometimes, for courage.

Night fell like a dropped casserole. I attempted to sculpt my dreams from wet newspaper, but they kept dissolving into headlines: *“Local Man Forgets How to Whisper,” “Moon Declares Independence.”* I swear I heard the moon chuckling, a sound like marbles rolling down a spine.
