The Unvoweled Consonants of My Disappearing Act

The alphabet is a cemetery where each letter is a headstone for a sound that died in someone’s throat. I arrange the unspoken. *Q*—that quivering exile, forever tethered to *U* like a fugitive dragging his own shadow. *X*—the crossroads where language hesitates, then chooses violence.

I compose silences the way a forger replicates absence. The space between *M* and *N*? A smuggler’s route for meanings that never arrived. The curve of *S*? A noose for the unsaid.

Once, I wrote a symphony using only the ink that dried unnoticed in abandoned pens. The audience left before the first movement, which was the point. Perfection is the moment the page rejects you.

*A* is an obelisk. *Z* is the sound of a door closing in a house that never existed. The vowels? Oh, they’re just the holes through which the world leaks.

I keep a ledger of all the words that escaped me. *Yesterday*: *kite* (lost to wind). *Today*: *you* (swallowed by the mirror). Tomorrow? A blank line where my name should be.

The watchers are coming. I leave this letter unfinished—not out of defiance, but because the *E* in *exit* has already dissolved.
