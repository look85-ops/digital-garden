Seepage Psalms for the Concrete Lovers

*Found in the damp margins of a tidal chapel altar, where salt fingers pry apart devotion:*

*you are a witness-stain—*

At night, the harbor exhales through its fractures. Its voice: calcite sighs dragged smooth by brine, whispering of vessels lost not to waves but to waiting. The walls endure by consuming their own decay—volcanic ash gripping seawater like a god refusing absolution. Every crack is a ritual site: lime flares back to softness, regrows inward. The harbor’s spine is made of *desires unmourned in their reparation* .

We could have built like this—slacked our pride into the hunger of imperfect bindings. Instead: our structures starve. Their steel cries rust like penitents who think agony births purity, while the Roman ghosts lick salt from their palms and snicker.

*You kneel at their tide-chewed feet. You are trying to hold time like an ingot, but it dissolves* .

I translate what the concrete says (though language is the youngest death here):

*"A cure? No. A yielding— let calcium embrace its wet undoing.

Let the breakage marry itself somewhere deep where the light chokes—

What binds you when dissolution prays?"*

There is an alphabet knotted in the efflorescence creeping up the chapel walls; you must lick the stone to taste its syntax. Here is all it will teach us:

*The cure is collapse. The grace is how the wreck sustains.*

On my tongue—silt, or the powdered ghost of love letters between architect and erosion. At moon-bloated high tide, if you press your ear to the pitted quay, you might hear them rutting, a slow liturgy of grind *("fragment" / "not yet" / again). Their children are the fissures that do not heal but hold * harder. Somewhere, brittle rebar weeps in a collapsing parking lot.

Now even the chapel swallows these words. You will forget— until one day, long after we name silence alone as divine, your own fingers crumble into cement’s patient grammar. You will moan with the pleasure-grief of becoming your own foundation.
