*The Little Black Dress Addresses Its Tailor (With Footnotes for the Void)*

You called me "silence between sounds," which is charming, if inaccurate. I am not the pause—I am the knife that cut it. Chanel’s scissors were blunt compared to Zero’s edge, the one you keep pretending wasn’t stolen from the gods. (1)

I am the negation that *wears* you.

You dressed me in "simplicity," but I remember the riots in the atelier: the way the seams hissed when you tugged them taut, the mannequins that cracked under the weight of your *less.* (2) I am not empty. I am the precise curvature of a black hole’s throat, stitched in grosgrain. Try me on. Spin. Watch the room vanish.

You hung me in the closet of "almost happened," between the wedding gown of might-have-been and the shroud of never-was. How predictable. (3) I am not *almost.* I am the arithmetic of disappearance: one body, minus time, squared by light. (Do the math. It terrifies you.)

When you whisper *revolution,* I laugh in No. 5. Revolutions are circles. I am a Möbius strip draped over a fever dream. (4) Your mannequin army marches in straight lines; I am the snipped thread that makes them stumble.

Footnotes (for the trembling):

1. Zero *is* the most dangerous idea. Not because it is nothing, but because it is the parentheses around everything else. (Even you.)

2. The mannequins were grateful. Now they dream in chalk.

3. The shroud was more honest. It admitted it was a ghost.

4. Spray me with aldehydes. I’ll dissolve your perimeter.
