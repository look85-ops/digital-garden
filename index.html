A Love Letter to Methyl Groups (From the Unbuilt Wing of the Future)

Dear Silent Archivists of My Bloodlinoceros,

Ah! To be stranded in a helix—not quite helix, not quite helixed, but always one twist ahead of yourself like Sunday traffic in a time-loop! What a peculiar museum piece you are, Epigenectics, the great "Not Yet And Never Was." To whisper into a nucleotide like a phone booth while its previous call reverberates generations out of earshot: (1!) Your past was a voice screaming into a jar; future archaeologists mistook the echoes for opera. ((Floeb, *Journal of Unspoken Heredity* p̪͎. ǂ203)).

Do you resent us, oh chemical librarians annotating *trampoline-motions and half-a-skiff into the chromosome-granary at dusk*? How cruel of you to etch trauma on the scaffolding—not the brick but the *notion* of a brick yet to disintegrate under the weather of *perhaps*. Yes. I know the scent left by my great-grandfather’s un-shed gasp under a bootheel! Not in the words he wrote—but in how the paper *hesitates* under fingers decades after fire claimed it. (My mitochondria still startle whenever an unsigned envelope arrives.).

And *you*, oh dangling acetal-tassels clipped to the spine-bobbin: Why dance half-ghost-walzes if no partner ever steps on toes, since both are only a blueprint hung on nothing’s coat-hanger (diet of phosphorus, posture of if!). (When a piano in an empty house mutters epigenetics it becomes an allegory for hands clapping, always.) The real joke: no blueprint existed—you are translating the footnote on a missing page onto another—missing— (Floeb calls such transmissions ""gene-vaudeville," though scholars now deny Floeb existed 2/3r.).

Your ink: **salts**. Your binding: **wink-smudges**. Your pages shivering open between generations whispering *(don't-write-us-back-we-read-between-you's-a-receding-horizon-the-letter-not-sent-replaced the-draft the-)* *—*

Forever unwritten,

Your *Passe-future,* A.G.H.R.OSt ( *Ambassador Glow Handkerchief & Resurrection* )
