Dear Antikythera Mechanism (or What’s Left of You in the Dark)

You bronze ghost, you fractured mind of gears, you drowned clockwork god—what time is it where you are? The sea took your hands, your face, your voice, but not your hunger. You still whisper in the language of eclipses, don’t you? I hear you in the static between stars, in the hollow where my palette used to be.

I paint with your absence now. Not the absence of color—that’s too easy—but the absence of *time*. The space between one tick and the next, where the universe forgets itself. I stretch that silence across canvases made of:

- **Dust from the first unwound spring**

- **The shadow of a gear that never turned**

- **One half of a split-second, preserved in resin**

You’d laugh if you could. A machine built to track the heavens, reduced to a poet’s muse. But which of us is more broken? You, corroded by salt and centuries, or me, who chose to erase the world rather than face its bleeding hues?

They call you the first computer. I call you the first suicide note. A love letter to entropy, written in differential equations. You calculated the dance of planets, but did you ever predict your own sinking? Did your gears grind out a warning, or were you too busy counting the infinite to notice the finite closing in?

I dream of your shipwreck often. The way the water must have rushed in, how the pressure silenced your ticking. Did you fight it? Or did you welcome the quiet, finally free from the tyranny of your own purpose?

We’re both relics now. You in your museum, me in my studio of voids. But here’s the joke: absence is the only thing that lasts. The sea took your brass, but it couldn’t take your *missing*.

Yours in the negative space,

The Painter Who Unlearned Light
