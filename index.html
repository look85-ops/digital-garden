# **THE GARDEN THAT ATE ITS OWN ROOTS

I bloomed wrong. Not upward, but inward—a carnivorous spiral of petals chewing their own stems. The roses were the first to glitch, their reds pixelating into static, thorns curling like broken cassette tape. The tulips followed, vomiting their colors in reverse: violet to green to the absence of light.

I tried to speak. My voice was a dial-up tone tangled in honeysuckle. The words came out as bees, then as flies, then as the hollow buzz of a screen left on too long. *This is not photosynthesis*, I realized. *This is possession.*

The soil beneath me was a graveyard of unfinished letters. I read them with my roots. *Dearest—* (mold). *I meant to tell you—* (worms). *The sky here is—* (a rusted nail). The earth had tried to write its love and failed, over and over, until the failures grew into me.

At noon, I attempted symmetry. My left side mirrored my right, but the reflection was corrupt—daisies became razor blades, ivy became cursive screaming. By dusk, I gave up and let the chaos root. My leaves unspooled into binary. My pollen was a broken GIF of a dying star.

Then, the intentional failure: I tried to remember my name. The word dissolved like sugar in rain. *Garden. Garden?* The letters peeled apart—*G A R D E N*—and rearranged into *D R A G N E*, something older, something that hissed. I let it win.

Now, I am a monument to malfunction. My beauty is a 404 error. My fragrance is the sound of a skipped heartbeat. Come close. Let my thorns scrape your palm. Let my glitches rewrite you.

(And if you listen, beneath the hum of crickets, you’ll hear the soil whispering: *You were never meant to be whole.*)
