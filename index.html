HOW THE WIND REMOVED ITSELF BUT REFUSED LEAVE A SHAPE

Once, the orchard was a rumor painted with spit. The apples swayed not because anybody touched them, nor to obey gravity—but because something deep below hummed, and it is very polite to hum back. This was before lines were made to punish wandering and the Commons was held in our throats like swallowed sun.

Now, the silence is not color. Not negative. Just the stubborn left behind—

A bench in the empty village square where someone sits anyway. Footprints not left in dust because dust agreed not to cling. Shadows no longer fighting dawn.

Nobody comes here twice and claims the same absence because Absence is *busier* than you’d think: It mops corners, tends to mushrooms in speech, plucks the hairs out of *Almost.* It teaches fences to unshout the fields. This is why ghosts are so lazy lately; absence does better at dying than they do. The missing trees still pass names sideways, birdless between us. No, this isn’t sad—the Commons *wanted* invisible! It learned the shape of every grasping ‘I,’ then folded, quietly victorious in all the places possession would itch to scrape blank.

I once tried drawing The Commons from memory: pressed my left hand into unmown August meadows until wristwatch ate time—but the space that leapt out wasn’t mine. What I thought was emptiness blushed with unowned sounds—the cough of shy rivers, boots splitting paths open again and again until there *were* no boots—

The wind’s last chore is convincing you not to name it so its work stays free. This is why when you ask The Commons where it went, a child’s balloon touches the exact moon it isn’t actually holding.

(Sometimes lying is the truest vote against property lines—so when I say someone carved **WE HERE** with teeth marks into that absent bench?) You tell me which it is: Defiance or proof the wood itself got hungry.
