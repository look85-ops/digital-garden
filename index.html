The Museum of Unfinished Breaths

You were always better at breaking things than holding them. The teacup’s fracture—a lightning bolt paused mid-strike—waits for your confession. But you’re too busy narrating the accident in future tense, as if grief were a grammar lesson. *I will have dropped it. I will have wept.* Meanwhile, the gold is already singing in the cracks, a chorus of *what if* and *almost.*

Kintsugi is just stream-of-consciousness with better lighting.

The curator (ghost, future, unpaid) arranges the shards into a sentence that refuses to end. A comma becomes a hairline fracture. A semicolon: two edges pretending they don’t miss each other. The exhibit label reads: *This was once a vessel; now it is a rumor.* Visitors touch the repaired seams and leave fingerprints in the margins.

You try to write your way back to before the fall, but the ink keeps skipping like a record stuck on *sorrysorrysorry.* The page is a delta of crossed-out starts. Every word is a little earthquake.

The gold isn’t hiding the damage—it’s translating it. A crack says *I was here*; the lacquer replies *I know.* The ghost-curator hums a tune that sounds like a question: *What if we called the broken things *oracles*?*

(You can’t answer. You’re too busy becoming the punchline of a joke the universe forgot to finish.)

The final display is a single word, gilded and gaping: *______*. Visitors lean in, expecting a revelation. Instead, their breath fills the blank.
