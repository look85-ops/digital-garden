Recipe for the Reconstruction of a Forgotten Machine (or How to Bake Time in Twelve Impossible Steps)

**Fact:** The Antikythera Mechanism, recovered from a shipwreck in 1901, is an ancient Greek device of bronze gears and cryptic inscriptions, designed to predict celestial events.

**Fabrication:**

1. **Harvest the Absence**

Begin with the silence of a shipwreck. Strain it through a sieve of unanswerable questions. Discard the answers—they are irrelevant. What remains is the raw material: a fine, metallic dust that tastes like lost centuries.

2. **Preheat the Cosmos**

Light a candle made from the wax of bees that fed only on eclipses. Place it in the center of a room with no walls. Wait until the shadows cast by the flame begin to move in reverse.

3. **Knead the Gears**

Take 30 bronze fragments (or as many as you can pry from the fists of sleeping historians). Fold in one part speculation, two parts wild conjecture. The dough should resist you. If it doesn’t, you’ve misunderstood the assignment.

4. **Leaven with Paradox**

Add a pinch of "what if" and a dash of "never was." The mixture should bubble with contradictions. If it doesn’t, you’ve failed to invoke the ghosts of sufficiently puzzled archaeologists.

5. **Carve the Inscriptions**

Using a chisel forged from unanswered letters, inscribe the following onto the surface of a thought: *"This machine was never meant to be found, only imagined."* If the letters dissolve upon contact, you’re on the right track.

6. **Assemble in Zero Gravity**

The gears must be fitted together in a place where time has no weight. A closet where you once forgot a childhood fear will suffice. The mechanism should click into place with the sound of a question being swallowed by the sea.

7. **Baste with Starlight**

Dip a brush into the Milky Way and paint each tooth of every gear with unresolved longing. The device should gleam with the sheen of something that almost makes sense.

8. **Let It Rest in the Dark**

Place the mechanism inside a drawer filled with unfinished sentences. Close the drawer. Open it exactly one century later. If the machine is gone, congratulations—it’s working.

9. **Serve with Silence**

The final product should be presented on a plate made from the last page of every burned library. Accompany it with a side of whispered apologies to the future.

10. **Consume with Caution**

Do not eat the machine. Instead, let it eat you. The aftertaste should linger like the echo of a civilization that dreamed too hard.

11. **Disregard All Instructions**

The true recipe was lost before it was written. This is merely the shadow of a possibility.

12. **Repeat Until Forgotten**

The only way to perfect the dish is to ensure it never existed.
