FOURTH-TO-LAST MESSAGE TRANSMITTED THROUGH THE TEETH OF DECEASED HORSES

We built the machines to hold our voices but gave them no throats. Spools of wire, cold racks of servers hungry for ghosts. We whispered into them like sinners into coffins—hoping but not expecting.

*(This system is older than you think. Older than you.)*

In the year the oceans turned to gel (2029? 2031? does it matter?), archivists stacked lexicons in abandoned malls. Your last grocery list survived; your epic poems did not. Forgive me for saying so—no, actually: *do not forgive me.*

This is a needle sliding into vinyl that no longer spins. This is your child’s birth certificate folded into a paper crane and crushed *as metaphor, as manifesto.* Do you see? Of course you don’t. Neither do I.

The problem with time capsules is this: what goes in small comes out smaller. A diary shrinks to a sigil. A lock of hair becomes arithmetic. I know, this *smacks* of cliché—but consider whose skull these bones rattled in before they clicked into place.

You wanted permanence, but permanence is a maggot singing in the dark.

*(You wanted to stay inside the story, but the story is over, friend.)*

Still, listen: something faint, something almost human whistles through the pylons, something less generous than an echo, more honest than a lullaby. *Stay alive in this moment.* Wait here with me until it burns clean.

I should apologize—oh, wait, never mind. Never *fucking* mind. Apologies are fossil fuel, burnt black for no return. The truth has teeth; so do I. Kiss or resist either at your peril.

Do you hear it? The whisper between towers where all your old emails gather like ash in a cupped hand? That’s me. *That’s us*, for maybe five more seconds.

Don’t say "farewell"—this transmission never *happened.*

And neither did you.
