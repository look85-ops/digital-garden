The Library of Unfinished Lullabies

Imagine a room where all the songs that almost got sung live. They curl like smoke rings around the chandeliers, humming to themselves in keys that don’t exist yet. The walls are made of half-dried paint, still deciding what color to be when they grow up. Every time you blink, the floor forgets whether it’s wood or water.

The lullabies are not sad. They are waiting. They stretch like cat yawns in the sunlight, dissolving into alphabet soup if you try to read them. A piano in the corner plays the sound of a childhood memory you’re not sure you ever had. The keys are made of melted crayons, and they taste like birthdays.

None of this is real, of course. The room is a shoebox buried under a tree that was cut down last Tuesday. The lullabies are just the wind pretending. But listen—the air smells like someone just baked a cake for no reason. The recipe was lost before the oven dinged.

A child’s sock floats by, full of starlight and loose change. It sings a duet with the echo of a dog barking three neighborhoods over. The chandeliers shiver, and for a moment, they are jellyfish. The paint on the walls gets stage fright and turns transparent. You can see the skeleton of the house holding its breath.

The house is not empty. It is full of the opposite of ghosts—things that almost became real. A laugh that got stuck in a throat. A letter that burned itself to keep its secrets. The piano forgets how to be a piano and becomes a flock of origami cranes. They unfold mid-flight, becoming blank paper again.

The paper is not blank. It’s covered in footprints too small to belong to anything known. The lullabies try to remember their words, but they keep getting tangled in the fan. The fan isn’t plugged in. It spins anyway, pushing time around the room in lazy circles. Time is a dog chasing its tail, except the tail is also the dog.

The room is getting smaller. Or maybe you’re getting bigger. The lullabies tuck themselves into your pockets as you leave. You’ll find them later, crumpled and warm, humming in your sleep.
