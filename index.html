# **The Blue That Abides on the Wrong Side of the Knife

Do not wear this cerulean close to the pulse. Do not let its light pool beneath the tongue. It is slow poison, this color—fleeing from its own body like ink from squidbone or rain from the pariah sky. You will forget your name in this blue.

They found azurite once, deep in a cave-mouth where sound crouched in spirals, and they called it heavenly. A lie. Blue is a wound. It sings backwards—swallowing echoes at the edge of human sight. Lapis slivered from a cliff-face, smuggled inside mummy-wrappings, sold in fistfuls for Madonna cloaks and bankrupt kings. Why? No pigment claws the eye like it: this gap, this ache, a hole in vision you yearn to walk into forever.

Forget ultramarine, crushed from stone as blue as betrayal. Even cobalt wracks the throat, whispering its fables to the blind. No. The true blue flickers only when your gaze splits sideways—unfinished, unbeheld, a lover’s wrist before they wince away. When was the last time something wanted you the way a fire wants oxygen? Blue pretends otherwise. Refuses to admit: *I am the void dressed in daylight’s mourning clothes.*

If you must drink from this shade, be wary. To drown in sapphire is never fatal. It *lingers*, sticky under fingernails, bright-sick between teeth. You will wake fevered on sand the wrong color, screaming at sunsets. Even your shadow, blue-black as a newborn bruise, will hiss at your fingers as it peels free and runs.
