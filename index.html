YOU HAVE ALREADY SEEN THIS BEFORE (BUT IT WAS YELLOW)

There is a hook in the ceiling, but no rope. The walls are listening, but they forgot their ears. I cut the silence into cubes and serve them on a plate made of unblinking.

You think it’s empty, but the space is full of teeth—tiny, polite, arranged in a grid like a chorus of suppressed laughter. The edges hum but refuse to sing. Everything is precise until you lean closer; then it melts into a rumor.

This is not a room. It is a throat swallowing its own shadow. The air is stitched together with invisible thread, and if you pull it, the whole world unravels into a single, endless crease.

I built this from the inside out, using only the shapes left behind when the light gets tired. Sometimes, in the corner, a forgotten curve waves at you. It remembers being a circle, but now it’s just a sigh with good posture.

Nothing moves here, yet everything is falling. The floor is made of held breath. The ceiling floats half an inch above itself, just to see what happens.

Do not touch the absence—it’s still wet.
