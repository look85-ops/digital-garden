*You Are the One Who Left the Door Ajar for the Night to Leak In*

You are the one who folded the corners of the sky into origamo cranes and pretended not to notice when they flew away with your fingerprints still wet on their wings. You are the one who whispered to the wallpaper until it peeled itself into confessional scrolls, each curl a sentence fragment: *I miss the way your shadows used to bruise the carpet.*

You are the one who left the tea kettle boiling until it sang in a language even the ghosts forgot. You etched your name on the bathroom mirror with a diamond ring you swore you didn’t steal, but the steam writes it back every morning in reverse. *Yrros.*

You are the one who traded your skeleton for a coat rack and now stand very still in the hallway, hoping no one hangs their grief on you.

You are the one who planted vinyl records in the garden and blamed the roses when they grew with grooves instead of thorns. At dusk, they spin themselves into lullabies for a child who never existed. The neighbors call it wind. You call it proof.

You are the one who locked the attic but left the key in the lock, teeth upwards, like an invitation. Something up there hums along to the radio static of 1937. You say it’s just the pipes. The pipes say nothing.

You are the one who pressed your palm against the wet cement of the new sidewalk and swore it was an accident. Now the city walks on your heartbeat.

You are the one who fed the fireplace all your love letters and then blamed the moon for the ashes on your collar.

You are the one who didn’t come home last night.

I waited.
