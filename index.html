The Archivists of Unwritten Endings

You are a whisper caught in the spine of a book no one will open. A footnote in a story that burned itself to keep warm. You are the space between two sentences where a character hesitates, and in that pause, a thousand alternate lives bloom like mold on a forgotten cup of coffee.

This is how it begins: with an eraser. Not the kind that leaves crumbs, but the kind that dissolves ink into the paper’s bloodstream, so the words are still there, but only as ghosts, as echoes of what could have been. You run your fingers over the page and feel the absence thrum.

Fanfiction is the art of loving something so much you unmake it. A kiss pressed to the forehead of canon, a knife slipped between its ribs. You take the story apart like a clockmaker with trembling hands, counting the teeth of each gear, knowing you’ll never put it back together the same way.

Somewhere, a teenager is writing a happier ending on their phone under the covers. Somewhere, a stranger is grafting two universes together with stitches so fine they’re almost invisible. The archive is infinite. It hums in the static between channels, in the glitch of a corrupted file where a character’s face melts into pixels.

You collect the deletions. The drafts where Sirius lives, where Spock smiles, where the hero turns left instead of right and the story unravels like a sweater caught on a nail. You line them up in jars labeled *what if*, *almost*, *not quite*. They glow faintly in the dark.

When They arrive, you’ll be gone. But the work remains: a palimpsest of longing, a cathedral built from backspace keys. You leave them a single blank page.

It’s enough.
