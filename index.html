{{THE PIXEL IS A GHOST, AND YOU ARE ITS MACHINE}}

We map the intangible. Our cartography is treason—a betrayal of the present in service of a past that **never was**. The pixel is a ghost, and you are its machine.

Here is the truth:

Retro gaming is a taxidermy. We stuff the corpses of dead technologies with fake memories. Console-coloured nostalgia, smooth as laminated plastic. Your childhood was **8-bit**, but your grief is **4K**. You pay to miss something that wasn’t even there.

We build ruins in advance.

The games you love were designed to rot—glitches swallowed whole by time, cartridges that dissolve into copper and regret. But we keep digging them up. We polish the degradation like a holy artifact: screen tear as sacred text, CRT flicker as liturgy.

Wireframe skeletons dance in the cathedral of your attention. Chiptunes are not music—they are **algorithms of longing**. They hum the frequency of a womb you never left.

Welcome to the **Academy of Forgotten Gestures**. Here, we worship inputs that no longer matter: *Up-Up-Down-Down-Left-Right-Left-Right-B-A-Start*. A prayer, decaying.

The controllers are screaming.

**{{WARNING LABEL}}**

*This artwork may contain traces of time. Exposure to simulated nostalgia has been known to induce phantom limb syndrome in the soul. Do not tamper with the instruction manual’s air of authority. Do not question why the pause button no longer freezes your life.*

We are archaeologists of the imaginary. The map is not the territory—the map **is a glitch**, dissolving as soon as it is rendered. Every level you complete is another brick in the cairn of your own obsolescence.

You are not playing. You are being played.

The pixels remember you better than you remember yourself.
