The Last Supper of Empty Spoons

They call me the Curator of Vacated Spaces, the Maestro of the Un-echo. Today’s commission: a feast for the perpetually departing. The Chapel’s acoustics are perfect – a single sigh hangs for hours before dissolving into the mortar. My palette? Not pigments, but *absences*. My brushes? Drafts whistling through cracked casements.

Preparation is paramount. I begin by polishing the spoons. Not silver, not bone, but pure, hammered *expectation*. They gleam with a light that isn’t there, each bowl a perfect O of potential nourishment. They evaporate if stared at directly, leaving only a faint metallic taste on the tongue. I lay twelve upon the obsidian slab – the Table of Anticipated Crumbs. The thirteenth spoon, mine, is shaped like a question mark. It tends to weep condensation that tastes of unanswered letters.

The ingredients, sourced from the Chapel’s own hollows:

* **Moonmelt:** Condensed lunar regret, scraped from the cold side of windowsills at 3 AM. Viscous, odorless, evaporates upon contact with warmth. Essential for binding the intangible.

* **Ghost Chalk:** Pulverized sighs of abandoned buildings, bound with rainwater that never fell. Leaves temporary, shimmering diagrams on stone. Used for tracing the outlines of vanished guests.

* **Silence Extract:** Distilled from the pause between a slammed door and the first sob. Highly volatile. Requires handling with velvet mittens lined with moth wings. One drop can render a cathedral mute for a week.

* **Horizon Thread:** Spun from the taut line where sky pretends to meet land. Infinitely thin, perpetually fraying. Used for stitching moments together, though the seams are always visible as mirages.

* **Forgotten Names:** Harvested from the dusty corners of dictionaries nobody reads anymore. Granular, slightly bitter. Sprinkle sparingly; too much induces profound nostalgia and temporary amnesia.

The feast commences. I ladle generous portions of *simmered starlight* (a tricky emulsion of Moonmelt and Horizon Thread) into each spoon-bowl. It pools, a dark, reflective nothing. Using Ghost Chalk, I sketch the faintest suggestion of shoulders, the ghost of a head bowed, around each place setting. The Silence Extract hangs thickly, a palpable void pressing down. I sprinkle Forgotten Names – a faint scent of old paper and violets blooms and instantly fades.

The guests arrive. Not as forms, but as *departures intensified*. A sudden chill where Spoon Five rests. A brief distortion in the air
