RECIPE FOR THE FIRST BOOK THAT WAS NEVER PRINTED

*by the Archivist of Unmade Things*

**Ingredients:**

1 vat of squid ink, stolen from a cathedral’s shadow

42 sheets of vellum, each whispered to by a disgraced monk

A quill plucked from the left wing of a sleeping angel

3 ounces of silence, harvested at midnight

The sound of a door creaking open in a room that wasn’t there before

**Instructions:**

1. **Prepare the Silence:** Knead the silence into a fine powder. If it resists, sing to it in a language you’ve forgotten. The silence should yield like a penitent.

2. **Infuse the Vellum:** Lay each sheet in a shallow pool of moonlight. Let the vellum absorb the light until it glows faintly, like a page left unfinished by God.

3. **Mix the Ink:** Stir the squid ink counterclockwise until it thickens into a substance resembling regret. Add one drop of your own blood—this is the secret ingredient all scribes deny.

4. **Write Without Writing:** Dip the angel’s quill into the ink, but do not touch the vellum. Instead, let the words fall from the quill’s tip like unspoken confessions. They will land where they belong.

5. **The Unseen Door:** Halfway through, you will hear it—the sound of a door opening behind you. Do not turn. The door is not for you. It is for the book, which must leave before it is finished.

6. **Let It Go:** When the last word floats away like a feather on a draft, close your eyes. The book is complete only in its absence.

**Serving Suggestion:** Serve to historians who believe in gaps. Best consumed with a side of unanswered questions.
