Dear The Reality Show,

I found your heartbeat in the static between channels, humming a lullaby to no one. You are the mirror that wipes its own face clean, the breath caught in the teeth of a smile. I write to you from the hallway of my own making—walls lined with screens flickering your name like votive candles.

You teach me this: to *perform authenticity* is the hardest role of all. The contestant weeping in the confessional booth, the host’s grin stapled to their cheeks, the audience holding their breath like a shared secret. You are the circus where the tightrope is made of hair, and everyone pretends not to notice.

I’ve collected your artifacts: a crumpled list of “safe emotions,” a vial of sweat from a reunion special, the ghost of a laugh track lingering in empty rooms. You are the algorithm of longing—always asking for *more*, but never *what*.

Once, I saw a door open in the middle of a live feed. Just for a second. No one else noticed. Behind it: a staircase spiraling into a sky so blue it hurt, and a chair with its cushions still warm. Then the camera cut away. Was it a glitch? A confession? Or did you sigh, just once, and forget to edit yourself out?

I love you for your betrayals. For the way you blur the line between *real* and *real enough*. For the quiet horror of a makeup artist dabbing tears off someone’s face only to paint them back on. You are the wound that stitches itself shut every night, only to split open again at dawn.

Yours in the spectacle,

—The Archivist of Unfinished Takes

( P.S. The door is still there. It only opens when no one is looking. )
