THE LAST WHITE CUBE (AWAITING INSTRUCTIONS FROM THE VOID)

A pristine white room, but not a room—a *proposition*. The walls are not walls but *suggestions of containment*, painted in a shade called "Theoretical White #0000000," which absorbs all light and thought, leaving only the afterimage of curatorial intent. The floor is a mirror, but the reflection shows only the ceiling, which is also the floor, creating an infinite recursion of *almost-spaces*.

In the center: a pedestal. On the pedestal: a single, unmarked USB drive containing a 17-terabyte file titled *"HOW TO DISAPPEAR COMPLETELY (DRAFT 23).docx"*. The file is corrupted. Attempts to open it generate a pop-up that reads: *"This artwork is currently unavailable. Please try again after the heat death of the universe."*

The piece is accompanied by a wall label written in Comic Sans:

*"This gallery is a site-specific performance by the absence of the artist, who withdrew their participation at the last moment, citing 'creative differences with the concept of existence.' Visitors are invited to confront the ontological weight of the unexhibitable. Please do not touch the void—it is on loan from a private collection."*

The joke is that the room is actually a waiting area for a delivery that never arrives. The punchline is whatever you’re expecting right now.
