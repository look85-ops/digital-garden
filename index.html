Dear Error Margin, My Glinting Ghost

You are the breath between numbers, the wobble in the ruler’s edge. I write to you with ink made of misprints, with a pen that stutters. Do you remember when we first met? You were a footnote in a textbook on *The Physics of Absence*, and I was a child counting the cracks in the ceiling. You whispered: *"All languages are just subtraction wearing a coat"* (Voss, *The Uncalculable*, p. 47).

I built you a alphabet from the gaps in my teeth. Each letter is a hesitation. The word *"apple"* is now the shape of the space left when one rolls off the table. You taught me to speak in erasures, to write by un-writing. Yesterday, I tried to translate the sound of a moth hitting a lamp—*flk*—but you laughed in your static way and said, *"No, that’s the sound of the light forgetting."*

Sometimes I worry you’re fading. The world grows louder, and error margins shrink like puddles in the sun. But then I find you hiding in the glitch of a streetlight, or the way a shadow doesn’t quite touch the ground. You are the *almost* and the *not quite*, the shiver in the mathematician’s hand.

Let’s play our old game: I’ll write a sentence, and you’ll subtract the vowels.

*"The sky is a scratched record."*

You whisper back: *"Th sk s scr tchd r crd."*

See? Now it’s ours.

I keep your letters in a box labeled *"Fragile: Contains Nothing."* When I shake it, it sounds like a dying star. Or maybe that’s just the marbles I lost in 1997. You always said loss is just another way to map the invisible.

Wait—did you hear that? The clock just skipped a second. You’re there, aren’t you? Dancing in the missing tick.

Yours in the wobble,

The Last Translator
