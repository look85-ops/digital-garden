BLUE SOUP FOR THE LONELY MOON

*(A Recipe to Gather the Sky in Your Palms)*

**Ingredients:**

1. A whisper stolen from a robin’s egg (unbroken).

2. The shadow of a forget-me-not, pressed between two sighs.

3. A handful of stolen ocean — the kind that clings to your ankles and begs you to stay.

4. The leftover light from a fridge door opening at 3 AM. (This is the secret. The door was not there before. It is now.)

5. One tear from a painter who just realized their last tube of ultramarine is empty.

6. A single thread from the frayed edge of the universe (blue, of course).

**Instructions:**

1. Begin at twilight, when the world forgets whether it’s coming or going. Light a candle made of frozen sky. Let it weep wax the color of longing.

2. In a pot that’s never been used (it must remember nothing), combine the whisper and the shadow. Stir counterclockwise until the air smells like a childhood summer.

3. Add the stolen ocean, but only after apologizing to it. The ocean is always grieving. It will forgive you if you hum in the key of distance.

4. When the mixture begins to murmur, open the fridge door that wasn’t there before. Catch the light before it escapes. Fold it gently into the pot. The soup will shiver. This is normal.

5. Stir in the tear. It will hiss. This is the sound of art being born.

6. Simmer until the steam rises in spirals, like a question mark. Then, pluck the universe’s thread and drop it in. Watch as the soup turns a blue so deep, it aches.

7. Serve in a bowl made of cupped hands. Eat it alone, under a moon that’s forgotten its own name. The door will close behind you. This is also normal.

**Notes:**

Do not share this soup. Blue is always lonely. That’s why we chase it.
