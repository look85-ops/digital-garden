Unsentimental Geometry: A Silence Folded Into Twelve Uneven Parts

There is nothing as violent as unwriting a name you once pressed deep into your palm to remember. That’s the thing about futures addressed as if they already exist—no one warns you (here) has no zip code. I paint with the white space around fingerprints lifted from doorknobs or hospital railings. Exhibit A: an envelope glued shut with saltwater. Exhibit B: (the curator levitates, feet tethered to the ceiling, then dissolves like unrecorded weather).

The archive of the undone is heavier than regret — stacked high with telegrams addressed *To Gravity*, postcards scrawled backward to decipher in a dream, suicide notes recanted in lemon juice. All I've left you are erasures shaped like sonograms. *Behold epistles unspooling beneath floorboards* [screams ossify into violins]. Even time moves inwards here. The future shrinks to the width of one unclaimed tooth clenched around a sentence never begun. Did you expect tenderness?

There is no moral in disappearing so precisely into static. This is not about the past growing moss on its underside.

It is what’s left of desire when all the names fall off (that was not yours on the return envelope), when geography becomes a slow argument of stains.
