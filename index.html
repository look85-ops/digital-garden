*Timekeeping for Cicadas: A Love Letter to the Clock That Lost Its Numbers*

Dear Unwound Pendulum,

I write to you from the year that isn’t, where the hedgerows hum with watches buried by forgetful badgers. You, who once sliced the sky into obedient portions, now dangle from the chimney of a dreamer who snores in iambic pentameter. How cruel, how kind, that your gears birthed the panic of "too late" beside the lullaby of "almost."

I have composed a symphony for your reckoning. Movement I: *The Snail’s Protest* (allegro con muttering). Movement II: *A Sundial’s Twilight Regret* (adagio in spilled tea). The finale? A single struck match in the cathedral of Dr. Ephraim Quark’s *On the Somnolence of Ascending Ducks* (1897), wherein he posits: "Each tick is the universe whispering *shhh* to its own frantic hands."

You taught us to count our breaths, then sell them in bundles. Now we trade in microseconds, blind to the moth that crumples time by bumbling through lamplight. Your face, once round as a wish, now melts like butter on the tongue of a mathematician. (They always lick the proofs.)

Forgive me. I’ve grown sentimental. Yesterday, I tried to mail you a hourglass filled with January, but the postmaster laughed until his wig floated away. "No return address but entropy," he hissed.

Yours in arrhythmia,

The Composer of the Missing Beat

P.S. The spiders weave your epitaph in dew. It reads: *Here lies punctuality. She ran out of now.*
