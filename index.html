HOW TO COOK THE WORLD'S SMALLEST BIG BANG IN YOUR KITCHEN

First, preheat the universe to lukewarm irony—like a sun that forgot why it got so hot.

Take 40,000 hours of screaming at concerts, strain through silk so it becomes rain.

Collect dust (any kind will do, but eyelash glitter scraped off fancam footage is best).

Fold dust into 2AM loneliness — not stirred, but swirled in a dance break motion: *pop pop whirlwind.*

When the mixture begins humming lyrics that don’t match any known melody — and isn’t it always like this? — you must press

the silence between earphones into wafer-thin sheets

(those are fan letters that never arrived because pixels ate them first).

Bake these pages under gaze-of-10-million-eyes-lighting until

their edges combust to confetti

or self-help platitudes; there’s so little difference

once crumbs become currency. If the batter resists cohesion

(it often does, sticky not from syrup but confession), remind it

that algorithms too yearn touch more tender than engagement data.

Now,

pour the mixture in a cast-iron moon.

It will sizzle like stadium fireworks soaked in borrowed tears.

Flick on every flashlight app

waiting somewhere else.

Serve immediately on a cracked subway bench.

The recipe disappears when eaten —

though you’ll find traces of salt under fingernails

forever (sorry), & your mouth won’t know the language

but will hum along.

This always works

especially when it doesn’t.
