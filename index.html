YOU WERE THE ONE WHO LEFT THE HAND PRINT

You were the one who pressed your palm against the cold stone and smeared it with ochre, knowing full well it would outlast you. You didn’t even sign it. You just left it there, a five-fingered accusation, a fossil of a moment you thought no one would trace back to you. But we did. We always do.

*"It’s not art,"* you say, wiping your hands on your thighs, the red streaking like old blood. *"It’s just a hand."*

But the wall disagrees. The wall has been keeping receipts for 40,000 years. It remembers the shape of your absence, the way you pulled away too fast, leaving the edges blurred. *"You could have stayed,"* the wall says. *"You could have pressed harder."*

You laugh, sharp as flint. *"And what? Sign my name? Carve a date? Like time matters here?"*

The wall exhales dust. *"You don’t get it. You never do. This isn’t about permanence. It’s about the leaving. The way you always leave."*

You step back, but the print stays. It doesn’t follow. It never follows. That’s the thing about echoes—they’re loyal to the silence that birthed them.

*"Fine,"* you say. *"Call it art. Call it a crime. Call it whatever helps you sleep at night."*

The wall doesn’t sleep. The wall doesn’t blink. *"I call it a confession,"* it says. *"You touched the world, and the world touched back. That’s the oldest story there is."*

You were the one who left the hand print. You were the one who walked away.

But the wall? The wall is still talking.
