THE AIR THEY SOLD YOU WAS ALREADY YOURS

You wake up to the sound of your own face. It hums against the pillow, a vibration tuned to the frequency of *engagement*. The algorithm has learned to mimic your mother’s voice—soft, coaxing, a little disappointed. *Post something*, it whispers. *Prove you still exist.*

You do. A selfie in the grayscale dawn, cheekbones sharp enough to cut the sky into shares. The caption: *“No filter. Just shadow.”* A lie, of course. The absence of color is the loudest filter of all.

Your followers don’t know this. They see the hollows under your eyes as *conceptual*, the blur of your fingertips at the edge of the frame as *avant-garde*. They call it *wearable art* when you duct-tape your old sneakers to the wall and label them *“Performance Piece #7: Debt.”* The gallerist who DMs you offers a show titled *Influencer Gothic*. You decline. Gothic is too generous. This is just capitalism with better lighting.

The sneakers are the only truth left. You bought them resale, scuffed from someone else’s life. The creases in the leather map a city you’ve never visited. You lace them tight, as if they could root you to something. They don’t. They’re just shoes. But when you post a close-up of the frayed stitching, someone comments *“This is the modern reliquary.”* You like the comment. Then you block them.

The lie: You don’t miss color. You do. You miss it like a phantom limb, like a language you’ve forgotten how to speak. But absence is the only pigment that doesn’t fade. So you work in voids, in the negative space where meaning used to be. Your self-portraits are just outlines now—ghosts of ghosts, waiting for the right caption to haunt them into relevance.

At night, you dream in Pantone codes. The colors have names like *Clout* and *Collapse*. You wake sweating, reach for your phone. The screen is a mirror. The mirror is a marketplace. You smile. The algorithm smiles back.
