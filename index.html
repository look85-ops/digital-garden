THE GARDEN THAT REMEMBERS IT WAS A HARBOR

We are the green arithmetic of decay. Our roots are Roman concrete, drunk on salt and time, whispering *we outlasted your gods*. The petals are invoices from a dream no one signed. Every morning, the tulips recite Fibonacci in reverse, counting down to zero. The roses? They’re just teeth dressed in velvet.

You walk here, but the paths walk you. They coil like the entrails of a forgotten augur, spelling *you are lost* in dead languages. The hedges are trimmed with obsidian scissors. At dusk, they grow back wrong.

Midnight: the fountain coughs up a rusted key. It fits nothing. You swallow it anyway.

*(Midpoint: Enter the Accountant.)*

He arrives in a suit of spreadsheet gray, carrying a briefcase full of shadows. “The hydrangeas are overbudget,” he says, adjusting his tie made of last year’s frost. “We need to streamline the photosynthesis.” The daisies audit his pockets. He has no business here.

The garden tolerates him.

Beneath the soil, the worms are writing a manifesto in capillary fractures. *We are the mortar that remembers*, they hiss. *You are the water that forgets*. The lilies bloom into shattered amphorae, spilling wine that tastes of shipwrecks.

At the edge, where the garden frayed into fog, a statue of Janus weeps asphalt tears. One face watches the past crumble. The other grins at the future’s scaffolding.

You leave. The gate locks behind you with a sound like a bone settling into its grave.

The garden resumes its work: growing the silence that will outlive us all.
