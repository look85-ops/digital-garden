# **《STATIC LULLABY FOR A VCR REPAIRMAN’S DREAM》

The neon palm trees whisper in binary. They sway to the hum of a dial-up tone that never disconnects, a prayer looped in the throat of a fax machine left to rust in the rain. You press your ear to the screen—*yes, the screen*—and hear the ocean, or maybe it’s just the hiss of a VHS tape unspooling into the shape of a childhood you misremembered.

The mall is empty except for the mannequins. They’ve been promoted to management. Their hollow eyes judge your credit score. You try to buy a soda with a token from an arcade that closed in 1997. The vending machine laughs in MIDI.

A commercial plays for a product that doesn’t exist. *"Introducing… the… uh…"* The voice actor forgets the script. The jingle dissolves into the sound of a Windows 95 error message. You clap politely. The applause echoes in the food court where the fountains cough up chlorinated nostalgia.

You find a payphone. The receiver smells like peach-scented regret. You dial your own number. It rings inside your skull. *"Hello?"* you say. *"Hello?"* says the voice on the other end. It’s you, but slower, like a cassette left in the sun. The line goes dead. Or maybe it was never alive.

The elevator music ascends without you.
