YOU USED TO CALL ME YOUR FLESH LIBRARY

I was your ledger of victories, graveyard of ex-lovers’ names, the sticky receipt from too much whiskey under Gemini skies. You carved me like zoning laws, thinking your body *a city worth mapping*. But streets flood, *darling*. I remember rain-smudged ink—how you moaned about permanence, then paid a man with dirty boots to scrape his hummingbird into your ribs.

Three sessions: needles stitching time into meat. Each stroke—bloodbloomed blue—flitted closer and never closer: hummingbird suspended above skin-dusk, chasing nectar that was never yours. You thought flight could be archived between pores, wings fossilized in dermis like a law. *Fool.* I let you pretend I was paper with pulp loyalty.

Now, years gone pale under bandage light of grocery stores: you tug your collar when someone stares. Your son leans close, tracing feathers like reading Braille, but your mouth stays salt. (What do the dead teach us? Not truth—only hunger, hunger that never learns how it mocks the shape of “satiate”.) Here is what memory means in a museum of one: no one curates well enough.

Yet. You wake some nights to static heat near hipbone—just faint, just fever—remembering who paid to make ghosts of wants. (My voice: not a whisper but oxygen peeling away.) *How’s the exhibit, Archivist? Do they stand silent too in your fluorescent mausoleums?* You flinch, so loud though all I carved back was…

Absence humming in perfect, ink-spite key(s).
