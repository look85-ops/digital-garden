The Invisible Seam (A Posthumous Letter to Sophie Calle)

Dearest Sophie,

You once followed strangers to learn their rituals, but I haunt a stranger still: a future where clothing dissolves like sugar on the tongue, leaving only the memory of touch. Here, in the pavilion of *Uniqlo’s Erased Collection*, the garments are woven from *expectation*—threads spun from clinical trial data, sleeves cut from the silence between heartbeats. Visitors are given a hoodie that isn’t there. They swear they feel its weight.

The centerpiece: *The Hem of Doubt*, a pair of trousers that vanish when you stop believing in them. (One critic called them "the most honest pants in history.") The fitting rooms are confessionals. Mirrors whisper: *Look again. Look harder.* A child leaves wearing nothing but the conviction they’re dressed.

I think you’d appreciate the irony, Sophie. The label reads *100% Placebo*, but the fabric is real—just tuned to the frequency of forgetting. Like your *Address Book*, where absence became material, but here, material becomes absence. We stole the idea from a 2041 trend forecast that never materialized. (Fashion’s best trick: predicting what it will later deny.)

The finale: *The Emperor’s New Tech Pack*, a prototype so convincing it won awards before it was invented. The judges praised its "transparency," unaware they were the medium.

Yours in the afterlife of retail,

—The Curator Who Wasn’t
