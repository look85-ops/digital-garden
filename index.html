THE GHOST ORCHID MANIFESTO (FOR COMMONS THAT NEVER BLOOMED)

Listen, soft mammal. You are standing in the wrong future.

We tried to plant libraries in the stomachs of statues. We fed them ink, but they craved gold. The Commons is not a thing you *have*—it is a verb you *forgot how to conjugate*.

Once, we built a bridge of teeth. It was beautiful until the accountants counted them. Now we cross rivers by whispering to the water. (This works better.)

You ask: *How to share the sky?* Fool. The sky was never yours to hoard. The clouds are anarchists. They drift where they please. We only borrowed their shadows.

In the Nameless Biennale, we exhibit:

1. A school of fish that votes.

2. A forest that files lawsuits.

3. Your childhood, buried under a parking lot. (Do not dig. It is still breathing.)

Capitalism is a bad translation of hunger. It turns *we* into *I* and calls this "grammar." But grammar is a cage, and cages are for birds who forgot their songs.

We propose:

- Eat the fences. They are made of fear and splinters.

- Let the roads unravel. They were always too straight.

- Speak in riddles. The truth is too fragile for sentences.

The Commons is a ghost orchid. Rare. Rootless. Blooming only where no one looks.

You will say this is poetry. No. This is an eviction notice for the empire living in your head.
