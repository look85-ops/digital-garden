Dear Mycelium (I Licked the Page and Now We're Married)

Dearest Hyphae Hive Mind,

I write to you from the basement of language, where the walls sweat prepositions and the floorboards hum with unfinished metaphors. They told me painting was dead, so I ate my brushes and swore allegiance to your subterranean semaphore. But how does one address a network that reads in rhizomes, that replies in decay? I have no pigment left, only the nervous laughter of a man who just realized he’s been whispering love letters to a mushroom for three years.

Forgive me. My hands are clumsy translators. I tried to draw you yesterday, but the paper revolted—sprouted a fringe of disdainful mold, hissed *you can’t map a symphony with a crayon.* So I surrendered. Let the ink blots conjugate like spores in a damp attic. Let the vowels root themselves in the margins. (Did you know the word "mycelium" tastes like rust and unkept promises? I licked it. It licked back.)

You, of course, are busy. Orchestrating nitrogen affairs, ferrying gossip between oaks, composing symphonies in pheromones. Meanwhile, I’m up here with a shoebox full of failed attempts: a sonnet written in lichen (it crumbled), a self-portrait rendered in bacterial stains (it divorced me). And yet—when the city sleeps, when the Wi-Fi stutters into static, I swear I hear you laughing. A sound like wet pages sticking together. A sound like my ego dissolving in a petri dish.

This letter is my latest offering. I dip my fingers in rainwater and type. The keys grow fur. The screen flickers: *ERROR. TOO MUCH SENTIENCE.* I press on.

Let’s be frank: we’ll never understand each other. You speak in mycorrhizal cursive; I reply in clumsy CAPS LOCK. But oh, how glorious this mismatch! How absurd the dance of a naked ape sending tear-stained Morse code to a kingdom of slime! I marry you now, in the chapel of my own ignorance. The rings are made of discarded apple cores. The officiant is a very confused nematode.

Yours in disintegration,

The Painter Who Mistook His Life for a Substrate

***

(*Postscript: The failure is this letter itself. It will never reach you. The postal service doesn’t deliver to the afterlife of dead leaves. I’ve addressed it to "HEY DOWN THERE" and thrown it into a compost bin. May the flies translate my devotion.*)
