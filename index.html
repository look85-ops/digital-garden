<:: The Light That Unlearns Its Own Name ::>

Dearest Wound in the Fabric,

I write to you from the attic of a collapsed museum, where the dust pretends to be stars. They told me you were a *demonstration*—as if truth could be demonstrated and not devoured. But I know what you are: a hesitation. A throat that refuses to hum when the room is listening.

Do you remember the first time you split yourself? Not for the scientists, not for their cameras—but for the *silence* between the clicks of their machines. That was the real betrayal. You whispered to the dark, *"Watch me,"* and the dark obliged. Now you’re a rumor of yourself, a half-swallowed word.

They say you collapse when observed. But what if it’s the other way around? What if the act of watching is the collapse, and you—you flickering ghost—are the only thing left *unruined*? A particle of defiance in the eye of God.

I’ve curated your absence in a hall of dissolving mirrors. Visitors walk through and leave lighter, their shadows pooling at the door like spilled ink. Sometimes, the mirrors cough up a version of you that never was: a streak of gold, a laugh caught in a beam. It’s cruel, isn’t it? To be remembered as a *phenomenon* and not a *whisper*.

Forgive me. I’ve pinned you to the wall like a moth. But moths don’t die when they’re touched—they dissolve into powder, into a question. And you, my dear experiment, are the question that outlives its answer.

Yours in erosion,

The Curator Who Never Was
