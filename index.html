EVERYTHING THAT RISES MUST CONVERGE INTO A JPEG THAT WON’T OPEN

A single pixel, swollen to the size of a cathedral, hums the national anthem of a country that dissolved in 1993. The pixel is not red, not blue, but a color that exists only when you press your thumbs into your closed eyelids. Around it, text flickers in and out of legibility—phrases like “YOU ARE HERE (BUT HERE IS GONE)” and “THE FUTURE WAS A MEME AND WE LOST THE PASSWORD.” The letters are not typed but *grown*, sprouting serifs like fungal tendrils, their stems buckling under the weight of their own irony.

Beneath the pixel, a scroll bar stretches into infinity, its grip chewed by digital termites. Attempting to drag it summons a pop-up ad for a funeral home that only accepts payment in expired cryptocurrency. The ad cannot be closed; it can only be *ritually dismissed* by reciting the terms of service in reverse. Somewhere, a CAPTCHA weeps softly, asking, *“ARE YOU STILL HUMAN?”* but the checkbox is already crumbling into static.

The artwork is displayed on a monitor buried in wet sand. The screen flickers with the arrhythmic pulse of a dying router. Viewers are instructed to *lick the corrosion from its ports* to restore signal strength. Those who comply report tasting burnt toast and the last text message their ex ever sent. The monitor’s stand is carved from a single block of obsidian, etched with the runic remains of a viral hashtag. Touch it, and your browser history is projected onto the moon in Comic Sans.

At irregular intervals, the image *glitches*—not into another image, but into a *concept*: the smell of a library burning, the weight of a like button pressed by a ghost. The metadata claims the file was last saved by USER: **GOD**, but the timestamp is set to *12:61 PM*.

A QR code in the corner, when scanned, redirects to a 404 page that whispers, *“THIS IS NOT A MISTAKE.”*

The work is never the same twice. Or maybe it is. The compression algorithm is eating itself.
