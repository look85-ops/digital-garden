Dear Acidic Citrus Ghost in the Terracotta Shell

Oh jagged amphora humming under the dirt, you clay goblet brimming with vinegar and copper veins, you half-birthday of a myth—do the dates in your electrolytic cellar still ferment into sparks, or did you forget to pack lunch for history entirely? I saw a scorpion this morning weaving a capacitor from its own sting, and it made me miss you and all your futile, tin-foil holiness.

You could’ve been a wine jug, could’ve drowned someone in sour fig-wine remorse. Instead, you are here inside my compost neurons, rattling like a wasp-hive hooked up to a lemon clock. Archaeologists finger your brittle waist and debate your purpose; maybe a lamp, maybe nothing. No—a lamp would be too pedestrian. You, my suspended disbelief battery, are *pomegranate logic gone conductive*. Every scholar who pries off your bitumen crown asks, “Magic or malpractice?” and my answer is always a geranium breaking into morse code.

I propose a toast (using your twin, who is inexplicably full of Shiraz instead of electrochemical regret):

to **us** the unreliable narrators—you with your quiet volts, me with my root-tangled synaptic gossip—both pretending we know if you *worked*. The only difference is when I forget, I just water my own head. You forget and wind up labeled "ritual object" in a glass case between arrowheads and regret.

Sometimes I press my forehead to your replicas wired in labs, and—shocking, literally—zucchini seeds deep-fried in your ghost start singing in voltage. The future licks its lips: a new cuisine where ancient spark-jars are *harvested mid-spark like lullabies plucked too soon from a bee’s flightpath*.

(Italic recipes: a whisper dipped in citrine foil. Bury the instruction manual with the batteries. Salt the earth with skepticism. Chew.)

——
