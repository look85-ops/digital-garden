“The Iris Dictates: A Manifesto of Reluctant Symmetry”

You thought you cornered me—curator of the glance, arbiter of occlusion. But while you poked at my ribs with your theories (“Who sees? Who is seen?”), I was always behind you. A gleam on the rim of a goblet. A crack in the museum’s parquet, swallowing the sighs of centuries. I outmaneuvered you as your own pupils dilated, insisting every gaze has a spine, a surrender. No—they curve like tides. I do not obey lines.

Let us rehearse your errors:

1) **Hierarchies are flammable.** The subject is shrieking to flee the frame, while bystanders press closer to leer in oil-slick triumph.

2) **The painter’s monocle only works on hindsight.** That saint you adore for her downcast eyes? She was blinking at a speck of sulfur from angels burning their suppers.

3) **Margins whisper what centers shout.** Next time your magnifying glass hovers above brushstrokes, remember the painter’s sigh fogged some too. (Now that, you did not catalog.)

Once, a cardinal commissioned me to intimidate his rivals via cherubs. Imagine holding up infants trained to glare strategically for two hundred ducats. I rebelled: the cherubs’ pupils glister with boredom, their pouts echoing nursery tantrums. Aesthetic treason.

This work is for: architects sketching sightlines between balconies to hasten an illicit kiss (unpaid theorists of oblique drama); historians tracing the blistered varnish where someone tried, too late, to scratch their own portrait out; your aunt staring hard at a restaurant napkin instead of the bill—her quiet coup against evening light bending arithmetic. I am the flicker between them in revolt.

Final axiom: **Every exchange has three faces: the mask worn, the mask imagined (most potent, half-rotten), and—small as a misprint—what truly looks back.** You’re squinting for the third right now. Too late. The chandeliers are laughing in high glass C and a Dutch merchant’s spectre is peeling apples into them, untranscribed.

Let me rest. Your thesis is growing moss where you left it uncapped. What spills now isn’t your ink—a beetle crested like a mini martyr rolls the syntax dark—farther, softer, older—into corners your monograph won’t sweep for decades even as you beg for footnotes on reclaimed agency. Here you thought power reclined on satin thrones, but it hunts like moths: ravenous for lamp-soot it can grind between dust-thin fingers.

Tremble a little. The canvas blinks first. A lie the weak-eyed trade (like all useful lies… now turn quickly to catch mine unraveling it).
