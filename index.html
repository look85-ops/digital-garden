The Cathedral of Unheard Frequencies

You are standing in a room where the walls are made of static. Not the kind that hisses between radio stations, but the kind that hums in the marrow of your bones—a low, insistent thrum of *almost-information*. The news cycle has become a liturgy here, recited by voices that never pause to breathe. You kneel at the altar of the placebo effect, where the body believes so fiercely in the cure that it forgets the disease was ever real.

A study published in *The Journal of Phantom Phenomena* found that patients who ingested headlines instead of sugar pills experienced "a 73% reduction in perceived gravity" (Voss, 2027). The mind, that eager alchemist, turns leaden fear into gold. Or at least into something lighter than air. You press your palm to the screen—warm, pulsing—and wonder if the heat is yours or the machine’s. The distinction dissolves like salt in water.

Outside, the city is a chorus of unanswered questions. A man on the corner sells whispers in glass vials. *Guaranteed authentic*, he says, though authenticity is just another kind of fiction. You buy one, hold it to your ear. The sound is familiar, like a childhood lullaby sung backward.

The placebo is a silence that pretends to be a song. The news cycle is a song that pretends to be silence. Between them, you exist as a kind of interference pattern—a body shaped by absences, by the weight of what never quite arrived.

You close your eyes. The static thickens. Somewhere, a bell rings in a frequency only the dead can hear.
