The Diary of a Cartographer Whose Map Ate Its Own Legend

*[The following text is carved into the side of a melting glacier, the letters pooling at the base like alphabet soup left out in the rain.]*

**Day 17:** The coastline keeps changing its mind. I draw a bay, and it becomes a yawn. I sketch a peninsula, and it shrugs off its skin like a bored mollusk. Today, I tried to map the exact moment a thought becomes a thing—the hinge between "maybe" and "marmalade." Instead, I found a door labeled "EXIT" that opened into a broom closet full of expired adjectives.

The compass spins like a dog chasing its tail, which is fitting because I’m fairly certain the North Pole is just a collective delusion invented by penguins. (Or was it polar bears? The map is unclear. The map is always unclear.)

I met a man who claimed to be the last surviving metaphor. He said, "I am the bridge between what is and what could be," then dissolved into a puddle of mixed tenses. I tried to bottle him, but the jar leaked similes.

**Day 18:** The ink refuses to dry. Every line I draw bleeds into the next, until the page is a Rorschach test administered by a sleep-deprived squid. I asked the sky for advice; it replied with a haiku made of falling birds.

I’ve started leaving breadcrumbs, but they keep evolving into full loaves, then bakeries, then entire economies based on sourdough futures. I can’t tell if I’m mapping the terrain or being digested by it.

**Day 19:** Found a note in my own handwriting that reads: "Beware the legend—it bites." Too late. The map’s key has grown teeth and is gnawing on the equator. I’ve taken to writing in spirals, hoping the shape will trap the meaning before it evaporates.

The horizon line just filed for divorce. It says we’ve grown apart.
