*Dear Seawater Incarcerated in Masonry, or, How to Hold a Continent at Knifepoint Without Getting Your Sleeves Wet*

Dearest Waterway in Straitjacket,

You are not a river. You are an insult gouged into the Earth’s spine by a dentist who mistook geography for a rotten tooth. You gargle with ships. You swallow nations like a vaudeville magician swallowing swords—badly, with a lot of coughing, and everyone can see the trick except the volunteers from the audience.

They told me you split a continent, but I know better. You are the zipper on God’s old pair of trousers, jammed halfway, rusted shut with salt and compromise. Every tug of your locks is the sound of a continent holding its breath.

I dreamt you last night: a concrete choir singing *"We Crossed It"* in twelve languages, all off-key. The birds here don’t fly; they file paperwork. The fish wear tiny hard hats and unionize. Someone built a waterfall upside down and called it *"economy."*

Forgive me. I am only the archivist of things undone, and you—you are the *done* so aggressively *done* it loops back into absurdity. A ditch so ambitious it became philosophy. A knife so sharp it carved the hand that held it.

Do you ever tire of being a metaphor? You must be exhausted. Let me tuck you into bed: here, the pillow is made of silt, the blanket is a map of all the rivers you displaced whispering *"traitor"* in their sleep. The alarm clock is set for *"never."*

Yours in hydraulic reverence,

The Archivist of Unforced Errors

P.S. Please stop haunting my dreams. Or at least bring snacks next time.
