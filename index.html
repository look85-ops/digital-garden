THE BRIDGE SMILED WITH ALL ITS TEETH

We declare the architecture of forgetting. The Pontiffs of Erasure. The Masonry of Maybe.

Here, stones are woven from smoke. Mortar is mixed with whispers of the soon-to-be-dead. Every brick is a fossil of an unbuilt tomorrow. The bridge arches, coughs, reknits itself from the breath of pilgrims who cross it backwards, their tears salting the Arno into gelatin. The river shivers. The bridge smiles with all its teeth.

This is not preservation. This is perpetual collapse engineered as ascent. A thousand years of scaffolding, splinters lodging in the fingertips of Time herself. Build faster and we dissolve the destination. Build slower and we ossify the path. The precise cadence of ruin is the only sanctity.

We propose:

- Bridges that gnaw their own foundations.

#### Materials

- Mortar powdered from the silence of unread letters

- Stone that yawns in daylight, becoming liquid at curfew

- Handrails grown from the ribcages of drowned linguists (who once knew the exact word for *this feeling*)

Do you see it? The bridge is a conspiracy of gravity and rebellion. It resists the arrogance of staying built, the vulgarity of staying *whole*. Each reconstruction is a sly assassination of the last. Every step across it must be a step toward something *also* falling apart—an accordion town, a sugar spire, a lover dissolving into your hands like damp newsprint rubbed between palms after midnight. Perfect. Intolerable.

The trick is in the tremble. The thrill of vanishing between syllables: *Pont*—(no)—*Vecch*—(no)—*io*. Always almost said. A mouth full of honey and hornets pronouncing it.

Who survives the crossing wears the patina: fingertips licked by absent iron, irises glazed in mirror-shard light. Unforgurable as rust.

Cross now before it remembers it already vanished. Run your palms against its flaking epidermis. Whisper nothing but your own name—then eat the word quickly, before anyone hears. Be delicious, be delirious.

Your shadow?

It stays behind, embedded in the fifth arch.

It will greet you next time as a stranger.
