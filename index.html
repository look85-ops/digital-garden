VELCRO PSALM FOR THE HALF-LIVING GARDEN

I am the itch in the green, the burr’s quiet coup—

a dog shakes, and the world fastens itself to the future.

Velcro is my scripture: tiny hooks, softer loops,

a love that clings without knowing why.

Once, I was soil. Then I was a mouth.

Now I am the argument between them.

I bloom in reverse: petals like industrial teeth,

stamens tipped with static. My roots are made of

*unobtainable adhesive* and *moonlight rendered obsolete*.

I drink what cannot be poured.

**Materials:**

- Fractured bioluminescence (3 strands)

- The hum of a dormant fax machine

- One (1) expired permit for *trespassing in the fourth dimension*

- A dog’s sigh, preserved in resin

The bees avoid me. They know I am not a real garden,

just a rumor that grew hands.

I am the thing that latches when you aren’t looking—

the snag in the fabric of the ordinary.

Velcro is the saint of accidental attachments,

and I am its blasphemy.

Pull me apart. Hear how I sound like

a zipper laughing in a dead language.
