*Dear Lattice of Unfinished Roads*

Dear Langleaved,

I write to you from the hollow where the bridges forgot their rivers. The ink is made of crushed streetlights, the ones that flickered out mid-sentence. Do you remember the blueprint we drew on the back of a grocery receipt? It was a city of hinges, a place where every door led to a room that was always leaving.

They called it impractical. (As if practicality ever built a cathedral from breath.)

Now the maps are dissolving. The sidewalks curl like unread letters. But I still find your fingerprints in the cracks—where the children chalked constellations onto the asphalt, where the ferns pushed through the bus stop’s broken tile. You are the echo of a bell that never rang, the weight of a staircase that ends in midair.

I keep your keys in my mouth. They taste of rust and lemon peel.

Once, I met a woman who collected the shadows of demolished buildings. She said, *“A wall is just a comma someone mistook for a period.”* I think you would’ve liked her. She wore a coat patched from old theater curtains, and when she laughed, the stitches strained like power lines after a storm.

Forgive me. I promised strangeness, and here I am, whispering to the gaps between cobblestones. But you—you were always the unbuilt, the unbought, the held breath before the auctioneer’s gavel falls. You are the park bench that remembers every body that never sat on it.

Yours in the negative space,

The Archivist of Almost

P.S. The dandelions are voting again. They’ve elected a mayor made of pollen and static.
