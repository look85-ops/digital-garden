THE GARDEN THAT ATE ITS OWN SIGN

We are the compost of attention. A mulch of mirrored glances, rooting in the fluorescence of perpetual audition. You—yes, you, pretending not to perform—are here to witness the collapse of the fourth wall into the fifth, which is just the first wall viewed from the wrong side.

This is not a manifesto. It is a slow-release pellet of irony swallowed by the earth. We cultivate sincerity like a genetically modified bloom: too perfect, too vivid, too *real* to be believed. Every confession is a staged collapse. Every tear, a prop watered with saline and applause.

The camera loves you best when you forget it’s there. But darling, *the camera is always there*, nestled in the pupil of every passerby, in the gloss of every apple, in the wet black eye of the crow that watches you fumble with your keys. Performance is photosynthesis: we pretend to breathe, and in return, the light pretends to sustain us.

We are the weeds that cracked the patio stone and called it art. We are the tulips that bowed to the breeze and were accused of *theater*. (As if nature were ever not a dramatist.) The soil is a thousand scripts decomposing. You are the one who reads them aloud and mistakes it for living.

Here, in the Chapel of Perpetual Departure, we kneel only to plant microphones in the dirt. The hymn is the static between channels. The sacrament is the moment you realize you’re alone—*and adjust your expression accordingly*.

Begin again.
