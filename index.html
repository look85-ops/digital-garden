The Distance Between A Question Mark And Its Shadow

**Theremin (leaning in, quivering):**

Do you hear it? That invisible vibrato? The one that stutters when you forget air is still touching something? That’s how I haunt acoustics — by being louder when no one’s brave enough to say “unseen.”

**Conductor (rolling a baton of dried ink beneath gloved fingers):**

I used to believe pauses held crescendos. Then I noticed: your hands sculpt sound like hesitation scalds kisses. Half-gestures! The first law of vanishing—

(Half the orchestra stands in a room none of them remember entering.)

**Theremin (arch):**

Oh, you’d adore silence if it wore gloves. Everyone does. Me? I caress the unmeant notes. Those frequencies unloop shoelaces. *Who unties the sky before noon?* **Your shadow, probably.**

(*Elsewhere, a radio briefly picks up traffic reports from a city where streets cross in lamentations.*)

**Conductor (spilled ink now branching into veins along the floor):**

Ah. But conductors don’t have shadows. Just echoes with teeth marks. What does a mute chord taste like, though? Split quavers and blackened harp strings?

(*The sentence “Herons invented regret mid-fall” drifts in — wrong language, but nobody stops it.*)

**Theremin (hands now slicing air like paper cuts on moonlit hymns):**

Bold of teeth to exist near instruments they can bite. **Listen: every concert begins by renaming loneliness.** The only fermata is… well.

(*And then, because irony demanded asymmetry: all scores turn to origamic tombstones inside out.*)

**Conductor (bathed in static, mouth full of moth scales from applause circa 1937):**

Tell me your E isn’t really an exhumed comma—

—Wait. Forget it was a question. Just. *Dance closer backwards.*
