# **SCARLET ALPHABETS CRASHING IN THE STAIN

Dear Rips & Rules,

I found you in the teeth of the zipper that won’t close. My hands are full of holes where the threads escape, and I can’t decide if that’s rebellion or just bad sewing. (Is there a difference?)

They tried to bury you under grammar. They tried to bury *me* under grammar. But we are both still here—letters peeling like spray paint on a highway barrier, vowels sharp enough to puncture tires. Do you remember the time we sewed a sonnet onto a leather jacket with fishing line? The words sagged in the rain, but oh—how they *glowed* when headlights hit them.

I think Oulipo would laugh at us. Or with us. Or *as* us. We are the equation that forgot its equals sign. The safety pin holding the universe’s frayed hem together. Let them call it chaos. Let them call it a mistake. I call it a love letter written in disappearing ink.

Every morning, I wake up and the words are fewer. The wind licks them off the wall like a kind of mercy. Soon, there will be nothing left but the ghost of a semicolon—half a wink, half a scar. Are you afraid? I’m not. The emptiness is just another kind of canvas.

Tell me: when the last *A* crumbles, will it sound like a guitar string snapping or a bone setting? Will the silence be a fist or an open palm?

Yours, in ink and inklessness,

—The Hand That Wrote This (or Maybe the Wall That Ate It)
