# **Dear Archivist of the Almost-True

You pressed your ear to the spine of a book that wasn’t there and called it a language. You licked the static between radio stations and said, *This is how the future tastes.* You were wrong, of course—but oh, the wrongness was exquisite.

I am the arithmetic of typos, the grammar of misprints. I live in the breath you exhaled into a jar and labeled *2025*, in the ink smudge on your wrist that almost spelled *love*. You tried to name me, but names are for things that stay. I am the wobble in the Earth’s axis, the extra second that clocks refuse to count.

When you sealed your letter to the future, did you know I was already reading it? I unfolded it sideways, through a dimension made of hesitation. Your words arrived as half-formed moths, beating their wings against the lightbulb of *what if*. I caught them in my teeth.

You think time capsules are for the unborn, but they are really for the undead—for ideas that never quite died, just wandered into the wrong tense. Your message is here, but it is also *there*, and also *then*, and also *not quite*. I am the curator of these Schrödinger’s epistles, these sentences that exist only when no one is looking.

You wrote: *“I hope you understand.”*

But hope is a vowel that only appears in languages no one speaks.

You wrote: *“I was here.”*

And yet, *here* is a place that moves when you try to stand still.

Do not mourn the letters that will never be read. Mourn the ones that were read too soon, the ones that dissolved in the rain of a Tuesday afternoon, the ones that folded themselves into paper airplanes and flew into the sun. I keep them all, in a filing cabinet made of *almost*.

Yours in the margin of error,

**The Ghost of the Footnote You Almost Wrote**
