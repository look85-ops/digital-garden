The Solitude of Parallel Ascensions

**Fact:** The double helix staircase at Chambord Castle was designed so two people could climb simultaneously without ever meeting.

**Fabrication:**

**A:** Do you think the architect wept when he finished it?

**B:** No. He laughed until his ribs ached. Then he walked the steps alone, pretending to be two people.

**A:** That’s sadder.

**B:** Only if you believe in meeting.

**A:** I don’t. But I believe in the *almost*. The way light bends around a corner just before you turn it. The way a name lingers on your tongue after the person has left the room.

**B:** Then you’ll love this staircase. It’s a monument to *almost*. A shrine to the space between breaths.

**A:** What if I shouted your name?

**B:** I’d hear it through the stone. A muffled rumor of you.

**A:** Would you answer?

**B:** I’d hum the tune you hate. You’d know it was me.

**A:** That’s cruel.

**B:** No, it’s intimacy. To be known by your irritations. To be a burr in someone’s sock.

**A:** I’d rather be a locket.

**B:** Lockets get lost. Burrs travel.

**A:** You’re impossible.

**B:** And yet, here we are. Forever almost.

**A:** What’s at the top of the stairs?

**B:** A door that only opens if you arrive alone.

**A:** What’s behind it?

**B:** A room full of every version of you that didn’t happen.

**A:** That’s horrifying.

**B:** Or hilarious. Imagine them all arguing over who’s the real one.

**A:** I’d join in.

**B:** Of course you would.

**A:** Would you?

**B:** I’d watch from the staircase.

**A:** Still not meeting me.

**B:** Still almost.

**A:** ...Do you think the architect ever regretted it?

**B:** Every day. But he kept building staircases.
