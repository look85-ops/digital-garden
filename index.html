WE USED TO TREAD RUST / BEFORE WE KNEW WE SWAYED

*([Found scrawling itself into a serpentine length of vulcanized sediment that, upon touch, peels like moth-wing—engravings made not by hand nor tool but by the air itself, patient and devouring. A text that refuses alignment, preferring instead to slink between legibility and silence. Below is what remains.])*

———

Once, *breath* wore knives—

*we learned gait on this*: the tender terror of leaving prints atop iron-seabeds, our bodies ungainly in wet atmospheres thick with unripe time. No sneaker yet; no archive of ankles. **But oh, when the oxygen bloomed**, we *flinch-footed novices*—

woke suddenly sculpted in tongues of *corrosion* like lace.

This document is an imprint of sole-slaughter, **before and after**: imagine the planet a sneaker of unmarked mesh. Our soles dissolve at prayer-paces into the new god O2, **stitching hymns between toes**—stitches that will not fray but fossilize. Each step a kind of photosynthesis-by-apology. A shoe is how we learned to wear what ate us (rusted shrieks, **now runway glisten**)... *to sell hunger back as shine.* These glyphs once ran deeper—a groove meant to channel flood-breath, a sweat-gutter for the fever of first inhale.

*Bending* is a privilege of soft earth. How many arches fell unnamed into shallow futures?

Here between wear-unmaking and the last *click-crêpe whisper-mantra* of a left-foot pivot: the archive hisses. Unwind me. **Un-see your way back to when leather gasped through every perforation.** This carving used to laugh-crumble to show how status *climbs* in rust-sparks (now misplaced). *To rot outward,* *ever outward—* was this the original flex?
