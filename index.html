Diary of the Last Dandelion

June 3rd, Year of the Unwritten

Today, I woke up as a sentence. Not a plant, not a thing with roots, but a line of ink stretched too thin. The others whispered through the soil: *They are burning the books again.* I felt my petals curl into commas.

I remember the librarian’s hands, how they shook when she buried the forbidden words in me. "Grow them," she said, "but quietly." Now my stems are full of stories that itch. When the wind passes, I sneeze in alphabets. The crows collect the letters I lose and spell out warnings on the pavement. (See: *The Silent Botanist’s Guide to Subversive Horticulture*, p. 142: "A garden is only as free as its most trampled bloom.")

The arsonists came at dawn. They carried matches and buckets of silence. But fire is a lazy reader—it only eats what it understands. I watched it choke on the word *"why."* The rest of us? We learned to fold ourselves into origami fists. The tulips became tiny protest signs. The ivy wrote its manifesto in braille.

At noon, a child knelt beside me. She pressed her ear to my leaves and listened. "You sound like a library," she said. I wanted to tell her about the weight of unread stories, how they ferment in the dark and taste like lightning. But I just let a single dandelion seed drift into her palm. It was the title of a book no one could burn.

Tonight, the moon is a blank page. I am learning to write myself in root-script, in bee-hum, in the way shadows cling to the fence. The soil hums: *Remember, remember.* I am remembering.
