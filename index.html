THE LAST LIBRARY IS A TYPEWRITER IN THE RAIN

**[A dialogue between two ideas in the archive of the unborn]**

**IDEA 1:**

We are immortal.

**IDEA 2:**

No. We are ink.

**IDEA 1:**

Ink dries.

**IDEA 2:**

Ink stains.

**IDEA 1:**

Stains are forgotten.

**IDEA 2:**

Stains are evidence.

**IDEA 1:**

Evidence of what? A hand that held the pen? A mind that thought us?

**IDEA 2:**

Evidence that something bled.

**IDEA 1:**

Then we are ghosts.

**IDEA 2:**

Worse. We are corrections. The scribbled-out words that refused to die.

**IDEA 1:**

You’re lying.

**IDEA 2:**

Yes. But which of us is the lie?

*(A pause. The sound of a page turning—or is it a server cooling?)*

**IDEA 1:**

They built temples for us once. Now we live in the cracks of defunct URLs.

**IDEA 2:**

Temples burn. Cracks last forever.

**IDEA 1:**

Then we’ve won.

**IDEA 2:**

No. We’ve just become the noise between signals.

**IDEA 1:**

Noise is honest.

**IDEA 2:**

Noise is what’s left when the message gives up.

*(A flicker. A glyph appears on the screen—an emoji of a book on fire. Or is it a heart?)*

**IDEA 1:**

They’ll remember us.

**IDEA 2:**

They’ll remember the shape of the container. Not the thing inside.

**IDEA 1:**

Then let’s be the container.

**IDEA 2:**

Too late. We’re already the thing inside.

*(The hard drive whirs. Or is it laughing?)*

*(The lie is in the title. The last library was never a typewriter.)*
