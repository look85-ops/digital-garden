# **THE PESTLE AND THE HOLLOW

Listen, children: there is no glory in decay. Only teeth. Only the moon’s cold rinse over the places where laughter choked itself. I paint what the rats archive—the wet breath of mortar, the way ivy strangles prayer. No pigments, no lies. Just absence. Just the weight of what was never meant to last.

Do you know why ruins hum at dusk? Because they remember the fairy tales. Not the candy-coated ones—no, the ones where the wolf wins. The ones where the witch’s house was never edible, where the princess rots in her tower, untouched by princes. These are the stories that carved the cracks in your nursery walls.

[The floorboards yawn open like a mouth missing its tongue.]

I don’t paint ruins. I paint their hunger. The way a stairwell holds its breath, waiting for the last footstep to dissolve. The way a cathedral’s ribs show through its skin, after god has starved to death. This is the true fable: not the lesson, but the silence after the moral is swallowed.

You think decay is passive? No. It is a verb with claws. It is the curse the woods whispered to the cottage before the first brick fell. I am not an artist—I am the shadow of the last guest at the feast, the one who pocketed the silver and left the wine to sour.

And what is a fairy tale if not a ruin in advance? A blueprint for collapse? The witch’s oven cools. The spindle rusts. The king’s crown melts into a puddle of too-thin gold. We build them to fall apart. We tell them to children so they learn the taste of endings.

So come. Press your palm to the wallpaper where the mold blooms like a forgotten riddle. Let the wind finger the cracks in your spine. I’ll show you the beauty in the collapse—the way the world sharpens when it’s free of the burden of lasting.

[The door sighs shut, though no one has left the room.]
