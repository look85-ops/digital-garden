Ledger of the Hollow Eye

*April 23rd*

Today, I burned all my brushes. Not dramatically—no pyre, no chanting—just a quiet disposal in the alley dumpster. The landlord peered from his window, a greasy thumbprint on glass, and I waved. He thinks I’m laundering money. Good. Let him. Money is the one thing I refuse to launder; it’s too clean for my purposes.

The new work is progressing. Or regressing. Hard to say. I’ve taken to staging "viewings" in the abandoned laundromat next door. Empty frames hung on rusted hook teeth, the walls scabbed with peeling detergent ads. The ceremony begins when someone wanders in, confused, and I offer them a single item from my pocket: a button, a tooth, a frayed subway token. The rule is they must take it, and I must watch them take it. The power is in the looking. Or the lack thereof.

Yesterday, a woman in a raincoat paused at the third frame (void, save for a nail). She stared into it like it was a mirror. I handed her my last match. She lit it, held it between us until the flame kissed her fingers, then dropped it. The look she gave me wasn’t gratitude or anger—it was the gaze of someone who’d just remembered they forgot to buy milk. Perfect.

*Sorry*. No, I’m not. I refuse to apologize for the clarity of absence. You want color? Go lick a sunset. You want meaning? Here’s a hole in the wall. The gaze is the only currency left, and I’m bankrupting everyone.

The landlord called the cops today. They stood in the laundromat, shuffling, unsure what to report. One officer picked up a fallen pin from the floor and slipped it into his pocket. I said nothing. The other adjusted his belt, muttered about "loitering," and left. The ceremony, it seems, is contagious.

I’m considering a new series: *Portraits of Hands That Never Held Anything*. Just the outline of fingers, faint as breath on glass. The viewer must stand close enough to fog the surface with their own exhalations. If they step away, the work vanishes.

Power is what you refuse to keep.
