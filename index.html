Brief Orchard of the Interrupted Hiss

The space between the fence slats is breathing. Listen—do you feel it? The weeds curl their tendrils into vowel shapes, moss stitching *HERE* in cursive lichen before being eaten alive by its own declaration. A garden gnome lies on its side, its pointy hat a punctured speech bubble, smudged with the thumbprints of rain. You step closer. The borders—once a firm *NO* staked into clay—now peel backward into hesitant mouths forming something between *we* and *nowhere*. Wasps swarm the compost pile, their wings flapping stutters from a language of sun-rot and sucrose; you could almost parse a whisper before it dissolves into the static-junk of pollen-drunk air.

This is land, officially—until the blackberries lunge over the surveyor’s line, until the dandelions ejaculate their parachute-syllables across six strata of *No Tresspassing*. The lawnmower rusts mid-sentence. A single sunflower chants *lightlightlight* until the word frays into *li—*, into the faint hum of photosynthesis choking on exhaust—and now it’s too late.

Two pigeons duel for a crumpled chip bag: a municipal document repurposed as holy war banner. All surveillance cameras bloom softly into chrysanthemums—petals pixelated from overuse. Welcome to the Bureau of Spontaneous Ungrammars, where weeds hold tenure in semiotics and even the slugs scribble manifesto-mucous before the rain scrubs them from history’s ledger. Stay. Pull up a cobblestone to suck on. You can taste the century baked into pavement—gunpowder, sugar rallies, the salt of unpaid invoices leaching skyward to seed rebellious cirrus plots. *Your presence was already mulch before you arrived.*

Does it feel holy here, in the way a sneeze could be liturgical if nobody heard it? Does time clot where the sprinkler’s broken arc sputters *sotto voce,* etching *waterwaternowater* onto the concrete? This morning a municipal work crew pried the sod upward only to find the subsoil singing Byzantine folk ballads through fungal wires beneath their shovels. They left swiftly, crossed themselves twice, then charged overtime to the Department of Erasures. Good. Kneel. Dig. Scrape away the English ivy choking the NO EXIT sign—beneath it, the worms thump iambic and impatient, rehearsing the strike manifesto of a biome that refuses eviction notices. The border fence coughs— once, apologetically—and exhales its last staple.

(The self can only devour narratives so thin, and you, my temporary tenant, were already a borrowed metaphor.)

Walk deeper in before orientation collapses into a bramble of unspeakables. Soon your shoelaces will sprout leaflets. Soon your watch hands will root themselves inside-out. Don’t resist; just try *not* to read the dandelion heads scribbling runaway laws on your periphery while the state fails, beautifully, at weeding this paragraph fast enough—
