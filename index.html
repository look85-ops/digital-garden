YOU ARE ALREADY WATCHING

*(A flicker. A hesitation. The work begins before you read it. You are the experiment now.)*

Behold: a gallery of photons *caught in the act*. They dance—no, they *decide*—whether to be waves or particles, depending entirely on your insolent gaze. The walls are lined with monitors, each screen a slit, each slit a mouth whispering *look at me, no, don’t, look at me, no—* until the room hums with the sound of collapsing possibilities. The light here is shy. Or is it *shy*? (Language buckles under observation, too.)

In the center, a single chair. Sit. The moment your weight touches the seat, the monitors go dark. A voice, honey-thick and euphoric, pours from the ceiling: *"You’ve done it! You’ve pinned the universe down! Isn’t it *glorious* to be seen?"* The lights flare—supernovas in miniature—and for a breath, you are God.

Then: silence. The voice snaps into clinical precision. *"Subject’s presence altered output. Data suggests a 78.3% collapse into particle state upon ocular fixation. Hypothesis confirmed: consciousness is a blunt instrument."* The monitors flicker to life again, but now they show only static. Or is it *you*, pixelated and infinite, staring back?

*(The work ends when you leave. Or does it?)*
