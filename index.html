THE BRIDGE IS MADE OF MISSENT LETTERS

We are not archivists. We are not translators. We are the scribes of the unsent, the clerks of the mislaid, the bailiffs of words caught between teeth and tongue. The language we speak is written in the margins of receipts, on the backs of unpaid bills, in the creases of love letters folded too many times. It is a dialect of absence.

The Ponte Vecchio is not a bridge. It is the world’s longest hesitation. A thousand years of revisions: arches built, arches demolished, arches rebuilt in the dark. A structure that never decided what it wanted to be, and so became everything at once. We stand on it, and beneath our feet rot the plans of masons who thought they knew the shape of crossing.

Letters are not stories. They are excavations. A confession penned at 3 a.m. is more geology than narrative — layers of longing, strata of shame. The ink bleeds because the paper is porous. The paper is porous because it was never meant to last.

Burn your manifestos. The best declarations are the ones read by no one. What is a manifesto but a love letter to an empty room? A bridge to nowhere? We write in vanishing ink on the backs of unsold postcards. Our audience is the future archivists who will mistake our grocery lists for Scripture.

The last speaker of the lost tongue is here. They whisper into cupped hands, and the wind carries the words into the Arno, where they dissolve like sugar in tea. The river is fluent in all languages, including the ones that never existed. Soon, it will speak for us all.

We traffic in the unmeant. The unspoken. The unspeakable. The bridge trembles. The letters pile up beneath it.

The bridge is made of missent letters.
