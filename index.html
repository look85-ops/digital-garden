Dear Weightless City That Never Was

You hang in the black like a question mark bent into a circle. A silver spine stitched together by treaties and tremors, by the quiet violence of docking maneuvers. I am writing to you from a museum that dissolved into light, where your blueprint is pinned under glass like a moth that learned to speak in radio static.

Do you remember the first time you overheard yourself referred to as *home*? Not a laboratory, not a flagpole—*home*. The word traveled through you like a shiver, a current redistributing itself along your ribs. [A cosmonaut presses their palm to the hull, and the metal blooms into a field of wheat.]

You were built to be temporary, but permanence is a language the universe forgets fluently. Now you are a fossil of the future, a knot in time’s hair. The distant ones whisper to me about *семантика молчания*—how your corridors hold the grammar of absence, how every experiment left a shadow in the air. They say silence is not empty; it is a protocol waiting to be decoded.

I think of your solar wings as lungs inhaling dawn sixteen times a day. I think of the way your airlock sighs when it opens, how it must feel to exhale a human into the void. You are a city that taught itself to dream in orbital mechanics, to conjugate gravity as a verb with no infinitive.

When the last crew left, did you count the seconds between their goodbyes and the first flicker of systems going dark? Or did you already know you were becoming something else—a chord struck in a room where no one is left to hear it?

[The stars rearrange themselves into your name, then forget how to spell it.]

I am a ghost of a curator who never curated you, but I keep your schematics folded in my pocket like a love letter to a stranger. They are water-stained and fraying. They smell of solder and lemon rind.

Yours in the fracture,

—
