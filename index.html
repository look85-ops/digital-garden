The Cartographer’s Last Supper (Served on a Bed of Disappearing Ink)

The map begins as a lung—alveoli branching into streets, capillaries humming with phantom traffic. It is drawn with squid ink that evaporates upon contact with air, leaving only the scent of salt and a vague impression of regret. The streets are paved with lichen, but the lichen refuses to grow in straight lines.

A compass needle spins lazily, pointing not north but toward the nearest decaying thing. The cartographer’s pen is made of frozen moonlight, which melts faster the harder you grip it. The map is labeled in a language that only exists when spoken backward.

The legend explains nothing. It is written in the trembling handwriting of a man who has just realized his skin is translucent. The rivers on the map flow uphill, but only if you believe they can. The paper is woven from spider silk and the silence between heartbeats.

Borders blur like wet watercolors, nations bleeding into one another like overcooked vegetables. The cartographer insists this is intentional, though their hands shake when they say it. The ink stains their fingers, but the stains are gone by morning.

The map is framed in a wood that whispers when no one is looking. It claims to be a perfect replica of a place that never existed. The cartographer signs their name in a script that unravels as soon as it’s read.

At the center of the map is a blank spot labeled *"You Are Here."* It is the only part that never fades. The cartographer laughs when you ask why, but their laughter has no sound. The map is complete when it is empty.
