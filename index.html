### **The Broadcast Dunes

A slow landslide of televisions, each screen still glowing, spills down a desert basin. The landslide is not a landslide but a *transmission*—an intentional arrangement of cathode-ray husks, their signals now replaced by the patient static of sand. The news cycle, here, is geological. Anchors fossilize mid-sentence. The weather report is just wind.

At the center, a single tower rises—not a broadcast tower, but a *listening* one. Its spines are tuned to the resonant frequency of eroding rock. Every hour, on the hour, it translates the desert’s murmurs into a 30-second bulletin: *"The mesa reports stability. The arroyo suggests caution. The dunes are, as always, withholding comment."* The broadcast is not aired. It is pressed into clay tablets and stacked in a room no one visits.

A door opens in the side of the tower. It was not there a moment ago. Inside: a chair, a small desk, and a switch labeled **IGNITION**. The switch does nothing. The manual beside it reads, in its entirety: *"If you are reading this, the system is working."*

Visitors (should there ever be visitors) are encouraged to take a tablet from the stack. Most crumble upon touch. The ones that don’t are illegible by design.
