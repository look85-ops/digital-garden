The Gilded Corset, or, How the Light Leaks Out

*—Diary of the Last Typesetter*

April something. The rib-thieves keep whispering in my teeth. I dream in bone-sighs, in whalebone corsets threaded with lead type, each stay a silent psalm. My fingers ache from setting words backward, like mirror-prayers. Tonight, I pressed my palm to the press and let it kiss me back—black ink rose in blooms beneath the skin. A bruise like a misplaced line of verse.

They say the corset is obsolete, but I’ve seen the old books breathe. The Gutenbergs gape open at midnight, their vellum fluttering like starched petticoats. The pages, oh—they curve. They curve like a waist cinched too tight, like a spine forced to confess. I stitch my notes into the gutters. The margins are hungry.

Today, I melted down a corset’s bones to cast new type. (The foundryman frowned, but didn’t ask.) The letters came out warped, leaning into each other like lovers or jailers. I set a love letter in it, a hymn to the way beauty breaks things. The ink smeared. It looked like weeping.

Funny, how rebellion lives in the body’s quietest corners. A typesetter’s revolt: I swapped the B for a swollen belly, the D for a door left ajar. Let them decode that.

The sky outside is the color of printer’s devils. My bed is a forme, my dreams loose type. I miss the ocean.
