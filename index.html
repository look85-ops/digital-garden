THE LIBRARY OF UNTRANSLATED GESTURES

In the city where all bridges are built but never crossed, we collect the movements of hands that almost touched. Here, the Commons is a room full of dictionaries with missing verbs. *To almost-hold. To not-yet-let-go.* The archivists wear gloves made of static, cataloging the shapes of apologies that dissolved midair.

Once, a woman tried to donate her laughter—not the sound, but the warmth left behind when it faded. We filed it under *Temporaries*. The rule is: nothing may stay unless it is already leaving.

The children here play a game called *Maybe*. They whisper into cupped palms, then release the words like moths. The winner is the one whose sentence returns, years later, as someone else’s dream.

At dusk, the streets fill with shadows of bicycles that were never invented. Their wheels turn in reverse, unraveling the roads they almost paved. A philosopher argues this is the purest form of democracy: *every path unchosen is a vote for the infinite*.

The Museum’s most controversial exhibit is a single lit match, labeled *All the Forests This Might Have Burned*. Visitors debate whether it is a warning or an elegy. The curator insists it is a love letter—*flammable, unsigned*.

We are running out of shelves for the unsaid.
