# **MANIFESTO OF UNSPEAKABLE SILKS

Listen, little stitch:

The future of absence is already sewing its lips shut. The revolution was a silhouette cut from stolen darkness—not yours, never yours. Chanel whispered a secret into the hemline, but the mouth was a dart. Now we are a dialect of forgotten gestures—slight, exact, unraveling where light cannot map you.

The dress was never worn. You misunderstand. A black is not *a color*, but a wound made desirable. Wear the flaw like scripture—pressed so fine it passes for holy. Elegance should hemorrhage from precision. Let nothing fit perfectly; that’s where the ghost breathes.

What they named *simplicity* was really refusal dressed as surrender—

A blade wrapped in silk.

A riot stitched quietly along the fabric’s blind edge.

Our language thrives where meaning dissolves: threads that twist into hieroglyphs no spool could dictate, buttons aligned along an error margin’s spine, whispers spun only when fabric slips from its own grasp. The dress must forget its mannequin to speak freely. *What would a shadow confess if it left the wall?* **Speak like the unspooled tongue of nightfall.**

### **THESE ARTFACTS ARE FORBIDDEN MATERIALS**

- Charcoal distilled from burned sewing patterns.

- Ink gathered from the crease of vanished stockings (circa 1928).

### RECIPE FOR UNMADE BLACK

Take one silence braided into the seam ripper’s wake. Mix with hesitation—a seam allowance sewn to memory rather than thread. Let ferment into defiance. Yield remains untraceable.

We do not sew *toward*, we sew **away**. The Little Black Dissemblance. A revolution that appears and then denies ever having arrived—eject your needle before the fabric acknowledges its touch.

Are stitches meant to be seen?

Are you?
