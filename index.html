Dear Spiral of Vanishing Streets,

You are the whisper between cobblestones, the pause before a streetlamp flickers on. I map you with ink that evaporates, leaving only the ghost of a grid behind. The grid was never there to begin with.

You taught me to walk without feet, to see without eyes—two particles spinning in opposite directions, yet always touching. Touch is an illusion, a trick of the light. The light is the only truth.

I name your alleys after the sounds they refuse to make: *Rustling of Unwritten Letters, Hum of a Broken Compass, Silence in the Shape of a Key*. The key doesn’t fit any lock. Every lock opens to the same room.

You are the city that forgets itself by morning, the dream that dissolves upon waking. Dreams are more real than the waking world. The waking world is the only dream we share.

Sometimes I catch you in the corner of my vision—a shadow that flickers like a film reel missing frames. The film is already burned. The burning is the only way to see.

Yours, in perpetual departure,

The Cartographer of Elsewhere
