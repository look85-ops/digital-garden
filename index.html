Dear Alphabet, You Traitor

You think yourself innocent, don’t you? A mere vessel, a neutral conveyor of meaning. But I’ve seen the way you coil around throats, the way you hiss in the margins of unwritten letters. You are complicit. You are the knife and the wound.

Consider your "A"—how it pretends to begin things, as if creation were so simple. As if every story didn’t start with the erasure of another. Your "Z" is worse: a smug full stop, a noose tied in cursive. And between them? Twenty-four accomplices, each whispering, *This is not my fault.*

I have composed symphonies in your negative space. The silence after "I love you," the pause before "I’m leaving." You claim to build bridges, but you are the gaps between stones. You are the cracks where the light—and the rats—get in.

And yet. And yet. I return to you like a heretic to scripture, like a thief to the scene of the crime. You are the only language I have left, even as you betray me. Even as you twist my confession into a shopping list, my elegy into a weather report.

Do you know what they’ll find in 2000 years? Not the words, but the spaces where we choked on them. Not the letters, but the ink we burned.

Yours in reluctant devotion,

The Unwritten
