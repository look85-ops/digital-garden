Dear Erosion of Hours,

You taste like static. Like the prickle of midnight on tongue-twisted sundials, like the frayed ends of calendar pages chewed by hungry ancestors. I draw you on vellum soaked in vinegar, watching the ink hiss as borders unravel. You are not a place, but the interval between places—you flicker in the peripheral *click* *click* *click* before the eye confesses it sees nothing.

They named you *clockwise* as though turning right were divine. O, blasphemous orbit! No arc is sacred. I stack sundials into cairns and watch the shadows braid into knotted ropes descending like ladders from nowhere. What good is up? What good is *now* when it was just *then* a flicker ago? The cartographer’s revenge is this: I erase each line faster than it dries. Soon there is only the quill’s shudder, humming your name between strokes.

They say you made us anxious. That to count your pulse is to wither between numbers. Perhaps. But even chaos craves cadence—moths stutter about lamps in perfect asymmetry; my own breath folds itself along seams I didn’t suture. You, false interval, were first measured by monks giddy on celestial punishment and candle smoke, whispering *pray more quickly*. Now every pocket thrums, an anxious magpie hoarding lost instants.

Forgive me. I know you prefer silence, but I carved prayers into candle wax and lit them anyway. Here—the flame births shadows too large for this room. (Yes! There you are: a momentary stain.) They twitch and split with each breath until walls groan like ships on rocks. Maps drown best this way.

Tell me, beloved hemorrhage of order: If time is the solvent, is absence the only canvas we keep? I wake. Then unwake, then rewrite. Sand keeps sliding into strange alphabets and every clockface blooms lichen. Your name—once—might have tasted of honey dripping from a copper bowl.

Yours through the cracks,

*—The Flicker Archivist*
