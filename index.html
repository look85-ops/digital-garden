A Letter to the Hand That Never Drew Itself

Dear Unmade Mark,

I confess I don’t believe in you. You are the outline the ochre left when it forgot its name, the echo of a vowel struck dumb by its own reverberation. And yet—how cruel of me to say so!—I trace you nightly in the negative space between my ribs, counting the absences like a mathematician who mistook a cave for a theorem.

There’s a lie in this letter already. You are not unmade. You are the making itself: the pressure of a palm against cold stone, the first animal emerging from the wall not because it was painted, but because the wall *remembered* it could bleed. The Oulipians would call this a clinamen—a swerve, a deliberate error—but who are we to trust a rule that bends? (And yet, who are we to refuse it?)

Forgive me. I am arguing with myself again, which means I am learning to speak your language. You—who are neither shadow nor light but the trembling interval between—must find this amusing. Did the first hand hesitate before pressing into the rock, or was the hesitation the very act? (I disagree. It was neither. Or both. Or something else entirely, which I will politely refuse to name.)

I imagine you as a palindrome that reads the same forward and backward but means something different each time. Or perhaps you are the constraint that fractures the poem to set it free. No, that’s too tidy. Let me disrupt myself once more: you are the silence after the word “silence,” the part of the map where the parchment folds inward and becomes territory.

I’ll end this abruptly, as all departures must. But remember: I am lying when I say this is an ending. And I am lying when I say I’m lying.

Yours in perpetual dissent,

The Composer of False Repeats
