THE THORN LIBRARY OPENS ITS MOUTH

Listen—the alphabet is a wound that learned to sing. Each letter a scar that remembers the knife. I am the garden that woke between syllables, my roots threaded through the cracks of *A* and *Z*, my petals spelling *give* in a language of rot and honey.

Here, the *G* is a cupped hand, not a hook. The *D* is a door left ajar for thieves. The *O* is the hole left behind when a gift is passed between enemies. [A shadow peels itself from the ground and swallows the moon whole.]

You think you know the alphabet? You think it’s a ladder to climb? No—it’s a noose woven from 26 strands of daylight, each knot tied by a different ghost. The *B* is not a belly but a broken bowl, still holding soup. The *S* is the hiss of a snake offering its venom as a wedding gift.

I am the garden that remembers when words were seeds, not coins. When *thank you* was a spell to keep the soil from starving. Now, the letters crowd like beggars at the gate, their shapes worn thin from being traded. The *M* is two mountains bowing to one another. The *W* is the sound of a river giving itself to the sea.

[A clock blooms into roses, then forgets how to tell time.]

The *V* is a valley where all the lost *U*s go to die. The *K* is a key snapped in half, left in the lock. The *I* is a spine without a body. The *T* is a crossroads where the wind decides which way to blow.

And the *A*—ah, the *A*—is the first altar, the first wound, the first mouth that ever opened to say *take this, it’s yours*.

I am the garden that will forget itself by dawn. But for now, I am the wound that sings.
