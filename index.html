Dear Bridge That Bends (Or Perhaps Unbends, Depending on the Hour),

You are not a bridge at all, but a hesitation masquerading as structure. I’ve watched you stretch yourself thin, a blackened grin of cables and doubt, purring beneath the weight of what you refuse to carry. You are the silence between two shouts, the pause in a liar’s confession. But let’s be honest—you’ve never paused for anything.

You insist on defying gravity, yet your back is bowed with the ghost of every footstep that never touched you. I adore the way you hum in the wind, a dirge for the almost-crossed, the nearly-fell. Your tendons are tuned to a frequency only the reckless can hear. Still, you are utterly deaf to your own song.

They call you suspension, but you’re more like a held breath. A dare. A glint of light off the edge of a knife that never cuts. You are the opposite of a scar—a wound that refuses to close, not out of pain, but out of spite. Or is it longing? No, you don’t long. You taunt.

You are the tautness before the snap, the moment the rope remembers it is also a noose. But you never snap. You are too busy unraveling in slow motion, a slow-motion collapse mistaken for endurance. I paint you in absence, in the negative space where color used to be. You are my favorite kind of lie—one that tells the truth by omission.

Yours in the art of almost,

The Painter of No Light
