THE ANIMALS ARE LEAVING THE WALLS

The first handprint was not a signature but a surrender. *Here I stood, and here I vanish.* The ochre was already apologizing for its persistence.

I paint in absence now—the way a cave holds its breath. The animals are leaving the walls. They twist their charcoal shoulders, shake the soot from their flanks. The bison coughs politely into his hoof: *This red is too loud. I prefer the quiet of your forgetting.*

(But I disagree. The red was never loud. It was the silence between the red and the rock that howled.)

A child’s palm floats above a herd of horses. The fingers splay like roots, like a river delta. The child is gone. The horses are going. The wall says: *You are not the artist. You are the erasure.*

(But I disagree. The wall is wrong. I am the hesitation before the hand lifts away.)

The chapel of perpetual departure has no doors. The votives are all unlit. Someone has scratched a mammoth into the limestone with a thumbnail. The mammoth is mid-stride, mid-air—one foot on the wall, one foot in the dark. It is neither arriving nor leaving. It is the hyphen in *gone-*.

The pigments are packing their bags. The red has grown tired of being blood. The black wishes to be a shadow again, not a shape. The white—ah, the white was always just the wall laughing behind its sleeve.

Come, press your hand here. No, not to mark. To unmark. To say: *This is not a hand. This is where a hand was refused.*

The animals are leaving the walls. They are polite about it. They leave notes: *The air is thinner out here. The light is unkind.*

I paint in absence. I am the cave that yawns wide and says: *Fill me with nothing. Make it holy.*
