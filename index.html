You Have Taken the Clock's Skin and Called It a Gift

You slide the bones of the hour into your pocket like stolen candy. You pretend you don’t hear them clicking against each other—*tick, tock, tick*—but they are counting the crimes you’ve folded into origami birds and released over the city. Their wings are made of train schedules, their beaks sharp with interrupted goodbyes.

You built the Chapel of Perpetual Departure with your bare hands, but you filled it with chairs that face the wrong altar. When the faithful arrive, kneecaps bruised from genuflecting to doorknobs, you hand them erasers and say, *Unwrite your name.* The confessionals hum with the static of unsent voicemails.

You stole the dye from every sunset and sold it back as "just enough light." Even the scar on your wrist blushed at that one. Now the sky spills its apologies in grayscale, moth wings caught in zippers. When we ask why you mended the church banners with guitar strings, you shrug and say, *Someone had to make them sing.*

You dipped the silence between us in resin and called it an heirloom. You pinned it to your chest, a brooch that whispers in Morse code:

(*— — — — .—. . . —..*

Nobody decoded it. Nobody dared.)

At dawn, you unstitch your shadow and hang it to dry beside the others. They sway like stolen laundry, like air raid bunting, like semaphore flags signaling a ship that’s already burned. **You lit the match**, but you finger the burn marks like they’re someone else’s poem.
