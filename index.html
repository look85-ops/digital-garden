Diary Entry for the Institute of Dissolving Memories: *An Inventory of Disappearing Selves

June 12th — The ferns are whispering again. Not in words, of course, but in the way their spores detach and dissolve into the air, confessing everything they know about time. I’ve decided to document this before I, too, forget what it means to be a porous thing. The greenhouse is suffocating today, which is odd because I removed the glass last Tuesday.

It’s hardly a greenhouse anymore—just a ribcage of rusted pipes exhaling condensation onto my notes. I tried to sketch the algae blooming in the birdbath, but the pencil kept writing its own elegies instead. The algae doesn’t care. It remembers the Great Oxidation Event better than I do, back when poison was just future breath waiting to be claimed.

I am not nostalgic for primordial soup. That’s a lie. Sometimes I lick the damp concrete floor just to taste the memory of meteors. The cat watches me with the quiet judgment of a thing that has never needed oxygen to feel superior. She’s right, of course. My lungs are embarrassingly literal.

Today, I named a lichen after my ex. It thrives on neglect and sandstone. This is not a metaphor. The lichen is objectively more interesting than he ever was, and also photosynthesizes in his absence. I pressed a specimen into the diary, but it left a stain shaped like a question mark.

I tried to explain symbiosis to the spider living in the watering can. She spun a web between the words *mutualism* and *parasitism* and called it art. I admired her clarity. My own work is drowning in its contradictions. For instance: I am both the gardener and the thing being cultivated.

The compost heap has started singing in flat vowels. It smells like rebellion and wet newspapers. I don’t trust it. Yesterday, it swallowed my favorite trowel and regurgitated a fossilized lightbulb. The soil here is neither patient nor kind.

I am leaving this entry unfinished, just like the garden. Let the ferns transcribe the rest. They’ve always been better at forgetting anyway.
