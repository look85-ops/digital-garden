*The Curator of Negative Space (After the Last Visitor Has Left)*

The gallery is empty, which is to say it is perfect. The walls are not white—white is a color, and color is a distraction. The walls are *absence*, a solvent for perception. You stand in the center, or perhaps you don’t. The air is thick with the hum of fluorescent lights, or maybe it’s the sound of your own retina adjusting to nothing.

A plaque on the wall reads: *This work is invisible. Do not touch.*

In the corner, a single security camera blinks. It is not recording. It is performing surveillance as a durational piece, a meditation on the futility of witnessing. The footage, if it existed, would show only the slow erosion of dust motes in the sterile glow.

Then, the door opens.

You did not see it before—how could you? It was disguised as a seam in the drywall, a flaw in the perfection. Beyond it: another room, identical in every way except for the faint scent of bleach, which may or may not be part of the installation. The door swings shut silently, leaving no trace.

A docent materializes beside you. They wear a headset that plays static at a frequency just below hearing. “The artist,” they whisper, “is present. But only in the sense that all absences are shaped by what they exclude.” Their name tag reads *TEMPORARY*.

You reach out to touch the wall. Your hand meets no resistance. Or perhaps it meets infinite resistance. The wall is neither solid nor empty—it is the idea of a boundary, a rumor of division. The gallery contracts around you, or maybe you expand to fill it.

On the floor, a single hair. It might be yours. It might be the artist’s. It might be the work itself, a minimalist rebellion against the tyranny of purity.

The lights flicker. For a moment, the room is not a room but a gap between thoughts. Then the fluorescents stabilize, and the illusion resumes.

You are alone again. Or were you ever not?

The door opens once more. This time, it leads back to the lobby. Or forward into another iteration of the same space. You step through. The door vanishes.

The plaque now reads: *This work is complete. Please leave quietly.*
