Syllable Fen

The mapmaker scratches their latest failure into the damp slate of the ruin’s only pillar. Glyphs slouch like drunk saints as the stone softens beneath the knife. Cartography of the sinking. The island vanishes even as I name it—not because the tide is patient, but because the tide *plays*. Its teeth leave the coastline ambiguous, puckered with revision. (Once, we called erosion “murder”; then “history”; now, lunch.)

A boy steps close, peeling a pomegranate with dirty thumbs. *It eats back*, he notes approvingly—smashes the fruit and sticks it, weeping juice, to the pillar. A new continent blooms red under the cipher of my knife. *Quick now*, mutters the rock-chewer between licks, *write what you have before the pages molder*.

So here rests *Here*, provisional as breath, half-liquid already:

• *The Market of Teeth*, where vendors tally debts in molars. Some still singing under glass.

• *The Well of Hands*, fingers knotting upward when it rains—strangers’ fingers, always strangers. This isn’t an allegory, or at least not the one you think. Ask me later [wet laughter dissolves sentence].

• *The Ribbon Road*, a path that unfurls when the horizon grows bored. Its silk tastes of metal and lemons when torn during shortcuts. Pilgrims choke beautifully.

Each landmark smears at mention. This is the joke. Writing is digestion in reverse: spit the ghost, make it food again. My hand hesitates, and he grabs the knife. *Your towns float like oil.* Carves an ocean, just one furious groove, then the tide exhales and the slate goes sleek-cheeked once more.

The island winks at its myth: [rusty giggle lost midscript].
